# The abstraction ladder

Six families of illustration for a concept section, ordered roughly from most literal
to most abstract. Phase 3 draws at least one candidate from each family before choosing
three. The families differ in **what evidence they offer**, not in how they look.

| Family | Evidence it offers | Good for | Implies falsely when misused | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product-true | "this is what you will see" | features, onboarding, hero, anything the reader will meet in the product | a screen or control the product does not have; a number that reads as a metric | medium; decays when the product changes, so name what it copies |
| Mechanism | "this is how it works" | pipelines, hand-offs, sync, anything with order, overlap or waiting | a sequence the system does not follow; timing that implies speed | medium-high; needs a beat list, step controls, a still form |
| Transformation | "this is what changes" | automation, cleanup, extraction, before/after value | a before that is a strawman; an after nobody gets on day one | low-medium |
| Spatial metaphor | "this is how the parts relate" | architecture, ecosystems, integrations, hubs and layers | structure the system does not have (a hub where there is none) | medium |
| Physical metaphor | "this is like something you know" | abstract capabilities with no screen (memory, trust, reasoning) | properties of the object that the product lacks (a relay team drops batons) | low-medium; ages well |
| Data-as-art | "this is the real shape of it" | products whose data is the story (events, connectors, logs) | data volumes or variety the reader will not have | medium; needs real nouns |

## Choosing the triad

The default is **one product-true + one mechanism or transformation + one metaphor**.
It gives the owner three different *arguments* for the same claim:

- product-true answers "what will I see?",
- mechanism/transformation answers "how does it work / what changes?",
- metaphor answers "why does it matter?".

Break the default when the brief says so:

- **No real artifact** (the capability has no screen): replace product-true with
  data-as-art or a second metaphor.
- **A state, not a process**: replace mechanism with transformation.
- **The page already leans on one family** (five sections of mockups): weight the triad
  away from it. Distinctness from the rest of the page is scored.

## Tests every candidate must answer

1. **Would it change?** If the claim were false, would the picture be different?
2. **Swap test.** Would it read wrong beside the neighbouring section's claim?
3. **Caption test.** One line of what to notice, not a restatement of the heading.
4. **Still test.** Take one frame (thumbnail, reduced motion, print). Does the claim
   survive? For a mechanism, it survives only if the art gives time an axis or ships a
   filmstrip.
5. **False-implication test.** Name what the picture implies that the product does not
   do. If it cannot be removed, the candidate is out.
