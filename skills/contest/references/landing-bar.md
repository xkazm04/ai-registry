## The bar for a landing page

This is a representative page. A stranger decides in three seconds whether the person behind it
has taste, so visual quality, motion and creativity are what is judged hardest here. Utility still
counts, but it is the floor, not the prize.

What won before, in the owner's words: **"visually dominant with nested layers and custom
illustrative elements"**, together with "creative and execution excellence".
In practice:

- **Visually dominant.** The composition owns the whole viewport like a film frame or poster:
  lit, graded and layered in depth. It should not read as a dark template with cards on it.
  One dominant element at a time, typography at display scale.
- **Nested layers.** The page has levels you move through: an overview, then an item that opens
  into its own composed scene or surface, then detail, with choreographed transitions between
  them. Show the way back at every level.
- **Custom illustrative elements.** Each item gets artwork drawn for it: SVG, canvas or CSS, in
  its own colour and motif, such as a cut-paper robot for a dev tool or a book-tree for a story
  app. Stock icons alone are the anti-pattern. Label designed art as stylised; never pass it off
  as a real screenshot.
- **Motion that carries the story.** Arrival, choosing, opening and returning are each a beat.
  Particles and light are smooth and soft, not aggressive. Any intro is skippable and hands over
  control within about four seconds, and `prefers-reduced-motion` gets a calm version.
- **A composition that holds at every size.** Fixed proportions that scale consistently from
  1280x800 to 2560x1440, with no stretching on large screens and no clipping or overlap with the
  corner controls on small ones.
- **The item under attention takes the dominant space.** A hovered item's name belongs in the
  centre stage, not in a caption under it.
