## 1.1.0 - 2026-11-09 - paypal
- Director audit of one real census context (4 files), two runs of gpt-6.1-sol at medium. Run 1, with an `other` kind and a loose definition of a part: 7 parts, 3 wrong, all falsely outbound (a helper, a game screen, a button). ## 1.2.0 - 2026-11-09 - ascent
- Full-tree census of ascent: 113 chunks, 2615 of 2615 scoped files returned to Codex, 1237 parts, 4 dropped for a quote not in the file. Coverage is solved. The audit still failed, 5 of 15 wrong (33.3%), so no ledger record and no leads.
- The error moved. By audited relation: `reinvents` 4 of 5 wrong, `no-standard` 1 of 5, `consumes` 0 of 5. Reading every file stopped the misses and exposed over-claiming: a standard gets applied to something it does not govern.
- The main shape is a list judged by the table standard: 86 of 440 `reinvents` are `list-or-feed` against `table`, and 98 name a pagination or row-window rule. The audited wrong ones were a terminal formatter, a bare layout primitive, a briefing card and a demo feed. The table golden path itself says a list is not a table. Asking for a named missed rule does not stop this, because the rule is real and only its applicability is wrong.
- Adjudication moves parts in one direction: it looks for a standard to attach, and 121 of 235 moved. It also kept a retry wrapper as `no-standard` while the retry-backoff subject covers it, so it does not reliably find the standard either.
- Measured cost: census 145.0M input tokens, 132.1M cached, 0.59M output, about 1.3M per chunk. Adjudication 36.5M input, 33.9M cached, 0.09M output. An adjudication batch costs about 2.3M because Codex searches the registry for each one: it is not a small add-on. Forecast the two passes together.
- A coverage probe built from a file's longest line under-reported 80% when that line held non-ASCII that the Windows console mangled. Match a share of the file's ASCII lines instead.
- Added the rejudge pass on the same scan: 440 `reinvents` gave 131 upheld with a rule quoted from the standard, 12 follow it, 256 not governed, 41 upheld without a verifiable quote. It took 19.9M input tokens (17.6M cached), 0.66M per batch against adjudication's 2.3M, because each batch was sorted by subject and named its standard files instead of searching. Give Codex the addresses.
- All four list-as-table errors from the failed audit came back `not-governed`. That is the control, not the proof, since the brief was written from them. A freshly salted sample audited 4 of 15 wrong (26.7%), a pass by one row. By relation: `reinvents` 1 of 5, `no-standard` 1 of 5, `consumes` 2 of 5.
- The over-attachment problem is now in `consumes`: a chart citing the delivery-analytics metric standard with no accessible table, a `useMemo` hook citing `table`. A `consumes` with a subject and no `builtOn` got no second look, so its count is the least trustworthy number in the report.
- `apply-rejudge` sends an unverifiable upheld accusation to `no-standard`, which is wrong: the audit found one where the standard clearly governs and a race really exists. Those 41 need their own state. `not-governed` also inflates the outbound count, 411 of which most are local code the registry should not absorb. Do not read outbound as a backlog.
- 69 rows repeat another row's file and name, because a part's callers and its definition fall in different chunks. Deduplicate before counting.

## 1.3.0 - 2026-11-09 - golden set
- The judging chain ran four times on 39 known-answer rows (30 audited ascent parts, 9 planted), 2.8M to 4.7M input tokens a run. Accuracy went 74.4%, 84.6%, 89.7%, 89.7%. Inbound precision went 75%, 90%, 83.3%, 83.3%. The fixed bar needs 85% and 90%, so 1.3.0 has not earned a full-scan run.
- Every gain came from a harness defect, not a brief: Codex's Windows shell turns an em dash into a hyphen and a middle dot into a control character, so verbatim rule quotes and code quotes failed exact matching. Compare letters and digits only. Registry search keyed on a part's own name returned a civic scraper for a retry wrapper; key it on the kind and the capabilities. Search also picked bounded-enumeration as "the standard for a table"; map kind to subject explicitly.
- The confirm pass tilts toward accusing: given a real table that follows the pagination standard, it found a different broken rule on each run (columns as markup, then error states, then a missing caption). Two of those were genuine flaws in the planted fixture and were fixed; the third is minor. A pass that asks "does this follow the standard" over a whole golden path almost always finds a sentence it breaks. Asking for harm cut style rules but not this.
- One ascent row has been upheld by both judges in all four runs against the Director's recorded answer (`useRepoSegmentsPanel`, client-state invalidation). The answer was not changed after seeing scores; it needs a deliberate second look, not a fix to pass.
- Contested accusations become `unverified` and count as wrong in the golden score. Two of the 39 sit there on every run. Abstaining is honest, but it costs accuracy by design.
- Scoping an accusation to the cited technique's file (run 5) passed the golden bar: 89.7% accuracy, 10 of 11 accusations right. The full ascent chain then ran fresh from the surveys: 98.8M input tokens (90.3M cached), every batch done, 1237 rows to 1171 after 66 merges, 155 accusations left standing, 83 unverified.
- The fresh 50-part audit (10 per relation, new salt) was 7 wrong, 14% overall, and failed only on `reinvents`, 3 of 10 against 15%. `consumes` 1 of 10, `no-standard` 0, `not-governed` 1, `unverified` 2. Passing the golden set did not carry over to the accusations in the wild.
- All three wrong accusations failed the consequence test and nothing else: a chrome rule on a server-rendered table with no data in flight, pagination on a table bounded by lane concurrency, a retry-classification rule on a dev seed script calling its own local server. Two of the three were agreed by the second judge, so repetition shares the first judge's blind spot instead of catching it. The next lever is the consequence test, made concrete: the judge must name the input that triggers the harm here and where it comes from.
- The 50 audited rows were added to the golden set, now 79 rows. The ascent census is recorded as failed: no ledger record, no leads.

## 1.4.0 - 2026-11-10 - golden set
- Required every accusation to quote the line where its harmful input originates, checked in code, and listed the bounded inputs that clear a part (server-rendered, capped by the code's own limit, constants, a dev script on its own server). On the 88-row golden set, 7.4M input tokens: 75.0% accuracy, 8 of 9 accusations right. Fails the bar (85%, 90%), so no ascent run.
- The mechanical check never fired (0 of 23 kept accusations lacked a real trigger line). Judges can always quote a fetch. All of the effect came from the exclusion list, and it over-corrected: 6 true accusations were cleared with "your trigger rule excludes bounded inputs", including a real silent-zero violation dismissed on scope. A list of escape shapes becomes the judge's argument, not its test.
- 7 of the 8 golden rows whose recorded truth is `unverified` were scored wrong. A borderline row audited as "unverified is fine" has no single right answer, so as a golden truth it only rewards repeating the abstention. That is a defect in `golden-add`, found after this score, and it does not change this result: without those rows the run is still about 81%.
- The 1.3.0 briefs were never scored on the 88-row set, and its 50 newest rows came from 1.3.0's own output, so no fair baseline exists for this comparison yet.
- InFlightLanes, recorded as bounded by concurrency, was accused again with a new argument: the pulse keeps completed lanes, so the table grows with the selection, not the slot count. It needs a deliberate re-read of the code, not a truth change to fit a score.
- During this change I found that the 1.3.0 "Accuse from" paragraph had gone into each brief's header, which `templateOf` drops, so golden run 5 passed on the code-side scoping alone. Now it is in the prompt.

## 1.4.1 - 2026-11-10 - golden set, three arms
- Same 88 rows (80 scored, 8 borderline set aside), one scorer, run in sequence. A, 1.3.0 briefs at medium: 87.5% accuracy, 17 of 20 accusations right (85%). B, 1.4.1 at medium: 87.5%, 16 of 18 (88.9%). C, 1.4.1 at high: 87.5%, 16 of 18 (88.9%). All three pass accuracy and miss the 90% accusation bar. The new harm brief cut one false accusation and cost one true one. High effort changed which rows were wrong but not how many.
- Input tokens: A 9.1M, B 12.0M, C 16.3M. Asking for `guards`, `trigger` and `harm` adds about a third to medium, and high adds another 36% with output nearly doubled (61k to 95k). On this set high effort bought nothing measurable.
- The code check on trigger lines has still fired 0 times in four runs. Its value is in making the judge write the path down, which made the two persistent misses checkable by reading: one judge argument held up (below), one did not (a seed script with no retry, accused under the retry rule on the strength of a manual re-run).
- `useRepoSegmentsPanel`, upheld by every judge in every run since 1.3.0, was re-read deliberately: the comparison picker re-renders a server component with fresh segments, and the unkeyed client manager keeps its `useState` copies, so it is stale whenever another writer changed segments. The recorded answer moved to borderline (`reinvents` or `not-governed`). It was changed after these arms were scored, so it counts only toward the next run.
- The lane-table row was re-read before the arms ran: the table holds every lane of the current cycle, one per selected repo, and no cap on repos per run was found. Its recorded reason ("bounded by concurrency") was wrong, and it now accepts `reinvents` too.
- Fresh confirming golden run D (1.4.1, medium, every stage new, after both re-reads): 87.5% accuracy, 16 of 17 accusations right (94.1%). The only wrong accusation left is the seed script, wrong in every run, brief and effort.
- Full ascent re-judge on the 2026-11-09 scan and adjudication: 83.4M input tokens (76.1M cached), 70 of 70 batches done. 155 accusations standing, 76 unverified. The trigger check fired once in 1171 rows: a sample-data table paged by five, accused by the confirm judge, was held back as unverified.
- First census audit to pass the gate: 50 parts not in the golden set (10 per relation, salt d141), 5 wrong (10%). `consumes`, `no-standard` and `not-governed` 0 of 10 each, `reinvents` 1 of 10 (a five-button diagram on a sample-data playground, accused under canvas accessibility), `unverified` 4 of 10.
- `unverified` is where the error now sits: 4 of 10 were clear calls (a CLI with no retry, a terminal benchmark chart, the sample table above, a human-approval flow that visibly follows its standard). Abstention is safe, since nothing acts on it, but a pile that is 40% clear cases is Director work the judges should have done. It is the next lever, before outbound counts are trusted.
- The judges filled `trigger` mechanically when the harm is not input-driven: a motion-token accusation quoted a contributors query as its trigger. For design-system rules the field is noise. The accusation itself held as borderline, since ascent has no motion ladder at all.
- Several accusations are borderline by nature (a per-repo table with no page bound, where an org may have tens or hundreds of repos). The golden set now records a set of acceptable answers for these, so later runs are not scored against one side of a judgment call.
- The 50 audited rows joined the golden set, now 129 ascent rows plus 9 planted.
