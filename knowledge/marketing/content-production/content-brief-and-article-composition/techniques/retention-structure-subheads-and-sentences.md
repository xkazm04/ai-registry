# Retention structure: subheads, sentences, and something to look at

A search-driven article is read by a scanner, and the body is paced for the scanner
first: a subhead at a regular interval whose first words carry the point, sentences
short enough to be understood on one pass, paragraphs of one idea, and something to
look at before the eye tires of text. The pattern is measured; most of the intervals
are convention and this technique says which is which.

## The rules and their footing

| Rule | Footing |
| --- | --- |
| A subhead every 150-300 words | The heading-to-heading scanning pattern is measured in eye-tracking; the interval is practitioner convention |
| Information-carrying word first in every subhead | Measured: the vertical scan runs down the left edge and reads two or three words per line |
| Subhead stack reads as the whole story alone | Convention, derived from the measured pattern |
| Sentences average 14-17 words; rewrite past 25 | Measured: comprehension near full at eight words, about ninety percent at fourteen, collapsing past twenty-one |
| Paragraphs: one idea, two to four sentences, idea first | Convention derived from measured skipping of second ideas |
| Line length 50-75 characters | Measured in reading-speed studies; a layout rule, not a writing rule |
| Something to look at within every ~350 words | Convention; the failure it prevents - a page that ships with every section present and 9,000 pixels of unbroken paragraphs - is real |
| No more than two text-only blocks in a row | Convention, same failure |
| Bold one or two phrases per section | The contrast mechanism is measured; the count is convention |
| Every image carries information | Measured: readers scrutinise content images and ignore decorative ones |
| Table of contents past a six-minute read | Convention |
| Bullets only for genuinely parallel items | Measured: about 70% of a list is examined versus 55% of paragraph text; chopping prose into bullets fragments it |

## Procedure

1. **Read only the subheads.** If they do not tell the story, rewrite them before
   touching the body. Cute or clever subheads collapse the scanning pattern back into
   the worse one and get treated like advertisements.
2. **Front-load every unit** - page, section, paragraph, sentence. The second idea in a
   paragraph is skipped; put it in its own paragraph. A sentence starting "However" or
   "In order to" wastes the three words that get read.
3. **Measure sentence length and paragraph length mechanically**, not by feel. Average
   words per sentence over the prose the piece ships; count paragraphs over four
   sentences. A writer's sense of rhythm is not a check.
4. **Walk the rendered page as blocks and count text-only runs.** A block counts as
   visual only if it contains something to look at - an image, a table, a form, a
   numbered list, a figure - never because a component is named "card". A bordered box
   of grey text is text.
5. **Place the second-most-important thing at the midpoint**, not the end; median
   scroll is half the page.
6. **Distribute retention devices through the body.** Attrition is continuous, not
   one cliff. Close every loop within a few hundred words - the pull to resume an
   interrupted thing is measured; the claim that unfinished things are remembered
   longer did not survive meta-analysis - and end sections on tension rather than
   resolution. Cut mid-article digressions; every sidebar is a fresh exit.
7. **Replace category nouns with instances.** Concrete wording is stored as language
   and image both and measurably lifts engagement; "a plumber's van" beats "a service
   vehicle" everywhere.

## Decision rules

- **When a page has every required section and still reads as a wall, the failure is
  layout, not copy; fix it with a visual block, a table or a list, never by cutting
  the sections, because the check that caught it counts blocks and the check that
  demanded the sections counts sections.**
- **When average sentence length exceeds the warning band, split sentences rather than
  delete them, because the comprehension curve is about length per sentence, not
  words per page.**
- **When a section is over the subhead interval, add a subhead only if a new point
  begins there; otherwise cut, because a subhead without a point breaks the story the
  stack is meant to tell.**
- **When a rule above is put into a prompt, put its check into something that runs,
  because instructions get skipped and a counter does not.**

## When NOT to use

- **A short answer post under a few hundred words.** The quick answer, one section and
  the FAQ carry it; a subhead every 150 words on a 300-word page is scaffolding without
  a building.
- **Structured reference content** - a specification table, a price list - where the
  reader is looking something up rather than reading through. Tables and anchors are
  the structure; prose pacing rules do not apply.
- **Applying the rules to somebody else's approved copy during an audit.** Rhythm
  fixes touch body sentences, and body sentences a person approved are out of bounds
  for a mechanical pass; route the page back to its author with the measurement.
