# Humour costs credibility, so the proof zones stay straight

Humour buys attention and liking and spends source credibility. A meta-analysis of 369
correlations on humour in advertising (Eisend, 2009) measured a large effect on
attention and positive affect - a correlation near 0.42 - about twice the effect on
attitude toward the content as on attitude toward the business, no effect on liking of
the advertiser, and a **significant reduction in perceived source credibility**. The
transfer from advertising to editorial prose is an inference, and it is labelled as
one; the direction of the trade is not in doubt. The composition rule that follows is
a zoning rule, not a volume rule: the passages carrying the page's credibility, and
the passages a machine will lift verbatim, stay straight; the prose between them may
be as funny as the brand's voice allows.

## The zones

| Zone | Register | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The quick answer under the headline | Straight | Carries the page's credibility and is the passage most quoted verbatim |
| The proof line | Straight | A credential told as a bit reads as a bit |
| Prices, numbers, tables | Straight | A joke beside a figure makes the figure look approximate |
| FAQ answers | Straight | Lifted verbatim by answer boxes and answer engines |
| The call-to-action line | Straight | An action asked for in a joke is declined in a joke |
| Body prose between the zones | As funny as the voice allows | Retention is won here and nothing here is quoted |
| Section subheads | Clear over clever | A cute subhead collapses the scanning pattern and reads as an advertisement |

## Procedure

1. **Write the piece straight and correct first.** The humour pass is a separate,
   full rewrite over a draft whose facts are settled, because a bit written into a
   sentence that later changes leaves a joke about a number that is no longer there.
2. **Sweep bottom-up.** Drafts fatigue: writing copies its own recent output, so
   humour starts strong and thins through the back half, and a top-down punch-up
   fatigues the same way. Working last section first gives the tired sections the
   freshest attention.
3. **Prefer small beats.** A word choice, a half-sentence aside, a personification -
   with one or two builds per section. Ten set pieces per section exhausts the
   reader; specificity is the joke more often than the joke is.
4. **Keep the writer the butt and the reader the audience.** Self-deprecation spends
   the writer's authority, which the proof zones have already secured; teasing the
   reader is a register choice the voice profile decides, never a default.
5. **Count per section, then check the zones.** A publisher who wants a beat every
   second sentence gets a per-section count in review; then every zone in the table
   is read alone and any bit inside it is deleted. A funny intro does not carry a dry
   section six, and a funny quick answer is a defect.
6. **Never let a joke stand in for a source.** A stat still carries its link; a
   comic hedge ("roughly a bazillion") beside a real number is a fabricated number.

## Decision rules

- **When a passage will be lifted verbatim - answer block, FAQ answer, table cell -
  write it straight, because the lifted passage is read without the voice around it
  and a bit out of context reads as an error.**
- **When the business's authority is the product - a clinic, a law practice, a
  financial adviser - dial the body down to a grin or two, because the credibility
  cost is paid against the thing being sold.**
- **When the voice rule demands a fixed joke density, treat the density as the
  publisher's convention and the zoning as the measured part, because the
  meta-analysis prices the trade and says nothing about a cadence.**
- **When a humour pass changes a fact, the pass failed; restore the fact and rewrite
  the bit, because copy approved for accuracy is not reopened by a register pass.**
- **When a page needs to be shared rather than read, humour alone will not do it;
  what travels is arousal - awe, anger, anxiety - and a pleasant post is the
  worst-sharing profile there is. Say so rather than promising reach from jokes.**

## The credibility ledger

A useful review habit: list every claim on the page a sceptical reader would want to
check, and beside each one note whether a bit sits within a sentence of it. Each such
pairing is a place where the humour is spending the exact credibility that claim
needed. Move the bit or move the claim. A page whose jokes cluster around its
strongest proof is a page that argued against itself.

## When NOT to use

- **A money page.** The charm dial there is one or two grins; the page is a sequence of
  proof and one action, which is nearly all zone.
- **A crisis or complaint reply**, a legal or medical answer, or any surface where the
  reader arrived anxious. Humour there reads as dismissal regardless of quality.
- **A brand whose voice profile forbids it.** The zoning rule says where jokes may not
  go; it never says jokes must go anywhere. Voice is per surface and belongs to the
  brand-voice subject.
