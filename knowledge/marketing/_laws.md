# Laws - marketing

Cross-cutting invariants of the craft. Techniques cite these by anchor; they are not
subjects and get no folder. Each one recurred across several subjects of this bundle,
usually with an incident attached - a keyword map built on the wrong country's data, a
pricing page that killed purchase intent, a converting query blocked by a rounded zero,
a threshold typed by hand that left a band where two cells disagreed. They are the shape
of the domain, not style preferences.

---

## <a id="the-results-page-is-the-verdict"></a>The results page is the verdict

Search intent, cluster membership, difficulty and competitor choice are all read off
the live results page, never off a tool's label or a modifier word. A tool's intent
label is a hint that is wrong often enough to be dangerous; a modifier list is a hint
that systematically over-labels queries as informational. When a decision about a
keyword is made, somebody has searched it and read what the engine chose to rank. The
corollary is that any threshold placed on that reading - six of ten, four shared
results - is an internal convention and is labelled as one.

## <a id="never-invent-proof"></a>Never invent proof

No number, review, credential, price, address, contact, guarantee or competitor fact
goes on any surface or into any prompt output unless the business supplied it. A
missing proof is an open question put to the owner, or an explicitly empty slot - never
a plausible value, never a blank left silently, never a disclaimer padding it. In
generated output this is enforced structurally: schemas that cannot carry a number,
validators that drop entities not in the request, grounding that hands only facts to
the model. An instruction alone does not satisfy the law.

## <a id="not-measured-is-not-zero"></a>Not measured is not zero

A missing denominator, an absent prior window, a metric a platform never reported, a
link nobody clicked yet, a channel with no read-back - each is absent, and absence is
rendered as absent: null, blank, "not measured", a dropped column. Zero is a verdict,
and a fabricated zero seeds every baseline, average and comparison downstream with a
lie. The same rule forbids summing money across currencies and blending a predicted
score into a measured one.

## <a id="label-convention-as-convention"></a>Label convention as convention

Every rule in this domain sits on one of three footings: documented platform
behaviour, a published measurement, or practitioner convention. A technique says which,
and a generated recommendation says which. The difficulty-below-authority ceiling, the
six-of-ten intent count, the subhead interval, the four-field form, the cadence cap -
useful habits, all of them, and none a law of the engine. A convention presented as
documented behaviour is the domain's most reproduced error and the reason a corpus of
marketing advice ages into folklore.

## <a id="provenance-is-binary-and-labelled"></a>Provenance is binary and labelled

Every number a marketing surface shows is either the business's own synced data or
disclosed illustrative data, decided per surface and per request, never blended, and
the label sits beside the number rather than in a footer. Illustrative data is never
indexed as proof, never diagnosed as if it were the client's, never scored as a track
record, and never lets a generated recommendation read as an account win. The
disclosure is dropped on the real branch, because a caveat on real data is the opposite
lie.

## <a id="efficiency-is-not-profitability"></a>Efficiency is not profitability

Return on ad spend and its inverse measure spend against revenue, not against profit.
Without a margin, a surface speaks of efficiency and refuses to judge profitability;
with one, break-even is the inverse of margin, a channel is profitable when its net
profit is non-negative rather than when its ratio clears a target, and reallocation
ranks by profit destroyed rather than by revenue missed. A zero-cost channel is never a
false loss and a high-ratio low-margin channel is never a false win.

## <a id="one-target-one-threshold"></a>One target, one threshold

A business has one agreed performance target per scope, and every badge, cell colour,
sort, alert, report line and prompt reads the same one; a derived threshold is the
reciprocal of its sibling by construction so two cells cannot disagree about one
campaign. Lower-is-better metrics invert the verdict, never the number. When a target
has a narrower scope than the whole business - paid portfolio versus blended - every
surface labels the scope, so a looser number is never read as a contradiction.

## <a id="a-gate-before-money-and-copy"></a>A gate before money and copy

Nothing changes spend without simulate, guardrail, explicit approval and a reversible
ledger; nothing publishes without the channel confirming it; nothing auto-sends to a
customer while a risk is listed, whatever the confidence claims. Audits and fix passes
change the mechanical layer only - tags, metadata, markup, links, images - and never
the body copy a person approved. Deletion of a page, a keyword map, a listing or a
review is a recommendation awaiting a yes, because a deleted URL loses its links and
rankings permanently.

## <a id="statistical-honesty-before-a-verdict"></a>Statistical honesty before a verdict

Comparison windows are weekday-balanced and equal in length, year-over-year lands on
the same weekday, partial buckets are flagged and never compared, ratios of sums carry
no confidence claim, an experiment needs its sample before it needs its winner, and
the multiple-comparison correction is applied before any arm is declared. A verdict
without these is a coin flip with a badge; an honest "not yet measurable" outranks it.

## <a id="platform-reported-is-not-causal"></a>Platform-reported is not causal

An ad platform's conversion count is its own attribution of itself, and a before/after
read around a change is a description, not an experiment. Last-click credits the
channel that closed and starves the channel that prospected; two platforms blended
together double-count the same order. Causal claims need a holdout, a geo split or a
model that says its own error; everything else is labelled as the descriptive read it
is, and a marketer who acts on it says so.
