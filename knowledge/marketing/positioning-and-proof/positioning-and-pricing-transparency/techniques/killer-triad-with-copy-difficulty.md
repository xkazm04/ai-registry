# Killer triad with copy difficulty

A value case is a small set of claims, each made against a named incumbent
and each carrying a stated reason that incumbent cannot copy it. The set is
small on purpose: three is the convention - a reader holds three, a founder
rehearses three, a sales conversation covers three before the buyer's
attention is spent - and it is a convention, not a measurement. Two is fine
when the third would be padding; five is a feature list wearing a suit.

The clause that makes a claim a triad member rather than a feature is the
copy-difficulty, and it must describe the incumbent's business, not its
backlog.

## Procedure

1. **Name the incumbent per claim, not per document.** Each claim is against
   the rival a buyer would actually shortlist for that job. A diagnostics
   claim is made against the diagnostics tools; a national-channel claim is
   made against the local feed and campaign tools. One claim may name two
   sets if their blockers differ - and when they do, write both blockers.
2. **Write the claim in a checkable form.** "The generation step reads the
   same data the measurement step wrote" is checkable in a demo. "Smarter
   optimisation" is not.
3. **Write the reason the incumbent will not follow.** Test it against the
   three structural shapes below. If it matches none, the claim demotes to
   the feature grid.
4. **Attach the ships-today line** from the sibling technique. A triad member
   without one is a promise.
5. **Rehearse it against the sceptical buyer.** The buyer's line is "I have
   heard this pitch five times; what is different?" Each claim must answer in
   one sentence with the incumbent's name in it.

## The three structural shapes of copy difficulty

- **Cannibalisation.** The incumbent's price, sales motion or cost structure
  assumes a budget the newcomer does not need. A vendor priced for agencies
  cannot go free without hollowing out the base that pays it. This is the
  counter-positioning shape from the strategy literature: the incumbent does
  not mimic because mimicry damages what it already has.
- **Market indifference.** The claim serves a market too small to move the
  incumbent's revenue, and serving it means rewriting product assumptions - a
  second platform's dialect, a currency's conventions, diacritics-aware
  limits, an authored rather than translated voice. The incumbent could; it
  will not prioritise it.
- **Architecture.** The claim rests on owning something the incumbent bolted
  on. A tool can put a model on a dashboard; it cannot honestly claim its
  output is conditioned on live data it does not hold. Owning the data spine
  is the product; retrofitting it is a re-architecture.

A fourth shape is often offered and is not one: **absence** - "they have not
built it." Absence closes in a sprint. When the only reason is absence, say
so in the working document and keep the claim out of the triad.

## Decision rules

- When a claim's copy-difficulty is a roadmap gap, move it to the feature
  grid, because a grid item is allowed to be copyable and a triad item is not.
- When two incumbent sets face different blockers on one claim, write both
  blockers rather than the stronger one, because a buyer who knows the market
  will test the one you left out.
- When a claim cannot be stated in one checkable sentence, split it or drop
  it, because the buyer's two minutes do not stretch to a paragraph.
- When the triad member's incumbent fact - a price band, a missing feature -
  has no source and date, the claim waits for one, because a value case that
  is wrong about the incumbent is wrong about itself
  ([never invent proof](../../../_laws.md#never-invent-proof)).

## Where the count is convention

Three is a habit, and this technique says so. Nothing in the buyer research
fixes the number; what it fixes is that the buyer wants specifics over
adjectives and a legible answer inside a few minutes. Label the count as
convention wherever the document is templated
([label convention as convention](../../../_laws.md#label-convention-as-convention)),
so a team with two defensible claims does not manufacture a third.

## When NOT to use

- A product with no incumbent - a genuinely new category - has no one to be
  hard-to-copy against; write the value case as jobs-to-be-done instead and
  return to this technique when a rival appears.
- Internal or investor positioning where the audience wants the full
  competitive map; the triad is a buyer's instrument and would be read as
  hiding the rest.
- A one-feature utility whose whole value is a single claim; forcing three
  produces two decoys.
