# Informational pages as linking fuel, not traffic

An informational page whose query shows an AI answer is still worth writing
when it does one of two jobs that are not traffic: it covers a sub-question the
engines fan the topic out into, which makes the money pages it links to look
like the complete source; or it is itself the citable passage, which puts the
brand inside the answer the searcher reads instead of clicking. It is not worth
writing as a traffic play, and it is never forecast as one.

## Why the two jobs survive the lost click

Answer engines retrieve passages against several sub-queries, not whole pages
against one query; a vendor analysis of citation patterns found pages covering
a topic's fan-out sub-queries markedly more likely to be cited, and the same
tracker found topical depth transfers to adjacent topics several times better
than to distant ones. Coverage is therefore worth something even when the
covering page earns no click, because the business's money pages sit inside a
cluster the engine recognises as complete. And a cited brand under a box
outclicks an uncited one on the same query in every vendor study that has
looked, so the informational page that gets lifted is doing conversion work one
step removed. Both findings are correlations on vendor samples; the mechanism
(fan-out retrieval) is documented by the engines.

## Procedure

1. **Give each informational page a stated job before it is briefed.** One of:
   covers sub-question X for money cluster Y; carries original data Z the
   business alone holds; already ranks and should be kept liftable; none. A
   page with job "none" is not briefed.
2. **Wire it the day it ships.** An informational page links down into its
   money cluster from body copy, high on the page, with a varied anchor; the
   money pages never link back up. The linking contract belongs to the
   architecture subject; this technique only says the link is the point of
   the page and a page shipped without it has failed its job.
3. **Blank the traffic forecast.** The content calendar's traffic column for a
   boxed informational page is empty, with the job written in its place. Not
   zero - zero seeds every average downstream - and not the tool's volume
   number, which is an ordinal and, on a boxed query, an ordinal of impressions
   that no longer convert to clicks at the old rate.
4. **Report the page on the citation scoreboard, not the traffic report.** Its
   success measure is whether the business is cited for the sub-question and
   whether the money pages it feeds moved. Its traffic line, if shown, is
   labelled as not the goal.
5. **Keep it liftable.** Answer block under the question heading, no orphan
   pronouns, an original number if the business has one. The writing craft is
   in the sibling techniques; the job assignment is here.

## Decision rules

- When a client asks for "a blog" and the map's informational queries are all
  boxed, propose fewer pages each with a stated job rather than the same count
  with blank forecasts, because a calendar full of unforecastable articles is
  a budget nobody can defend at renewal.
- When an informational query shows a box but the business holds a number on
  the topic that nobody else does - its prices, its job counts, its measured
  before-and-afters - write the page and make the number the passage, because
  original data is the highest-leverage content type in every citation study
  and it is the one thing a competitor cannot copy.
- When an existing informational page already ranks under a box and is not
  cited, rewrite its opening section as a liftable answer before considering
  anything else, because the rank shows the engine already trusts the page and
  the missing piece is structure.
- When a page's only job is "topical coverage" and the cluster it would cover
  has no money page yet, hold the page until the hub exists, because hubs are
  built before spokes and a spoke with nothing to link to is an orphan.

## What is convention here

That a boxed informational page should be built at all is a judgement about
the value of coverage and citation, and different businesses will weigh it
differently; the technique's contribution is that the judgement is written on
the brief rather than hidden in a traffic number. The fan-out sub-query counts
quoted by vendors (eight to fifteen per query) are one vendor's observation and
change with the engine.

## When NOT to use

- When the query shows no box. An unboxed informational query is still a
  traffic play with an ordinal forecast, and downgrading it to "linking fuel"
  throws away a real click.
- When the business has no money cluster for the topic and no original data.
  There is nothing to link to and nothing to be cited for; the honest verdict
  is not to write the page.
- As a way to keep an article programme alive after it has stopped doing
  either job. If the scoreboard shows no citations and the money pages did not
  move over two refresh cycles, the job was not real and the programme is
  reported as such.
