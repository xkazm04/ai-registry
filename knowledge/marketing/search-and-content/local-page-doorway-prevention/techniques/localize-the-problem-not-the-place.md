# Localize the problem, not the place

The failure mode of area pages is a page about the service with the place name
inserted into the headline, the intro, one section and every FAQ. That is localizing
the *place*: the page knows where it is and nothing else. A page that ranks is one
where the *service* is different there - what goes wrong in that housing stock, what
the local rules require, what the weather does, what it costs in that market, which
neighbourhoods have which problem. Same service, different problem, different page.

The measured direction: a 300-page practitioner study of location pages found
hyperlocal content associated with about twice the odds of outranking (+107%), custom
photos of the location +84%, and inbound links to the page +105%, while an embedded
map widget correlated negatively (-34%). One study, correlational, labelled as such -
but the pattern it describes is the one every documented deindexing of a location set
lacked, including a case in which roughly half-unique body copy was still not enough
because the unique half was colour and not substance.

## Procedure

1. **Start from the material that passed the gate.** The job done there, the
   neighbourhoods, the different FAQ answer, the person from there. These are the
   page's spine; everything else is connective tissue.
2. **Write the problem section first, not the intro.** "What [service] runs into in
   [area]": the housing stock, the local regulation, the climate effect, the typical
   job size and price band. If this section could be moved to a sibling page without
   edits, it is not written yet.
3. **Tell the job.** What it was, where, what it cost, what went wrong and what was
   done about it. A real job with a real number is the page's proof and its most
   uncopyable section; the sibling page has a different job or it has no job.
4. **Answer the FAQ differently.** The question may repeat across areas - "how long
   does it take" - but the answer carries the local reason: the permit wait there, the
   access constraint there, the season there. An answer that reads identically with
   the area name swapped is deleted, not kept for structure.
5. **Place the person.** The review from that area quoted verbatim with its rating and
   the reviewer's name as given; the staff member who covers the area. Never
   composed, never paraphrased into something better ([never invent proof](../../../_laws.md#never-invent-proof)).
6. **Use the place name as a place, not as a keyword.** It appears where a human
   would say it - in the headline, once in the intro, in the job's location, in the
   FAQ where the answer depends on it. Inserting it into every heading is the
   signature of the failure this technique exists to prevent.
7. **Run the swap test.** Replace the area name throughout with a sibling's. If the
   page still reads as true, it was never local.

## Decision rules

- When the only local content is the place name and a generic "we serve [area]"
  section, the page is a clone whatever its length; because the similarity measurement
  will count its unique phrases at a dozen and the engine will decline to index it.
- When a writer or a model is asked for local detail and the material gate supplied
  none for that item, the item is omitted, not composed; because a plausible
  neighbourhood or a plausible job is fabrication and template-filling at once.
- When a local condition genuinely does not differ - the service is identical in two
  adjacent towns with the same housing and rules - the pages are one page, and the
  sibling sentence from the gate says so; do not manufacture a difference.
- When choosing between a local photo and an embedded map, use the photo; the map's
  negative correlation is one study's, but it is also the element that carries no
  information a sibling page lacks.
- When the page's price differs by area, say so with the local figure from the price
  list; a price band that genuinely differs is among the strongest local signals and
  among the most honest.
- When the target is a unique-phrase count, the phrases come from the problem and the
  job, never from rephrasing the boilerplate; a rewrite that reaches the count by
  paraphrase has fooled the instrument, not the engine.

## The heading test for a model-written page

A model given the area name, the service and a price will produce a headline with the
area in it, an intro with the area in it, a section titled "[service] in [area]" and an
FAQ asking whether the service is available in the area. All of that is localizing the
place. The brief to the model - and the check on its output - is that at least one
section must state something about the service that is true in this area and not in
the sibling, and that the FAQ answers must differ from the sibling's beyond the place
name. If the model was given nothing that differs, no brief produces a local page, and
the page returns to the gate as held.

## When not to use this

Do not apply it to a physical-premises page, where the place *is* the content: address,
hours, the team there, directions. Those pages localize the place because the place is
the entity.

Do not stretch a genuine non-difference into a fabricated one. Two towns ten minutes
apart with identical housing, rules and pricing get one page that names both; the
technique writes different pages for different problems, not different words for the
same one.

Do not treat the study's multipliers as targets. They are correlations from one
practitioner dataset and are cited for their direction ([label convention as convention](../../../_laws.md#label-convention-as-convention)); a page with a local photo and
no local problem has adopted the tell without the substance.
