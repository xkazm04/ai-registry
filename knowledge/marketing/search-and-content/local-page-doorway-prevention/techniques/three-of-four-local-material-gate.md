# The three-of-four local material gate

Before a single word of an area page is written, the owner answers four yes-or-no
questions about that area, each with real evidence. Three yeses and the page is
built. Fewer and the page is *held* - not built thin, not built anyway, not filled in
by a writer's imagination or a model's - with one line saying which of the four is
missing, so the owner knows what would unlock it.

The four, converging from the three most-cited local-search practitioner guides and
labelled as their convention:

1. **A real job done in or near that area** - what it was, what it cost, what went
   wrong. Photos if they exist.
2. **Real local specifics** - two or three named neighbourhoods, landmarks or local
   names, plus any condition that genuinely differs there: climate, building rules,
   permit process, housing stock, the problem that area actually has.
3. **An area-specific FAQ answer** - a *different answer*, not the same answer with
   the area inserted.
4. **A real person from there** - a customer review from that area, or a staff member
   who lives or works there.

## Why the gate runs first and is answered by the owner

Catching a clone after it is built means the keyword research, the writing, the
on-page pass and the hub link were all spent on a page that will be held anyway. The
gate is cheap and the build is not, so the gate goes first.

The gate is answered by the owner because its inputs are proof. A writer cannot supply
a job the business did not do; a model asked to "add local detail" will produce a
plausible neighbourhood, a plausible job and a plausible reviewer, and the result is a
fabricated page that also happens to be the template-filling the engine's rater
guidelines describe as lowest quality. [Never invent proof](../../../_laws.md#never-invent-proof)
is structural here: the gate's inputs come from the business or the slot stays empty,
and an empty slot is a *no*.

## Procedure

1. **List the candidate areas** from the keyword map or the coverage matrix - every
   service-by-area combination that lacks a page.
2. **Put the four questions to the owner per area**, as a table with a yes/no and the
   evidence beside each yes: the job description, the neighbourhood names, the FAQ
   answer in the owner's words, the review text or the staff member's name.
3. **Score each area.** Three or four yeses: *build*. Two or fewer: *held*, with the
   missing items named.
4. **Run the sibling sentence** on every pair that passed: in one sentence, what is
   genuinely different about each page beyond the place name. A pair with no sentence
   is one page; keep the one with more material.
5. **Report held areas as a group**, each with what it needs - "a job and a review
   from there" - because the fix belongs to the owner and a grouped list is something
   an owner can act on over a quarter.
6. **Re-run the gate when material arrives.** A held area is a queue entry, not a
   rejection.

## Decision rules

- When an area scores two of four, hold it, because a page built on two items has
  only two things a sibling lacks and the sibling sentence usually fails; the page
  would be a clone with a review attached.
- When the owner offers to "write something local" for a held area, decline unless
  it is one of the four with evidence, because the gate measures material the
  business has, not material it can compose.
- When a model is generating the page, feed it only the material that passed the
  gate and give its output nowhere to put material that did not - a schema with no
  review field, no address field, no job-count field - because an instruction not to
  invent is not a gate and a schema is ([a gate before money and copy](../../../_laws.md#a-gate-before-money-and-copy)).
- When the keyword map asks for more areas than pass the gate, the map is over-built,
  not the gate too strict; the practitioner convention of ten to fifteen area pages is
  the number most businesses can support before material runs out, not a quota
  ([label convention as convention](../../../_laws.md#label-convention-as-convention)).
- When the gate passes for many more areas than the convention - a business with forty
  real jobs, photos and reviews in forty towns - build them; what kills a set is
  cloning, not counting.
- When an area is more than roughly two hours' drive from base, or overlaps another
  area's territory, hold it regardless of score; the drive-time cap is convention and
  says so, but a page for a place the business does not actually serve fails the
  honesty line whatever its material.

## What "held" looks like

Held is a status with a reason, not an absence. The plan lists the area, "held", and
the missing items; the coverage matrix shows the area as a gap with a cause rather than
as a gap; and nothing downstream - no draft, no slug, no hub link - is created for it.
The failure this prevents is the one where a held area is quietly built anyway because
the matrix showed a red cell and someone wanted it green.

## When not to use this

Do not run the gate on a page for a genuine physical premises. A shop with its own
address, staff and hours is a distinct entity and the page exists for the same reason
the shop does; the engine's staff have said so on the record. The gate governs
service-area pages for a business operating from elsewhere.

Do not run it on the service hub itself. The hub carries the full sales content for the
service and is a money page first; it is the parent the area pages hang from, not a
sibling of them.

Do not use it as a word-count proxy. Three items of real material can be 150 words and
pass; 900 words of composed local colour with none of the four is held. The gate counts
evidence, not length.
