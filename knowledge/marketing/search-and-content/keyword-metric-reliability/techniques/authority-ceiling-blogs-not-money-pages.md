# The authority ceiling governs blog posts, not money pages

A difficulty ceiling tied to the site's authority score is the most widely used
guardrail in keyword research and the most widely misdescribed. It is kept here as a
convention with a specific scope: it is a hard gate for pages whose only job is to
rank, and it is not a gate at all for pages that exist because the business sells the
thing.

## The ladder, and its footing

The usual form: difficulty must sit at least 30 points below the site's authority
score, and any site under authority 60 gets a flat ceiling of 30. Authority 70 gives a
ceiling of 40; 80 gives 50; a new domain gets 30.

Its footing is practitioner convention and nothing else. No study supports the
subtraction. Difficulty and authority are computed by different vendors, or by one
vendor on different bases, and subtracting one from the other is not a dimensionally
meaningful operation - a useful habit dressed as arithmetic. It is kept because it stops
a new site spending a quarter on terms it cannot win. It is never defended as a law, and
every map that uses it says so
([label convention as convention](../../../_laws.md#label-convention-as-convention)).

## Two page types, two rules

**A blog post's only job is to rank.** If it cannot rank, it is wasted work, so a term
above the ceiling is cut or quarantined until authority catches up. No exceptions, and
no "but it is a good topic" - a good topic that nobody reaches is a draft.

**A money page is built for what the business sells.** Ranking is one of several ways a
visitor reaches it: ads land there, the business-profile listing points there, internal
links flow there, a referral checks it before calling. A business with no page for its
flagship service looks broken to every one of those visitors regardless of what it
ranks for, and there is nothing for the rest of the site to link up to. So a hard head
term still gets its page, and the *rankable* version is the city or modifier variant
underneath it: the national head term is the hub that converts and is linked to, and the
three city pages beneath it are what actually earn the rankings. Say that on the map
rather than hiding it.

## What the ceiling still decides on a money page

- **Which phrasing becomes the primary keyword.** Between a soft variant and a hard
  head term for the same service, the winnable one is the title and the heading; the
  hard one appears naturally in the copy and is never the target.
- **How many spokes the hub needs, and how soon.** A hub whose head term is far above
  the ceiling leans harder on its local or modifier spokes.
- **The order.** Winnable money pages are built before unwinnable ones because they
  return something this quarter.

## Procedure

1. **Get the authority score before anything else**, because it sets the ceiling; then
   write both the ceiling and the score it came from in the map header, so the number is
   never a mystery and the reader knows exactly what unlocks as the score climbs.
2. **Apply the ceiling to blog candidates as a gate.** Above it: quarantine.
3. **Apply it to money candidates as a selector**, never as a gate: choose phrasing,
   spoke count and order; build every service the business sells.
4. **Quarantine, never delete.** Everything cut goes to a section grouped by *when it
   becomes useful* - "reopens at authority 45", "reopens when the hub exists" - not by
   rejection reason. A wrong cut is invisible forever; a quarantined term gets a second
   look. Deleting a keyword from a map is a recommendation awaiting a yes, like any
   other deletion ([a gate before money and copy](../../../_laws.md#a-gate-before-money-and-copy)).
5. **Re-run the pull as the score climbs.** The ceiling lifts; the quarantine is the
   refill list.

## Decision rules

- When a term is above the ceiling and the page would be a blog post, do not build it,
  because a post that cannot rank has no other route to a reader.
- When a term is above the ceiling and the business sells the thing, build the page and
  target the rankable variant beneath it, because the page has four other routes to a
  visitor and the site needs the linking target.
- When a service is cut from a map because its keyword is hard, reverse the cut: that
  optimises the keyword map at the cost of the website.
- When the ceiling ladder is quoted anywhere - a report, a prompt, a client call - it is
  quoted as a rule of thumb, with the authority score it was computed from.
- When difficulty and authority come from different vendors, do not subtract at all;
  use the weakest incumbent's referring-domain count as the gate instead.

## When not to use this

Do not apply the ceiling to paid landing pages, which rank by bid and relevance rather
than links. Do not apply it to branded terms, where the site is the incumbent. Do not
apply it when the site has no authority score at all - a brand-new domain gets the flat
floor and, more usefully, the two better predictors: the age of the current top ten and
proximity to anything the domain already ranks for. And do not let the ceiling stand in
for a results-page read: a term under the ceiling with ten brand incumbents is still a
wall, and one over it with a churning, intent-mismatched top ten may be an opening.
