# Losers are routed to linking content, never deleted

Every verdict this subject issues carries a destination, and a verdict of
"wrong intent for the money page" is not "cut". An informational term that lost
the money-page slot is the top of the funnel: a real person with a real question
about the thing the business sells, one article away from the page that sells
it. Deleting it loses that demand for good. Leaving it silently on the money
page produces a page that sells to someone who came to learn and converts
neither. The rule is that no term leaves the map without an address.

## Where each loser goes

- **An informational term that lost to a money page** becomes an article whose
  job is traffic, trust and a link down to the page that won. It never tries to
  sell in the first screen; it answers, then it points.
- **The informational half of a mixed page** becomes an article against the
  informational variant or a supporting phrase, linking to the money page that
  took the primary - never against the same primary.
- **A comparison term** becomes a comparison page with the money page linked
  from every section. It was never a loser; it was mis-filed.
- **A local term a national page could not win** becomes a locality page and a
  business-profile task.
- **A `[service] for [audience]` term** that lost the root to a smaller
  buying-noun term becomes the article it was always shaped as, linking to the
  service page that took the root.
- **A brand term that is not the business's own** is the one genuine cut, and
  it is still recorded with its reason, not silently dropped.

Quarantine groups the cut list by *when a term becomes useful* - after the
money page ranks, after a locality is added, after a competitor can be named -
not by the reason it was rejected. A wrong cut is invisible forever; a
quarantined term gets a second look.

## Why routing rather than cutting is the standard

Two reasons. The first is demand: the largest terms in most maps are
informational, and a map that cuts them has cut most of the people who will
ever search the topic. The second is the money page itself: it ranks on the
strength of the pages that link to it, and the routed articles are the most
natural links it will ever have - same site, same topic, a reader who has just
been taught why the service matters. The routed loser is the money page's
supply of links and of warmed-up readers, which is why the routing is recorded
as a pair: the article, and the page it feeds.

Where the pair sits in the site's linking structure - hub, spoke, standalone -
is the architecture subject's decision. This technique guarantees only that the
pair exists and that the loser has an address before the map ships.

## Procedure

1. **For every term that failed a money-page verdict, write its destination
   page type** beside the verdict, using the list above.
2. **Name the money page it links to.** An article with no downstream page is
   an orphan; it is routed to the nearest money page on the topic, and if none
   exists that is an open item for the owner.
3. **Never let the loser and the winner share a primary.** The article's
   primary is the informational variant; if there is none, it targets a
   supporting phrase and the block says so.
4. **Move the genuine cuts to quarantine**, grouped by the condition under
   which they return.
5. **Report the routed count**, because a map that skews informational is a
   map whose clicks an answer box has largely taken, and the client hears that
   before the article list, not after.

## Decision rules

- When a term fails the money-page intent check, route it to an article that
  links to the money page, because an informational verdict is a routing
  decision and never a deletion.
- When a routed article would target the same primary as the page it links
  to, re-target it to the informational variant, because two pages of one site
  on one primary split the signals of both.
- When a term's only possible destination page does not exist, record it as an
  open item to the owner rather than an article, because a page that sells
  what the business does not offer is a page the owner never approved.
- When the cut list is written, group it by the condition of return, because a
  cut grouped by rejection reason is never re-read.

## When NOT to use

- For a brand term that is not the business's own. Nothing is built, and the
  record says why.
- For a term on the owner's do-not-offer list. It is junk by the owner's
  instruction, and routing it to an article about a service the business
  refuses is a page the owner will have to delete.
- As a reason to forecast traffic from the routed articles. Routing preserves
  demand and feeds links; the click loss on informational queries is a
  separate subject and the forecast is not this technique's to make.
- For a term the results-page count has not yet classified. Routing follows a
  verdict; a term with no verdict is unverified, not a loser.
