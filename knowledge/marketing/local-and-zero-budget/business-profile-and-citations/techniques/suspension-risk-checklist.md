# Suspension-risk checklist

A suspended profile loses every review and every ranking at once, its recovery is
measured in weeks, and it sometimes does not come back. It is the classic local
emergency, and it is almost always self-inflicted: not by a competitor's report but by
a change the owner made in good faith after reading that a keyword in the name helps.
The checklist is short because the causes are few, and it is re-run on every change
because the record does not stay put.

## The conditions

None of these may be true when a record is finalized or edited:

1. **A keyword-stuffed name.** The name field holds the real-world name as it appears
   on the sign, the van and the invoice - nothing appended, no city, no service, no
   "best" or "24/7". This is the one field where a ranking tactic is a suspension
   trigger, and the most common cause reported by practitioners who handle
   reinstatements.
2. **A virtual office, a mail drop or a parcel-shop address.** The address is where a
   customer can be received; a rented address with no staff is a policy violation.
3. **A co-working or shared address**, or several profiles at one address, without
   distinct businesses genuinely operating there.
4. **A home business with a visible address.** A business that travels to customers
   hides its address and lists service areas instead; forgetting that one switch after
   moving from storefront to mobile is the common snag.
5. **A listing in a city the business does not operate in.** A "location" that is a
   phone number and a hope is a fake listing.
6. **A service area wider than a plausible drive** from the verified location. The
   drive-time cap practitioners use - around two hours, twenty localities - is
   convention; the underlying policy is that the area must be one the business
   actually serves.
7. **A burst of edits.** Many changes landing at once - name, category, address, hours
   in a single sitting - is reported by reinstatement practitioners as a trigger for
   re-verification; make changes slowly and one field at a time.
8. **Ownership hygiene.** The primary owner is an account on the business's own domain
   rather than a free mailbox, and the website field is a live page on that domain
   rather than a redirect.

## The name verdict, said aloud every time

A silent pass is a wasted warning. The name almost never gets stuffed at setup, when
someone is following a spec; it gets stuffed months later. So every report carries an
explicit verdict on the name, in one of two forms.

**Clean.** Say so, name the reason, and write the bad version out in full using their
name and their city: "'[Business]' carries no keywords, so there is no suspension
risk from the name. Never change it to '[Business] - [City] [Service]'; that is an
instant suspension." A person who has seen the exact string spelled out as the thing
that suspends them does not type it later; an abstract "do not add keywords" does not
survive contact with a blog post that says otherwise.

**At risk.** Quote the current name, name the offending part, and give the exact
string to change it to - the registered name and nothing else.

## Procedure

1. Run the eight conditions before a spec is finalized; flag any that hold and stop
   for the owner before generating the final record.
2. Re-run the checklist before any edit to the name, address, service area or
   primary category, because these are the fields whose change triggers review.
3. State the name verdict in the report, in whichever form applies.
4. Where a profile-status readout exists - a roster import, a dashboard export - any
   status that is not an explicit affirmative ("verified", "connected", "live") is
   treated as needing attention, never as healthy. "Suspended", "pending
   verification" and an unrecognised word all land in the attention queue; only an
   empty status cell, meaning the column was absent, is left unflagged, because
   absence of a column is not a problem signal.
5. When a suspension has happened: fix every violated condition first, then file the
   single reinstatement request with evidence of the real-world business (signage,
   registration, utility bill). Multiple appeals and unfixed violations extend the
   outage.

## Decision rules

- When a change would improve rank and trips a condition, the condition wins,
  because a suspension takes the rank and everything else with it.
- When an owner reports a competitor with a stuffed name that ranks, say that the
  competitor's suspension has not happened yet, and do not copy it.
- When a status readout is present but unrecognised, surface it as attention, never
  bury it as healthy, because an optimistic default hides the worst-off profile from
  the urgency queue.
- When a roster ranks locations by urgency, a disconnected or suspended profile
  outranks a flagged item, which outranks an unanswered-review backlog, because the
  first loses everything and the others lose some of it.

## When NOT to use

- The checklist does not grade rank, reviews or coverage; a clean profile can still
  be invisible, and that diagnosis belongs to the visibility subject.
- The drive-time and locality numbers are not to be enforced as policy on a business
  whose genuine service pattern differs - a rural trade that drives three hours to
  every job serves that area, and the record says so with the pages and citations to
  prove it.
