# Admissibility before fit and effort

A free-visibility plan carries two numbers per channel and neither of them can
refuse one. A fit score is a prediction about audience match; an effort level is a
statement about labour. Both answer *how well and how dearly*, and a channel that
should not be on the plan at all scores well on both. So the plan needs a stage
before its ranking: a channel is admitted, then ranked - never ranked and thereby
admitted.

## Why the ranking cannot catch it

The quick-win rule surfaces the first untracked low-effort channel with fit at or
above seventy. Read that rule against the shape of an inadmissible channel and the
problem is structural rather than unlucky. A supply that arrives by *enrolment* -
submit a URL, receive inbound links from other enrolled members - costs no labour,
so its effort is low by construction. It is matched to the business by industry,
so its fit is high by construction. It has never been touched, so it is untracked.
The plan's own rule selects it first and labels it "start here".

**The plan's most confident output is therefore the one to check hardest**, which
inverts the usual reading of a ranked list. A high-fit high-effort channel has
already been argued for by its effort; nobody adopts a publishing habit by
accident. A high-fit zero-effort channel has been argued for by nothing.

## The test: who chose the outbound link, and may they qualify it

The discriminator is not reciprocity. A business and the non-competing business
next door recommending each other is a reciprocal arrangement and a legitimate
channel: each party chose to link, for a reason that survives the other party
leaving, and each could have marked the link as sponsored and did not have to.

The test is narrower and it runs on the *other* end of the link:

> Did the party whose page carries the link choose to place it, and were they free
> to qualify it?

A neighbour who links to the plumber chose that link and could have qualified it.
A member of a link pool whose generated article carries an outbound link that an
intermediary selected chose neither: the link is placed on their page, by an agent,
because both parties are enrolled - and the same enrolment that supplies their
inbound links spends their outbound ones. Two noes is a scheme; the relevance
filter changes the quality of the placement, not its class.

## The policy this sits on

The dominant search engine's published spam policy defines link spam as creating
links to or from a site primarily to manipulate rankings, and names as examples
excessive link exchanges, using automated programs or services to create links to
a site, and requiring a link as part of a terms of service, contract or similar
arrangement **without allowing the content owner the choice of qualifying the
outbound link**. The stated consequence is that a site may rank lower or not
appear at all. The same policy accepts bought and sold links outright when they
carry a qualifying attribute - which is the proof that the qualification, not the
commerce and not the reciprocity, is what the policy is actually about.

The third example is the one a channel plan needs, because it is the only one that
looks at what the member gives up rather than at what they receive. An offer read
purely as supply passes the first two tests by wording and fails the third by
construction.

## The second cost a plan cannot see

The effort column is a statement about *labour*, and it is the right column for
four of the five kinds. It cannot price a channel whose cost is an asset the
business already holds. An enrolled link supply is paid in the member's own
outbound links and in their domain's standing with the engine; the sibling rule
for a push channel is paid in a granted permission that an intermediary meters.
Both are channels that cost nothing to work and something to hold, and both are
invisible to a plan that reads labour and audience match.

So the gate has a second question beside the link test: **what does this channel
spend that is not hours?** A channel that answers "nothing" is ordinary. A channel
that answers with the business's own inventory, standing or permission is admitted
only with that cost written next to it, in words, where the two numbers cannot
carry it.

## Procedure

1. **Run the gate on the channel list before it is scored**, on every channel a
   model returned and on every channel a business brings to the plan. A curated
   seed per business type has already been gated by whoever curated it; a
   model-generated list has not, and it is the path that produces six to nine
   channels from open grounding.
2. **Apply the link test** to any channel whose payoff is inbound links or
   mentions. Name the party whose page carries the link, ask whether they chose it
   and whether they could qualify it, and record the two answers rather than a
   verdict word.
3. **Ask what the channel spends that is not hours.** Outbound links, domain
   standing, a granted permission, a list's tolerance, the business's name on
   someone else's claim.
4. **Refuse, or admit with the cost stated.** A refused channel is dropped from
   the plan with its reason, not scored zero - a zero fit is a prediction about
   audience match and this is not one. An admitted channel with a non-labour cost
   carries that cost as a sentence beside its effort level.
5. **Re-run the gate when a plan is regenerated**, because the gate is a property
   of the offer and offers change; a channel admitted last quarter under a
   different arrangement is a new channel.

## Decision rules

- When a channel's payoff is inbound links and its effort is low, gate it before
  ranking it, because low effort on a link channel means somebody else is placing
  the link and the question is who.
- When a channel is refused, drop it from the plan with its reason rather than
  scoring it zero, because a fit is a prediction and a refusal is not a weak
  prediction.
- When the offer is reciprocal but each party places its own link for its own
  reason, admit it, because reciprocity is not the disqualifying property and the
  plan already carries the arrangement next door.
- When the gate and the ranking disagree - a refused channel that would have been
  the quick win - report the refusal rather than the next-best quick win first,
  because the business will otherwise meet the same offer again without the reason.
- When a channel spends something that is not labour, write the cost beside the
  effort level in words, because no threshold on a three-level labour scale can
  represent it.

## What is convention here

The spam policy's definition, its named examples and the qualification carve-out
are **documented platform behaviour** and are quoted as such; what the engine
actually does to a participating site in practice is not documented and is not
claimed here. Placing the gate *before* the scoring rather than as a flag beside
it is **convention**, chosen because a flag on a ranked row still renders the row
first. The two gate questions are a practitioner reading of the policy's three
examples, not an enumeration the engine publishes. Nothing here is measured: no
figure is offered for how often an enrolled supply is detected, because none was
found that was not a vendor's.

## When NOT to use

- As a blanket refusal of partnerships, guest articles, directories or podcast
  slots. Every one of those is on the plan legitimately, and each passes the link
  test whenever the publisher chose the placement - which is the ordinary case and
  the reason the pitch had to be accepted at all.
- To refuse a channel on the *suspicion* that an intermediary is involved. The
  test needs the arrangement's own terms; where they are not available, the
  channel is admitted with the question recorded as open, because a refusal
  inherits the same burden of evidence as an admission.
- As a content-quality gate. Whether the article the business publishes deserves
  to exist is `local-page-doorway-prevention` and the content subjects; this gate
  reads the arrangement, not the page.
- To re-score an admitted channel. The gate is binary and upstream; it never
  adjusts a fit or an effort, because a gated score is neither a prediction nor a
  statement about labour.
