# Progressive disclosure: five things visible

A money page written for coverage - for the search engine, for the answer
engine, for the one researcher in ten - is long. A money page that sells is
short. The technique reconciles the two: write the depth in full, then
collapse nearly all of it, and hold a fixed short list of what stays on screen.

## The visible list

This list is complete; anything not on it collapses:

1. The headline and the hero promise.
2. The offer: the price or price range, and the guarantee.
3. **One** proof line - the strongest single number or review, not the stack.
4. Every call to action, and the form.
5. One plain-text line naming the service area or the audience served.

Everything else collapses, including the things that feel too important to
hide: the full proof stack, what is included, the process, the jobs done, the
specification tables, materials, timelines, the detailed service area, and
every answer in the questions section. Questions stay visible with their
answers shut, because the question stack on its own reads as the buyer's real
worries and is the best objection-handling copy on the page.

## Why hiding costs nothing

Under mobile-first indexing, content inside accordions and tabs is indexed and
weighted the same as visible content. This is documented platform behaviour -
the search engine has stated it - and it retires the older "never hide text"
advice, which was true under desktop-first indexing and is now folklore. A
practitioner who still refuses to collapse on ranking grounds is protecting a
rule the platform withdrew.

What is *not* documented is the half-minute read: the convention that the whole
visible page should read in under thirty seconds on a phone. It is a useful
target and the technique keeps it, labelled as convention. The same is true of
"five things": the count is a discipline, not a measurement.

## Procedure

1. Write the page in full - every section, every answer, every table. The
   depth is real; it is only its visibility that is decided next.
2. For each element, ask the reverse question: not "should this be hidden" but
   "does the page fail to sell without this on screen". The default is
   collapsed; visible is the exception that has to be argued for.
3. Write the visible label of each collapsed section so it carries the whole
   meaning alone. "Our process" is a wasted line; "What happens in the first
   48 hours" is a reason to click. Assume nobody opens it.
4. Open one accordion by default - the dominant objection - so the pattern is
   visibly a pattern and skimmers know there is more.
5. Screenshot the page with every accordion shut. If the screenshot does not
   sell on its own, cut one line or promote one line. Never reopen a section.

## Decision rules

- When a fact decides the sale, it does not go inside a collapsed section; it
  goes on the visible list or gets cut, because a fact nobody opens is a fact
  the page does not have.
- When a section's answer-first paragraph has to win a featured snippet or an
  answer-engine citation, keep that paragraph visible and collapse the rest of
  the section, because the citation lever is the first sentences under the
  heading, not the depth.
- When the guarantee or anything the leak order places beside the form is in a
  collapsed section, move it out; nothing conversion-critical is ever behind a
  click.
- When a price is genuinely unpublishable - it varies per job - the offer slot
  shows the honest range or the quote promise, never an empty slot that makes
  the visible list read as four.

## When NOT to use

- On a blog post or a guide. Those pages are documents by design; readers came
  to read, and a collapsed article is a broken article.
- On a comparison or pricing page whose entire value is the table. The table is
  the sale there; collapsing it hides the offer.
- When the page has fewer than five sections of depth to begin with. A page
  with one collapsed section is a page with a strange button; write the depth
  or leave the page flat.
- On an experiment arm where visibility itself is the variable under test.
  Then the visible list is what the experiment decides, and
  `landing-page-experiment-statistics` owns the call.
