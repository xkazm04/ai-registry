# What a rescission owes, by ground — statutory anchors read on 2026-09-29

The technique says the ground selects the process. This application records the
anchors found when the claim was tested, with how each was read. It is a map for a
team choosing what a rescission door must carry, not legal advice, and it is
deliberately short: the substance per regime belongs to
`multi-jurisdiction-hiring-compliance`. The refresh clock is six months because
fair-chance rules and consumer-report guidance are amended on that order.

## A ground resting on a consumer or background report (United States)

15 USC 1681b(b)(3)(A), read from the govinfo text: *"before taking any adverse action
based in whole or in part on the report, the person intending to take such adverse
action shall provide to the consumer to whom the report relates— (i) a copy of the
report; and (ii) a description in writing of the rights of the consumer under this
subchapter"*. Nothing found exempts a person who has accepted and not started.

- **The waiting period is not in the statute.** The FTC's employer guide says the
  advance notice gives the person "the opportunity to review the report and tell you
  if it is correct" and names no number of days. The commonly quoted five business
  days traces to a 1997 FTC staff letter that was not fetched here; treat it as a
  benchmark, and do not call it a requirement.
- **After the action**, 1681m(a) requires a notice naming the reporting agency,
  stating that the agency did not make the decision, and describing the right to a free
  report and to dispute it. Read from a secondary text, not the primary.
- **The remote-applicant shortcut** in (b)(3)(B) allows notice within three business
  days after the action for a person who applied by mail, telephone or computer. It
  depends on a definition in (C) that was not read; do not build on it unread.

## A ground resting on a conviction (United States, two local regimes)

- **California, Gov. Code 12952(c)**, read from the legislature's site: the applicant
  "shall have at least five business days to respond to the notice ... before the
  employer may make a final decision"; the notice carries the disqualifying conviction
  and a copy of the report, and the final notice is written.
- **New York City Fair Chance Act**: a written copy of the inquiry and analysis, then
  no less than five business days, position held open. The five-day figure is a 2021
  amendment to a three-day rule, so older materials are stale. Read from the city's
  employer page and a search summary, not from the code text.
- **Federal guidance**: the EEOC's 2012 enforcement guidance describes an
  individualised assessment as notice, an opportunity to show the exclusion should not
  apply, and consideration of what is shown. It states that it lacks the force of law.

## A ground resting on a medical result (United States)

The ADA's post-offer examination rule (42 USC 12112(d)(3)) permits the examination
only if all entering employees in the job category take it, the results are held in
separate confidential files and used only as the subchapter allows. The EEOC's 1995
guidance adds that a rejection on the result must be "job-related and consistent with
business necessity", and, for safety, a direct-threat showing that accommodation cannot
reduce the risk. The condition rests on the guidance, not on the statute's own words.

## A ground that is not about the person (contract)

- **United Kingdom.** Acas: *"If all the conditions have been met and the employer
  withdraws a conditional job offer, this could be a breach of contract."* and, for an
  unconditional offer, the contract "should be ended fairly by giving notice and
  following the contract's terms". Equality Act 2010 s.39(1) still bars discrimination
  in not offering employment. The statutory notice period in the Employment Rights Act
  1996 s.86 starts at one month's continuous employment, so the notice due before a
  start date rests on the contract; how tribunals treat that was not verified.
- **Czech Republic.** Labour Code §34(3): if the employee does not start on the agreed
  day without an obstacle, or the employer does not learn of the obstacle within a
  week, the *employer* may withdraw from the contract; §34(4): withdrawal is possible
  "only until the employee has started work". A written form for the withdrawal is
  reported by commentary, not read in the statute. The right covers non-start, not a
  change of mind.
- **Germany.** An ordinary notice before the start date is possible unless excluded;
  the Federal Labour Court has held that, absent another agreement, the period runs
  from receipt of the notice (2 AZR 324/03, read through summaries). A contractual
  penalty for non-start can imply that pre-start notice is excluded.
