# What the silence-gap evidence supports — a literature and figures read on 2026-09-29

The technique's claim is that a person who hears nothing during the notice period is
likelier to renege. This application records what was found when that was tested, so the
technique can say what it stands on. The short answer: the mechanism is plausible and
unmeasured; the numbers in circulation are vendor-survey figures. Refresh clock six
months.

## Contact during the gap

- **Capitano and Gesualdi (2025)**, *Organizational Studies*, read from an abstract page:
  *"communication satisfaction during the pre-boarding phase was associated with greater
  job satisfaction. Additionally, most participants who thought the communication
  frequency from their new employer could be improved desired more (rather than less)
  communication during the pre-boarding phase."* Cross-sectional survey; the sample size
  was not stated on the page read. Satisfaction, not reneges. Correlational.
- **Bauer and colleagues (2007)**, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 92(3), a meta-analysis
  of 70 samples: socialization tactics and information-seeking predict adjustment, and
  adjustment predicts intentions to remain and turnover. Post-entry; anticipatory
  socialization is not tested as a renege lever.
- **Cable, Gino and Staats (2013)**, *Administrative Science Quarterly* 58(1), a field
  experiment at a call-centre operator: a one-hour first-day intervention cut six-month
  quits. A first-day intervention, not pre-start contact.
- **No field experiment or trial on employer contact during the notice period** was
  found. No evidence that more contact backfires; none that it does nothing.

## Gap length

- No peer-reviewed dose-response between the acceptance-to-start gap and reneging.
  Vendor claims (a "60 days" line, a "four weeks" line, an eight-month internship gap)
  are unsourced and disagree.
- The one methodologically stated adjacent figure: the 2025 recruiting benchmarks survey
  of a graduate-hiring association (197 of 790 member employers, 24.9% response, fielded
  April–May 2025, mostly employers over 5,000 staff, US-focused) reports reneges rising for
  full-time positions and attributes it, with a hedge, to delays in *making* offers.
  Employer-reported, correlational, students; it concerns the wait before the offer, not
  the gap after acceptance.

## The thresholds the technique uses

The above-median-gap and more-than-a-fortnight rule, the cadence points and the cap on
midpoint touches: **no source found for any of them.** One vendor claims reneges peak two
weeks after acceptance and two weeks before the start; it was not verified.

## Typical gap lengths (bounds, not observations)

Statutory notice figures are floors or defaults, not typical gaps. UK: Employment Rights
Act 1996 s.86(2), a statutory minimum of one week after a month's employment, with
contracts routinely longer. Germany: BGB §622(1), four weeks to the 15th or the end of a
month, and §622(6) bars a longer period for the employee than the employer. Czech
Republic: Labour Code §51(1), a notice period "nejméně 2 měsíce" equal for both sides
(read from a legal-information site; a fetch summary of the statute site had said the
opposite, and a second source and a search summary agreed with the first; the 2025
amendment changed when the period starts and was not read). United States: no statutory
notice; two weeks is convention, from commercial legal-information sites, no primary
source fetched. So "two weeks to three months" is a practitioner range that understates
the floors in some jurisdictions.

## Ghosting and no-show figures, 2023–2026

All employer or vendor surveys, with different definitions:

- Gartner, 2023, via SHRM: "50 percent of candidates accepted a job offer between May
  2022 and May 2023, backed out, then started working for another employer"; n nearly
  3,500. Definition is backed out and joined elsewhere. A later 3Q24 figure (33%) was seen
  only in a search snippet and could not be fetched.
- Resume.org, 2025, via a magazine: over 1,100 hiring managers; 54% of Gen-Z applicants
  "ghosted them after an offer was extended", with sub-cases summing to 82%, base
  unclear. Hiring-manager recall, one generation.
- Other figures (28%, 65%, "1 in 4") came from vendor blogs with no stated method.

Do not carry any of these into a business case as a base rate for your own hires.
