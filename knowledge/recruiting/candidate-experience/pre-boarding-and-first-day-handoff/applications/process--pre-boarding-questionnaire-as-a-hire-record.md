# Pre-start collection: what each item needs before it may be asked — read on 2026-09-29

The technique says to ask before day one only what provisioning blocks on, and to ask
sector-gating items as evidence rather than self-assessment. Tested item by item, the
rule holds but each item carries a condition the technique did not state, and one figure
the research started from turned out to be unsupported. Anchors and how they were read;
a map, not legal advice; six-month clock.

## Work authorisation

**United States (Form I-9).** From the employer handbook (M-274): *"You may not ask an
individual who has not accepted a job offer to complete Section 1"*; employees "may
complete Section 1 before the time of hire, but not before the employer extends the job
offer and the employee accepts it"; Section 2 may be completed from acceptance to within
three business days of hire. So asking after acceptance and before day one is lawful, and
the deadline is day three, not before day one. Three conditions follow:

- **The employer cannot specify which documents the employee presents.** *"You cannot
  specify which documentation an employee will present."* The statute (8 USC
  1324b(a)(6)) makes a request for more or different documents an unfair practice when
  made with intent to discriminate. A questionnaire field that says "upload your
  passport" is the shape to avoid.
- **Copies do not complete Section 2.** Originals are examined physically, or, for an
  employer in good standing in the federal verification programme, by live video under
  the alternative procedure available from 2023-08-01, applied consistently at a site. An
  e-mailed scan alone does not complete the check. That non-participants cannot use the
  procedure rests on the eligibility sentence plus a synthesis; no explicit primary text
  was found.
- **So the pre-boarding item is an appointment and a route**, not a document collection.

**United Kingdom.** The right-to-work check must be made before employment starts, by
the online share-code service or by original documents, with the check date recorded. The
"no earlier than 60 days before the start" figure the research began with **is not in the
current guidance or the code of practice** (searched in full): the only 60-day rule found
is a business-transfer grace period. Do not repeat it. A share code is valid for 90
calendar days, and the 28-day grace does not apply to checks made before employment
starts. A draft guide dated 2026-09-11 adds identity-service providers and is stated to
come into force on 2026-10-01; recheck it after that date.

## Health and immunisation

**United States.** A post-offer, pre-start medical inquiry is permitted if all entering
employees in the job category are subjected to it regardless of disability, results are
kept on separate forms in separate confidential files, and used only as the ADA allows
(42 USC 12112(d)(3)). Withdrawing on the result must be job-related and consistent with
business necessity, per EEOC guidance. The bloodborne-pathogens standard shows why a
sector item is an *offer*, not a demand: hepatitis B vaccination is made available after
training and "within 10 working days of initial assignment". Asking for proof of
vaccination is, per EEOC guidance dated 2023-05-15, not itself a disability-related
inquiry; follow-up "why not" questions are (summariser wording, unverified).

**Europe and the United Kingdom.** The regulator's own guidance says a records-of-
vaccination request is health information for which consent is hard to rely on. Health
data needs an employment-law condition backed by national law (GDPR Art. 9(2)(b)).

## The legal basis (the "purpose changed, so the basis changed" claim)

EDPB Guidelines 05/2020, read from an archived copy: *"it is unlikely that the data
subject is able to deny his/her employer consent … without experiencing the fear or real
risk of detrimental effects … the EDPB deems it problematic for employers to process
personal data of current or future employees on the basis of consent as it is unlikely to
be freely given."* Exception: consent is free only where refusal has "no adverse
consequences at all". So a *mandatory* pre-boarding field rests on contract steps
(Art. 6(1)(b), including steps before entering a contract) or a legal obligation
(6(1)(c)), and an *optional* one can rest on consent only if declining costs nothing. The
technique's sentence was too strong: the basis changes when the old basis was consent, or
did not cover the new purpose, not automatically.

## Copies of identity documents

Germany's identity-card statute (PAuswG §20(2)) permits copying only with the holder's
consent and requires the copy to be identifiable as a copy; the Czech identity-document
act (269/2021 §39(c)) forbids copying an identity card without the holder's consent (the
earlier provision was repealed 2022-02-01). Both treat consent as the gate for the copy
while the data-protection regulator treats consent as weak in employment — a tension the
technique should not paper over: prefer *sight and record* (who saw which document,
when) to *copy and hold*. The UK regulator's line: verify only successful candidates,
"the minimum information you need", and unlikely both a passport and a driving licence.

## Retention if the person does not start

The "shortest life of anything you hold" wording is a design stance, not a finding. The UK
regulator says only that information must not be kept "longer than you need to" and names
no period; unsuccessful-candidate records should not outlast the period in which a claim
can be brought. Nothing was found from a regulator on hired-but-never-started people.
