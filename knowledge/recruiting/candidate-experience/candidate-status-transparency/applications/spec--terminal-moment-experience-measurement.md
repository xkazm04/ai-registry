# Measuring how the ending landed: what the research and the benchmark show

**Pin.** Retrieved 2026-09-28. PDF texts were extracted and matched verbatim;
journal findings are cited from their published abstracts (Crossref, arXiv),
not from full texts, which are paywalled. The benchmark is annual, so the
clock is six months.

## The outcome confound

- **SIOP, *Applicant Reactions to Selection* (2012), PDF p.10:** "We know that
  applicants' perceptions are largely a function of how well they did in the
  selection procedure (e.g., passed or failed)."
- **Chan, Schmitt, Jennings, Clause and Delbridge (1998), *International
  Journal of Selection and Assessment*, abstract:** test performance affects
  applicants' perceptions "indirectly through perceived performance".
- **Talent Board 2023 benchmark report, Table 1:** North American overall NPS
  28, hired candidates 73; in the report's words, "the only time companies
  will see NPS ratings in the 50s or higher are from candidates who were hired".
  The gap between the hired and the overall figure is the size of the confound
  in one real population. It is why the technique now requires a split by
  outcome, not only a recorded outcome.

## When employers ask

Talent Board 2023, Table 3 ("When Employers Ask for Candidate Experience
Feedback", global): 27% ask after candidates apply but before the interview,
46% after the interview but before hire, 67% after hire, 21% not at all.
Surveying across the journey is common practice. None of these rows is a
survey at a *decline*, which is the population this technique exists to
reach. The report's own benchmark is Likert-derived ("NPS" from 1-5 scales),
not the 0-10 instrument, so its numbers compare to each other and not to a
product's net-promoter figure.

## Silence against a rejection

- **Waung and Brice (2007), *Journal of Applied Social Psychology*, abstract:**
  in the laboratory, "those rejected without notification reported the lowest
  level of organizational obligation fulfillment"; in the field, those with no
  rejection communication "reported more negative intentions toward the
  organization compared to those who received a rejection communication".
- **Greenhouse's 2024 candidate survey (vendor survey, n=2,900 across four
  markets), p.5, US:** "Compensation not meeting expectations (salary,
  benefits, etc.) (40%)" ranks first, "Lack of communication from companies
  and recruiters during the hiring process (38%)" second. This is why the
  golden path now says "at or near the top" rather than "the answer".

## The sample floor

A net-promoter score is a difference of two proportions. Its variance per
answer is p+ + p- - (p+ - p-)^2. With 40% promoters and 20% detractors the 95%
half-width is about 46 points at n=10, 27 at n=30, 15 at n=100 and 7 at n=400
(computed for this page by the normal approximation). Rocks (2016, arXiv
1601.07235) finds an adjusted Wald interval performs best for this statistic
at small n. Its intervals are of the same order. Keiningham and colleagues
(2007, *Journal of Marketing*) fail "to replicate … the 'clear superiority' of
Net Promoter" as a growth predictor, which is why the technique keeps it as
one indicator among several.
