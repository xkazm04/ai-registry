# Automatic replies as the standard marks them, and as the field handles them

**Pin.** RFC 3834, *Recommendations for Automatic Responses to Electronic Mail*
(Standards Track, August 2004), retrieved 2026-09-28 from
`rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3834.txt`. The second half reads vendor documentation and
one vendor's published measurement, all retrieved 2026-09-28. Those pages move on
the vendors' schedule, hence the short `refresh_by`; the RFC part does not age.
Every quotation was matched against the fetched text after drafting.

## What the standard gives you

**A machine-authorship marker exists and is only recommended.** The technique's
steps 1 and 2 both rest on this. §3.1.7: "The Auto-Submitted field, with a value
of "auto-replied", SHOULD be included in the message header of any automatic
response." It is SHOULD, not MUST, so a reply without the field has not been
shown to be human. The technique therefore reads the field first and treats its
absence as unknown.

**The two machine values mean different things.** §5.2: `auto-generated` "SHOULD
be used on messages generated by automatic (often periodic) processes ... which
are not direct responses to other messages", and `auto-replied` "SHOULD be used
on messages sent in direct response to another message by an automatic process".
Both "MUST NOT be used on manually generated messages". An out-of-office answering
your outreach is the `auto-replied` case.

**The marker does not separate a bounce from an out-of-office.** This is a
condition the technique's step 3 needed, and the reason that step exists.
`auto-replied` "MAY be used on Delivery Status Notifications (DSNs)", and the RFC
itself notes that "Some widely-deployed SMTP implementations currently use
"auto-generated" to label non-delivery reports." A failure report can carry either
machine value. Separate reports by their report structure, which the delivery
report standards define and the communication-integrity subject reads, and never
by this header.

**Mark your own automated sends, and expect the responder to stay quiet.** §2:
"Automatic responses SHOULD NOT be issued in response to any message which
contains an Auto-Submitted header field ... where that field has any value other
than "no"." A sequence message marked `auto-generated` asks compliant responders
not to answer it at all. That trades an out-of-office you could have read for a
responder loop you avoid. The technique leaves the choice to the sender and
requires only that inbound classification never depends on it.

## What the field ships (vendor pages, retrieved 2026-09-28)

**Recruiting sequencers stop on any reply and name no auto-reply case.** Gem: "If
a prospect replies, the Sequence stops immediately to prevent over-messaging."
Ashby: "Ashby automatically stops a candidate's sequence if they reply to one of
the emails in that sequence." Greenhouse: "Emails stop immediately when the
prospect responds and/or when a calendar invite is created with the prospect's
email address." LinkedIn Recruiter cancels the scheduled follow-up "If the
candidate responds to the initial message". None of these pages describes
out-of-office handling, so the halt default is what recruiting ships. Whether any
of them treats an auto-reply as a reply internally is not documented.

**Sending through is the documented failure, and detection differs by client.**
HubSpot's sequences documentation: "out-of-office replies are excluded from the
sequence and your contacts will remain enrolled" for several client pairs, and
"Gmail out-of-office replies sent to Outlook inboxes aren't recognized and
therefore contacts will be unenrolled". One product therefore both sends through
some absences and halts on others, and the difference depends on the mail
clients involved. A field report against an open-source sequencer describes the
cost of sending through: "the sequence is over before they are back."

**Pause-and-resume is measured only in sales, and a fixed delay is the variant
that fails.** Outreach (a sales sequencer) resumes at a return date it extracts
from the reply. Its own data team found that "relying on a ruleset led to nearly
16% of Sequences being resumed too early, and if the prospect was OOO longer than
two weeks, this increased to almost 60%." That is the measurement behind the
technique's "undated means halted". The same vendor's outcome figures measure
meetings booked, not the recipient's experience, and nothing retrieved here
measures a candidate's reaction to a resumed sequence.

## What this pin does not settle

- Header coverage in practice: how often real responders omit the field. The
  standard permits omitting it; nobody retrieved here counted how often they do.
- Non-email channels, where no such header exists.
