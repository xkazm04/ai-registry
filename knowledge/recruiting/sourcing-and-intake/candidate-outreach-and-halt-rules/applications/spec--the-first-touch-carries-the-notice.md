# The first-touch notice as the texts write it

**Pin.** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Articles 14 and 21, as consolidated
and retrieved 2026-09-28 from `gdpr-info.eu/art-14-gdpr/` and
`gdpr-info.eu/art-21-gdpr/`. The CRTC's CASL FAQ (`crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm`),
read from the Wayback snapshot of 2026-01-20 because the live page refused the
fetch. 15 U.S.C. § 7702 (CAN-SPAM definitions) from `law.cornell.edu`. Every
quotation below was matched against the fetched text after drafting.

## Rule by rule

**The deadline is the first message.** Confirmed. Art. 14(3) gives three
deadlines for data not obtained from the person, and the one that binds outreach
is (b): the information is due "if the personal data are to be used for
communication with the data subject, at the latest at the time of the first
communication to that data subject". Paragraph (a) caps the whole thing at "at
the latest within one month" in any case, so holding a sourced profile silently
for a quarter and then writing is late twice.

**The source is part of what is owed.** Confirmed, and it is the technique's
step 2. Art. 14(2)(f) lists, among the information the controller must provide,
"from which source the personal data originate, and if applicable, whether it
came from publicly accessible sources". "We found your public profile" is not
decoration; it is the clause.

**The objection line has a position and a form.** Confirmed, and wider than the
direct-marketing reading. Art. 21(4): "At the latest at the time of the first
communication with the data subject, the right referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2
shall be explicitly brought to the attention of the data subject and shall be
presented clearly and separately from any other information." Paragraph 1 is the
general objection to processing based on legitimate interests, which is the
basis sourcing normally rests on, so the duty does not depend on calling the
message marketing. "Separately" is the technique's step 4: its own line, not a
clause inside the privacy link.

**Anti-spam law mostly does not reach a direct employer's job message.** A
condition on scope, not a licence. The CRTC: "most employment recruitment
messaging would not meet the definition of a commercial electronic message (CEM)
in CASL as these would not normally offer, advertise, market or promote a product
or service. However, some recruitment messages may in fact constitute CEMs",
and a message to someone about an opening "would not constitute commercial
activity as long as the message is solely for the purpose of offering them that
position." CAN-SPAM's definition is the same shape: a commercial message is one
"the primary purpose of which is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a
commercial product or service". Neither text finds that a recruiting message is
outside their scope in every case, and the CRTC names the agency case as the one
that tips over. The technique's point stands either way: the data-protection
duty is triggered by where the data came from and its use for contact, and it
does not wait on the anti-spam answer.

## What this pin does not settle

- Whether a given sourcing platform's terms already delivered the notice on the
  controller's behalf. That is a fact about the platform, and a message that
  states the source costs one sentence either way.
- The exemptions in Art. 14(5), including disproportionate effort. The effort
  argument is at its weakest for a person you are in the act of writing to.
- National implementations and employment-specific rules layered on the
  regulation, which belong to the multi-jurisdiction compliance subject.
