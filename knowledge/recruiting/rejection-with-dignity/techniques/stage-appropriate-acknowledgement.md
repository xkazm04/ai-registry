# Stage-appropriate acknowledgement

The opening of a decline must match the process the person actually went
through. A candidate who uploaded a document and was screened out in minutes,
and a candidate who gave four hours across three interviews, are owed different
messages — and the difference is not warmth dialled up or down, it is
*accuracy about what happened*.

The rule that does the most work is negative: **never reference an interaction
that did not occur.** "Thank you for taking the time to speak with us", sent to
someone who never spoke to anyone, is a small lie that discredits every other
sentence in the letter — including the true ones about the reason. It is also
the most common defect in templated decline copy, because the warm template was
written for the late-stage case and then reused for the early one, where it is
false.

## Variants, and what each may claim

Derive the variant from the recorded process state, then let the variant decide
what may be referenced:

- **Pre-screen decline.** May reference: an application received, a document
  reviewed. Short, prompt, no feedback expected. Speed is the courtesy here —
  a fast no is worth more than a decorated slow one.
- **Post-screen decline.** May reference: a review against the role's
  requirements, a screening questionnaire the person answered. May carry the
  decisive reason.
- **Knockout decline.** A special case: the disqualifying fact is
  candidate-supplied and factual, so the letter is brief, unambiguous, and
  carries no assessment of the person at all. Confusing this with a
  quality-based decline is a category error that reads as an insult.
- **Post-interview decline.** May reference: the specific conversation that
  happened, by round, and the people's time. Must not upgrade one screening
  call into "our interviews".
- **Finalist decline.** A human, synchronously, first. The written message
  follows the call and records what was said; it never breaks the news.

Two orthogonal modulations are legitimate on top of the variant. **Effort**
already spent raises the acknowledgement. **Candidate archetype** — early
career, career changer — may adjust encouragement, since the same neutral
sentence lands very differently on a first application and on a twentieth year
of a career. Encouragement modulated by archetype must still be true: it may
change emphasis, never facts, and it may never promise consideration that will
not happen.

## Stage comes from the record, not from a board label

Teams rename their pipeline columns constantly, and a display string is not a
process state. Per
[meaning-does-not-live-in-a-label](../../_laws.md#meaning-does-not-live-in-a-label),
the variant keys off a stable stage vocabulary — applied, screened,
interviewed, offered, terminal — mapped from whatever the team calls its
columns. A renamed column that silently reclassifies an interviewed candidate
as a fresh applicant produces exactly the phantom-interview failure in reverse:
a person who met four of your colleagues receiving a thanks-for-applying note.

## Register rules that survive translation

- **Gender neutrality by recast.** Rewrite the sentence so gender never has to
  be expressed — address the person directly, use the role or the plain second
  person. Never use slash forms or bracketed alternatives, and never use a
  plural form to describe one person: both read as clerical processing, and in
  inflected languages the naive "neutral" construction is precisely what
  produces a misgendered letter. Recasting is the only technique that survives
  every language you send in.
- **One letter, one language authority.** When a generated body is wrapped in
  deterministic chrome — a subject line, a footer, a feedback intro from a
  locale catalogue — both halves must resolve the language from the *same*
  recorded source, ideally the locale the candidate chose when they applied. Two
  independent language decisions in one message produce a letter that greets in
  one language and closes in another, and the guess-from-the-document heuristic
  is what fails first.
- **Quoted record text keeps its own language.** Reasons recorded by a recruiter
  live in the language they were written in; only the sentence around them is
  translated. A machine-translated quotation is no longer a quotation, and the
  small mixed-language seam is more honest than a smooth mistranslation of the
  one sentence that has to be exact.
- **Register consistency.** The salutation, the body and the closing must sit
  at one level of formality. A formal opening with a casual sign-off reads as
  machine assembly, which undercuts a message whose entire credibility rests on
  sounding like a person made a decision.
- **Honest warmth only.** No "we will keep your details on file" when the
  retention window is short; no "we were very impressed" from an automated
  first pass; no "please apply again soon" when the requisition is closed and
  nothing similar is planned. State the actual next facts — how long the record
  is kept, whether a reapplication is welcome, and how to reach a human.

## Decision rules

- When the record cannot establish the stage, use the **lowest** variant that
  is certainly true. Under-claiming an interaction is a minor loss of warmth;
  over-claiming is a falsehood.
- When effort and stage disagree — a long take-home at an early stage —
  acknowledge the effort, not the stage.
- When the decline is late-stage, do not let an automated pass send it before
  the human conversation happens; the machine's speed is exactly wrong here.
- Never let acknowledgement text be generated freely. It is variant copy,
  reviewed once, reused deterministically — the only per-candidate parts are
  facts pulled from the record.

## When not to use this

- **Where a single statutory notice is prescribed** and variation would put the
  compliance text at risk.
- **Where a candidate has asked for no further contact** — the withdrawal path
  governs, and the acknowledgement is minimal and final.
- **In bulk withdrawal of a closed requisition**, where the honest message is
  about the role, not the person: variant selection by stage is the wrong axis
  when the reason is organisational.
