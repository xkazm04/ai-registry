# Accomplishment statements without invention

A duty statement says what the job was ("responsible for the reporting pipeline"). An
accomplishment statement says what the person did and what changed because of it
("rebuilt the reporting pipeline; month-end close went from five days to two"). Readers
prefer the second for a simple reason: it is checkable. It names an action, an object and
an outcome, each of which a reference or an interviewer can probe. The evidence for this
is modest - detailed, clear content correlates with more interviews, recruiters say they
like outcome words and dislike self-descriptors - and no study isolates the formula; the
rule holds because it serves the reader's real question, not because a formula was
validated.

The whole difficulty is the third part. The outcome is the part most CVs lack and the part
a writing tool is most tempted to supply.

## The shape

**Action + object + outcome**, in that order, one line, rarely two:

- **Action** - a verb that states what *this person* did, at the level they did it.
  Built, fixed, ran, negotiated, taught, audited. Not a verb chosen for strength.
- **Object** - the specific thing: the system, the process, the client, the team, named
  as the record names it. Specific nouns are where credibility lives; a rewrite that
  swaps "the invoice matching service" for "key systems" has made the line worse.
- **Outcome** - what changed, stated as the record states it: a number, a scope, a
  before/after, a consequence ("adopted by three other teams", "became the onboarding
  standard").

Lead with the element that matters most to the target reader; the order inside the line
is free, the three parts are not.

## The outcome ladder - use the highest rung the record holds

1. **A measured result the owner supplied** - a number with its unit and its period:
   "cut close from five days to two".
2. **A stated scale or scope** - volume, frequency, audience, money handled, team size:
   "for 40 stores", "weekly, to the leadership team", "a 12-person team".
3. **A stated before/after or consequence without a number** - "replaced a manual
   spreadsheet process", "became the template the other offices adopted".
4. **The action and object alone** - an honest duty line in active voice. This is a
   valid resting state, not a failure.

Descending the ladder is always honest. Climbing it requires a fact from the owner.

## Decision rules

- **When the record holds a number, lead with it or end on it** - never bury it
  mid-sentence, and never round it upward ("about 30%" does not become "30%+").
- **When the record holds no outcome, do not estimate one.** Not "approximately", not
  "significant", not a plausible percentage. [Absence of evidence is not
  evidence](../../../_laws.md#absence-of-evidence-is-not-evidence): the missing metric is a
  question with a slot - "what changed because of this, and how would you show it?" -
  and the line stays on rung 3 or 4 until the owner answers.
- **A suggested outcome is a proposal, never a line.** If a tool drafts "reduced X by
  [how much?]", the placeholder renders as an unanswered question the document cannot
  be exported with, never as text a reader could mistake for a claim; [inference must look
  like inference](../../../_laws.md#inference-must-look-like-inference).
- **A verb may not raise the claim.** "Responsible for" may become the verb the record
  supports ("ran", "maintained"); it may not become "owned", "led" or "spearheaded"
  unless the record says the person led. Participation into leadership, contribution into
  sole authorship, "we" into "I" for work the record attributes to a team - each is an
  embellishment and belongs to the owner's choice, not the tool's.
- **Cut self-descriptors, keep facts.** "Results-driven", "passionate", "team player",
  "hard-working" carry no checkable content and are among the phrases recruiters most
  often report disliking. Removing them is safe; replacing them with another adjective is
  not an improvement.
- **One idea per bullet, most important first.** A paragraph cut into one bullet per
  sentence is not construction; merge sentences that describe one piece of work, drop the
  ones that restate the title, and order what remains by weight.
- **Keep the owner's specifics verbatim** - product names, domain terms, numbers, client
  types. Tidy their spelling and casing; never generalise them.

## What a deterministic pass can and cannot do

Rule code can do the safe half: spelling and canonical casing of names, re-joining broken
lines, capitalising, stripping a self-descriptor list, converting a weak opener to a
*neutral* active verb, flagging lines with no outcome, and extracting any number already
present so it can lead. It cannot supply an outcome, and it should say so: its most
valuable output is the list of lines that stopped at rung 4, each with the question that
would lift it. That list, shown to the owner, is the "boost".

## When not to use this

Academic and some public-sector CVs are registers where a listing of positions,
publications and grants *is* the accomplishment and outcome-phrasing reads as
salesmanship; follow the register. Early-career documents with little paid work should
apply the same shape to projects, study and volunteering rather than inflating thin roles.

## Measured boundary: a screen that compares digits cannot see these

A rewrite screen built on "no number the record lacks" and a closed list of leadership
words still passes five invented claims: a bound glued to a stated figure ("about 30%"
becomes "30%+", "over 30%"), a magnitude adverb on an outcome with no number
("significantly reduced"), a participation verb replaced by an everyday leadership verb
("assisted with" becomes "drove" or "managed"), and "we" rewritten as "I". Close each
relative to the replaced line and the owner's own words: a bound, a magnitude word or a
first-person pronoun the line did not carry becomes the owner's question, and one it
already carried passes. Measured on one tree with five constructed cases (n=5, 2026-10-14);
the localized forms were not tested.
