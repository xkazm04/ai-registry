# Market conventions and personal data

What a CV is expected to contain differs by market, and the differences are not
cosmetic: a photograph, a birth date or a marital status on the page opens a channel for
exactly the discrimination the fairness subjects of this bundle exist to prevent. The law
almost everywhere restricts what an *employer may ask*; almost nowhere does it restrict
what a *candidate may volunteer*. So the convention of each market - and the defaults of
the tool writing the document - decide what actually reaches the reader.

## What the evidence says about the channel

- A meta-analysis of hiring-discrimination field experiments (169 studies, published
  2005-2020) found callback ratios for disadvantaged groups of about 0.63 for physical
  appearance, 0.69 for age, 0.88 for marital status and 0.59 for disability, with age
  discrimination stronger in Europe than in the Americas and no downward trend over time.
  Estimates resting on few studies (appearance among them) are uncertain.
- Photo experiments: attractive men received roughly twice the callbacks of plain men,
  while women *without* a photo were called back more than women with one (one country,
  5,312 CVs); in a market where photos are customary, an applicant with a minority name
  and a headscarf in the photo received 4.2% callbacks against 18.8% for a majority-name
  applicant (1,474 applications).
- Anonymisation is not automatically protective. A public pilot of anonymised applications
  in one European country broadly equalised invitation rates for women and migrants
  (8,550 applications, not randomised, self-selected employers); a randomised trial of
  anonymous CVs in another *widened* the gap for minority candidates, because it stripped
  context that sympathetic employers had used to excuse a patchy history. The lesson for a
  writer: withhold the traits that trigger bias, keep the context that explains the career.

## The per-market table (conventions, graded)

Almost every "recruiters in market X expect Y" figure in circulation is blog-grade or
misattributed; treat this table as convention, and prefer the law column where it exists.

| Market | Photo | Birth date | Marital status | Signature | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | no - employers avoid it, identification photos come after an offer | no - also avoid graduation years that reveal age | no | no | 1 page early career, 2 accepted above |
| United Kingdom | no (convention) | no | no | no | 2 pages |
| Germany | traditional, declining; optional | traditional, fading | traditional, fading | place, date and signature traditional, fading | 1-2 pages, tabular, reverse-chronological |
| Czech Republic | common, disputed | customary but advised against by careers services | omit | no | about 2 pages |
| France | cannot be required; public guidance advises against; still common | common | declining | no | 1 page |
| European standard format | optional field | optional field | no field | no | tends long |

Legal anchors, by kind: an equality or anti-discrimination statute naming protected
grounds (all of the above); a rule that pre-offer questions must be job-relevant or have a
"direct and necessary link" to the role; a labour-code prohibition on asking about family,
pregnancy or origin; and data-protection minimisation. A photograph is ordinary personal
data, not biometric data, unless processed by technical means for unique identification -
but data that *reveal* a special category by inference (a headscarf, a visible disability)
are arguably special-category data wherever they are processed for that.

## Decision rules

- **Default every protected-attribute field to off, in every market.** Photo, birth date,
  age, marital status, number of children, nationality (unless work authorisation is
  asked for), religion, health. A template slot is not a reason to fill it.
- **Offer the market's convention as an owner choice, stated with its trade-off.** "In
  this market a photo is common; field experiments show photos carry appearance and
  religion effects. Include one?" The choice and its default are recorded. When the market
  is unknown or mixed, the answer is off; [uncertainty resolves toward the
  candidate](../../../_laws.md#uncertainty-resolves-toward-the-candidate).
- **Work authorisation, not nationality.** Where right-to-work matters, the honest,
  minimal statement is "authorised to work in X", not a citizenship line.
- **Address at city level.** A full street address carries neighbourhood and wealth
  signals and exposes the candidate; city (and willingness to relocate) is enough.
- **Languages on the common European six-level scale** where the reader will know it;
  "native" for first languages.
- **A signature and date line only where the market still expects it**, as an owner
  option, never as an image of a real signature embedded without the owner supplying it.
- **Headings, date formats and the word for "present" follow the document's language,
  not the tool's interface language.** The document is content; its labels come from a
  per-language table keyed off a stable internal vocabulary; [meaning does not live in a
  label](../../../_laws.md#meaning-does-not-live-in-a-label).
- **Never state a market norm with false precision.** "Most German employers expect a
  photo" is not established; "a photo is traditional in Germany and increasingly
  optional" is. Show the basis of a recommendation; [a claim carries its sample and its
  basis](../../../_laws.md#a-claim-carries-its-sample-and-its-basis).

## When not to use this

Where an employer's mandated form or a regulated sector requires a field (a date of birth
for a licensing check, a photo for a role where appearance is a genuine occupational
requirement such as acting or modelling), the requirement governs - and the requirement
belongs to that form, not to the general CV.
