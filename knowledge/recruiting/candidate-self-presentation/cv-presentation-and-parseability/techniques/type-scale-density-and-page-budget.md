# Type scale, density and page budget

A CV page is a printed or on-screen document read at arm's length, fast. Its typography
has one job: make the hierarchy obvious and the text comfortable at the speed it is read.
Nothing in this technique is CV-specific research - the legibility evidence comes from
general reading studies, and the page-count evidence is a vendor simulation that confounded
length with detail - so the numbers below are defended conventions, and the rules are the
reasons they hold.

## The scale

| Level | Size | Weight / style |
|---|---|---|
| Name | 20-28 pt | bold or display face |
| Section heading | 11-13 pt (about 1.2-1.4x body) | bold, optionally small rule |
| Entry head (title - employer - dates) | body size to +1 pt | title bold, rest regular |
| Body and bullets | 10-11 pt | regular |
| Secondary (dates, locations, context) | body or 0.5-1 pt smaller | regular, muted but high-contrast |

**Decision rules:**

- **Body type does not go below 10 pt to save a page.** Public careers guidance asks for
  11 pt or larger; 10 pt is the floor for a dense experienced CV in a compact face. When
  content does not fit, cut content (the recency and bullet-budget rules), never shrink
  type or leading.
- **Secondary text is smaller only by a small step and never low-contrast.** Grey dates
  at 8 pt fail printing, photocopies and older readers - and dates are among the first
  things read.
- **One or two families.** A serif for the name and headings with a sans body, or one
  family throughout. Reading studies show no serif-versus-sans legibility difference;
  claims that a typeface signals "competence" or "trust" have no retrievable evidence.
  Choose a face with complete diacritics for every language the document will be in.
- **Emphasis budget.** Bold for the name, section headings and entry titles; at most a
  lead phrase per bullet. Italic sparingly. No underline except links in a digital copy.
- **One accent colour**, used on the name, rules or employer names. Every text colour
  meets normal contrast for body text; nothing is conveyed by colour alone.

## Density

- **Line length about 55-90 characters.** Wider lines slow reading. A single column on A4
  with margins of 15-20 mm does **not** land in range at 10-11 pt in a common sans: measured
  over five reference documents, it set full lines at 103-113 characters, every one past 90.
  Reaching the range takes a cap on the running text (about 36 em; bullets and summary
  only, the entry head keeps the full width so dates stay at the edge) or margins near
  30 mm. Two-column layouts are already narrower and land at 73-84.
- **The measure trades against the page budget; decide it with both numbers.** Narrowing
  the measure rewraps about a fifth of the text onto extra lines. In the same measurement a
  fourteen-year document that filled one page exactly went to a second page 4-8% full at
  every cap tried (36, 38 and 40 em) - the spill the page-budget rules forbid. So a measure
  change is not a free fix applied under existing documents: measure line length and page
  fill together, and when narrowing spills a page, either trim content per the spill rule
  below and show the owner the cut, or keep the wider measure and say so. Never reach the
  measure by shrinking type.
- **Leading 1.2-1.4** for body text; bullets separated by a small gap (2-4 pt), entries by
  a visible one (6-12 pt), sections by more. Whitespace groups; rules and boxes are optional
  reinforcement.
- **Bullets of one or two lines.** A three-line bullet is two ideas or one idea written
  loosely.
- **Margins not below about 12 mm** for print safety, and consistent on every page.

## The page budget

- **Early career (up to about three years):** one page in most markets.
- **Experienced:** two pages is normal in most of Europe and acceptable above entry level
  in the US; a vendor simulation found reviewers preferred two-page documents, but those
  documents carried nearly twice the words, so the finding is about detail, not paper.
- **Senior and specialist:** two pages, with an annex (publications, projects) only if the
  market expects it.
- **Academic CVs** are complete records and are exempt.

**Decision rules:**

- **When a document spills onto a new page by a few lines, fix the content, not the
  layout** - compress the oldest role, cut the weakest bullets. A second page holding three
  lines looks like an accident.
- **When a second page is used, fill at least a third of it**, repeat the name in the page
  header or footer (in addition to, never instead of, the name in the flow), and never
  split an entry head from its first bullet across the break.
- **When a template needs 9 pt type to fit typical content on one page, the template is
  wrong for that content**, not the content for the template.
- **Measure overflow and report it to the owner** with the specific cut that would fix it,
  rather than silently scaling the page.

## When not to use this

Markets and sectors with a mandated form (public-sector templates, standardised European
formats) set their own typography; comply and apply only the legibility floors. Visual
portfolios are judged as design and may choose their own scale, with a plain version
alongside.
