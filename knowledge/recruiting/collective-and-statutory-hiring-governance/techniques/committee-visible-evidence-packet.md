# The committee-visible evidence packet

Stepping back from the decision does not mean producing less. A deliberating body
has one characteristic failure — it anchors on whoever speaks first and on
whatever document opens the meeting — and a well-built packet is the highest-value
thing a tool can contribute to a collective process precisely there.

A packet is: **the same evidence, in the same shape, delivered to every member
before deliberation, with each candidate's evidence and its limits stated at the
same altitude.** Every clause in that sentence is doing work.

## The four properties

**Same evidence.** Every member sees an identical packet. Personalised ordering,
per-member "highlights", or a view that surfaces different strengths to different
readers destroys the one thing a shared document provides: a common factual
baseline that disagreement can be about.

**Same shape.** Each candidate gets the same sections in the same order with the
same headings, including the sections where they have nothing. A candidate whose
teaching-evidence section is absent looks different from one whose section says
*no evidence recorded* — and only the second is honest.

**Before deliberation.** The packet is a pre-read. Distributed in the meeting, it
becomes the anchor rather than the input. Where the process supports it, members
record their independent reads **before** seeing each other's, or the panel
produces one person's opinion with five signatures. Many tools have no
interviewer-level identity at all and therefore cannot support this; that is a
real gap to close, not a step to skip.

**Limits at the same altitude as the claims.** A strength and the thinness of the
evidence behind it appear together, in the same visual register. A packet that
puts confident prose in the body and caveats in a footer has decided which one
the committee will read.

## Procedure

1. **Bind the packet to what it judged.** Stamp the version of the role brief,
   the rubric, the candidate set and the evaluation run that produced it. A
   committee reconvening in six weeks over a stale packet is deliberating about a
   job that no longer exists
   ([a verdict is bound to what it judged](../../_laws.md#a-verdict-is-bound-to-what-it-judged)).
2. **State the basis of every comparative claim.** Cohort size, what varied, what
   was measured and what was not. A packet that says "strongest on domain depth"
   over a field of three thin profiles has made a claim its sample cannot carry
   ([a claim carries its sample and its basis](../../_laws.md#a-claim-carries-its-sample-and-its-basis)).
3. **Quote the record; do not characterise the person.** Every line traces to
   something a candidate submitted or an assessor recorded. No invented gaps, no
   confident-sounding inference presented as observation, no advice about a
   person nobody made
   ([say only what the record holds](../../_laws.md#say-only-what-the-record-holds)).
4. **Include the ceiling and the mode.** The packet says which governance regime
   produced it and what step remains outside the machine. A packet that reads as
   a complete case for a decision has misrepresented its own status.
5. **Seal the packet as issued.** What the committee was given, when, and to
   whom, retained unchanged. Later regeneration produces a new packet, visibly
   superseding — never a silent in-place edit, because the deliberation happened
   over the old text.
6. **Write for a hostile reader.** In public-sector and academic hiring, assume
   open-records requests, candidate access requests and discovery reach this
   document. That is a *writing* constraint before it is a storage one: anything
   the packet cannot defend on its face should not be in it.

## Decision rules

- When evidence for a candidate is materially thinner than for the rest of the
  field, say so as a property of the *packet*, not of the person. "Less evidence
  was available" and "weaker candidate" are different sentences and the packet
  must not merge them.
- When the packet includes a machine ordering, it appears with its separation and
  robustness stated, and without a crown — the packet informs a vote, it does not
  pre-empt one.
- When a member requests additional material on one candidate mid-process, it is
  distributed to all members with the same stamp. Evidence that reaches part of a
  body is a procedural defect.
- When the underlying evaluation changes after distribution, notify rather than
  swap. Members must know they are reading a superseded packet before they
  discover it in the room.
- When the packet would contain a field that is inadmissible to the decision —
  a redacted attribute, a protected status, a compensation figure that cannot be
  compared across schemes — withhold the field entirely rather than annotating
  it. Nobody unsees a number.

## When not to use it

Do not produce a packet for a process that has no deliberating body; a
single-decider does not need the anti-anchoring machinery and the ceremony
obscures the recommendation they are entitled to.

Do not let the packet become a substitute for the committee's own record. The
packet is what the machine gave them; the minutes, the votes and the recorded
recommendation are theirs, produced by them, attributed to them. A tool that
generates both has quietly become the committee.
