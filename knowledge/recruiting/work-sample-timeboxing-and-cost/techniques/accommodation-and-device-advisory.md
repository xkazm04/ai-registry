# Advisory, not blocker — and adjustments as normal practice

Two rules that are really one. Anything you know about the working conditions of
a timed exercise is told to the candidate as **information they may act on**, and
anything a candidate needs in order to sit it fairly is **granted as a matter of
course**. Neither becomes a gate, because every gate on a timed assessment
selects on circumstances rather than capability.

## The device advisory

A long editing task is genuinely harder on a phone. That fact belongs to the
candidate, phrased as a warning: *this exercise involves extended writing; a
computer with a keyboard will be more comfortable, but you can continue on this
device.* What it must never become is a check that refuses to start.

The reason is that a device gate is a wealth filter wearing a usability costume.
A meaningful share of qualified candidates in most markets have a phone and no
laptop — more so among younger, lower-income, and non-office applicants, and in
regions where mobile is the primary computing device. Blocking them produces a
selection effect on income, and it produces it invisibly, because the blocked
candidate never enters your funnel to be counted.

The same shape governs every environment condition: browser version, screen
size, connection quality, whether the page can go full-screen. State what is
harder; let them decide. They know their circumstances and you do not.

## Adjustments

- **Published, not conditional on asking.** The invitation itself says that
  adjustments are available and how to request one. An adjustment process
  discoverable only by candidates confident enough to ask selects for confidence.
- **Extra time is the default adjustment for a timed exercise**, and it is
  granted without a diagnosis, a letter, or an explanation of the underlying
  condition. Requiring medical evidence to add thirty minutes to an unpaid
  exercise is a barrier far more expensive than the thirty minutes.
- **Format alternatives count.** A live session instead of a written one, a
  split across two sittings, a written brief instead of a video one, a task
  delivered in a screen-reader-friendly form. Some candidates need a different
  shape, not more clock.
- **The request goes to someone who is not scoring the candidate.** Whoever
  handles adjustments must be outside the assessing panel, and the panel learns
  only the resulting arrangement, never the reason.
- **The granted adjustment must be invisible in the assessment record.** An
  accommodation that surfaces to an assessor as "took ninety minutes longer" has
  been converted into a penalty. Store the effective allowance separately from
  any comparative timing statistic, and exclude adjusted sittings from timing
  comparisons rather than letting them drag a distribution.

## Procedure

1. **Enumerate the exercise's real environment assumptions** at design time —
   input device, screen, time contiguity, connectivity, audio, language.
2. **For each, write the advisory sentence and check no code path turns it into a
   refusal.** Detection may inform copy; it may never bar entry.
3. **Publish the adjustment offer in the invitation, with a route that does not
   go through the panel**, and state the response time. A candidate waiting on an
   unanswered request while a deadline runs is being penalised by your process
   ([a-candidates-process-never-stalls-on-your-constraints](../../_laws.md#a-candidates-process-never-stalls-on-your-constraints)).
4. **Grant time-based adjustments by default**, and reserve any review for
   requests that change the instrument itself rather than its conditions.
5. **Make the deadline flexible before you make the timebox flexible.** Most
   caring, shift-work and health constraints are about *when* the contiguous
   block exists, not how long it needs to be. Widening the window from three days
   to ten costs you nothing and recovers a large share of the pool.
6. **Record adjustments as process facts** — what was granted, when, by whom —
   separately from anything an assessor reads.

## Decision rules

- **When a condition would block, downgrade it to a warning.** If a genuine hard
  requirement exists — the task cannot be done without audio, say — disclose it
  *in the invitation*, before the candidate has spent any time, and offer an
  alternative route. Discovering a blocker after the clock starts is the worst
  case in this whole subject.
- **When you are unsure whether an adjustment is warranted, grant it.** The cost
  of an unnecessary thirty minutes is nil; the cost of a refused necessary one is
  a person excluded
  ([uncertainty-resolves-toward-the-candidate](../../_laws.md#uncertainty-resolves-toward-the-candidate)).
- **When an adjustment request arrives mid-exercise, honour it mid-exercise.**
  Stop the clock, extend, or offer a fresh sitting.
- **When accommodation data would enter a scoring surface, it does not travel.**
  It is among the most sensitive facts a hiring process holds, and it has no
  legitimate assessor-facing use.
- **When a candidate declines to explain a request, that is complete.** The
  request is the fact; the reason is theirs.

## When not to use it

An advisory is not the right instrument for a condition that genuinely destroys
the measurement or the candidate's chance of success — one where continuing means
certain failure rather than discomfort. Those belong in the invitation as
prerequisites with an alternative offered, so the choice is made before any time
is spent. The technique's claim is narrower and firmer: a condition that merely
makes the exercise *harder* is information, and a condition discovered at the
starting line is never a gate.

## The failure it prevents

A candidate reaches the exercise, is refused by a device or browser check, and
exits the process. Nothing in your funnel records a rejection — they appear as an
invitation that was never accepted. The whole cost lands on them and none of the
evidence lands on you, which is why this failure survives for years in systems
whose owners believe their assessment is accessible.
