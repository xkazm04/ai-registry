# Certification regimes in US federal hiring, September 2026

**Pin.** Retrieved 2026-09-28 with curl (the Federal Register text from govinfo,
the statute from Cornell LII, the OPM memo and FAQ as PDFs converted to text).
Every quotation below was matched against the fetched text after drafting. The
federal examining rules changed twice in eighteen months, so the clock is six
months.

## What changed, and when

The rule of three is gone. OPM's final rule "Reinvigorating Merit-Based Hiring
through Candidate Ranking" (90 FR 43135, 8 September 2025) says: "The National
Defense Authorization Act of 2019 eliminated the rule of three, mooting the
requirement to use category rating in lieu of the rule of three", and "Under
this final rule, agencies have the choice to use either category rating or the
rule of many." Its dates: "This rule will be effective on November 7, 2025.
Agencies must be in full compliance with this final rule not later than March
9, 2026." OPM's FAQ puts it in one line: the Rule of Many "replaces the former
'Rule of Three' in federal hiring."

So a federal register reaches a selecting official in one of two shapes.

## Shape one: the score-ordered certificate (Rule of Many)

OPM's memo to agency human-capital officers (13 January 2026): "This new process
uses a numerical ranking system. Applicants are assigned scores (augmented for
veterans' preference) and placed in rank order. Agencies then make selections
from a pre-determined number of the highest-ranked qualified candidates."

The FAQ's worked example shows who does what, in order: "the agency scores the
assessment; adjudicates veterans' preference and adds preference points; and
creates a ranked list in score order, including veterans' preference points."
And: "Disabled veterans with a 10% or more service-connected disability are
placed at the top of a certificate of eligibles regardless of score (except for
General Schedule (GS) scientific and professional positions at GS-9 or above
(or equivalent) grade levels)."

The amended 5 CFR 332.404 bounds the choice: "A selecting official may select
any eligible candidate referred for selection. However, a selecting official
may not pass over a preference eligible to select a lower standing
non-preference eligible unless the agency has complied with the pass-over
procedures in paragraph (b) of this section. The selection mechanism must be
determined before soliciting for applications and be made available to
applicants upon their request."

## Shape two: quality categories (5 U.S.C. 3319)

Candidates go into "2 or more quality categories based on merit ... rather than
assigned individual numerical ratings." Then: "Within each quality category
established under subsection (a), preference-eligibles shall be listed ahead of
individuals who are not preference eligibles. For other than scientific and
professional positions at GS–9 of the General Schedule (equivalent or higher),
qualified preference-eligibles who have a compensable service-connected
disability of 10 percent or more shall be listed in the highest quality
category." And the official's discretion: "An appointing official may select
any applicant in the highest quality category or, if fewer than 3 candidates
have been assigned to the highest quality category, in a merged category".

## What this changes in the technique

- "The decision object is a rank-ordered list" held for one regime of three.
  In the category regime there is no merit order inside a category; in the
  Rule of Many the order is the statute's, preference included.
- In both, preference is adjudicated and applied by the examining office before
  the certificate exists. A tool used by a selecting official is downstream of
  that. Its honest ceiling is the pass-over procedure, not "apply preferences
  before certifying".
- A fit ranking laid beside a certificate as a competing order lists whom to
  pass over, without the procedure. So the fit signal annotates the
  certificate's order and never re-sorts it. Where the statute leaves order open
  (inside a category, apart from the preference-first listing), a fit ordering
  is a legitimate aid, labelled as one.
- "The selection mechanism must be determined before soliciting for
  applications": the regime is per-posting configuration, fixed at posting
  time, inside the eligibility-list mode.

State and local civil service varies further (rule-of-N windows, banding,
absolute preference). This page pins only the federal texts that were read.
