# When advice is the decision: the reliance tests, September 2026

**Pin.** Retrieved 2026-09-28 with curl. EU texts came from the Publications
Office's CELEX resource (the official Journal and court texts); the rest came
from each authority's own site. Every quotation below was matched against the
fetched text after drafting. Two of these texts are less than a year old and
one high-risk timetable has just moved, so the clock is six months.

## Weight: the score a decider draws on

**CJEU, C-634/21 SCHUFA Holding (7 December 2023).** Paragraph 50: where a
probability value "plays a determining role in the granting of credit, the
establishment of that value must be qualified in itself as a decision producing
vis-à-vis a data subject 'legal effects concerning him or her or similarly
significantly [affecting] him or her' within the meaning of Article 22(1) of the
GDPR." Paragraph 61 names the reading it rejects: one "according to which the
establishment of the probability value must only be considered as a preparatory
act", which would create "a risk of circumventing Article 22". The operative
ruling: automated decision-making exists "where a third party, to which that
probability value is transmitted, draws strongly on that probability value to
establish, implement or terminate a contractual relationship with that person."
The case is about credit. The reasoning is about any score passed to someone
who decides, which is the shape of an advisory ranking handed to a committee.

**The Article 29 Working Party's guidance (WP251, as last revised 6 February
2018)**, quoted by the Spanish supervisory authority: "The controller cannot
avoid the Article 22 provisions by fabricating human involvement. For example,
if someone routinely applies automatically generated profiles to individuals
without any actual influence on the result, this would still be a decision based
solely on automated processing." And: oversight must be "meaningful, rather than
just a token gesture. It should be carried out by someone who has the authority
and competence to change the decision."

**UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, s.80**, inserting Article 22A: "a decision
is based solely on automated processing if there is no meaningful human
involvement in the taking of the decision". The UK test is written in the same
terms.

## Override: the output used against the humans' own conclusions

**New York City, 6 RCNY 5-300** (the rule under Local Law 144). A tool
"substantially assist[s] or replace[s] discretionary decision making" when it is
used "i. to rely solely on a simplified output (score, tag, classification,
ranking, etc.), with no other factors considered; or ii. to use a simplified
output as one of a set of criteria where the simplified output is weighted more
than any other criterion in the set; or iii. to use a simplified output to
overrule conclusions derived from other factors including human
decision-making." An advisory ranking falls outside only while none of the three
is true in practice, and practice is what the record has to show.

## Screening: ranking for the final authority is deciding

**Florida's open-government law, as summarised by the Florida Bar (2018).** A
university search committee: "By screening applicants and deciding which
applicants to reject from further consideration, the committee performed a
policy-based, decision-making function delegated to it by the president of the
university" (Wood v. Marston, 1983). And from a later case, "[g]overnmental
advisory committees ... at least those recommendations which eliminate
opportunities for alternative choices by the final authority, or which rank
applications for the final authority" are subject to the open-meeting law
(Silver Express, 1997). The body that ranks is treated as deciding. The case law
was read here as the Bar quotes it, not in the reporters.

## The oversight duty, and its date

**AI Act, Annex III point 4(a)**: high-risk includes "AI systems intended to be
used for the recruitment or selection of natural persons, in particular to place
targeted job advertisements, to analyse and filter job applications, and to
evaluate candidates". An advisory ranking evaluates candidates. **Art. 14(4)(b)**
requires that oversight persons can "remain aware of the possible tendency of
automatically relying or over-relying on the output produced by a high-risk AI
system (automation bias), in particular for high-risk AI systems used to provide
information or recommendations for decisions to be taken by natural persons".

**Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI, 8 July 2026, OJ
24.7.2026)** moved the date. The replaced Art. 113(c): "Chapter III, Sections
1, 2, and 3, with the exception of Article 6(5), shall apply from: (i) 2
December 2027 as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article
6(2) and Annex III". Its recital records the old date, "2 August 2026". GDPR
Article 22 is unaffected and applies now.

## What this changes in the technique

"Never seal a winner" stands, and it is no longer the whole test. The technique
gains a section on what not sealing does not settle (weight, override,
screening), the rule that surfaces outside the seal read the sealing predicate,
and the pointer to the new sibling technique that makes reliance visible.
