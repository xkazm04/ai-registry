# Hook taxonomy bounded by what is provable

The concern: the **closed set of angles** a generator may take on a role, where
membership is decided by one question — *can this angle be grounded entirely in
stated facts?* An open-ended "write something compelling" has no such boundary
and will find the compelling thing wherever it lives, including in invention.

## The taxonomy is a safety device disguised as a creative aid

Two arguments are usually given for a hook menu: variety and usability. Both
are true and neither is the point. A closed taxonomy is the mechanism that
turns the fact-set gate from a filter into a *design*: each hook declares in
advance which facts it consumes, so the system knows before generating whether
the angle is available, and knows afterwards exactly which claims the copy can
contain.

That declaration is what makes the format excludable. Without it, "employee
voice" is a tone the model might adopt in any draft; with it, it is an entry
that either exists in the list or does not.

## Building the taxonomy

For each candidate angle, state three things:

1. **The claim it makes.** Not the vibe — the assertion the reader takes away.
   A "mission" hook asserts that working here advances a stated purpose. A
   "growth" hook asserts that the role leads somewhere. A "team" hook asserts
   something about who you will work with.
2. **The facts that ground it.** Named slots from the fact set. If the list is
   empty, the angle is ungrounded by construction and does not enter the
   taxonomy; if the list contains a fact the record cannot hold, the same.
3. **The behaviour when a required fact is absent.** Every hook needs a defined
   degradation: omit the beat, fall back to a narrower hook, or become
   unavailable. Undefined degradation is where invention enters, because the
   formatter improvises.

A workable menu for most roles: what the team builds; what the role is
accountable for; what the first months contain; the skills the work develops;
the work mode and place as stated; the pay as stated; the process shape and
how long it takes. Each is a claim about the role, sourced from a slot, and
checkable by the hire on day thirty.

## The exclusions are the important part

Angles that fail the test, and the reason each fails, should be written down
next to the taxonomy — a list of exclusions is the only durable form of a
design decision that looks like an omission:

- **Employee testimonial / day-in-the-life in a first-person voice.** Grounded
  only in a real person's experience, which the record does not hold and the
  generator cannot obtain. Excluded on format, not on wording.
- **Culture claims.** "Supportive", "collaborative", "no politics" — the claim
  is about a property of a workplace that nobody measured, and per [a claim
  carries its sample and its basis](../../_laws.md#a-claim-carries-its-sample-and-its-basis)
  an unmeasured property has no basis to carry. If an engagement survey result
  exists and can be cited, it becomes a fact and a hook can use it.
- **Comparative claims.** "Better than the market", "the best team in the
  city" — grounded in a comparison nobody ran.
- **Outcome promises.** "You'll be promoted within a year", "you'll lead a
  team" — a claim about the future of a person not yet hired.
- **Unbounded benefit claims.** "Unlimited holiday", "generous budget" —
  either a stated policy with a number, or absent.

## Decision rules

- **A hook that can be written without any fact is not a hook, it is filler.**
  The cleanest membership test: strip the fact slots and see whether the beat
  still produces text. If it does, that text is genre language and will be
  emitted for every role identically. The one deliberate exception is a
  designated *floor hook* grounded in the role itself — the title is always
  present — kept so that a record with no other facts still yields one honest
  asset instead of an empty result, which teams treat as a defect and route
  around.
- **Enforce membership at the boundary, not in the instruction.** When a
  generator returns an angle outside the taxonomy, map it onto a designated
  fallback member rather than passing the new category through. An unrecognised
  angle that reaches storage becomes an angle nobody defined, nobody
  labelled and nobody grounded, and it will be rendered to a recruiter as
  though it were a designed one.
- **Replace an excluded hook rather than leaving a hole.** Removing the
  highest-converting format is durable only if something occupies its slot in
  the menu. A concrete, groundable angle offered in its place — the stack, the
  work, the first months — is what stops the exclusion being relitigated every
  quarter.
- **The taxonomy is closed at the point of generation, not at the point of
  review.** A free-text angle field that the recruiter can fill with anything
  reopens everything the taxonomy closed. If custom angles are needed, they
  are a human-authored asset with a named author.
- **Per [say only what the record
  holds](../../_laws.md#say-only-what-the-record-holds), an angle survives only
  as far as its facts.** When two of a hook's three facts are present, the hook
  runs with two beats and emits a code for the third; it does not
  substitute a similar-sounding claim.
- **Adding a hook is a governance change, not a feature.** The question to ask
  is not "would this convert" — the answer is always yes — but "what does it
  assert and where does the assertion come from".
- **Structure the copy as a fixed sequence of beats.** A short, named beat
  order — the opening claim, the substance, the honest qualifier, the action —
  keeps the generator inside the hook's fact budget, because a free-form
  narrative expands to fill the requested length by adding material, and the
  material it adds is invented.

## When not to use it

- **Not for employer-brand content written by a person.** A brand writer works
  from research, interviews and their own accountability; the taxonomy governs
  what a machine may originate.
- **Not as a ceiling on human editing.** A recruiter who edits generated copy
  is now its author and may add what they can stand behind. The taxonomy
  bounds generation; the language lint and the honesty policy still bound the
  result.
- **Not where a hook's facts exist but the fact set does not yet carry them.**
  The answer there is to extend the fact set with a real assertion path, not
  to let the hook improvise. An angle blocked for want of a slot is a backlog
  item, not a reason to relax the taxonomy.
