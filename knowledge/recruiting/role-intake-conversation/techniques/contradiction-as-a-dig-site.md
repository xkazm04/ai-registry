# Contradiction as a dig site

An intake contradiction is not an error to be corrected. It is the highest-
yield signal the session produces, because it marks the exact place where two
real forces meet and nobody has yet decided which wins. "Senior, but the band
is mid." "Must be onsite three days, but the team is in four time zones."
"Needs to be hands-on, and will manage six people." "We need them in six
weeks, and they must have led a transformation."

Each of these is a **decision the organization has not made**, arriving
disguised as two requirements. Left alone, it does not stay unresolved: it is
resolved silently and badly, by whoever hits it first — the sourcer who
quietly drops the seniority to make the band work, the screener who filters
on the location line, the interviewer who tests for the management skill
nobody agreed was required. Each of those is an unattributed decision about
what the job is, made by someone without standing to make it.

The technique is to **name the contradiction aloud, in the session, without
accusation, and dig at the point of tension** — because the excavation
produces the requestor's real priority ordering, which is the single most
useful thing intake can extract and the one thing they will never volunteer.

## The procedure

1. **Detect.** Contradictions cluster in known pairs — seniority against
   budget, autonomy against oversight, breadth against depth, urgency against
   rarity, location against the team's actual geography, hands-on against
   headcount, "we want diverse candidates" against a requirement that filters
   for one background. Watch these pairs across the whole session, not
   turn-by-turn; the halves usually arrive ten minutes apart.
2. **Name it plainly and without blame.** "Let me put two things side by
   side, because I think they're pulling against each other: you said X, and
   also Y." The framing is *the requirements* conflict, not *you*
   contradicted yourself. Requestors who hear the second version defend; the
   defence produces a rationalization, and a rationalization is worse than
   either original answer because it sounds resolved.
3. **Refuse to resolve it yourself.** Do not offer the compromise. The
   interviewer's compromise will be accepted out of politeness and recorded
   as a requirement nobody chose.
4. **Dig at the tension with a priority question, not a fix question.** "If
   you could only have one — the depth or the availability in six weeks —
   which one keeps the ninety-day outcome alive?" Anchoring the trade-off to
   the outcome (rather than to preference) is what makes it answerable.
5. **Record the resolution, its owner, and its date — or record it
   unresolved.** An unresolved contradiction is a legitimate and valuable
   output: it is an escalation, usually to whoever owns the band or the
   headcount. What is not legitimate is a brief in which the tension has
   quietly disappeared.

## Decision rules

- **Name it in the session, never reconcile it afterwards.** Silent
  post-session reconciliation by the recruiter is the failure this technique
  exists to prevent. The recruiter's tidy-up is an inference, and when it
  enters the brief looking like a stated requirement it breaks
  [inference-must-look-like-inference](../../_laws.md#inference-must-look-like-inference)
  at the worst possible place — the document every downstream decision cites.
- **The resolution names its actor.** Per
  [every-decision-names-its-actor](../../_laws.md#every-decision-names-its-actor),
  a trade-off resolved in the room is attributed to the requestor; one
  escalated is attributed to whoever finally decided; one still open is
  attributed to nobody and marked open. A brief that shows a clean, single
  seniority with no trace of the conflict has laundered a decision.
- **Dig at most two contradictions per session, chosen by blast radius.**
  Every named contradiction costs the requestor something — it is mildly
  uncomfortable, and the budget for that discomfort is small. Spend it on the
  ones that will otherwise be resolved wrongly by strangers: the
  band-versus-level conflict outranks a mild inconsistency about
  nice-to-haves every time.
- **When the contradiction is between the requestor and a document, dig the
  document.** "The description says degree required; you've described the
  work as entirely portfolio-judged." Documents lose these arguments quickly
  and the excavation is cheap, because nobody in the room feels personally
  contradicted.
- **When the two halves came from two different people, stop digging and
  route.** A conflict between the hiring manager and their own leadership is
  not resolvable inside your session, and pressing it puts the requestor in a
  position they cannot answer from. Name it, record both positions with their
  owners, and escalate.
- **When a contradiction touches a protected characteristic, do not dig it
  as a trade-off at all.** "We want more women applying, but they must have
  fifteen unbroken years" is not a preference conflict to be optimized; it is
  a requirement whose justification needs examining on its own terms. Ladder
  the requirement instead, and route the fairness question to the review that
  owns it.

## Why contradictions are ore rather than noise

A requestor who contradicts themselves is telling you that both statements
are true *from somewhere*: one from the work, one from the constraint; one
from what they need, one from what they have been told they can have. The
contradiction is the seam between the role and the organization, and the
brief is only defensible if it records which side won and who decided. This
is also why contradictions concentrate at the seniority line: level is the
one field that simultaneously encodes capability, budget, internal equity and
status, and those four rarely agree.

## When not to use it

- **On a slip of the tongue.** A misspoken word corrected in the next breath
  is not a contradiction. Digging it makes the requestor feel monitored and
  costs the technique its credibility for the one that matters.
- **In the first few turns.** Early answers are exploratory and are supposed
  to be inconsistent; the requestor is still finding the shape. Collect and
  wait — a contradiction is best dug after both halves are on the record and
  the requestor has warmed up.
- **When the session's remaining budget cannot hold the excavation.** A named
  contradiction with no time to dig leaves the requestor unsettled and the
  brief no better. If two turns remain, record both halves as an open
  conflict for the follow-up rather than opening it.
