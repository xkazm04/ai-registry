# Adjustments are asked at the booking step

The booking step says what the interview will involve, asks whether the
candidate needs any adjustment to take part, and gives a route for the answer
that can change the slot itself. The question is neutral and optional, it is
asked of everyone, and the answer goes to someone who does not score the
candidate.

## Why the booking step, and not earlier or later

The booking step is where the round's shape gets fixed: its length, its format,
its time, and who is in the room. Most adjustments are changes to exactly those
things. Extra time makes the slot longer. Breaks split it. A captioner or an
interpreter has to be booked into it. A different format (written instead of
live, a call instead of video) may change which interviewer can take it. A
need about *when* ("mornings only", "not straight after a shift") changes which
slots are workable at all.

Ask earlier - on the application form - and the answer is detached from the
round it concerns, often read by the wrong person, and asked before the
candidate knows what the round will involve. Ask later - after the booking -
and every answer that changes the slot's shape forces a reschedule, and a
reschedule the candidate did not cause. Ask nowhere, and the process relies on
the candidate volunteering a disability to a stranger unprompted, which selects
for the candidates most confident that they are wanted.

Disability guidance in the jurisdictions this lane has read says the same thing
from two directions. The United Kingdom's guidance puts it as a duty to ask
anyone coming to an interview. United States guidance allows an employer to
describe what the process involves and ask whether an adjustment will be needed
for it, while forbidding the wider question about the job itself before an offer.
Both allow the process-scoped question. Only one requires it, so the technique
rests on the candidate, not on the stricter regime: an ask costs a sentence,
and a need discovered at the start of the call costs the round.

## What the ask must be able to change

An adjustment request that can only produce a note on a file is decoration. The
booking flow has to be able to act on the answer:

- **The slot's length.** A request for extra time is honoured by booking a
  longer slot, not by asking the interviewer to "be flexible" inside the
  standard one.
- **The grid.** A timing need may put the workable time outside the offered
  grid. That is the same route as proposing your own times
  (propose-your-own-times-escalation), and it has to accept times the grid
  would refuse.
- **The format and the people.** An interpreter, a captioner, a different
  medium, a step-free room. These are scheduling constraints and are resolved
  before a time is confirmed, not on the day.

## Procedure

1. **Say what the round involves** in the invitation and on the booking page:
   its length, whether it is live or written, audio or video, and whether there
   is a timed exercise. A candidate cannot ask for an adjustment to a format they
   were not told about.
2. **Ask one neutral, optional question** in the same place, of every candidate:
   "Do you need any adjustments for this interview?" Never ask about health or
   a diagnosis, and never require an explanation.
3. **Offer a free-text answer and a human route** - the field on the page, and
   a reply address or number for anyone who would rather not type it. Several
   routes are part of the accommodation.
4. **Route the answer outside the assessing panel.** The person arranging the
   adjustment is not the person scoring the interview. The panel learns the
   arrangement ("this slot is 90 minutes", "a captioner will join"), never the
   reason.
5. **Hold the booking open while an adjustment is being arranged.** The
   invitation's clock stops, and the request does not spend a reschedule. The
   same rule governs an open proposal: the candidate answered, and an expiry
   that fires while a person is still arranging their answer punishes them for
   your latency.
6. **Record the adjustment as a process fact** - what was asked, what was
   granted, by whom, and when - separately from anything an assessor reads.
7. **Keep the question reachable after booking.** Needs are discovered late,
   and the manage page and the confirmation carry the same ask.

## Decision rules

- **When an adjustment changes the slot, the company changes the slot.** A
  longer or moved slot booked to honour a request is a company-side action. It
  is never charged against the candidate's reschedule budget
  (reschedule-cap-with-a-recruiter-bypass).
- **When you are unsure whether a request is an adjustment, treat it as one.**
  "I'd need a bit longer" or "mornings are hard for me" is enough. The request
  is the fact; the reason belongs to the candidate.
- **When the request cannot be met as asked, the reply names an alternative**,
  and a person makes that decision. An adjustment request is never refused by a
  rule.
- **When the request would enter a scoring surface, it does not travel.** An
  adjustment that shows up to an assessor as "needed extra time" has been
  turned into a penalty.

## When not to use this

The question is about the process, not the job. Do not widen it into a health
questionnaire, and do not ask about the candidate's capacity to do the role at
the booking step. That question has its own rules and, in some jurisdictions,
its own stage.

This technique does not own adjustments inside a timed exercise - extra time on
the clock, a paused sitting, a device advisory. Those belong to the work-sample
subject (accommodation-and-device-advisory). What it owns is the moment the
interview's shape is fixed, and the obligation to ask before it is.

## The failure it prevents

A candidate who needs an adjustment opens a booking page that says nothing
about format and asks nothing. They pick a standard slot, because that is all
there is, and either raise the need at the start of the call or do not raise it
at all. The first case costs the round and a rebooking the candidate is blamed
for. The second produces an interview that measured the missing adjustment
rather than the person. Neither leaves a trace in the record, so the process
looks accessible to the people who run it.
