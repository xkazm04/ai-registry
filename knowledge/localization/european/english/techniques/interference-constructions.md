# Interference constructions

Grammar interference from source languages that have no articles, mark roles with case, or put
the verb last. Czech and the other Slavic languages, and German, are the worked examples; the
mechanism transfers to any source that encodes definiteness, roles or focus differently from
English. These strings pass a spellchecker and often a grammar checker, and a native reader still
sees a foreign author in one line.

The evidence gives the order of yield. In a spoken corpus of Czech learners of English, articles
made up 30.8% of grammar errors, tense more than 13%, prepositions about 14% (Neumanová 2023); 87%
of preposition errors in a written Czech learner corpus involved *in*, *at* and *on* (Gráf 2015);
Czech writers substitute a demonstrative for the definite article (Young 1996); Swan & Smith's
*Learner English* (2001) and the Cambridge learner-corpus notes for German speakers catalogue the
rest. Most carry low communicative cost. A nativeness standard still flags them, and in a source
catalog each is multiplied by every locale translated from it
([the source locale is the source of truth](../../../_laws.md#the-source-locale-is-the-source-of-truth)).

## EN-ARTICLE · Give singular count nouns a determiner

> **Trigger** — a singular countable noun with no *a/an*, *the*, possessive or quantifier.
> **Rule** — *a/an* on first mention; *the* for an established or unique referent; a bare plural
> for a generic class. Source: Neumanová 2023; Swan & Smith 2001.
> **Exceptions** — telegraphic registers where English drops articles too: labels, buttons,
> headlines, table cells; uncountable nouns. A full-sentence message is not telegraphic.

✗ *We send you email when report is ready.* → ✓ *We'll email you when the report is ready.* In
one derived catalog this was the dominant source-side defect of the first review wave.

## EN-ARTICLE-DEM · "the" or "your", not "this", for a referent not in view

> **Trigger** — *this* or *these* on first mention of something not visible beside the text.
> **Rule** — use *the* or *your*: a Slavic demonstrative does the definite article's job, while
> English *this* points (Young 1996).
> **Exceptions** — true deixis, where the text sits beside its object (*This chart shows…*).

✗ *Connect this account to start syncing.* (no account on screen) → ✓ *Connect your account.*

## EN-ARTICLE-GENERIC · Drop "the" before generic plurals and abstracts

> **Trigger** — *the* before a generic plural or abstract noun: the hypercorrection of a writer
> who has over-learned articles.
> **Rule** — generic plurals and abstract nouns take no article (Swan & Smith 2001).
> **Exceptions** — a defined set (*the users on this plan*).

✗ *The security matters for the small businesses.* → ✓ *Security matters for small businesses.*

## EN-PREP · Use the English preposition, not the mapped one

> **Trigger** — *in/on/at* before a place, image, event or institution; any translated preposition.
> **Rule** — *in the screenshot*, *at university*, *at the conference*, *on the internet*, *at
> the end*. Source: Gráf 2015; Neumanová 2023.
> **Exceptions** — *on the website/page/screen* are native; *at the weekend* is British. Severity
> minor, never zero: the communicative cost is low and the foreign impression is real.

✗ *See the detail on the picture below.* → ✓ *See the detail in the screenshot below.*

## EN-DEPENDENT-PREP · Learn the preposition with the word

> **Trigger** — a verb, noun or adjective whose dependent preposition was translated.
> **Rule** — *discuss a plan* (no *about*), *take care of*, *depend on*, *consist of*,
> *interested in*, *responsible for*. Source: Swan & Smith 2001.

✗ *We will discuss about your requirements.* → ✓ *We'll discuss your requirements.*

## EN-PERFECT · Present perfect for a span that reaches now

> **Trigger** — a span begun in the past and still running, in the present simple or continuous.
> **Rule** — present perfect or perfect progressive (Neumanová 2023 on tense; Swan & Smith 2001).
> **Exceptions** — closed past spans (*We helped 40 teams in 2024*).

✗ *We help teams since 2019. We are on the market 10 years.* → ✓ *We've helped teams since 2019.
We've been in business for 10 years.*

## EN-PROGRESSIVE · Simple present for standing features and states

> **Trigger** — *is/are* + *-ing* for a permanent capability; a stative verb in the progressive.
> **Rule** — simple present.
> **Exceptions** — something genuinely temporary or under way (*We're migrating your data*).

✗ *The app is supporting 30 languages.* → ✓ *The app supports 30 languages.*

## EN-CONDITIONAL · No "will" in if- and when-clauses

> **Trigger** — *will* inside a condition or time clause about the future.
> **Rule** — present tense in the subordinate clause; *will* belongs in the main clause.
> **Exceptions** — volitional *will* (*If you'll bear with us…*).

✗ *When you will sign up, you will get a trial.* → ✓ *When you sign up, you get a trial.*

## EN-COMPLEMENT · Give allow, enable and possibility their English patterns

> **Trigger** — *allows to*, *enables to*, *possibility to*; *opportunity* for a capability.
> **Rule** — *lets you X*, *allows you to X*, *you can X*, *the option to X*. *Opportunity* means
> a chance, not a feature.

✗ *The tool allows to export reports. Possibility to cancel anytime.* → ✓ *You can export
reports. Cancel anytime.*

## EN-COUNTABLE · Keep uncountables uncountable

> **Trigger** — a plural or *a/an* on *information*, *software*, *feedback*, *advice*,
> *equipment*, *know-how*, *research*.
> **Rule** — no plural, no indefinite article: *some information*, *a piece of advice*.
> **Exceptions** — singular versus plural *data* is a genuine disagreement (technology guides
> accept *the data is*; academic style often wants *are*). Declare it.

✗ *Send us your feedbacks and informations.* → ✓ *Send us your feedback.*

## EN-ORDER · Subject, verb, object; new information last

> **Trigger** — a fronted object or adverbial carrying emphasis; new information before given.
> **Rule** — restore subject-verb-object; given first, new last; carry emphasis lexically (*only*,
> *even*, *every*) or by end-focus. Languages that mark focus by position, or put the verb last,
> do not transfer their order (Swan & Smith 2001).
> **Exceptions** — a deliberate headline inversion.

✗ *Every invoice checks the system automatically.* (object-first, meaning inverted) → ✓ *The
system checks every invoice automatically.*

## EN-ADVERB · Put adverbs where English puts them

> **Trigger** — an adverb between a verb and its object; *a quite*; a sentence opening on *Also*.
> **Rule** — frequency and focus adverbs before the main verb, after an auxiliary or *be*;
> manner, place and time after the object; *quite a*, not *a quite*.

✗ *We check regularly all backups. Also we offer training.* → ✓ *We regularly check all backups.
We also offer training.*

## EN-INDIRECT-Q · Statement order in embedded questions

> **Trigger** — an embedded question that keeps question word order.
> **Rule** — subject before verb inside the embedded clause.

✗ *Tell us how can we help.* → ✓ *Tell us how we can help.*

## EN-COMMA-THAT · No comma before restrictive or trailing subordinate clauses

> **Trigger** — a comma before *that*, a restrictive *which*, or *because*, *if*, *when* after a
> main clause.
> **Rule** — remove it. Czech and German require a comma before every subordinate clause; English
> punctuates by meaning.
> **Exceptions** — non-restrictive *which* clauses; a fronted *if*- or *when*-clause.

✗ *Pick the plan, that fits your team. Contact us, if you need help.* → ✓ *Pick the plan that
fits your team. Contact us if you need help.*

## EN-BY-UNTIL · "by" for a deadline, "until" for a duration

> **Trigger** — *until* with a deadline, or *by* with a lasting state.
> **Rule** — *by* means no later than; *until* means continuously up to that point.

✗ *Submit the form until Friday.* → ✓ *Submit the form by Friday.* (*Access lasts until Friday.*)

## EN-REFLEXIVE · Remove calqued reflexives

> **Trigger** — *itself*, *yourself*, *himself* after a verb calqued from a source reflexive.
> **Rule** — English carries the middle voice with a plain verb, a passive or an actor. Translated
> English carries measurably more reflexive pronouns (Volansky, Ordan & Wintner 2015).
> **Exceptions** — English reflexive verbs (*enjoy yourself*); emphasis (*we built it ourselves*).

✗ *The layout adapts itself to your screen. Register yourself in two minutes.* → ✓ *The layout
adapts to your screen. Sign up in two minutes.*

## Using the set

Walk the triggers after drafting, in order of yield: EN-ARTICLE, EN-PREP, EN-PERFECT,
EN-COMMA-THAT, EN-ORDER. Cite the ID with a minimal rewrite
([every finding cites an anchor](../../../_laws.md#every-finding-cites-an-anchor)); a native
rejection no ID explains is a new rule, not a one-off fix. A defect filed under the nearest
favourite rule hides its cause, so file each fix under the rule that names it. A sentence shape
recurring across many keys (event feeds, notification templates) is fixed as one coordinated
rewrite, after checking how the code joins names and fragments into it.

When NOT to apply: telegraphic UI (labels, buttons, cells) for the article rules; quoted speech and
user-generated content, which are evidence rather than product voice; names and code. Weight
severity by surface: a missing article in a settings tooltip is minor, in a pricing headline it is
not.
