# Remediation roadmaps

An assessment ends with a verdict. A roadmap is the answer to the only
question the reader actually has next: **given this verdict, what should I do,
in what order, and what will it buy me?** The roadmap is therefore not a
summary of the assessment in a different shape — it is a *derivation*, with
its own inputs (the scored dimensions, their weights, their headroom, the
estimated cost of each available move), its own arithmetic, and its own
honesty obligations. Most assessment products stop one step short of it,
shipping a scorecard and trusting the reader to translate. They almost never
do. A score with no derived ordering is read once, felt, and closed; a
ranked set of moves is worked.

The naive derivation is to sort the dimensions by how bad they are and call
that a plan. It is the single most common defect in the subject, and it is
wrong for a structural reason: **severity ranks problems, and a roadmap ranks
opportunities**, and the two orderings diverge exactly where the decision is
hard. The worst dimension is frequently the one with the least available
upside — it is weak because it is expensive, structural, or gated on
something outside the reader's control, and putting it at the top of a list
of next moves recommends the immovable object first. Meanwhile a middling
dimension carrying heavy weight, with a well-understood cheap lever, sits at
row seven and never gets read. The roadmap's whole value is that it computes
the ordering the reader cannot compute in their head.

## Where this subject begins and ends

The score comes from elsewhere. [Scoring rubrics](../scoring-rubrics/scoring-rubrics.md)
own which dimensions participate, what each weighs, how raw signals become
comparable, and — in [gap-ranking](../scoring-rubrics/techniques/gap-ranking.md) —
the ordering of *gaps within one rubric run*. This subject assumes that
machinery and builds the reader-facing artifact on top of it: a catalog of
concrete moves (not dimensions), each attached to the gap it closes, each
carrying a projected gain, an effort estimate, and a wording that a person can
read about their own work without flinching.

Downstream, the boundary is equally sharp. Once the reader has accepted items,
turning those items into work an external executor can pick up — the payload,
the target system's conventions, the acknowledgement that the work was
received — belongs to the sibling [`remediation-handoff`](../remediation-handoff/remediation-handoff.md) subject. **This
subject stops at the moment of acceptance.** It owns deriving, ordering,
projecting, wording, and letting the reader commit a chosen set; it does not
own what the receiving system does with them.

Sideways, [triage queues](../triage-queues/triage-queues.md) own inbound work
items and their queue lifecycle — items arriving from N producers, ordered by
policy, verdicted, drained toward empty. A roadmap is not an inbox: its items
are *generated by one derivation from one assessment*, they are proposals
rather than obligations, and the healthy end state is not empty but *chosen*.
Once accepted items become tracked work that ages, the queue discipline
governs. Finally,
[measurement honesty](../measurement-honesty/measurement-honesty.md) owns what
any computed number may claim; every roadmap item carries a projection, and
those rules apply here without exception.

## Upside, not severity: what the ordering actually computes

A roadmap item's priority is the **upside actually available**, which
decomposes into three factors that must all be present:

- **Weight** — how much the affected dimension moves the composite. A gap on
  a dimension worth a fifth of the score is worth five times the same gap on
  a dimension worth a twenty-fifth, and no amount of felt severity changes
  that arithmetic.
- **Headroom** — the distance between the current value and the realistic
  ceiling for this move, not the theoretical maximum. A dimension at 90 with
  a hard ceiling of 92 offers two points however heavy it is.
- **Achievability** — the probability the move actually lands, discounted for
  effort, prerequisite structure, and dependence on parties the reader does
  not control. An item that requires a decision from someone else is worth
  less than its arithmetic gain, and the ordering should say so.

The product of those three, not any one of them, is the ranking key
([weighted-upside-ranking](techniques/weighted-upside-ranking.md)). Two
overrides sit above it in declared order — a floor violation that blocks
everything downstream, and prerequisite structure that makes a sequence
*buildable* rather than merely attractive — and both are declared policy, not
ad-hoc exceptions.

One constraint binds the ranking to the rest of the artifact and is easy to
violate in a pipeline with several intermediate scores: **rank against the
number the reader is shown.** Assessments commonly compute a raw signal
value, then adjust it — a judgment layer, a blend, a guardband — before
display. Ranking on the pre-adjustment value while the page shows the
adjusted one produces the worst kind of incoherence: a top recommendation
whose stated reason cites a number that appears nowhere near it, sometimes
for a dimension the adjustment already lifted out of trouble. One number
serves both the display and the ordering, or the roadmap contradicts the
report it is attached to.

## The reader's real question is a threshold, not a slope

Ranking by upside answers "what is the best single move". It does not answer
the question readers actually ask, which is almost always banded: *what is the
least I can do to stop being in this band?* Bands are how people hold a score
— they remember "we were amber", never "we were 63.4" — so the roadmap owes a
second, differently-shaped output: the **cheapest set of moves whose combined
projected gain crosses the next threshold**, with its total cost stated
([cheapest-path-projection](techniques/cheapest-path-projection.md)). This is
a small covering problem, not a sort, and it routinely selects a different set
than the top of the ranked list — three cheap moves can cross a boundary that
the single highest-upside move cannot reach alone. Shipping only the sort and
letting the reader mentally sum projected gains down the page is both a worse
answer and an invitation to the arithmetic error the next section forbids.

## Silence is a claim, so coverage is guaranteed

The hardest-won rule in the subject, and the one that most often has to be
imposed against the grain of a ranking: **every dimension below the healthy
band gets at least one item naming it, whether or not that item ranks well.**
The argument is about how absence is read. A reader who opens the roadmap
looking for their weakest area and finds nothing about it does not conclude
"there was no good move available" — they conclude *it must be fine*, and the
roadmap has silently retracted the assessment's own finding. This is a
higher-order form of the same law that forbids a scanner reporting failure as
empty success; here the empty result is a *section*, and the reader's
inference is the damage.

The guarantee is structural, not editorial: after ranking and truncation, a
pass re-examines every below-threshold dimension, and any that is unnamed gets
a follow-up item appended — explicitly marked as coverage rather than as
top-ranked opportunity, so the reader can tell the difference between "this is
your best next move" and "this is weak and we owe you a mention"
([coverage-guarantee](techniques/coverage-guarantee.md)). Truncating the list
for readability is legitimate; truncating it in a way that erases a weak area
is not.

## A projected gain is a model output wearing a number's clothes

Every item claims something like "+6". That number is the most-read and
least-scrutinized figure in the whole artifact, and it is not a measurement —
it is the output of an impact model whose inputs are the dimension's weight,
the assumed post-move value, and a coefficient someone chose
([projected-gain-modelling](techniques/projected-gain-modelling.md)). Four
rules keep it honest:

- **Projections are labelled as projections** — in the type, the field name,
  and the rendering — never sharing a field or a visual register with a
  measured score.
- **Gains are not additive.** Overlapping moves, dimension ceilings and
  non-linear normalization mean a naive sum of the top five routinely exceeds
  the rubric's entire remaining headroom. Any displayed total is computed by
  re-running the score with the moves applied.
- **The model is conservative by construction.** Over-claiming is discovered
  at exactly the moment the reader has done the work and earned less than
  they were told — which costs the assessment its credibility for every
  future run. Under-claiming costs nothing.
- **The projection is never re-reported as an achievement.** Claimed gain and
  realized gain are different fields with different provenance; conflating
  them is how a product reports improvements nobody made.

## Wording is not decoration — it decides whether any of this is used

An assessment tells a person their work is deficient. The roadmap is where
that judgment turns into instructions, and the grammar of an instruction
determines whether the reader engages or defends. The rule is that an item's
title names the gap as an **observation**, never as an imperative: describe
what is missing or thin, and let the body offer the move. "There is no
recorded owner for the release step" invites; "Add a release owner" commands,
and a command about someone's own work, issued by a tool, is answered with
justification rather than action. The stance is a companion to a transition,
not a supervisor of one — the reader keeps the authority to decline, and the
artifact's shape must make declining a first-class, unpunished outcome
([invitational-framing](techniques/invitational-framing.md)). This is not
softness. An item nobody acts on because they resented its tone has a
realized gain of zero, whatever its projection said.

## A plan is not real until the reader has shaped it and committed it

A generated roadmap is a proposal, and proposals are wrong in ways only the
reader knows: the item that is already in flight, the one blocked on a
contract, the ordering that ignores next month's release. So the interaction
model is two-phase. The reader gets a **sandbox**: reorder, deselect, adjust,
and see the projected outcome recompute live — with nothing yet leaving the
artifact. Then an explicit, single commit turns the chosen set into tracked
items, with identity minted at commit time so the same recommendation
committed twice does not become two work items
([sandbox-to-tracker-commit](techniques/sandbox-to-tracker-commit.md)).
Exploration and commitment have opposite safety requirements — free and
reversible versus deliberate and idempotent — so a surface that writes on
every interaction serves neither. Skip the sandbox and you get plans nobody
believes; skip the explicit commit and you get trackers full of items nobody
chose.

## What good looks like, compressed

- The top item is the best available *opportunity*, and the reader can see
  why — weight, headroom, and effort are all visible on the row.
- A second, differently-computed answer states the cheapest crossing to the
  next band, with its total cost.
- No dimension below the healthy band is absent from the artifact, and
  coverage items are visibly distinct from ranked opportunities.
- Every claimed gain is typed and rendered as a projection; any total is
  recomputed, never summed.
- Every title reads as an observation about the work, never as an order to
  the reader, and declining is a supported outcome rather than an ignored one.
- Reordering is free and local; committing is explicit, once, and idempotent.

## The techniques

- [weighted-upside-ranking](techniques/weighted-upside-ranking.md) — priority
  as weight times headroom times achievability; why severity ordering
  recommends the immovable; declared overrides and stable tie-breaks.
- [cheapest-path-projection](techniques/cheapest-path-projection.md) — the
  minimal set of moves that crosses the next band, as a covering problem with
  a stated total cost.
- [coverage-guarantee](techniques/coverage-guarantee.md) — the post-ranking
  pass that ensures no weak area is silently unmentioned, and how coverage
  items are marked so they do not masquerade as top opportunities.
- [projected-gain-modelling](techniques/projected-gain-modelling.md) — the
  impact model behind a claimed number; conservatism, non-additivity, and the
  strict separation of projected from realized.
- [invitational-framing](techniques/invitational-framing.md) — observation
  over imperative; the decline path as a first-class outcome; the wording
  rules stated as checkable constraints.
- [sandbox-to-tracker-commit](techniques/sandbox-to-tracker-commit.md) — free
  local reshaping with live recomputation, then one deliberate idempotent
  commit that mints identity.
