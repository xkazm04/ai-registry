# Generous strawman rendering

Factual narrative earns its turns by first building the position it will
reverse — the obvious reading, stated plainly and generously, with
evidence, so that the reversal overturns something the viewer had actually
accepted. This technique is that discipline applied to the screen: the
frame for the to-be-reversed beat renders the case at **full evidential
strength**, while withholding the **conclusion-grade marks** that would
make the frame assert, as the piece's own verdict, a position the piece
goes on to refute.

## The two failures it sits between

**The cardboard strawman.** Drawing the position weakly — a deliberately
flimsy chart, a visibly skeptical composition, irony in the framing —
protects the piece's epistemics and kills its narrative. A reversal of a
position nobody believed refutes nothing; the viewer feels argued at rather
than thought with. Worse, it is its own dishonesty: the obvious reading is
usually obvious *because* real evidence supports it, and drawing that
evidence feebly misstates the evidence. The strength of a turn is exactly
the strength of what it turns over.

**The premature verdict.** Drawing the position with the full authority
kit — the settling arrow, the checkmark, the conclusive composition — makes
the frame assert the position as established. Frames travel alone:
thumbnailed, screenshotted, paused on, clipped. A frame that reads, out of
context, as "this piece concludes X" when the piece concludes not-X has
outrun its evidence in the most public way available, and no narration can
chase the screenshot down.

## The discipline: full evidence, held verdict

Render everything the evidence layer grants and nothing the conclusion
layer would add:

- **Real figures, really cited.** The case-for beat draws its actual
  numbers, bound to fact records like any other —
  [figure-must-cite-a-fact](figure-must-cite-a-fact.md) does not relax for
  positions you privately disagree with. The facts in a good strawman are
  true; it is the *reading* that the reversal revises.
- **Precision limits still bind.** Building the case generously never
  means drawing it more precisely than its facts support; a laundered
  chart in service of a setup is still a laundered chart, and it hands the
  reversal a corrupted foundation.
- **No settling marks.** Withhold the grammar of conclusion: no causal
  arrow completing the position's mechanism (the arrow is precisely what
  the reversal will contest — see
  [causal-arrow-discipline](causal-arrow-discipline.md)), no verdict
  iconography, no compositional finality. The frame presents evidence
  arranged toward a reading; it does not stamp the reading.
- **Compose for revision, not contradiction.** The strongest reversals
  *revise the same picture*: the setup's chart returns with the
  annotation that changes its meaning; the stacked evidence remains on
  screen while the frame around it turns. Direct the setup frame with its
  reversal in view — leave the space, the axis, the persistent visual
  thread that the turn will re-enter. A reversal that must throw away the
  setup's image and start a new one merely contradicts; one that
  re-annotates it demonstrates.

## Procedure

1. **Identify the reversal pairs at direction time.** The script's
   structure marks which beats state a reading a later beat overturns.
   Every such beat gets this treatment; the pairing (setup frame ↔ turn
   frame) is recorded so the two are directed together.
2. **Grade the setup's marks.** Evidence marks (figures, bars, series):
   full strength. Relational and conclusive marks (arrows, verdicts,
   settling motion): held back, or drawn as the *question* — the gap the
   turn will fill.
3. **Design the turn as an edit of the setup.** Decide what the turn
   frame adds, removes, or re-annotates in the setup frame — ideally an
   annotation or a subtraction rather than a replacement.
4. **Audit each setup frame in isolation.** The test: shown this frame
   alone, does a viewer read "here is evidence and a reading" or "here is
   the conclusion"? The first is honest setup; the second needs its
   verdict marks stripped.

## Decision rules

- When the position being set up is *someone's actual claim* (a company's
  statement, an advocate's argument), attribute it on screen — a kicker or
  label marking whose reading this is. Attribution converts the frame's
  assertion from "this is so" to "this is claimed", which is the exact
  truth, and it survives screenshotting.
- When the piece's structure includes attacking its own reversal (the
  steelman move — "but in practice, does the objection matter?"), apply
  the same discipline recursively: the objection is also rendered
  generously, also without verdict marks, until the synthesis beat, which
  is the first frame entitled to conclusive grammar.
- When the setup's evidence is weak and the position survives only as a
  vibe, the problem is upstream in the script: a reversal needs something
  real to reverse, and no rendering choice manufactures it.

## When not to use it

Positions the piece endorses need no held verdict — the thesis beat is
entitled to the full authority kit, and hedging it out of habit produces a
piece that never lands anywhere. Likewise pure contrast structures (two
options weighed, neither refuted) are comparisons, not strawmen; render
both sides evenly and let the comparison grammar carry it. This technique
is specifically for the beat whose job is to be believed *and then*
overturned.
