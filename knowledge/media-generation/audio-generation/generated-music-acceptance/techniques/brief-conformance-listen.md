# Brief-conformance listen

The first listen to a delivered track is an acceptance event, and it is the
easiest gate in the pipeline to corrupt, because the question it should
answer — *is this the piece that was briefed?* — degrades under an
untrained ear into *do I like it?* Those questions disagree often: a
likeable track that ignored the brief is a miss that will cost its price
again downstream, and a track that served the brief exactly can be
unlikeable in ways the brief caused, which is a briefing lesson, not a
generation failure. The listen is structured so the brief keeps custody of
the question.

## The checklist is derived from the brief, mechanically

Before playing anything, turn the brief into check items — one per
commitment the brief actually made. A plan-based brief yields them almost
line by line:

- every briefed section present, in order, doing its stated job;
- instrumentation: named inclusions audible, named exclusions absent (the
  exclude list is checked item by item — absence does not announce itself);
- vocal or instrumental as briefed; if vocal, the lyrics are the briefed
  lyrics, and the load-bearing words are intelligible on first listen;
- mood and energy per section, in the brief's own words;
- the ending has the briefed shape — a hard out is a hard out, not a fast
  fade.

Each item gets a verdict from the three-state vocabulary: **pass**,
**fail**, or **unmeasured** — the item the listen could not resolve (a
phone-speaker translation not yet run, a lyric inaudible under the mix) is
recorded as unmeasured, never rounded to pass. The verdict sheet, not the
listener's overall impression, is the acceptance artifact.

## Listening conditions are part of the method

- **First listen at delivery volume, in the delivery context.** A trailer
  cue is auditioned against picture at the level the mix will run; a
  standalone piece at normal listening level. The studio solo listen at
  generous volume flatters everything.
- **One translation pass on deliberately bad speakers** — a phone, a
  laptop. Generated mixes lean on low end and stereo width that small
  speakers discard; what survives translation is what the audience gets.
- **Limit repeats before the verdict.** The ear normalizes defects with
  exposure; by the fifth listen a smeared transient is "the sound of the
  track". Verdict on the sheet within two listens per item, then stop.

## The fail path is a routing decision

A failed item routes by what failed: a style or structure miss goes back to
the brief (and the fix is a section edit, not a re-roll, when the plan
allows addressing it); an exclusion violation extends the exclude list; a
technical artifact hands off to the defect taxonomy, which owns remedies.
The sheet should say where each fail went — an acceptance record whose
fails vanish without routing is how the same miss gets paid for twice, and
the per-usable-output economics depend on knowing how many listens and
re-briefs a usable track actually cost.

## When not to use this

Exploration candidates carry no acceptance obligation — slating five
directions to find an identity is selection, not acceptance, and running
the full sheet on disposable candidates is process theater. The sheet
starts when a take could become the piece. And properties the brief
committed to numbers — durations, offsets, loudness — are not listened
for at all; they are measured, by the neighbouring techniques, because an
ear is the wrong instrument for a number.
