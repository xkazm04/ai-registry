# React: the picture is downstream of a plan, so the plan is the timing brief

`stat-reel` (branch `main`, `origin/main` at `4ef0e99`, 2026-10-22) renders
30-90 s data-viz videos as Remotion compositions. The witness for the stack is
`packages/compositions/package.json`, which pins `react` `^19.1.0` and
`remotion` `4.0.530`. The format decision (ADR 007) makes audio "a music bed
(plus optional SFX)", and the design names SFX "for transitions/whooshes".
No SFX is placed yet. `AudioTracks.tsx` places voice and music cues only, and
the live `DataRace` composition carries no audio track at all. This makes the
tree a good test of which route the first hard-synced effect should take.

## The seam, chosen to falsify

The technique says hard sync is solved upstream by conditioning generation on
the picture, with no verbal timing and no manual spotting pass. A caught
outcome would show a tree where the picture is *not* the best timing
authority, and that is a condition the technique's scope marks do not name.
Here the picture is computed: `computeTimingPlan` runs before rendering, and
the committed fixture `sample-data-race.TIMING.md` says so in its first line,
"Generated by `computeTimingPlan` ... **before** rendering". Each event card
enters at its plan `start_frame` (`DataRace/state.ts`, `enterT = frame -
e.start_frame`). Cuts sit at the plan's `sections` boundaries, and a leader
change sits on a period knot (`DataRace/leader.ts`).

## What A and B were

- **A (the technique):** render the picture, then generate audio
  conditioned on it in fixed windows of a few seconds, one mixed track per
  window.
- **B (plan-spotted):** place one cue per plan event at its frame, each
  generated from a text brief of the event's character, one stem per event.

Neither arm was rendered, and no generation call was made. The experiment is
a count over the committed fixture
(`packages/compositions/fixtures/sample-data-race.timing-plan.json`), run by a
scratch script that read the plan and changed no product code.

## What was read

- **Target:** sync points that end up sharing a generated track with another
  event, and so cannot be levelled or muted alone.
- **Floor:** sync error at each event. In B this is 0 frames by construction,
  because the cue's `from` is the plan frame. A can only re-derive that frame.
- The sample cut is 1,230 frames (41.0 s at 30 fps). It has 11 sync points:
  6 card entrances, 4 section cuts and 1 leader change. The smallest gap
  between them is 1.07 s.
- With 8 s windows, A needs 6 clips holding [2,1,3,2,3,0] events, and
  **10 of 11** sync points share a clip. With 10 s windows, A needs 5 clips
  and all 11 share one. B has 11 stems and none is shared.

Verdict `not-better` for the technique *on this seam*. A computed picture
gives the conditioner nothing that the plan does not already state exactly,
and it costs stems, a render-before-audio dependency and approximate sync.
The technique gained a boundary section from this row: when the picture is
rendered from a plan, the plan is the brief.

## What the tree adds that the technique did not say

A card's entrance spans `CARD.enterFrames` = 14 frames (ease-out). Its
visibility ramps over the first 6 frames. So "the event's frame" is two
frames: an onset at `start_frame` and a settle about 14 frames later. A
whoosh that carries the card wants the onset, and a hit that lands it wants
the settle. A plan-spotted cue has to name its anchor. A picture-conditioned
model would have chosen one silently.

## What this realization cannot do

- It says nothing about character. Whether a text-briefed whoosh suits
  abstract bars sliding is a listening question. Conditioning on data-viz
  motion has no physical sound source to learn from, so that half is
  reasoning, not measurement.
- One fixture is n=1. A sparse cut with a single event per window would
  close most of the stem gap. Only the sync and render-order costs remain
  for every plan.

Return: when the first SFX cue is wired into a composition, check that its
`from` is read from the plan's event frame and that its anchor (onset or
settle) is declared.
