# Reference track anchoring

A reference track is the highest-bandwidth style directive available — one
file carries palette, energy curve, production character, and tempo feel
that would take a paragraph to approximate in words. It is also the
hazard-densest line in the brief, because a reference carries **more than
style**, and everything else it carries is a liability: its melody, its
identity, and its rights.

## What a reference may carry, and what it may not

- **May carry:** palette (what instruments, what space), energy (where it
  sits and how it moves), production character (how finished, how modern),
  motion (the rhythmic posture). These are style — the legitimate cargo.
- **May not carry: melody.** A brief whose real intent is "this tune,
  slightly different" is asking for a derivative work with deniability, and
  a generator anchored hard on one track will sometimes deliver exactly
  that. The output's resemblance is the evidence that matters — a
  generation pipeline does not launder a melody, and "the model did it" is
  not a provenance the rights record can stand on.
- **May not carry: a commercial track's rights.** Referencing commercial
  material pulls its exposure into the output and, under most generation
  terms, off the edge of what the vendor's commercial grant covers. The
  rights consequence is recorded by the acceptance craft; the brief-side
  rule is simpler — do not anchor on material the production could not
  afford to have audibly influenced the output.

A route that transcribes the reference into notation and regenerates from
that score hands the generator no audio, changes the style and the lyrics, and
still carries the melody, because the melody is what the notation holds. It
is the plainest case of a pipeline that does not launder a tune. On the one
model measured, with one of our own songs as the reference and objective note
distance only, the result sat at least as near the reference's notes as a
re-render of its own score at a new seed. The model's authors report the same
direction over 948 works: retrieval found the source work about seven times in
ten with the score and almost never without it. Neither figure says how it
sounds or what it is in law. Using this route on someone else's work is the
licensing decision below, not a style brief.

## The temp-track trap, in its generated form

Productions that cut picture against a temporary track fall in love with
it — the oldest failure in scoring, and generation gives it a new edge:
the temp is now one anchor line away from being quietly imitated. The
symptom is a brief that keeps tightening toward the temp ("closer...
closer...") until the output is a soundalike nobody decided to commission.
The discipline: when a temp exists, translate it into layered vocabulary
once — axes, not essence — then retire it from the brief. If the room
cannot accept any cue that is not the temp, the production has a licensing
decision to make, not a prompting problem.

## The disciplined use: your own accepted work

The reference that carries no rights exposure and no soundalike trap is the
production's **own prior accepted cue**. Anchoring cue 4 on approved cue 1
is how a multi-cue production holds one musical identity without
re-deriving it — the audio form of the approved-reference discipline the
visual craft runs, and the same restatement law governs it: the reference
rides *with* the restated style block in every brief, it does not replace
it. A reference without the block reproduces the identity; the block
without a reference describes it; together they hold it.

## Decision rules

- When a production will have more than two cues, promote the first
  accepted cue to the standing reference and anchor the rest on it, because
  identity drift across cues is otherwise the default outcome.
- When the only available reference is commercial, spend one pass
  translating it to vocabulary and retire it, because every additional
  exposure to the anchor tightens the imitation.
- When a route transcribes a reference into notation, treat the output as a
  cover of that work whatever its new style and lyrics, because the melody is
  the part the route keeps (one song measured locally, plus the authors' own
  948-work benchmark).
- When a delivered cue audibly resembles a specific existing work, treat it
  as a defect regardless of how it happened, because resemblance is judged
  on the output, not on the intent.

## When not to use this

First explorations, where identity is the open question — anchoring narrows
the space exactly when it should be wide. And never anchor to solve a
structure problem: a reference cannot carry "38 seconds with the hit at 24";
that is the plan's job, and a reference dragged in for timing reasons
imports every hazard above for a property it cannot even deliver.
