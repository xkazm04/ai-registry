# Narrative engine selection

A narrative engine is the structural machine that makes a viewer want the next
beat. It is the first decision of any factual script — before the hook, before
the outline, before a single sentence — because every later decision inherits
its shape. Two scripts on the same subject with different engines are different
videos, not different drafts: one corrects the viewer step by step, the other
walks them through a contested question toward a verdict, and the facts they
share are arranged into different causal chains serving different pleasures.

The naive reading treats structure as a template to fill: pick "listicle" or
"explainer," pour the research in, publish. That reading produces the single
most common defect in factual video — the wiki timeline, a sequence of true
statements connected only by "and then," which a viewer abandons because
nothing in it obligates the next beat. The principal reading is that structure
is a *machine with a contract*: each engine takes material of a particular
shape, converts it into a specific viewer pleasure, and fails in a specific,
nameable way when fed material of the wrong shape. Selection is therefore not
taste. It is matching the shape of the material to the contract of the machine
— and pricing what a wrong match costs before rendering, because some wrong
matches cost a boring video and others cost a defamation claim.

## Engines are named by the viewer's pleasure

Catalogue engines by what the viewer *gets*, not by what the script *does*.
"Being corrected," "watching a question get settled," "one idea compounding" —
these distinguish engines even when two of them could run on the same subject,
because the pleasure is what the viewer stays for and what the structure must
keep delivering. A catalogue keyed by surface mechanics ("has a twist," "uses
an analogy") collapses distinct machines into one bucket and hides the fact
that the same facts render as completely different videos under different
engines. A working catalogue is small — on the order of seven entries — open
to new witnesses, and honest about how thin the evidence behind each entry is.
An engine witnessed once is a hypothesis with a good example, not a law.

Engines are also independent of duration. A given engine is the same machine
at forty seconds and at eighteen minutes; what changes with length is the
budget — how many reversals, rungs, or candidate theories fit — not the
contract. This matters practically: a catalogue that files structures under
"short-form formats" and "long-form formats" duplicates every machine and
loses the compression rules that actually vary with length.

And engines compose. A long argument may run one engine as its spine and
another as its final act. Treat the catalogue as a vocabulary, not a menu of
mutually exclusive options — but compose deliberately, as spine plus act, not
by smearing three engines across one script until none of their contracts is
honored.

## Fit and hazard are orthogonal, and both get asked

The selection question everyone asks is **fit**: does the shape this engine
needs exist in the material? A dispute a reasonable person could take the
other side of feeds a reversal chain; a mechanism a viewer could operate feeds
an effort/payoff demonstration; naturally ordered difficulty feeds a ladder.
Fit is a property of the *material*, assessable from the research asset alone.

The question almost nobody asks is **hazard**: what does a wrong render of
this engine on this subject cost? Hazard is a property of the *render on this
subject* — what the finished video asserts about the world, and about whom,
even when every sentence in it is true. A verdict structure applied to "whose
fault was it" convicts named parties by shape alone. A step-by-step
demonstration applied to an attack chain is a tutorial. An escalating ladder
whose rungs are cohorts of victims asks the viewer to enjoy each rung being
worse. An analogy engine's availability *rises* as evidence thins, because it
needs a rule from another domain rather than facts about this one — so it
produces its most confident output exactly where confidence is least earned.

These two axes must never be averaged into one score. The most dangerous cell
is high fit, high hazard: the engine snaps on cleanly, the script reads well,
and the damage lives in what the shape implies rather than in any sentence a
reviewer could flag. A hazard note is not a fit downgrade and not a veto — it
is a second, orthogonal finding that the arbitration step consumes first.

## When several engines fit, arbitrate; when none fits, stop

Rich, well-researched material legitimately fits many engines — breadth
measures tellability, not shapelessness, and a heuristic that reads "six
engines fit" as a smell fires hardest on the best research. The needed tool is
an arbitration order, applied in sequence: eliminate on hazard first (a strong
fit you would not defend on air loses to a medium fit you would); then match
the engine's pleasure to the material's own surprise; then prefer the engine
the research asset can actually feed, field by field — a recommendation the
asset cannot feed is a recommendation to do original work at render time, and
it will not survive a second render; then compose what remains. If arbitration
still cannot separate two finalists, record the pick as an editorial choice
rather than dressing a preference as a fit measurement.

Zero engines fitting is the opposite case and it is a blocker, not a prompt to
force the nearest match. Material with no engine has no tension yet — it is a
topic, not a video — and rendering it anyway produces the and-then timeline
with production values. The honest output of selection is sometimes "not a
video yet; go find the tension," and a pipeline that cannot emit that verdict
will convert every thin topic into a confident, boring artifact.

## The engine easiest to fake gets its own audit

The verdict-settling engine — enumerate candidate explanations, weigh each,
deliver a verdict — is the easiest to counterfeit, because the *appearance* of
weighing persuades whether or not any weighing occurred. Its honesty is
checkable structurally, without knowing the subject: is the premise itself
allowed to be a candidate, or does every theory presuppose it? Could any
candidate actually beat the author's prior, or are the "theories" three
framings of one conclusion? Is the strongest opposing case admitted in its own
strongest form, or pre-excluded by construction? A skeleton can be clean while
the execution is polemic wearing the costume of analysis — so take structure
and honesty standards from different exemplars when necessary, and never
encode a persuasion technique as an explanation technique because it arrived
inside a well-shaped video.

## Selection is verifiable after the fact

An engine choice is a testable claim about the script it produces, and cheap
mechanical diagnostics catch a broken or faked selection without judging the
subject. Causal-opener density — the share of sentences opening with but, so,
because, therefore — measures how much a script *derives* rather than asserts;
it is a property of the writer and the engine, not of length, and a verdict
engine running at the bottom of the observed range is announcing theories
rather than weighing them. Turn count per minute measures whether the viewer
is ever allowed to settle. And the and-then count must be zero: a single beat
joined to the next by pure sequence is the wiki timeline surfacing inside
whatever engine was claimed. A selection pipeline that never re-measures its
own output will drift back to lists, because lists are what unstructured
material becomes by default.

## Failure modes this standard exists to prevent

- **The wiki timeline** — no engine selected, or a chronology mistaken for a
  difficulty ladder because dates supply order for free; beats connected by
  "and then"; true, complete, and unwatchable.
- **The single-scalar trap** — hazard averaged into fit, so a structurally
  excellent choice ships because its one number was high, and the cost
  surfaces after publication.
- **The rigged verdict** — a conclusion fixed before the candidates were
  weighed, wearing the adjudication shape as decoration.
- **Confidence from thin air** — the analogy engine scoring a clean fit on a
  days-old topic precisely because it needs no facts about the subject.
- **The unfeedable pick** — an engine chosen on taste whose load-bearing half
  the research asset cannot store, so every re-render silently invents it.
- **Forcing the nearest engine** — a topic with no tension rendered anyway,
  because the pipeline had no vocabulary for "not a video yet."
