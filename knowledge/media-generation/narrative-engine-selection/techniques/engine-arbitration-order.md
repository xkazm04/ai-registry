# Engine arbitration order

Rich material fits many engines, and that is not a defect. Counting fits
measures how many ways material can be told — tellability — not how sharp it
is; process-shaped, argument-shaped, and simply well-researched topics
legitimately render six or seven ways, and a heuristic that reads breadth as
shapelessness fires hardest on the best research. What a many-fit result
needs is not a warning but a way to choose. This technique is that way: four
cuts, applied strictly in order, because each cut assumes the previous one
already ran.

## 1. Cut on hazard first

Drop every fit whose hazard line you would not defend on air. Hazard is the
first cut, not a tiebreak — a strong fit carrying "renders a finding of
culpability against a living person" loses to a medium fit that carries
nothing. Running hazard last, as a tiebreak among finalists, means the
dangerous engine survives every round it should have lost and gets eliminated
only if the field happens to stay crowded; running it first means no later
step ever weighs craft against harm. This ordering is the entire practical
payoff of keeping fit and hazard as separate axes: the axes make the cut
expressible, the order makes it binding.

## 2. Match pleasure to the material's own surprise

Among survivors, keep the engines whose pleasure coincides with what is
actually surprising in the material. If the interesting thing is that the
received view is wrong, that is the correction engine. If it is that a
question is genuinely contested, the verdict engine. If a familiar rule
reappears somewhere unexpected, the pattern-transfer engine. This step is
where "fits" narrows to "belongs": several engines can be *fed* by the
material, but the material usually has one native surprise, and the engine
that serves it needs no manufactured tension. An engine imposed against the
native surprise must generate its pleasure synthetically — manufactured
novelty on a settled subject, manufactured dispute where there is none — and
the audience reads the manufacture.

## 3. Prefer the engine the research asset can actually feed

Fit was assessed from the material — now check it against the *fields*. Some
engines have load-bearing halves that a research asset may not be able to
store: a verdict engine without stored candidates-and-weighings, a
pattern-transfer engine whose mechanised familiar-domain half has no home in
the schema. Recommending such an engine is recommending original work at
render time: the first script invents the missing half, the invention is
recorded nowhere, and the second render of the same asset invents it
differently. Under
[output-never-outruns-evidence](../../_laws.md#output-never-outruns-evidence)
this is disqualifying, not inconvenient — material that exists only in the
renderer's head is not evidence, and a structure resting on it outruns what
the asset can back. When the un-feedable engine is genuinely the right one,
the correct move is to extend the asset schema first, not to render first and
hope the invention gets re-invented consistently.

## 4. Compose the remainder

If two engines survive all three cuts, they are usually not competitors but a
**spine and a final act** — a correction chain that closes with a short
adjudication, a briefing whose last beat opens a paradox. Compose them in
those roles rather than picking one and discarding real structure the
material supports. Composition is deliberate: each engine's contract holds
within its span, and the joint between them is itself a "therefore," not a
gear change.

## The honest residue

If arbitration cannot separate the finalists, say so. The truthful note is
that the material is tellable several ways and the pick is an **editorial
choice** — record it as one, in the selection rationale, rather than dressing
a preference as a fit measurement. A false measurement corrupts the next
reader's trust in every real one; an owned editorial call costs nothing.

## When not to use

With zero or one fitting engine there is nothing to arbitrate — zero is the
blocker verdict (see no-engine-means-no-video), one is the answer. And do not
re-run arbitration to relitigate a recorded editorial choice with unchanged
material: re-arbitrate only when the asset gains fields (step 3 can change)
or the subject's hazard profile shifts (step 1 can change).
