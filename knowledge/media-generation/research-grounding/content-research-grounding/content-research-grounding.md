# Content research grounding

Research and writing are two different jobs, and the single most consequential
decision in factual media production is to refuse to let one model do both in
one pass. A model handed a topic and asked for a script produces a wiki
timeline: accurate facts, reasonable order, connected by "and then",
unwatchable — and unfalsifiable, because the claims and their support were
never separated enough to be checked. The principal practitioner's move is to
split the pipeline at the joint: research produces one **grounded, structured
research asset**, and scripting is a *rendering* step that consumes it. One
research run then pays for many renders — a long-form treatment, a short clip,
an adversarial adjudication format — all drawing on the same spine, all
traceable to the same evidence, all bound by the same declared limits.

The asset is not a pile of notes. It is a contract, and the contract has one
governing principle: **every field must name its consumer.** For each thing
the research records, you must be able to point at the step of the scripting
procedure that reads it. "Honesty checks throughout" is not a consumer — a
field justified that vaguely will be written, never read, and the dishonesty
it was meant to catch will ship anyway. A rule with nowhere to live in the
structure is a comment, and comments do not survive the second run. When a
post-mortem produces a lesson, the lesson is adopted only when it becomes a
field with a named reader; otherwise the next run repeats the failure with
the lesson sitting right there in the notes.

## What the asset holds

The structured output of a research run carries, at minimum:

- **A tension** — the one place where what people believe and what the
  evidence shows come apart. This is the point of the run, and it is
  judgment, not retrieval; adding more searches does not find it
  (tension-finding-taxonomy). A run that finds no tension reports that
  honestly and stops — a topic with no tension is not a video.
- **Facts with grades** — each claim typed by kind, dated three ways, sourced
  plurally, and graded by evidence class and confidence
  (evidence-grading-ladder). The grade is not decoration: it caps what any
  render may later assert.
- **Mechanisms as validated causal chains** — every causal story the script
  will need, pre-linked so that each step earns its connector
  (pre-linked-causal-mechanisms). The narrative spine is authored *here*,
  under evidence, not improvised at writing time.
- **A declared prior and a steel-man** — what the researcher believed before
  searching, and the strongest surviving case against the verdict
  (prior-declaration-and-steel-man). Without these the asset can only
  produce a polemic.
- **Scale conversions** — every significant number pre-translated into a form
  an audience can feel, with rules for which numbers may be rounded and
  which must be quoted exactly (scale-conversion-of-numbers).
- **Unknowns as constraints** — each thing the research could not settle,
  written as a binding rule about what a render may not say, and enforced
  against every render mechanically (unknowns-as-constraints).

## Load-bearing distinctions

**A found thing, a derived thing, an absence, an utterance, and a plan are
five different kinds of claim.** A pipeline that only records where a claim
came from, never what kind of claim it is, will render an announced reserve
and a built one as the same shape — and the announcement is doing the
narrative work the construction never earned. An absence is itself a dated,
scoped fact ("searched X registers over period Y, found nothing; here is what
appearing would look like"), not a shrug. An utterance separates *they said
it* from *it is true*.

**Checkability and exposure are orthogonal axes.** Every safeguard in a
research pipeline tends to calibrate to the first — is the claim sourced, is
it falsifiable — while the harm is a function of the second: who the claim is
about, and what saying it costs. A falsifiable accusation about a named party
is still an accusation; disprovable is not printable. The asset therefore
records the *subject* of a claim as its own field, and naming rules hang on
that field, never on how well-evidenced the claim is. The corollary: any
synthesis the model reasons *beyond* the facts is a different kind of object
from a fact, is off by default, states how far past the evidence it travels
and what would falsify it, and is admitted only by explicit human gate —
because placed beside sourced facts, a conclusion launders itself, reading as
more authoritative precisely because it sounds more insightful.

**Support is not the only edge.** Facts contest and qualify each other, and a
dependency model that tracks support alone will, when one of a contradicting
pair is cut, silently resolve a live source conflict and report confidence.
Where two series disagree, the disagreement is often the story; a structure
with no home for the sideways edge muzzles it into a footnote.

**Comparisons are recomputed, never trusted.** Any two quantities the asset
compares must carry unit, period, and denominator, and the comparison must be
redone arithmetically — magnitude, window, basis. A load-bearing "slightly
more than" that compared a 30-day figure against a 60-day one has passed a
dozen prose self-checks, because none of them was arithmetic. A checker that
cannot recompute a comparison flags it; it does not ship it.

## Failure modes of the naive reading

The naive reading of "do research first" is *gather sources, then write*.
That fails in characteristic ways, each of which a technique below exists to
prevent:

1. **The timeline.** Facts in order, no tension, no turns. Research that does
   not hunt for the gap between belief and evidence produces material with no
   reason to be watched.
2. **The polemic.** A verdict that mirrors the researcher's going-in belief,
   with the counter-case never searched. An unexamined mirror is the failure;
   the mirror itself is not.
3. **The laundering render.** Prose more certain than its facts: a hedged
   correlation becomes on-screen causation, a vendor estimate becomes a hard
   number, a claim about a low-confidence figure anchors the thesis. The
   grades and constraints exist so the render step can be *held* to the
   evidence, not merely informed of it.
4. **The dead lesson.** A post-run note says "require primary sources for
   load-bearing claims"; the next run ships all-aggregator sourcing anyway,
   because the note had no field and no consumer. Structure is the only
   memory a multi-run pipeline has.
5. **The self-attested pass.** The render is checked against the research by
   asking the writer whether it complied. Compliance is scored mechanically,
   per render, against the declared constraints — and a constraint that
   cannot be scored is reported as unscored, never assumed honoured.

## The economics

The split is also the cost model. Research is the expensive, judgment-heavy
step; rendering is comparatively cheap. One well-grounded asset amortizes
across every format cut from it, and — because the asset carries its own
shelf-life analysis (what expires first, which phrasings trade precision for
durability) — across time as well. The bottleneck in practice is not search
volume but tension-finding; optimizing the pipeline means sharpening the
judgment steps, not adding retrieval.
