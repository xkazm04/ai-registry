# Prior declaration and steel-man

Two bracketing disciplines that keep a research run from becoming a polemic:
declare what you believe *before* the first search, and construct the
strongest case *against* your verdict before you ship it. They are one
technique because they attack the same failure from both ends — a researcher
who quietly confirms their going-in belief while appearing to investigate.

## The prior

Before the first search, write down in two or three sentences what you
already think is going on. After the evidence is in, classify the verdict
against that prior: a **discovery** (the evidence moved you) or a **mirror**
(you found what you expected). A mirror is not a failed run — priors are
often right. An *unexamined* mirror is the failure, because without the
written prior nobody, including you, can tell the two apart. The declaration
costs one minute and converts an invisible bias into an auditable datum.

## The steel-man

Write the strongest case against your own verdict, with evidence, **in the
words its believers would use** — then re-test the verdict against it.

Decision rules, in order:

- **Search first, construct last.** The counter-case row of the factual-spine
  sweep is a mandatory search: explicitly look for the strongest argument
  that nothing unusual is happening. A *found* opposition beats a
  *constructed* one every time, because a constructed opposition is bounded
  by your own prior — you cannot invent the argument you failed to imagine.
- **When the search returns nothing, record a dated absence — not silence,
  not a fabrication.** State what you searched, when, and what the argument
  appearing would look like. "No counter-case exists yet, as of this date" is
  a passing result; a steel-man written merely to fill the box is a failing
  one.
- **When you do construct, mark it.** The record carries a provenance flag —
  found versus constructed — because the reader must be able to see that the
  opposition was authored by the same mind it opposes. An unmarked
  construction is this technique's own worst failure mode.
- **An unsearched counter-case is never a declared gap.** Research gaps are
  work you *chose* to skip and disclosed; the counter-case is a mandate.
  Filing it under gaps records a hole in the world as a shrug by the
  researcher, and downstream gates will read the box as satisfied.

## Re-testing the verdict

Three outcomes, all of them honest:

1. **The steel-man wins.** Then *that* is the finding — the run pivots, it
   does not abort.
2. **The steel-man survives without winning.** The tension's strength grade
   drops one step, and the record names which counter-case did it. Survival
   with no score movement means the exercise was theater.
3. **The steel-man collapses under its own evidence.** The verdict stands,
   and the collapsed case is still kept — stated fairly in the asset — so
   the render can either turn it or leave it standing on screen, rather than
   pretending it was never raised.

The requirement is hard: it is discharged by a found case, a marked
construction, or a dated absence — never by silence. An asset without one of
the three produces media that persuades and may be wrong, and no adversarial
review format can be run honestly from it afterwards.

## When not to use it

Skip the steel-man ritual (keep the prior) for material with no contested
verdict — process documentation, an explainer of settled mechanics. And do
not steel-man positions that are pure bad faith with no evidentiary case;
"stated fairly and left standing" is for real opposition, not for laundering
noise into false balance. The test: could a sincere, informed believer
recognize the case as theirs? If not, it is neither found nor honestly
constructible.
