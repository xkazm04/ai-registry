# The dynamic reset

The deliberate cessation of the cue — a stop, a fall to near-silence, sometimes
to black — placed so that what follows can be heard as an arrival. It is the only
true silence the form has, and it is the single device that separates a trailer
with a climax from a trailer that is merely loud for two minutes and then stops.

The mechanism is arithmetic rather than taste: a peak is perceived against the
level it rose from, so a cut that never falls has no headroom left to peak into.
Practitioners reach for the reset for three distinct jobs — isolating one line so
it registers, landing a punchline, and resetting the dynamic floor before the
climax — and the third is the structural one. Without it the escalation runs
straight into the peak and the viewer cannot tell the peak happened.

It is also the form's most detectable structural signature. Shot length falls
monotonically across a trailer's acts; the reset produces a **spike in shot
length immediately before the peak**, which makes it the one place a checker can
see the act boundary without understanding anything.

## Landing it

Four constraints, and violating any one of them makes the reset read as a
mistake rather than a beat:

- **Stop on a natural downbeat.** A cue cut mid-phrase reads as a dropout. The
  stop belongs where the music would breathe anyway, which is why the cue's own
  section boundaries decide where the reset can go
  ([cue-first-assembly](./cue-first-assembly.md)).
- **Put a visual on the final beat.** The last audible beat needs a cut, an
  impact, or a movement landing on it. Silence arriving over a continuing shot
  reads as a technical fault.
- **Sweeten the stop.** An added accent — a hit or a cymbal not present in the
  cue — makes the cessation deliberate. Without it the ear hears something break.
- **Fill the silence with one thing.** A line, an image, a breath. A reset that
  holds two ideas has spent its whole value carrying neither.

## What the silence holds

The choice of what sits in the gap determines what the reset means:

- **A line** — the reset is the form's substitute for narration. The job of
  stating the stakes was once done by a voice; it is now done by putting one
  sentence in silence, which is a real grammar substitution: what was *said* is
  now *punctuated*.
- **An image** — a single held detail, usually quiet and usually beautiful, that
  functions as an intake of breath before the peak.
- **Nothing at all** — a beat of black. The strongest and the most fragile; it
  works only when the escalation has genuinely earned a pause, and reads as dead
  air when it has not.

## Procedure

1. **Locate the peak first.** The reset has no position of its own; it sits
   immediately before whatever the cut's largest moment is, and moving the peak
   moves it.
2. **Find the nearest cue boundary before that point.** This is where the reset
   can land, not where you would like it to.
3. **Choose the one thing the silence holds**, and cut everything else out of
   the gap.
4. **Land the four constraints** — downbeat, visual on the final beat, sweetened
   stop, single idea.
5. **Count the other resets in the cut.** More than one before the peak and the
   device has stopped resetting anything.

## Decision rules

- **When the cut has no reset, it has no climax — regardless of how large the
  ending is.** This is the first thing to check on a cut that "builds and builds
  and doesn't land".
- **When there are three or more, remove all but one.** Repetition converts the
  device from a reset into a rhythm, which disrupts momentum and produces a
  disjointed cut. The literature's own warning about the technique is about
  overuse, not misuse.
- **In a scare-led piece, the silence precedes the shock and never follows it.**
  The accent is the *release* of tension already built. An accent dropped onto a
  viewer who was not leaning in is a noise, not a scare.
- **When a comic beat needs isolating, use a short reset mid-cut and keep the
  structural one intact.** A punchline reset and a pre-peak reset can coexist if
  the first is brief and clearly smaller.
- **Do not reset into a peak the escalation has not earned.** The device makes
  whatever follows sound like an arrival, which means it will faithfully announce
  an arrival that is not there.

## When not to use it

- **In the shortest cuts.** At fifteen or thirty seconds the standard move is to
  keep the cue's opening and its climax and edit the middle to connect them —
  there is no dynamic range to reset and the gap costs a tenth of the runtime.
- **In a mood-led piece with no peak.** A cut built as one sustained gradient has
  nothing to reset before, and inserting a stop invents a structure the piece
  deliberately does not have.
- **Where the cue has no natural stop.** Some material has no phrase boundary to
  land on. Forcing one produces a dropout, and the correct response is to choose
  different material rather than to cut it badly.
