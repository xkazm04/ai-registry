# Concealment and its tells

The concern: promotional cutting when the source material has weaknesses —
rough sections, unfinished surfaces, moments that read badly out of
context. Working editors name three concealment modes, in escalating
order of risk, and one honesty limit that binds all three.

## The three modes

- **Show less.** Short durations; cut around the defect. The cheapest and
  safest mode: a two-second glimpse of a rough asset reads as energy, a
  six-second hold on it reads as the truth.
- **Razzle-dazzle.** Flutter cuts, dramatic cameras, slow motion, overlay
  graphics — production energy layered over weak material. Legitimate as
  style, dangerous as cover, and the mode most likely to trip the tell
  below.
- **Mislead.** Omission and recontextualization — implying by arrangement
  what the work does not do. The mode that spends the promise ledger's
  credibility; every use is a row the ledger must carry.

## The tell, and the limit

**A fast cut hiding something is aesthetically identical to a fast cut as
style** — the audience cannot distinguish them, so a sustained
concealment texture reads as *hiding*, whatever it is actually doing. The
limit that keeps all three modes honest: a promotional cut for an
interactive or experiential work must still convey **how the work feels
to use** — a cut that conceals so thoroughly that the experience itself
is absent has advertised a different product, and the ledger will price
that as an unpayable promise.

## Shot-level subtraction

The constructive half of "show less": for each shot, **remove information
until removing more would destroy the shot's meaning**. Density is the
enemy of both comprehension and concealment — a shot carrying three facts
delivers none, and the extra facts are usually where the weakness leaks.
Subtraction is per-shot discipline, not a pass at the end.

## When not to use this

A piece whose material is strong needs none of it — concealment applied
to good footage buys risk for nothing. Factual and documentary cuts get
the first mode only: showing less is editing, but razzle-dazzle over
evidence and misleading arrangement are the exact failures the factual
lane's evidence techniques exist to forbid.
