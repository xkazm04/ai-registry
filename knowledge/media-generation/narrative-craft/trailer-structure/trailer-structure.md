# Trailer structure

A trailer is a piece of narrative whose product is an **unpaid debt**. Every
other short form in this bundle exists to deliver something: the explainer
answers its question, the derived short completes its causal chain, the
demonstration shows the thing working. A trailer exists to make somebody watch a
different artifact later, and it succeeds by opening a gap it deliberately does
not close. That single inversion is what makes it a separate subject rather than
a variant of one, and it is why almost every rule that governs a self-sufficient
short form runs backwards here.

The naive reading is that a trailer is **a highlight reel** — take the best
moments, order them by size, put the biggest last. This produces the failure the
whole form is organised against: a cut where every second is loud, nothing rises,
and the viewer leaves impressed and uncurious. The highlight reel has no promise
because it has no gap; it has already shown you the good parts, so there is
nothing left to want. Practitioners describe the correct shape as almost the
opposite of a ramp — open strong to arrest attention, then *fall back* to a quiet
setup, and build from there to a peak. The dip is not a lull. It is where the
debt gets opened.

The second naive reading, more common in tooling than in editing rooms, is that
the trailer is assembled from **picture and then scored**. It is not. In this
form the cue is the substrate and the picture is fitted to it — act boundaries
are musical boundaries, cut points are beat points, and a late request to change
the music is a request to re-cut everything. Anything that plans, checks or
generates a trailer and treats the audio as a final layer has the dependency
graph inverted, and will produce cuts whose structure cannot be felt because
nothing marks it.

## The spine, and what it is a spine of

Two practitioner communities converge on the same division from opposite
directions. Picture editors describe four parts — a **cold open**, an
**introduction**, an **escalation**, and a **climax**, with an optional **button**
after the title. Cue writers describe a four-movement structure with a five-second
tail for the title and logos. They agree because they are describing one object:
the cue is the skeleton, and the picture parts are named after the cue movements
they sit on.

What each part is *for*:

- **The cold open** buys attention with something that needs no context. Its
  test is whether the viewer wants to know how the moment resolves *and* believes
  it will resolve soon. It is not mandatory — a work with an existing audience,
  or an opening image strong enough on its own, is better off without one — but
  when a trailer opens on exposition or on a brand mark, the cold open is the
  thing that was missing.
- **The introduction** spends the least information that makes the stakes legible:
  who these people are, what they want, why the coming spectacle matters. This is
  the part that gets cut first at shorter lengths, and the part whose absence
  turns the escalation into noise.
- **The escalation** raises a variable — scale, threat, speed, emotional register
  — across successive rungs. Each rung closes one piece of information and opens
  the next; treated as chapter breaks, they are what stops a montage reading as
  one long beat.
- **The climax** is the peak, and it is emphatically *not required to be the
  work's own climax*. A trailer built to end on the third act's turn has spent the
  artifact it was selling.
- **The button** — a last joke, sting or flourish after the title — is optional
  and has one hard constraint: it must be smaller than the climax. A button that
  outperforms the peak retroactively re-reads the peak as its setup.

Two devices run *across* all of these rather than sitting in one. Dialogue is
**cobbled**: lines are sub-clipped from anywhere in the work, indexed, and
reassembled into a sentence the work never says — which is where the form's
central honesty problem lives ([promise-ledger](./techniques/promise-ledger.md)).
And the **dynamic reset** — a deliberate stop, a fall to near-silence before the
peak — is the only true silence the form has, and the only thing that makes the
climax legible as a climax
([dynamic-reset](./techniques/dynamic-reset.md)).

## The spine is a default, not a law, and the exception is a lane

The four-part spine is the grammar of wide-release promotion, and abandoning it
deliberately is the signature of the specialty lane. Award-winning cutters in
that lane map a work's **mood** rather than its plot, on the explicit reasoning
that the story is not what sells the ticket; others replace the beats with
graphics that tell the viewer what to think. Both positions are held by
practitioners with better track records than the doctrine has.

So: **use the spine as the default and as the diagnostic, never as the gate.** A
cut that deviates from it is either a specialty-lane choice or a defect, and the
structure alone cannot tell you which. What it *can* tell you is that the
deviation is real and deliberate — which is the useful thing to surface to a
human.

## Length is a ladder, not a knob

The form ships as a family: a teaser, a theatrical cut, spots, and a
platform cut, all from one campaign, none of them self-sufficient. Shortening is
not trimming — it is **dropping whole parts in a known order**, and the order is
itself craft knowledge ([length-ladder](./techniques/length-ladder.md)). The
theatrical cut carries a hard external ceiling (2:30, with a single annual
exception per distributor under the exhibitor-facing advertising rules), and the
measured centre of gravity across nine decades of releases sits just above two
minutes — a stability worth knowing, because "trailers keep getting longer" is a
widely repeated claim the measurement does not support.

## Withholding is the contract, and it is contested

Every neighbouring subject in this bundle treats a withheld answer as a defect.
Here it is the product. The craft position is stated plainly by practitioners:
the trailer is the question and the work is the answer; select imagery that hints
without exposing a resolution; do not "cheat" the work by either overpromising or
underselling it.

**And the counter-practice is real, deliberate, and driven by measurement.** The
most consistently reported finding from audience testing is that viewers say the
trailer did not show them *enough*, which pushes campaigns toward revelation; in
a low-confidence market a revealing cut functions as proof-of-delivery. Insiders
have described specific high-profile reveals as intentional. This is not
incompetence, and a doctrine that treats it as incompetence will mis-diagnose
every campaign that made the trade knowingly.

So state the trade rather than resolving it
([withholding-budget](./techniques/withholding-budget.md)): the trailer is
measured on its own conversion, the work is measured on the experience it
delivers, and the same footage optimises those two differently. Nobody in the
practitioner literature claims to have solved it.

## Failure modes of the naive reading

- **Front-loading.** The peak lands before the viewer has stakes, and everything
  after is downhill. The dip after the cold open exists precisely to prevent
  this; a cut without a dip has spent its ceiling in the first fifteen seconds.
- **Flat escalation.** The montage varies its *content* but not its *magnitude*,
  so a sequence of rungs reads as one repeated beat. This is the highlight reel
  wearing the spine's clothes.
- **Opening on an unearned mark.** A brand or studio card before the hook spends
  the most valuable seconds of the form on an identity the viewer does not yet
  hold. Where the mark carries real pedigree this is a deliberate asset; where it
  does not, it belongs at the end.
- **Best moment spent.** The strongest joke, scare or reveal is consumed by the
  promotion, so it is dead when the work ships. The incentive structure selects
  for this and the craft literature has no answer to it beyond naming it.
- **Impact fatigue.** When every cut carries a hit, no cut carries emphasis. The
  same applies to the reset: a stop used constantly resets nothing.
- **A button that outsizes the climax.** The last few seconds are what gets
  quoted, so a button bigger than the peak makes the peak read as its setup.
- **Over-explaining the premise.** A trailer that fully explains its own novum
  has nothing left to sell. The information floor is the *minimum* that makes the
  stakes legible, not the maximum the runtime allows.
- **Scoring last.** Picture cut first and music laid over it produces a cut whose
  act boundaries land nowhere, because there is nothing marking them.

## The techniques

- [promise-ledger](./techniques/promise-ledger.md) — account for what the cut
  promises against what the work can pay, including promises made by assembly
  rather than by claim.
- [withholding-budget](./techniques/withholding-budget.md) — decide what may be
  shown and what must be kept, as an explicit budget with a stated trade.
- [escalation-without-mechanism](./techniques/escalation-without-mechanism.md) —
  build rungs that rise in stakes rather than reveal how anything works.
- [dynamic-reset](./techniques/dynamic-reset.md) — the stop before the peak: how
  to place it, how to land it, and why it stops working when repeated.
- [cue-first-assembly](./techniques/cue-first-assembly.md) — treat the cue as the
  structure and fit the picture to it, including how to choose a cue at all.
- [length-ladder](./techniques/length-ladder.md) — derive the family of shorter
  cuts by dropping parts in order, not by trimming uniformly.

## Where this subject ends and its neighbours begin

**[short-form-narrative-structure](../short-form-narrative-structure/short-form-narrative-structure.md)
governs a piece that must pay every debt it opens; this subject governs a piece
whose entire function is to open a debt another artifact will pay.** When the
artifact under review *is* the thing being watched, the neighbour owns it; when
the artifact exists to make something else be watched, this subject owns it. That
line is sharp enough to run in both directions: the neighbour's rule that a
withheld answer "reads as not having one" is correct there and inverted here, and
this subject's rule that the peak need not be the work's peak is meaningless
there. Its beat-linking test
([but-therefore-beat-linking](../short-form-narrative-structure/techniques/but-therefore-beat-linking.md))
and its act-as-question architecture
([question-stack-architecture](../short-form-narrative-structure/techniques/question-stack-architecture.md))
both apply to a trailer's spine unchanged, and this subject does not restate
them — an escalation whose rungs link only with "and then" is flat for exactly
the reason the neighbour gives.

The opening seconds are shared ground and the neighbour of a neighbour owns
them: [hook-shape-selection](../../production-ops/platform-format-adaptation/techniques/hook-shape-selection.md)
already covers choosing an opening shape by a property of the subject and bans
the announced fact. A cold open is that selection under a harder constraint (it
must also promise a resolution), so read it there rather than looking for a
second hook technique here.
[derived-short-contract](../../production-ops/platform-format-adaptation/techniques/derived-short-contract.md)
is the length-ladder's mirror image and explicitly exiles the trailer: its four
rules exist to make one clip *self-sufficient*, while a cut-down family exists to
keep several artifacts *mutually consistent* while none of them is.

[cinematic-language](../../visual-generation/cinematic-language/cinematic-language.md)
owns what a single frame's camera, light and lens say, and
[music-spotting-against-picture](../../production-ops/video-assembly/techniques/music-spotting-against-picture.md)
owns spotting in general. This subject owns only the trailer's *structural*
dependency on the cue — that the acts are the cue's movements — and defers the
rest.

## What is measurable, and what is not

The structural properties of a cut are unusually amenable to counting, and two
of them are load-bearing. **Shot length falls across the acts**, and one
published analysis of 130 releases over sixty years found that trailer average
shot length *and its variance* both decline over time — the second result being
the more interesting, since falling variance is a measurable form of "generic".
And the dynamic reset produces the single most detectable structural signature in
the form: a **spike in shot length immediately before the peak**, in an otherwise
monotonically falling curve. Card ordering (does a title precede the button), the
presence of a call-to-action, the position of a brand mark in the first seconds,
and the concatenated text of every spoken line and copy card read as one
paragraph are all mechanically checkable.

None of that measures whether the trailer works. The instrument practitioners
actually use is a survey, and the revealing thing about the good versions is that
almost none of their questions are about the artifact — they ask what the viewer
*understood*, what it *reminded them of*, what was *unclear*, and how they would
describe it to somebody else. **A structural checker can establish that a cut is
malformed; it cannot establish that a cut works**, and per
[unmeasured-is-not-pass](../../_laws.md#unmeasured-is-not-pass) it must report
that gap rather than let a green structural verdict stand in for an unmeasured
one.
