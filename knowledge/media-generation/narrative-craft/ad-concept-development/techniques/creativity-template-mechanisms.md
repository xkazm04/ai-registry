# Creativity template mechanisms

## The concern

"Be original" is not a procedure. A concept surprises because of a **mechanism**, a
specific operation on the product's core claim, and the mechanisms are few. The
peer-reviewed anchor is a 1999 study of 200 award-winning print ads, which found six
recurring templates behind most of them:
- **The share of winners explained.** The templates accounted for close to nine in
  ten winners.
- **Winners against non-winners.** Comparing award winners with a matched set of
  non-winners, about half of the winners fit a template, against a few percent of
  the non-winners.
- **Training.** People trained in the templates produced ads judged more creative
  and better remembered than untrained people did.

The study is old, the medium was print, and an award is not effectiveness. Its claim
is narrow but well grounded: surprise in ads clusters into a small number of
learnable operations.

A blind, search-free read of the same problem reached an overlapping list
(inversion, exaggeration, genre transplant) independently. The convergence is why
the mechanism list is used here as a generator and not as trivia.

## The mechanisms

Labels vary between write-ups. These are the operations, stated as instructions:

1. **Extreme consequence.** Push the product's benefit until it breaks something.
   The result is too good, too fast or too loud, and the world has to adjust.
2. **Inversion.** Show the world without the product as absurdly worse, or reverse
   who needs whom.
3. **Absurd alternative.** Show the ridiculous lengths people go to without the
   product, or what they substitute for it.
4. **Extreme effort.** Someone goes to impossible lengths *for* the product or what
   it gives.
5. **Competition.** The product beats something it has no business competing with.
6. **Metaphor or transplant.** The product's claim is told in another domain's
   grammar: a nature documentary, a heist, a sports broadcast, a horror trailer.
   This is the template most often named by practitioners, and the one where the
   cliché risk is highest, because the transplant itself can be the obvious one.

## Procedure

1. Write the product's single claim in one plain sentence (one claim, not a list).
2. Generate premises **per mechanism**, at least three under each, before judging any
   (see [divergence-against-the-median](./divergence-against-the-median.md) for how).
3. For each premise, name its mechanism. A premise that fits no mechanism is either
   genuinely new or, far more often, a sincere demonstration. Keep the first kind
   and treat the second as the baseline to beat.
4. Choose the **register** with the mechanism. Extreme consequence and absurd
   alternative pull toward comedy. Extreme effort and metaphor can carry sincerity.
   For generated footage, the reception pattern of 2025-26 favours comedy, absurdity
   and stylisation over sincere imitation live action: viewers forgave visible
   generation when the premise was a joke, and punished it when the premise asked to
   be believed. That pattern is a strong prior, not a controlled finding.
5. Check the escalation. A comic mechanism needs a ladder: each beat raises the
   absurdity one rung, and the last rung lands on the product.

## Decision rules

- When the spot has under thirty seconds, use one mechanism. Two mechanisms in a
  short spot read as two ads.
- When the product is fictional or unknown, prefer a mechanism that **demonstrates**
  the claim (extreme consequence, competition) over one that **assumes** familiarity
  (inversion of a known habit), because the viewer has no prior to invert.
- When the rubric rewards originality explicitly, the metaphor template needs an
  extra resemblance check. Its common transplants are the most-used in the
  category.

## When not to use

- When the brief mandates a straight demonstration (a regulated claim, a product
  launch needing literal comprehension). Use the template on the wrapper and keep the
  demonstration plain.
- After the concept is locked. Templates are for ideation, not for redecorating a
  chosen premise.

## Failure modes

- **Template as costume.** A sincere montage with one absurd shot inserted. The
  mechanism must be the spine, not a moment.
- **Escalation without a floor.** Absurd beats with no grounded person to react,
  so nothing registers as absurd. Comedy needs someone in the frame who finds it
  strange.
- **The obvious transplant.** "Like a nature documentary" or "like a heist" with
  no further twist is the first idea in the metaphor family. Treat it as banned
  until the cliché pass has cleared it.
