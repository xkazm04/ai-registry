# Agency-preserving premise

## The concern

For a product that helps people do something (make, write, build, play, care), the
most natural premise is to show the product doing it. The person struggles, the
product arrives, and the work completes itself. It is the shortest line from problem
to product, and that is why it is everyone's first idea.

It is also the most consistently punished premise in recent technology advertising.
- **The fan letter.** A 2024 spot in which an assistant wrote a child's fan letter
  for her was pulled within days. The maker's own line in withdrawing it was that
  the technology can never replace human creativity.
- **The crushed tools.** A spot the same year showed creative instruments crushed
  into a device. Its maker apologised.
- **Technology-themed spots at the largest 2026 ad event.** They averaged well below
  the field in independent testing. The tester's diagnosis was "functional
  storytelling": the technology was the protagonist. The best-scoring exception made
  the technology the catalyst for a human connection.

These are reception events plus one industry tester's analysis, not a controlled
experiment. They point the same way, and they converge with an unprompted read of
the same failure from training knowledge alone.

The mechanism is not subtle. A viewer identifies with the person on screen. A premise
in which the product does the person's act removes the person, and the story
becomes a demonstration of the viewer's own replaceability. For a product about
creativity, that message cuts against the brand itself.

## Procedure

1. **Write the agency line before any premise.** One sentence: *the person still
   ___, and the product changes ___ about it*. Examples of the form: "she still
   writes every line; it lets her hear the whole band while she does"; "he still
   paints; it shows him where the wall wants the tiger". A premise that cannot be
   written in this form does not enter ideation.
2. **Classify what the product changes.** Usable verbs, roughly from strongest to
   weakest for this purpose:
   - **Reveals:** the person sees or hears something that was there.
   - **Amplifies:** the person's act becomes larger, louder or faster.
   - **Connects:** the person's act reaches others.
   - **Removes friction:** the chore around the act disappears and the act remains.

   "Performs" and "completes" are not on the list.
3. **Put the person's hands on the climax.** In the peak moment, the visible cause
   of the change must be something the person does: a gesture, a decision, a note
   played. The product's contribution appears as a consequence of that act, never as
   an independent agent.
4. **Check the product's visual signature against the default.** If the product's
   effect is rendered as autonomous light, sparkle or particles moving on their own,
   the image is telling the replacement story even when the script is not. Redesign
   the effect so it is driven by the person (it follows their hand, it sounds when
   they act) or replace it with a concrete, non-magical consequence.
5. **Test the reversal.** Remove the product from the premise. What remains should
   still be a person doing their thing, only smaller, slower or more alone. If what
   remains is a person doing nothing, the premise is a replacement premise.

## Decision rules

- When the product's job really is to do the task (a delivery service, a cleaning
  product), the rule relaxes. The person's act becomes what they do with the
  reclaimed time or attention, and that is what the spot shows.
- When the subtext is an automation or orchestration product, the human seat is the
  **conductor's**. Many hands do the work and one person directs them. Show the
  direction, not the hands.
- When a premise is funny *because* the product overreaches (it does too much, it
  takes the act too literally), replacement is allowed as the joke, with the person
  reclaiming the act at the end. That is the extreme-consequence template, and the
  reclaiming beat is not optional.

## When not to use

- Spots for products with no human craft in the frame (insurance, utilities,
  infrastructure). The principle still holds loosely, but its failure there is
  boredom, not offence.
- Pure brand-mood pieces with no product action at all.

## Failure modes

- **Agency in the script, replacement in the picture.** The voiceover says "you
  create", and the image shows the work completing itself in light. Viewers believe
  the image.
- **Agency only in the opening.** The person struggles with skill, then stands and
  watches in awe at the climax. Awe is the replacement premise's signature
  expression.
- **Treating the backlash as a taste issue.** Spots in this pattern have been pulled
  after airing, which is a commercial outcome. The objection is not to how the spot
  was made. It is to what it says.
