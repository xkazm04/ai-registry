# Process: one concept round for the Personas spec ad, counted

The first run of the technique on a real brief, made on 2026-10-15 for the operator's
ElevenLabs contest entry. The case and the rubric are in the sibling applications.

**Arm A** is the v2 bible's path. One concept, chosen before any prompt was written:
four makers, a glowing box, glasses, magic completes the work.

**Arm B** is the procedure:
- **The brief.** Rubric transcribed, product truth stated, and the v2 premise plus
  eleven category clichés listed and banned.
- **Generators.** Three independent model sessions, each forced onto one mechanism
  lane: comic templates, genre transplant, or formal device and extreme effort.
- **Per lane.** Twelve one-liners each, with a verbalised probability that a typical
  agency team would reach the idea. Then three developed concepts drawn from the
  low-probability tail.
- **Judging.** A resemblance self-critique per concept, then a separate judging pass
  scored on the rubric.
- **Selection.** The human picks.

## What was measured

- **36 one-liners formed 2 large clusters and 5 singletons.**
  - **"A masked crew of specialists performs around a person who decides": 19 of 36.**
    That cluster is the product's own truth. The brand is a crew of personas, so the
    technique's narrow-brief rule applied and divergence moved to the execution axis.
    Inside the cluster the 19 ideas spread across 15 distinct genres, among them an
    auction, kabuki, a handbell recital, a Greek chorus, a sea shanty, a courtroom, a
    square dance, a returns-desk crew battle and a 1958 newsreel.
  - **"One person plays every part": 12 of 36.** This was the true median: one
    generator rated its own split-screen variant at 0.45, the most probable idea in
    the run. It was dropped, as the procedure says.
- **9 developed concepts, all passing the agency line, and all sharing one climax.**
  In every one, the person waves the help off or takes the mask off and does the
  decisive thing alone. The brief had stated the agency rule. This is the condition the
  run gave back to the sibling technique: apply the agency line as a filter, not as an
  instruction.
- **Resemblance self-critique.** Every developed concept named a nearest existing ad or
  meme: a fast-talker courier spot, a car maker's choir ad, a viral shanty chain, a
  musical's snap prologue, and others. The judging pass scored the concepts with
  those resemblances in view. No concept was formally demoted on resemblance alone.
- **Rubric scores** (judging pass, 1-5 weighted): the top four sat at 4.4-4.75. Arm A's
  concept as handled in v2 scores low on catchiness, because it has no named hook, and on
  agency.

## The pick, and what the operator did with it

The operator picked the top-scored concept: an auction-chant spot in which a baker
auctions her week to masked specialists and keeps one lot. That concept's generator had
put it at a 0.07 probability. The operator then chose to **keep the v2 concept for the
v3 production**, judging that the concept was sound and its handling had failed (see the
agency application). The auction concept is banked as the rival premise.

## Verdict

**Better on the technique's own claim, which is diversity.** Arm A had one candidate,
the category's median. Arm B produced 15 genres inside the product truth and surfaced a
0.07-probability concept that won the rubric pass. **Unmeasured:** whether a B-arm
concept makes a better *film* than a re-handled A-arm concept. The operator's decision
to keep A sets up exactly that comparison.
