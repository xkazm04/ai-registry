# Recognizable cast

In a narration-free chart, the viewer's only reason to keep watching is
that they recognize the competitors and have a stake in the outcome. The
cast (bars, lines, countries, records) works the way characters do in
fiction. A cast nobody recognizes turns a correct chart into noise.

## Procedure

1. List the entities that will be on screen at the peak moment.
2. For each one, ask: would a general viewer in the target market know
   this name without a caption? Count the yeses.
3. Prefer datasets where most of the peak cast is recognizable: household
   brands, countries, all-time athletes, famous buildings, presidents.
4. If the source data is niche, reframe it around a recognizable anchor:
   a famous rivalry, a single legend's record, "how old was every
   president".

## Decision rules

- Universality is scored from the cast, not from the topic label.
  "Football" with an obscure cast scores lower than "fast food" with a
  famous one.
- Two famous names in a long rivalry beat ten semi-famous ones. A duopoly
  gives the motion a plot.
- A niche cast is acceptable for an owned niche audience, but score the
  platform fit against that audience, not against a general feed.

## When not to use it

- Size and record ladders where the object, not a name, is the cast
  (the tallest building, the biggest ship). There, scale contrast does
  the work that recognition does elsewhere.
