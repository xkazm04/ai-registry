# Tone as a falsifiable bet

A tone layer is not a preference; it is a claim that this register will make the
show better in a stated way. Written as a claim it can be tested, and a tone
that cannot say what it would move is a mood, not a variant. The discipline:
**every tone states its hypothesis, runs against a fixed control, is measured on
the same instrument as every other arm, may not spend the character to buy its
metric, and is retired when it loses.**

## The bet sheet

Each tone carries, alongside its register and examples:

- **the hypothesis**: what it should move, in which segments and room sizes, and
  roughly by how much (humour landing, responsiveness, a quiet stretch held
  without dead air);
- **the risk**: what it is likely to cost (a roast-heavy tone risks the cruelty
  line; a deadpan tone risks reading as disengaged);
- **the falsifier**: the result that would retire it.

One tone is the **control arm**: the house register, run in every comparison, not
itself under test. Without a control there is nothing a variant can beat except
last week, and last week had a different audience.

## Guardrails are not metrics

In-character rate, the hard lines and the safety rules are **guardrails**: every
arm must clear them, and a tone that clears its hypothesis while failing a
guardrail has lost. A tone may never trade holding the character for laughs
landing; a funnier host that is no longer recognisably the host has not won
anything the show wanted. Report the guardrails beside every result, and report
"unmeasured" for any guardrail the run did not check rather than leaving it
implied.

## Tone interacts with the performer

The same tone performed by two model tiers is two different arms. The tier that
performs a tone can move holding-character and humour more than the choice
between tones does. In one measured tournament of five tones on two tiers, the
spread between the best and worst tone on the stronger tier was about five
points of judged quality, while the weaker tier trailed the stronger on every
tone, held character on roughly nine turns in ten against almost every turn, and
landed its humour far less often. That makes any tone ranking taken on one tier a
ranking *on that tier* only, and it makes the tier the first decision and the
tone the second. Run tone × tier
as the design, not tone alone, and price it per usable stream: the cheaper tier
that falls out of character more often may cost more per stream the owner would
actually air. A tier change re-opens every bet.

## Procedure

1. Write the bet sheet before the tone is first run. A hypothesis written after
   the result is a story.
2. Run each arm across the same matrix of segments, room sizes and seeds, with
   the control in every cell, against a simulated or held-out audience that does
   not know which arm it faces.
3. Grade with a judge that is not the performer and does not see the arm label.
4. Read results per segment and per room size before pooling: a tone that wins
   in a tiny room and loses at scale is two findings.
5. Promote, keep testing, or retire, and write the verdict on the bet sheet with
   its sample size.

## Decision rules

- **When two tones differ by less than the run-to-run noise**, neither has won;
  keep the control, or keep both as rotation for variety, and say which.
- **When a tone wins on its hypothesis and fails a guardrail**, it is retired or
  rewritten, never promoted with a caveat.
- **When the owner prefers a tone that the numbers do not support**, the owner may
  run it; the record says it was chosen, not that it won.
- **When every tone fails the same guardrail**, the cause is not tone: look at the
  core, the performer tier or the input pipeline.

## When not to use it

A one-off themed stream (a holiday special) can run a tone that is not under test;
call it a special and keep its numbers out of the comparison. And tone testing
assumes the core is fixed: do not run a "tone" that edits the core, because the
result is a different character and says nothing about register.
