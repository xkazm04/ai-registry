# Prototype the cut from stills and a dry walk

A film built voice-first spends money and time at the earliest and least
reversible steps: the narration is generated before anything is recorded, and the
holds that fall out of it time the whole take. That order is right, and it makes
two mistakes expensive that a looser process makes cheap. A direction nobody
approved becomes a render nobody wants. A line written about a flow the product
does not actually follow becomes a clip, a hold and a take built on a false
premise. Both are cheap to prevent before voice exists, and neither can be
prevented after.

## Samples from stills: choose the direction by looking

Before any narration is synthesised, compose the candidate looks as **still
frames from real captures** of the running product, drawn with the same layer the
render will use, and put them on one page. The question is never "do you like
motion graphics" but "which of these amounts of motion, on these three beats, do
you want to watch for three minutes".

- **Use real captures and real figures.** A sample drawn over a mock-up approves a
  look that the product's actual density may not support. Figures in a sample come
  from the product's own screens, so the sample is also the first check that the
  numbers a plate will assert exist where the plate will assert them.
- **Show every balance on the same beats.** Comparing a heavy treatment on one beat
  with a light one on another compares the beats. The same three beats, in every
  balance, side by side, lets the operator pick by eye.
- **Expect the answer to be a mix and not a pick.** The first round in the case
  that taught this returned a reversal of the proposed default: the product was to
  lead and the motion layers were to open and close sections. The second round
  took that as the brief, and the page was redrawn the same day for the cost of
  stills.
- **Draw the parts that leave the product too.** A section that explains the
  method or the architecture in diagrams, with no recording, has nothing to
  review. Sample it with the same stills-and-layer approach, in the product's own
  visual language, and let the operator judge it before it exists as an animation.
- **Put the run of show on the page.** A single strip of beats against the length
  limit, coloured by balance, answers "does this fit" before any clip is made.

## The dry walk: take the flow from the product, not from memory

Before a line is written about a flow, walk it in the product with the same driver
the capture will use, and read what happens. In the case that taught this, the
script said nothing on the shelf fitted the person's chore and a custom command
would be written. The product's actual behaviour was that a library entry fitted,
offered to be made the person's own, and opened a case form of ten questions. The
true story was better and the false line was already written.

- **Walk it once, on the staged data, with the assertions the take will use.** The
  walk is a draft of the capture; what it learns is the beat list.
- **Keep what the product does that the script did not expect.** A flow that is
  richer than the script is the film's material, and a flow that is thinner is a
  claim to drop before it is promised.
- **Note every wait that depends on a model or a service.** Those beats start their
  footage when their end state is on screen, and the narration for them is written
  about the end state, not the wait.
- **Record the stage state the walk changed.** A walk leaves cases saved, chats
  opened and forms half filled; the real take must start from a reset stage, and
  the list of what to reset is made here, not discovered in the take.

## Failure modes

- **A line written from the product's brochure.** It describes what the product is
  meant to do, and the take then fails its own assertion or, worse, passes it by
  showing something adjacent.
- **A direction approved from a description.** Words like "dominant overlay" mean a
  different amount of screen to everyone who reads them.
- **A walk that mutates the stage and is not reset.** The take starts with state
  the story did not plant, and the figure on screen is from yesterday's walk.

## Decision rules

- When a film has more than one balance of footage and motion, sample them on the
  same real beats before narration, because the choice is the operator's and
  stills are the cheapest honest evidence for it.
- When a flow will be narrated, walk it first with the capture's own driver,
  because the cost of a wrong line is its clip, its hold and the take built on it.
- When the walk changes the stage, list the changes and reset them before the
  take, because the film's start state must be the one the story planted.
