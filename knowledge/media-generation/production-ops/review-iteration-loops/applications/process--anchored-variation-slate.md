# Process: what makers of praised generated spots report about anchors and volume

This application collects the field record from a 2026-10-15 research pass on how the
makers of widely praised generated ads and shorts work. Each figure carries its source
and its grade, because most of them are single, self-reported numbers. The research
pass was run for the operator's spec ad (the *Personas Production Bible* v2.2, 13 Oct
2026, kept outside the registry). That bible budgets two drafts per keyframe and one
motion retry before a fallback model, which is this subject's loop economics applied to
the divergence phase.

## Anchors are named outside references, every time

| Maker / work | Anchor | Source (grade) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kalshi NBA Finals spot, June 2025 | the look and tone of a forthcoming open-world crime game, which the client loved, set in the maker's own Florida, for a script of about ten invented characters | maker's own thread, mirrored at threadreaderapp.com/thread/1932893260399456513 (maker-primary) |
| Gossip Goblin series, 2025-26 | a designed world bible, with locations described as "Hong Kong meets Bangkok meets a Kowloon night market", and stills organised by location and altitude | pjace.beehiiv.com, interview of 2026-05-13 (interview) |
| *Kitsune* / *Electric Pink*, 2025 | 90s TV animation as the target look, and a recurring fox motif from the maker's own life | siddhisnewsletter.substack.com, 2025-06-30 (interview) |
| A Runway Gen:48 award entry | Impressionist painting, chosen because the maker's daughter loves it, plus a custom style model trained on it | runway.com/customers/48-hours-to-generate-a-film (vendor case study, undated) |

None of these anchors is a prompt-craft term. Each is material introduced from outside
the model's distribution, which is the technique's mechanism.

## Volume per kept shot

| Figure | Source (grade) |
| --- | --- |
| About 300-400 generations for 15 usable clips (one maker, 2-3 days) | Kalshi thread and a 2025-06-23 interview syndicated by AOL (maker-primary / interview). The interview gives "20-30 runs to fine-tune" a shot |
| 41 of 164 clips kept (25%); 17 final shots stitched from multiple generations | invideo.io FAQ, 2026-07 (vendor) |

| About 70,000 clips for a 2025 holiday spot, received as "AI slop" by much of the press anyway, while an audience ad-testing panel (System1) rated it 5.9 stars, the top of its scale | WSJ figure via secondary outlets (news, secondary); score from System1's own write-up (vendor) |

## Cut early

- The *Kitsune* maker starts rough cuts about 20% into generation and iterates in an
  "agile" loop (interview above).
- The Gen:48 entry built its animatic about 10 hours into a 48-hour window, before
  generation was finished (vendor case study above).
- The balloon-head short's makers kept 2-3 seconds of 20-second generations and kept
  regenerating during the edit (nofilmschool.com interview, 2024-04-02).

## Fate against the operator's bible

**Simulation, unmeasurable.** The bible spends its divergence on one axis, faces. Its
identity masters were drafted two at a time and picked by eye, which is correct for an
invariant. It has no anchored slate for the *look* and no volume phase for shots: every
keyframe was compiled once, and the rehearsal (2×2 storyboard sheets) was skipped. The
technique predicts the result the operator reported, a consistent set with no chosen
direction. Whether an anchored, high-volume divergence phase would have produced a better
spot is not measured.

**Return condition:** the redesign's look round. Run a slate of three named anchors on
the same keyframe brief, generate at volume, and record the keep ratio and which anchor
won here.
