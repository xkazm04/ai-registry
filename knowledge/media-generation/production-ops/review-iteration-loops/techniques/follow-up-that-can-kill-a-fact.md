# Follow-up that can kill a fact

Review does not only find rendering problems; it finds evidence problems —
a figure that feels thin, a claim resting on one source, a contradiction the
research left open. The follow-up channel is where those doubts go: a queue
of interrogation requests (deepen this card; answer this question) run
against fresh research. The technique is the outcome vocabulary, and its
non-negotiable core is that **a follow-up may come back with the fact's
death warrant** — a channel that only knows how to render "here is more" is
structurally a confirmation machine, and iteration through it converges on
confidence rather than truth.

## The effect vocabulary

Every follow-up result is a set of typed effects on the evidence base, and
the set must include the subtractive verbs:

- **confirms** — the claim survived independent scrutiny; its standing
  strengthens.
- **downgrades** — the claim survives but weaker: still single-sourced, a
  narrower scope than stated, a hedge it did not carry before.
- **kills** — the claim is dead: contradicted by better evidence, or still
  uncorroborated after a deliberate second look. Killing is closure, not
  merely non-use — a fact that was already cut from every render but left
  alive in the base remains a *temptation* every future edit can reach for;
  the kill removes it.
- **resolves an unknown** — an open question closes, and with it the hedges
  it imposed. Resolution propagates as license: a beat that is over-hedged
  *because* of a now-resolved unknown is a prime rewrite candidate.
- **adds a fact** — new material enters, with its own confidence and
  source, through the same admission discipline as original research. The
  strongest follow-up shape is kill-and-replace: the dead claim's successor
  is often *stronger* and argues the same thesis by a different route — the
  interrogation improved the piece precisely by subtracting.

A result also carries an overall verdict — strengthened, weakened,
resolved, inconclusive — stated explicitly because **a follow-up channel
whose results only ever confirm is the suspicious kind**: either the
questions are soft, or the researcher is telling the queue what it wants to
hear.

## Unanswered is terminal, not pending

A dispatched request that returns nothing must say so. Without an explicit
*unanswered* state, a request that will never be answered sits at "queued"
forever, indistinguishable from one still waiting its turn — and the
reviewer reads the queue as work-in-progress when it is actually a hole.
This is the refusal-is-a-state law applied to research: no elimination is
silent, and a dispatch that produced no answer is an outcome to display,
not an absence to hide.

## Kills propagate through the edit machinery

A killed fact is not done when the base is updated. It propagates forward
through the same revision discipline as any creator note:

- Beats attributed to the dead fact need edits — rewrite against surviving
  evidence, or cut with chain repair. The attribution ledger is the
  blast-radius query: it names exactly which beats, in which renders, rest
  on the casualty.
- If cutting the evidence would leave a rhetorical turn standing
  unsupported, the turn goes too — the argument-keeps-its-evidence rule
  does not care whether the evidence died by note or by research.
- Gates re-run. A verification computed while the fact was alive is void
  once it is dead — including probes *derived from* the fact's hedges.
- The transcript of the original research is not rewritten. It is evidence,
  and evidence can be wrong; the kill is a new event layered on top with
  its own source and reason, so the base's history shows *that* the claim
  was believed, and when it stopped being.

## Decision rules

- Queue a deepen automatically when a claim is load-bearing at low
  confidence — that combination is the standing danger flag, and waiting
  for a human to notice it is waiting for the incident.
- Interrogate contradictions as questions, not as choices. Two facts that
  seem to conflict may describe different cohorts, different windows, or
  different clocks — the resolution is often that both are true and the
  framing was wrong, which no amount of picking-one would have found.
- Grade the replacement independently. A fact minted by a follow-up gets
  its confidence from its own sourcing, not sympathy inheritance from the
  claim it replaced or the question that summoned it.

## When NOT to use it

The follow-up channel is for doubts about *evidence*; do not route
rendering complaints through it (that is the note taxonomy's job), and do
not use it as an open-ended second research phase — its requests are
targeted interrogations of existing material plus specific questions, each
with a stated reason. A queue that accepts "find more about X" without a
reason attached is research scope-creep wearing review's badge, and its
additions arrive without the doubt-driven framing that makes follow-up
results decisive.
