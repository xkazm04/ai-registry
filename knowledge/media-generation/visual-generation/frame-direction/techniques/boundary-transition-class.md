# Boundary transition class

Motif threads say what a picture *is* across the cut. They do not say what
happens at the join between two adjacent frames, and the join is where a
viewer loses or keeps their place. Declare, for every boundary, which of three
classes it is:

- **carry** — elements of the previous frame stay on screen and guide the eye
  into the next: the stable line, the coloured region, the object that moves
  into its new position. The viewer's mental model survives the cut.
- **agent** — the mark that carried the data *becomes* the next picture: the
  bar grows into the next comparison, the line continues past its high, the
  flywheel seizes. A thread's *change* operation across adjacent beats is
  almost always this class.
- **refresh** — a complete replacement, with nothing connecting the last
  instant of one frame to the first of the next. It is the right class for a
  new topic, and a costly one anywhere else, because it spends the viewer's
  orientation.

The classification converges from independent directions. A coded corpus of
professional data-video clips found refresh in roughly half of all
transitions, and reserved it for "a new topic or an abrupt turn"; preserved
guides and transforming data marks made up most of the rest. Narrative-
visualization design names "transition guidance" and object continuity as its
own tactic family, and animated-transition research found that keeping the
data object constant through a change is what lets viewers follow it.

## Why thread operations do not settle the join

A thread lifecycle answers "has the viewer seen this before?" The boundary
class answers "is it still on screen right now?" They diverge exactly where
direction is subtlest:

- **Hold beats.** "Nothing new" on a question or a thesis sentence can mean
  *keep the last picture* (carry, the viewer sits with what they just saw) or
  *clear the screen* (refresh to near-empty). These are different films. A
  thread column that reads "none" cannot tell them apart.
- **Reveal by subtraction.** Stripping a frame back is legible only as a
  carry with removal; a subtraction from a blank screen is just a blank
  screen.
- **Non-adjacent returns.** A *change* on a thread last seen three beats ago
  is a refresh at its own boundary, even though the viewer recognises the
  asset. The boundary is where the cost of that recognition is paid.

## Decision rules

- Turns on an owned thread are **agent** boundaries, not refreshes. The
  corpus convention of refreshing at abrupt turns applies when the turn has no
  established picture to break; when it does, breaking it in place is the
  stronger event, and a refresh there throws the pattern away before the
  viewer sees it broken.
- Spend **refresh** at topic openings and at metaphor entries. A metaphor
  that carries elements of the mechanism beside it reads as part of the
  machine.
- Every hold beat declares carry or refresh. An undeclared hold is the
  rendering step's default, and nobody chose it.
- A carried element must be decodable at a glance. Preserved context that the
  viewer has to stop and interpret costs more comprehension than no context
  at all; carry the one mark the next frame needs, not the whole layout.

## When not to use this

In a cut with no threads at all (a montage, a list of unrelated exhibits)
every boundary is a refresh by construction, and declaring it is ceremony.
Do not let the class become motion authoring either: it says what persists
across the join, not how anything moves within a frame.
