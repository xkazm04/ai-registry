# Curiosity title and thumbnail

A race video is a question. Revealing the answer in the title or thumbnail
removes the reason to watch. Corny claims ("everyone else is scrap") read
as clickbait.

## Procedure

1. **On-screen title = one plain line naming the topic,** e.g. "Search
   engine market share, 2009–2026". It must fit one row on a phone (≈ 60 px
   Barlow Condensed at 1080 wide). No in-chart slogan; give the height to
   the data (more rows).
2. **Hook (0–2 s) = the question,** e.g. "Who owns the search box?", plus
   scope ("21 engines · Jan 2009 → Sep 2026") and equal logo tiles of the
   key players. No answer, no number that implies one.
3. **Thumbnail (1080×1920 still) = the same question plus the players**:
   equal-size logo tiles, the likely winner **not** placed first, and a
   teaser device (a share bar with "? %" segments). Credit the data and mark
   the logos as trademarks in a micro-line.
4. The **answer lands at the end** (winner spotlight, ending-sequence).

## Decision rules

- No superlatives or outcome words in titles ("dominates", "never lost").
- Logo tiles are equal in size and treatment. Order them so the winner
  isn't the first read.
- The topic line stays on screen for the whole timeline.

## When not to use it

- **News-style updates** where the outcome *is* the news ("Bing hits a
  record"). There, lead with the fact.
