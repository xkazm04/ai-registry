# Concept/landing proposals: 4 seats per case

A data chart in a themed world can be correct and still look like every
other chart. In one studio's third triage round, the owner picked **a
concept/landing challenger over the provisional data-chart winner in all
four cases**. Each challenger had one non-obvious metaphor, artwork drawn
in code for each item, nested depth and a zoom lens. The approach comes
from the landing mode of the registry's `contest` skill. Its bar is
"visually dominant with nested layers and custom illustrative elements",
with creative and execution excellence, and drawn per-item art beats stock
icons in a nicer frame. Data charts still win when change over time is the
story, so the rule keeps both kinds.

## Procedure

1. **Plan 4 proposals per case**, on the same data, at the same moment,
   under the same hard rules (evidence card, top 10 with logos, type floor,
   safe box, motion budget, tags). Default to vertical 1080×1920.
2. **Seats A and B: concept/landing.** Each picks **one strong,
   non-obvious metaphor that fits the material**, and the two must differ.
   Then:
   - draw custom illustrative elements in code (SVG/CSS cut-paper, drawn
     objects, grain, paper shadows);
   - give each top-10 entity its own drawn object in its identity colour,
     plus its flag or logo, never a stock icon;
   - build nested layers back / mid / front;
   - go from overview to a **zoom lens** on the decisive gap or moment, then
     to detail;
   - state the claim at headline size, with one dominant element;
   - say "stylised" in the source line.
3. **Seats C and D: data-chart.** Use a case-native chart form, a
   leader-adaptive world and an evidence card (case-native-chart-form,
   leader-adaptive-theme, evidence-card-popup).
4. **Encode honestly inside the metaphor.** Use one common scale per frame,
   stated on screen ("ONE LAP = 90 WINS"), and size areas by square root.
   Draw projections and counterfactuals visibly different (scaffolding,
   dashed lines, a ghost) and tag them SPECULATIVE. Label any exaggerated
   tilt.
5. **Handle the two known risks:**
   - *Squeezed small entries:* add a legend strip in rank order, or a zoom
     lens on the crowded region. Move labels; never shrink them below the
     floor.
   - *Lost change over time:* add a time trace on the object (a sparkline on
     a device screen, a trail behind a moving object), or a burned/ghost
     memory device (a dashed "burned-away" earlier state).
6. **Give the pattern ledger as the floor.** Brief every seat with the
   ledger of past winners' transferable ideas and the instruction "do not
   copy these; find this case's own".
7. **Present the four side by side, labelled, with per-seat notes:**
   philosophy, how it stays readable, the wow moment, known limits, and
   parts worth fusing. The owner picks parts, not variants.
8. **After the verdict, update the ledger:** one idea per winner, with won
   and seen counts.

## Decision rules

- Two skins on one idea count as one seat. Each concept seat needs its own
  metaphor or information architecture.
- A metaphor that hides the story's move at its own moment loses, however
  pretty it is. Check that the decisive gap is visible without the lens;
  the lens adds detail.
- If the story is a trajectory, the concept seat must carry a time device,
  or a data-chart seat is likely to win.
- In motion, the object should grow, burn, swing or travel with the clock.
  The zoom lens opens at the event beat, and it counts toward the motion
  budget.

## When not to use it

- **A series that has already earned its form:** keep the form and vary
  the world.
- **A dense ranking where the order itself is the story** and every row
  must stay readable at phone size. There, two data-chart seats plus one
  concept seat is enough.
