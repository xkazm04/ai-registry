# Geometry transfers, tone does not — a round trip over 36 animation frames, August 2026

The replication-as-comprehension-test technique says a controlled vocabulary
earns its place by surviving a round trip. This is the run that produced it,
alongside the corpus-ingest measurements it depends on.

**The rig.** 36 frames sampled by scene detection from a ~7-minute animated
action compilation (third-party, held locally for evaluation only), annotated
by `qwen3.8:27b` on one 24 GB card. Replication generated by Flux 2 through
ComfyUI at 1280×720, then re-annotated by the same model. Craft fields only;
subject identity never crossed over.

## What sampling frequency should be

Measured before anything else, because the annotator's quality is irrelevant
if the frames are wrong. The source cuts at **38 cuts/min**:

| strategy | extracted | kept | distinct shots covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| scene detection | 276 | 235 | **136/162 (84%)** |
| every 5 s | 88 | 67 | 58/162 (36%) |
| every 10 s | 44 | 35 | 32/162 (20%) |
| every 20 s | 22 | 17 | 17/162 (10%) |

**Fixed intervals are not a tunable that reaches good coverage.** Five seconds
is four times the frames of twenty and still misses two thirds of the shots.
The sampling unit is the *shot*, because the shot is what a director composed;
seconds are an accident of runtime. Frames additionally pass keep/throw gates —
blank, no-detail, perceptual-duplicate — since a dissolve is two shots averaged
into a composition that was never on screen, and near-black fades contain no
cinematography at all.

## What the round trip found

Mean craft fidelity **77% (n=4)** — and the mean is the least useful number in
the run. The per-field transfer rate splits cleanly in two:

| transfers at 100% | partial | fails half the time |
|---|---|---|
| `camera_angle`, `depth_of_field`, `composition`, `lighting_direction`, `texture` | `shot_size` 75%, `lens_impression` 75% | `exposure`, `lighting_key`, `lighting_quality`, `contrast` — all 50% |

**Geometric vocabulary transfers; tonal vocabulary does not.** Where the camera
was, what was in focus, how the frame was composed, which direction the light
came from, what the surface looked like — all survive intact. How bright, how
deep the shadows, how hard the edges, how much separation — half of these are
lost in the round trip.

The tonal misses run in a consistent direction (`high-key -> low-key`,
`hard -> soft`), which makes them a **generator prior** rather than a schema
defect: Flux 2 has a house tonal style that overrides the brief. The fields
stay; the phrasing needs strengthening.

## Converging evidence: the same fields fail two independent tests

Before the round trip, two cheap probes needing no ground truth condemned the
same subset:

- **Definitional contradiction.** 22 of 36 frames came back both `high-key`
  *and* `high-contrast` — near-contradictory, since high-key means filled
  shadows.
- **A measurable proxy.** Mean luma of frames called high-key was 143 against
  68 for low-key: cleanly separated, proving the field tracked *brightness*
  rather than shadow depth. It was a brightness detector wearing a
  cinematography label.
- **Inertness.** `lens_impression` answered `wide-angle` 34 times out of 36,
  including on all 14 extreme close-ups, where nothing in the frame can reveal
  a lens register. A field that answers the same regardless of input carries no
  information.

Two independent instruments condemning the same fields is what promotes this
from a lead to a finding.

## The instruction-channel result

Attempting to fix those fields produced the run's most transferable result,
because it was a controlled single-variable test.

| | baseline | text in schema field descriptions | identical text in the main prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lighting_key` = high-key | 24 | 24 | **11** |
| high-key ∧ high-contrast | 22 | 23 | **7** |
| `lens_impression` = wide-angle | 34 | 34 | **0** |

**Corrective text placed in JSON-schema field descriptions was ignored
outright** — the output was byte-identical. The *same words* in the main prompt
moved both fields sharply and cut definitional contradictions by 68%. Schema
descriptions constrain structure; they do not instruct.

Steering is not the same as fixing, and this run does not claim it fixed
anything: `lens_impression` flipped from one constant (`wide-angle` 34/36) to
another (`indeterminate` 36/36), still inert, over-steered in the opposite
direction. An added observable, `true_black_share`, returned "none" for 35/36
dark frames while `lighting_key` split 24/11 *within* that group — incoherent,
so the observable did not rescue the label either. These two fields need an
adjudicator, not more prompt engineering.

## Ops: one card, two engines, explicit turns

The annotator holds **22.3 GB of 24 GB**. Alternating engines per frame killed
ComfyUI outright — closed socket, vanished process, no error raised. Two rules
came out of it, and both are now enforced by a preflight guard:

- **Batch by stage, not by item.** Generate every replica with one engine
  resident, then yield the card and annotate every replica. Per-item
  alternation pays N load/unload cycles and is what breaks.
- **An already-resident model satisfies its own budget.** Demanding 20 GB free
  before using a loaded 22 GB model can never pass — the memory in use *is* the
  model. A naive check reads that as a resource failure and halts a healthy run.

## Not established here

`n=4` for the round trip: the per-field split is a signal to act on, not a
published measurement. The round trip also cannot attribute a miss — generator
ignoring the brief and annotator misreading the replica are indistinguishable
from the score, and separating them needs a third party on the replica. That
adjudication pass is written but unrun, blocked on API credit.
