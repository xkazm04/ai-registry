# The animatability trial

A shortlist of character stills is a shortlist of pictures. Whether any of
them can be driven is a separate fact, and the only way to know it is to
drive them. The trial runs each shortlisted candidate through the cheapest
real drive before the owner commits, so that the choice is made between
stills that are known to move rather than discovered to fail after approval.

Beauty and drivability are **separate verdicts**, recorded separately. A
trial that collapses them into one score teaches the next round nothing about
which half failed.

## The cheapest real drive

Cheap means minutes per candidate, not a production rig. Real means it uses
the same kinds of operation the pipeline will use, not a judgement from
looking. Three probes, in rising cost:

1. **Layer split.** Separate the focal region, the speaking element, and the
   body from the ground — by matting, by a decomposition model, or by hand
   masks. Record what separated cleanly and what had to be invented behind
   an occlusion. This probe needs no face anywhere and runs on any form.
2. **Face and landmark detection.** Run the detector a neural driver depends
   on, then the dense landmark or mesh fit it needs after that. Record the
   detector's confidence and whether the mesh fit was accepted, separately —
   a detector can find a "face" in a box that the mesh stage then refuses.
3. **A short speech drive.** A few seconds of speech through whatever route
   the first two probes leave open, at the size the product will show it.
   Watch the speaking element, the gaze, and everything near them for
   tearing, smearing, or parts that move together that should not — and
   **measure that it moved at all**. A whole-frame driver meeting a non-human
   form often keeps the look perfectly and drops the speech: the render
   succeeds, the character is intact, and the speaking element never
   changes, because the driver's prior only knows what a human mouth does.
   Frame-to-frame difference inside the speaking element's region, against
   the same figure on a still known to move, separates "drove it" from
   "rendered it".

The animation note written with the prompt is the trial's hypothesis: it
said what would move on speech and blink and what the risk was. The trial
confirms or refutes those lines rather than inventing a new checklist.

## A rejection routes, it does not end

Per [refusal-is-a-state](../../../_laws.md#refusal-is-a-state), a detector
that finds no face has not declared the still undrivable. It has declared
the still unsuitable for the routes that need a face. A non-human being —
the very thing the rest of this subject argues for — will often be rejected
by every neural face detector, because those detectors learned on human
faces. That rejection is the expected result of a successful brief.

Such a still goes to a **parametric rig**: separated parts moved by rule —
a speaking element that scales or brightens with the speech envelope, a
focal region that dims on blink, a body that sways on an idle curve. A
parametric rig needs no face and costs little to run; on a non-human form
it is usually the base every viable route is built on, whether its motion
comes from rules or from a model. So the trial's
output per candidate is a route as well as a verdict:

- **Drivable by a neural face route** — detector and mesh accept it; the
  speech drive is clean.
- **Drivable by a parametric rig** — the split is clean, the slots are
  present, and the face route refuses it.
- **Drivable by a hybrid** — the parametric rig renders, and the motion it
  plays is predicted from the voice by a model rather than generated by
  rule. This is often the route that wins: rule-driven motion is cheap and
  can look mechanical on a form with large rigid parts, while the hybrid
  borrows natural timing without asking a face model to understand the
  being.
- **Drivable only after repair** — a named, small edit moves it into one of
  the above. The repair may be on the image (close the speaking element,
  clear an occlusion) or on the pipeline (a neutral fallback for the one
  input a stage could not derive from a non-human face).
- **Not drivable** — no route survives without regenerating the being.

Record every refusal with its stage and its number. A candidate that the
detector accepted at middling confidence and the mesh stage then refused is
a different finding from one the detector never saw, and the next brief
needs to know which.

## Drivable is not the same as moving well

The probes establish which routes can drive a still. They do not establish
which one moves it well, and that verdict belongs to the owner, made on the
same short lines rendered through every surviving route and shown without
labels, so that a route's reputation does not decide it. A parametric rig
that drives one candidate beautifully can look stiff on another whose parts
are large and rigid; the trial is per candidate and per route, never per
route alone.

## Reporting

Per [unmeasured-is-not-pass](../../../_laws.md#unmeasured-is-not-pass), a
candidate that was not run through a probe is **unmeasured** on that probe,
not passed. A trial report lists, per candidate: the beauty verdict (the
owner's), each probe's result or "not run", the route, and the repair list.
Where a route is chosen, record the cost of running it — compute per frame,
whether it needs dedicated graphics hardware — because two routes that both
move a still can differ by orders of magnitude in what they cost the
product every second it is on screen.

## Decision rules

- When the owner's favourite fails the face route, run the parametric
  probe before dropping it; the art may be right and the route wrong.
- When two routes both drive a candidate, prefer the one the product can
  afford continuously over the one that looks marginally better in a demo.
- When a candidate passes detection with low confidence, treat the route as
  fragile: it will hold on the still and fail on the frames the speech drive
  produces.
- When every shortlisted candidate fails every route, the slots in the brief
  were missing or ignored — go back to the brief, not to the model.
- When a trial result disagrees with the animation note, trust the trial and
  feed the disagreement into the next brief's slot wording.

## When not to use it

When the route is fixed and narrow — the product has one driver and only one
— the trial collapses to a pass/fail on that driver, and the route question
is moot. And where the still will never move, there is nothing to trial.
