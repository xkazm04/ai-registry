# Reject the default attractor

Every generative system has an answer it gives when the question is
underspecified, and for "draw the assistant" that answer is a realistic or
painted human portrait — a young, softly lit face, three-quarter, eyes warmly
on the viewer. The prompt writer has the same attractor: asked to imagine a
product's companion, most people imagine a person. When both the model and
the writer default the same way, the default arrives twice and looks like
consensus.

## Why the default is wrong for this job

The default is not ugly. It is often the most polished thing a round
produces, which is what makes it dangerous: it wins the beauty contest and
loses the job. Three reasons, and the brief should state them, because a
banned outcome without its reason gets reinvented one adjective away.

- **It is unrelated to the product.** A human portrait could front any
  product, so it says nothing about this one. A companion's first task is to
  look like it belongs to the thing it speaks for.
- **It sets the hardest animation bar.** Viewers read near-human faces with
  the full machinery of social perception, and every small error in motion —
  a mouth that opens a frame late, a blink without the lids folding right —
  reads as wrongness rather than as style. Behavioural realism matters more
  than photographic realism, and a photoreal still demands both. A stylised
  or non-human being earns forgiveness the human face never gets.
- **It carries an honesty cost.** A convincing person presenting software
  invites users to treat it as a person. A being that is plainly not human
  keeps the boundary visible without a disclaimer.

## Procedure

1. **Name the default as a banned outcome, with its reason.** "No human
   portrait, realistic or painted, of any age or gender — it ties the
   presence to nothing in the product and locks the animation to the
   uncanny bar." Put this at the top of the brief, not in a list of
   negatives at the bottom; a writer who reads the field first and the ban
   last has already drafted three humans.
2. **Brief a field, not a question.** Replace "design the assistant" with
   explicit axes, and ask that the set of candidates spread across them:
   - **Material** — what the being is made of: light, glass, paper, fibre,
     smoke, metal, liquid, a lattice, a field.
   - **Residual humanoid form** — from none (an object, an emblem, a
     creature) through a suggestion (a bust-like silhouette, a single gaze)
     to a strongly figurative but still non-human body. The point is that
     this is a dial the writer sets deliberately, not a starting point.
   - **How the namesake survives** — what the product is named for or
     stands for, carried as structure (the shape the being is built on)
     rather than costume (an emblem worn on a body).
3. **Require spread.** Ask for candidates that differ on at least two axes
   from each other, and say so: a set of ten that all sit at the same corner
   of the field is one candidate with ten renders.
4. **Grade the round before grading the images.** Before anyone picks a
   favourite, count how many candidates are the default in disguise — a
   human in armour, a human made of light, a face with a glowing visor.
   Those are the attractor with a costume change.
   Then name the **next-nearest attractors** in the ban as well. Banning the
   human portrait does not dissolve the pull; it moves it one step: the
   glowing hologram woman, the chrome robot, the cute mascot, the fantasy
   warrior. Each is the default with one property swapped, and a brief that
   bans only the first is answered by the second.
5. **Treat an all-default round as a brief failure.** If most of the round is
   the default, do not re-run the same brief with a sterner warning, and do
   not blame the writers or swap the model. Rewrite the brief: the ban was
   missing, unreasoned, buried, or the field was too narrow to escape.

## The default also lives in the tooling

An image-generation studio's automatic prompt enhancement, a "make it more
detailed" rewriter, a style preset tuned on popular outputs — each is a
second writer with the same attractor, inserted after yours. Asked to
improve a brief for a face made of fibres, it adds the literal creature; asked
to improve a hollow helm, it puts a face inside. When a non-default brief
comes back default, check whether anything between the prompt and the model
rewrote it, and turn that stage off for exploration rounds.

## The economics

Per [cost-per-usable-output](../../../_laws.md#cost-per-usable-output), a
round of polished default portraits is the expensive round: it costs as much
as any other, produces nothing adoptable, and consumes an owner review to
learn something the brief could have stated. The cheapest unit of quality in
this subject is a sentence in the brief, written before anything renders.

## Decision rules

- When independent writers or seats return the same kind of being, suspect
  the brief before suspecting any one of them; independent convergence on
  the default is the signature of an underspecified question.
- When the brief's own freedoms are human axes — age, ethnicity, wardrobe,
  hairstyle — the field it defines is a field of humans, and every writer
  will explore it faithfully. Audit the axes before the ban; a ban at the
  top contradicted by a freedoms list below loses.
- When a candidate is human-shaped but made of an unusual material, score it
  on the residual-form axis honestly; material novelty does not make a human
  portrait non-default.
- When the owner's rejection names a quality ("lacks relation to the
  product", "locked into mediocrity"), turn it into the reason clause of the
  ban in the next brief, verbatim if possible, so the next round inherits
  the verdict rather than rediscovering it.
- When a figurative form is genuinely right for the product, keep it — the
  rule bans the unchosen default, not the human dial position. Write down
  why it was chosen and what about it is not human.

## When not to use it

Where the product is a person — a real presenter, a named spokesperson, a
service whose whole promise is a human — a human likeness is not the
default, it is the brief, and the work belongs to identity and likeness
disciplines with their own consent rules. And where a style is already locked
and the being already exists, re-opening the field is regeneration of an
approved asset, not exploration.
