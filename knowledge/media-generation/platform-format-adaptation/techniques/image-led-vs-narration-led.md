# Image-led vs narration-led

Inside a single short format, the narration rate of successful work spreads by
**2×** — roughly 110–130 words per minute at one end, 220–250 at the other —
and the spread is not taste. It tracks a binary property of the clip's
construction:

- **Image-led** — the pictures make the argument; the narration only labels
  them ("this is not X — neither is this — and this is not X's storefront").
  Rate is slow because each image needs time to land, and the words must not
  outrun the eye.
- **Narration-led** — the words carry the whole argument; the visuals follow
  and illustrate. Rate is fast because the ear, not the eye, is doing the
  work, and dead air is the enemy.

Both modes produce excellent work. What fails is the mismatch: an image-led
plan narrated at narration-led density gives the viewer no time to read the
evidence; a narration-led script delivered at image-led pace pads a short
container with silence it cannot afford. Guessing the mode wrong by 2× is the
difference between a clip that breathes and one nobody can follow — and it is
the single largest pacing error available in short-form adaptation.

## The mode is derived, not chosen

The mature form of this technique removes the dial. The mode is decided by
**how much of the argument the visuals carry**, and that is knowable from the
script's own structure once each beat is tagged with its function. Beats whose
function is evidence, comparison, mechanism, metaphor, reveal, or state-change
are load-bearing for the pictures; beats whose function is texture are not.
When the load-bearing share of beats is high — somewhere around the majority,
a threshold that should be treated as assumed until calibrated against local
measurements — the clip is image-led; otherwise narration-led. A property that
the material decides, the tool computes and shows; only what remains is a
dial. Every dial exposed is a place the writer can silently be 2× wrong.

## Per-video mode, per-beat override

The mode is set **per video**, because rate is a delivery property — a
narrator who changes speed by 2× mid-clip sounds broken. But a per-beat
override exists, and it is a *writing* instruction, not a mode switch: marking
one beat "image-led" inside a narration-led video means exactly *write fewer
words here and let the picture land* — it is how a chart passage inside a
talking video gets room. The reverse override buys words for a beat whose
visual cannot be resolved. Neither override changes the video's rate; they
move words between beats under a constant total.

## The word budget is a range until the mode resolves

The script stage cannot commit to a single word count before the visual plan
exists, and must not pretend to. The honest artifact is a **budget range**
whose endpoints are the two modes' measured rates times the duration band
(for a 40–60-second clip, roughly 110–235 words), plus the declared or derived
mode, handed downstream to frame planning. The frame stage then holds the
script to the end of the range its mode selects. Emitting a single number
before the mode resolves is a parameter without a measurement behind it —
downstream stages will enforce it as if it were knowledge, which is precisely
how a template launders a guess into authority.

## Decision rules

- **When the argument can be photographed or charted**, prefer image-led and
  write labels, not prose. The test: could a viewer with the sound off follow
  the argument? If yes, every extra word is competing with the evidence.
- **When the argument is abstract or verbal**, go narration-led and make the
  visuals follow — and accept that the words must be twice as good, because
  they are carrying everything.
- **When the edit feels rushed**, check the mode before cutting content: a
  mismatch presents as a content-volume problem and tempts the wrong fix.
- **When adapting a parent video**, re-derive the mode for the clip — a
  narration-led essay often yields an image-led short (the derived clip keeps
  the parent's strongest visual evidence and sheds its verbal connective
  tissue), and inheriting the parent's rate is a silent 2× error.

## When not to use

The binary is a short-form instrument. Long-form work interleaves both modes
across chapters and can afford transitions between them; forcing a
twenty-minute essay into one mode throws away its best tool. And where a
format's rates have never been measured locally, use the spread as a warning
("expect up to 2×") rather than adopting anyone else's endpoint numbers —
rates are properties of a creator × format pair, and borrowing them across
either axis is an unmeasured claim.
