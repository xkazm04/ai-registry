# Theory-of-change framing

A theory of change is the causal claim underneath the whole proposal — *if
we do X, Y changes, because Z* — and its job is to keep the narrative from
going circular. The logic model is its tabular rendering; the two are
related, not interchangeable: the theory of change states *why* the causal
links hold, the logic model makes them concrete and assessable. A proposal
needs the theory threaded through its prose even when no funder asks for
either artifact by name.

## The red thread

The same causal spine must appear, recognizably, in three places: the need
section (the condition Y is currently failing), the design section (the
X built to move it, with the mechanism Z stated), and the evaluation
section (how movement in Y will be measured). When each element logically
produces the next without requiring the reader's charity, the proposal
holds together; when the outcomes promise change the activities were never
argued to cause, reviewers score the incoherence even if every individual
section reads well. Draft the one-sentence spine before any section, and
audit every section against it after.

## The standard logic model

The service-delivery rendering is four blocks, each earning its place:

- **INPUTS** — the resources brought: staff, funding, partnerships,
  facilities, expertise.
- **ACTIVITIES** — what is actually done: the intervention as delivered.
- **OUTPUTS** — the measurable *counts* of delivery: sessions held, people
  reached, materials produced.
- **OUTCOMES** — what *changes for the population*: the movement in the
  condition, stated as a named metric with a target.

The load-bearing distinction is outputs versus outcomes. Outputs measure
effort; outcomes measure change; the most common logic-model defect is an
outcomes block full of outputs — attendance and retention presented as the
change itself. Outcomes must be measurable — metric named, target stated —
*where verified data can ground the target*; against an empty fact set the
target is a placeholder for the writer, never an invented number. In prose
formats, render each block as a short labeled paragraph rather than a
table; the labels carry the structure.

## The advocacy variant

Systems-change work breaks the service logic model: its "outputs" are not
units of service, and forcing headcount blocks onto a campaign misstates
the work to the exact funder most alert to that misstatement. The variant
reshapes the blocks:

- **INPUTS** — resources *and relationships*: staff, members, coalition
  partners, standing with the constituency.
- **STRATEGIES** (replacing activities) — organizing, coalition-building,
  advocacy and policy tactics.
- **MILESTONES** (replacing outputs) — interim wins that evidence power
  building: turnout achieved, coalition size, hearings held, commitments
  secured.
- **CHANGE** (replacing outcomes) — the policy or systemic shift sought,
  stated as concretely and measurably *as the work allows*. Systems change
  legitimately resists point-target precision; a concrete description of
  the sought shift beats a fake numeric target.

The narrative around it follows the same inversion: name the systemic
problem and who holds power over it, the strategy, the constituency
mobilized, and the change sought — power and change, not headcount. Keep
any claims about lobbying activity within what the inputs actually
support; the boundary between advocacy and lobbying is compliance-bearing
for both funder and applicant.

## Decision rules

- When the funder's materials ask "how will you measure success" anywhere,
  build the logic model even if unrequested — it is the fastest way to
  make the evaluation section coherent with the design.
- When an outcomes block contains a count of things delivered, move it to
  outputs and ask what the delivery *changed*; if no answer exists, the
  model — not the wording — is what needs work.
- When the applicant does both service and advocacy work, pick the frame
  by *what this funder funds*, not by the organization's self-image.
- When a causal link needs a paragraph of explanation to seem plausible,
  treat that as a design smell: either evidence the link or shorten the
  chain.

## When not to use it

Arts applications generally do not want a logic model unless the funder
explicitly requires one — the causal spine there is the artistic vision and
its realization, and imposing an inputs/outputs apparatus reads as genre
misfit. Post-award reports invert the tense: the theory of change becomes
the frame for reporting what actually moved, variances honestly included.
