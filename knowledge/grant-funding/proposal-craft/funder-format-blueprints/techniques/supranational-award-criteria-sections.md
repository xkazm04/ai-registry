# Supranational award-criteria sections

The concern: supranational research and cooperation programmes evaluate
every proposal against a small set of **named award criteria** — canonically
*excellence*, *impact*, and *quality and efficiency of implementation* —
each scored independently on a fixed scale with its own pass threshold, and
with an overall threshold on the sum. A strong score on one criterion
cannot rescue a failing score on another. The application's structure must
therefore be exactly one section per criterion, each able to earn its
threshold standing alone, plus the budget table the form requires.

## The three sections and what each must contain

**Excellence.** State the objectives and make them measurable, specific,
and aligned to the call's own expected outcomes — evaluators check the
objectives against the call text, not against ambition in the abstract.
Describe the state of the art and how the project goes *credibly* beyond
it: the claim is a delta over named prior work, not novelty asserted.
Set out the soundness of the methodology, including whatever
cross-cutting dimensions the call requires (interdisciplinarity, gender
analysis, open-science practice). Weak excellence sections fail by
vagueness of objectives more than by weak science.

**Impact.** Describe the expected outcomes and the **credible pathways**
from project results to the wider scientific, economic, and societal
impacts the call's own destination names. Name who benefits and how — an
impact section without identified beneficiaries is a wish. Cover the
measures for communication, dissemination, and exploitation of results,
plus intellectual-property management, proportionate to the project. This
family is distinctive in scoring the *dissemination plan itself*: a
project that produces results nobody can find fails the criterion.

**Quality and efficiency of implementation.** A coherent work plan — work
packages, deliverables, milestones — with an honest timeline. The team or
consortium's capacity *and complementarity*: why these partners, and what
each uniquely contributes. Realistic resource allocation, and risk
management with named mitigations. Evaluators here are checking
deliverability, and an over-optimistic timeline reads as inexperience.

## Decision rules

- **One section per criterion, no merging.** Each criterion is thresholded
  independently, so each section must survive being read and scored alone.
  Cross-references are fine; dependence is not.
- **Mirror the call's sub-headings.** Where the template decomposes a
  criterion into sub-questions, answer them in the call's order and
  vocabulary — evaluators write their assessment per sub-question.
- **Objectives are measurable or they are not objectives.** Every
  objective carries a verifiable target; where the applicant has supplied
  no figure, emit a bracketed placeholder for them to fill, never an
  invented number — a fabricated baseline in a scored objective is the
  worst possible place for one.
- **Two-stage calls front-load excellence and impact.** Where a first
  stage evaluates only those two criteria at raised thresholds, the short
  first-stage document allocates its length accordingly; implementation
  detail waits for stage two.
- **Weighted criteria shift emphasis, not structure.** Innovation-flavored
  actions may weight impact more heavily in ranking; the response is a
  stronger impact section, never a restructured document.
- **Proportionate length per section.** These sections run long by nature
  (several hundred to under a thousand words each in a summary form; far
  more in a full template), but each has a band — an impact section that
  balloons past its band is usually restating excellence content.

## When not to use it

Only for calls in this family. Exporting the excellence/impact/
implementation frame to a small charitable trust or a community fund
misfires badly — the register reads as bureaucratic and the criteria do
not match what a trustee scores. Arts panels score a superficially similar
triad (work, audience, deliverability) but with different content and
register; use the arts form, not this one, even though the underlying
section keys may be shared.
