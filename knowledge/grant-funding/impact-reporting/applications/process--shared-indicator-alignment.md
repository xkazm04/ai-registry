# Process: the reporting-framework landscape an alignment decision chooses from (2024–2026)

A dated record of the named taxonomies, standards, and funder-practice shifts
a nonprofit aligning its indicators would actually encounter, as of
2026-08-20. The technique stays name-free on purpose; this is where the names
live.

## Shared indicator taxonomies and impact data standards

- **IRIS+** (Global Impact Investing Network) — the de facto indicator
  library for impact measurement: a catalog of generally accepted metrics
  with curated short-lists per impact theme. 2025 saw a concerted push for
  investors' general partners to adopt IRIS+ for portfolio-level reporting
  consistency — meaning grantees and investees aligned to it become
  aggregatable "for free". (iris.thegiin.org, accessed 2026-08-20)
- **Common Impact Data Standard** (Common Approach) — a linked-data standard
  built on the five dimensions of impact (what, who, how much, contribution,
  risk) plus "how"; explicitly designed to interoperate with the UN SDG
  Global Indicator Framework, IRIS+, and the IATI standard, so one mapping
  buys several. (commonapproach.org, accessed 2026-08-20)
- The surrounding method stack practitioners combine: a theory of change /
  logic model to name the outcome chain, a standard library for the
  indicators, the five dimensions to describe each outcome, and SROI where a
  dollar valuation is wanted. (sopact.com; ffwd.org, accessed 2026-08-20)

The technique's mapping rules land directly here: map to a library entry
only where it faithfully fits (rule 1), carry the entry's identifier and
version as provenance (rule 2), and keep the org's own outcome chain primary
with the standard as translation (rule 4).

## Trust-based philanthropy's effect on reporting expectations

- The Trust-Based Philanthropy Project's 2024 Grantmaker Survey found about
  six in ten grantmakers had adopted trust-based practices; its
  "simplify and streamline paperwork" practice explicitly targets grant
  reports. In 2025, 70+ funders signed a solidarity commitment extending
  these practices amid funding rollbacks. (trustbasedphilanthropy.org,
  accessed 2026-08-20)
- Concrete forms in the field: the Women's Foundation of Minnesota moved to
  one shared annual grant report for all grantee-partners (2025), and a
  sector workgroup (Kimber & Vance) developed oral/alternative reporting in
  place of written forms. (wfmn.org, accessed 2026-08-20)
- Implication for the technique: lighter formats do not lighten measurement.
  A conversational or single-page report carries only a few figures, so the
  award-time indicator choice does more of the work, not less — the golden
  path's reading of the same shift.

## Federal financial-reporting windows (the grace-window ground truth)

- Interim **SF-425** Federal Financial Reports are commonly due **~30 days**
  after the reporting period ends (quarterly/semiannual), with annual
  filings commonly at ~90 days — exact dates set per award in the notice of
  award. (acf.gov; cops.usdoj.gov SF-425 guides, accessed 2026-08-20)
- **2 CFR 200.344 (closeout)**: all final reports — financial and
  performance — due no later than **120 calendar days** after the period of
  performance ends (90 for subrecipients). (ecfr.gov, accessed 2026-08-20)
- Agency variation is real: NIH final progress reports at 30 days, NSF final
  project reports at 120 — the derivation technique's "the stated deadline
  wins, always" rule exists precisely because the class-level defaults have
  this much spread. (grantsights.com; ras.mit.edu, accessed 2026-08-20)
- The **2024 Uniform Guidance revision** (effective for awards on/after
  2024-10-01) raised the single-audit threshold to $1,000,000, raised
  equipment/supply thresholds to $10,000, and rewrote sections in plain
  language with an explicit burden-reduction goal — the federal side moving,
  more slowly, in the same lighter-touch direction as trust-based funders.
  (eac.gov; bdo.com, accessed 2026-08-20)
