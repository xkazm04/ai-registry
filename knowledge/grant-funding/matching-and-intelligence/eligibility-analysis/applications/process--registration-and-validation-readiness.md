# Process: registration readiness under the 2024–2026 US and EU regimes

A dated snapshot (researched 2026-08-20) of how the technique's generic
claims cash out in the two regimes it was forged against, with the names and
numbers the upper layers deliberately omit.

## US federal: SAM.gov / UEI

- The identifier is the 12-character **Unique Entity ID (UEI)**, issued in
  SAM.gov; it replaced the Dun & Bradstreet DUNS number on **2022-04-04**.
- A **full, active SAM.gov registration is required at time of application
  and at time of award**, and UEI deactivation automatically blocks
  Grants.gov submissions — the "active status is the gate, lapse flips it
  without a profile edit" claim, verbatim. Registration must be **renewed
  annually**.
- Lead-time reality vs marketing figure: SAM.gov's own documentation cites
  3–5 business days; practitioner guidance for 2025–2026 measures **10–14
  business days typical and 6–8 weeks in bad cases** (IRS validation,
  documentation issues, backlog), and NIH tells applicants to allow at
  least 10 business days before pleading a late submission. This is the
  technique's "realistic lead time, sourced from field practice" datum.
- SAM.gov also hosts the government-wide **exclusions (debarment/suspension)
  list** — the one hard-fail condition in this gate.

## EU: Funding & Tenders Portal — PIC, CVS, LEAR

- To apply, an organisation self-registers in the **Participant Register**
  for a **PIC (Participant Identification Code)**; a *self-declared,
  unvalidated* PIC is sufficient at proposal stage.
- Validation is staged on success: only when a proposal succeeds does the
  **Central Validation Service (CVS)** validate legal existence — Legal
  Entity Form, VAT document, registration extract — and require appointment
  of a **LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative)**, both of which must
  be in place before the grant agreement can be signed. Every consortium
  beneficiary needs its own validated PIC and active LEAR.
- This is the staging rule (procedure step 4) realized: the regime itself
  defers heavy verification, so a matching product that demands registry
  extracts at onboarding is stricter than the funder.

## The moving thresholds the capacity gate reads (same regulatory wave)

- The 2024 revision of **2 CFR 200** (US Uniform Guidance), effective for
  fiscal periods beginning on or after **2024-10-01**, raised the **single
  audit threshold from $750,000 to $1,000,000** of federal awards expended
  (2 CFR 200.501) and the **de minimis indirect cost rate from 10% to 15%**
  of modified total direct costs (2 CFR 200.414(f)) — the "audit threshold
  as dated jurisdiction data" the capacity technique points at.
- On **2026-05-29** OMB published a proposed rule reworking 2 CFR 200 into a
  binding "Uniform Grant Regulation", codifying 2025 executive orders
  (notably EO 14332, 2025-08-07, on federal grantmaking oversight); comment
  period closed 2026-07-13. As of this snapshot it is a **proposal** — the
  $1M threshold and 15% de minimis stand unchanged — but it is exactly why
  these figures live in this application layer and not in a technique.
