# The supranational rubric as currently published (process)

A dated verification of the technique against the family's canonical
instance — the EU's Horizon Europe programme and its planned successor —
using the funder's own published evaluation documents. Applications may
name programmes; the technique stays generic because, as verified here,
the generic claims are exactly what the named documents say.

## The rubric, per the 2026–2027 cycle documents

The Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026–2027 General Annexes (Annex 15,
draft published by the European Commission) restate the scoring mechanics
the technique describes: each of the three award criteria — *Excellence*,
*Impact*, *Quality and efficiency of the implementation* — is **scored
out of 5, with a per-criterion threshold of 3 and an overall threshold of
10** on the sum. The independence claim is structural in those numbers: a
5 / 5 / 2 proposal fails outright at 12 points, while three bare-pass 3s
sum to 9 and fail the overall threshold — so a proposal must both pass
every criterion alone *and* be strong somewhere. The standard evaluation
form for RIA/IA actions (v3.0, 28 February 2025) has evaluators write
their assessment per criterion, in the criterion's own sub-structure —
the technique's "each section must survive being read and scored alone"
is a description of how the score sheet is physically filled in.

Two of the technique's decision rules are also verbatim programme
mechanics: **weighted criteria shift emphasis, not structure** (for
Innovation Actions, Impact carries a weight of 1.5 — but only when
ranking, never in the threshold test), and **two-stage calls front-load
excellence and impact** (first stages evaluate only those two criteria,
at raised thresholds, per the same General Annexes).

## The successor programme: the criteria set codified, not revised

The framework programme planned for 2028–2034 (FP10, retaining the
Horizon Europe name, proposed at EUR 175 billion) settles the question
this subject's counter-evidence lane asked. **Article 25 of the proposed
FP10 regulation codifies excellence, impact and implementation as the
only award criteria** — the successor does not redesign the rubric, it
writes the existing one into the regulation. European Parliament draft
reports on the proposal (Ehler/Repasi, March 2026) debate governance,
budget and council structure, not the award-criteria set; weighting
details remain under member-state discussion, which is emphasis, not
structure. A blueprint built on the three-criterion form is therefore an
investment that survives the programme boundary: the same section keys,
guidance skeleton and independence discipline carry into the next
seven-year cycle unchanged.

## What this verification means for the blueprint

Nothing in the current documents required a change to the technique —
verified-and-untouched is the result, and this file is its record. The
one durable lesson is meta: the family publishes its rubric mechanics in
a general annex that is reissued per work-programme cycle, so the
blueprint's review cadence is *per cycle, against the annex*, not "when
something seems to have changed".
