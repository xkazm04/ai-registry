# Economy, institution, slang and code per region

The named concern: each region of a campaign gets a culture built from four fields, derived
in a fixed order — what it lives on, the one institution that organises that living, the words
its people coined, and the moral code they hold. The order is the technique. A region whose
fields are chosen independently for mood is a palette with a vocabulary; a region whose fields
are derived from its economy can answer questions the bible never asked.

## The four fields, in order

**Material conditions, first and unwritten.** Before the first field, read what the region is
made of: climate, ground, what is lying around, what kills people there. Usually the art
direction has already decided this, and the technique's first act is to take those choices
seriously as causes. Soot over burned yards, a salt flat that blinds at noon, a pass with too
little air — each of these makes some work possible and other work impossible, and that is
where the economy comes from.

**Economy.** Write what the region extracts, makes, moves or sells, and what it cannot produce
and must bring in. One sentence each. The import matters as much as the export, because
dependence is where the region's politics come from: a region that makes everything it needs
has no reason to deal with anyone, including the player. State the economy as work people do
with their hands, not as an abstraction — divers bring up hull plate, not "the region has a
salvage economy" — because the rest of the fields are derived from the work.

**Institution.** Name one, and only one, institution that organises the economy: who licenses
the work, who weighs and prices the product, who keeps the books, who settles disputes, who
buries the dead. A guild, a weigh-house, a chapel, a pit, a toll gate, a market court, a family.
One is a constraint, not a limit of imagination: a region with five institutions is a
city-state the campaign has no time to show, and the player will meet none of them twice. The
institution is where the player meets the region's rules in person, so it should own a place
on the map, a face in the cast and one recurring object — a board of tags, a roll call, a log,
a wall of seized names — that appears often enough for the player to read changes in its state.
The object is the institution's memory, and it is the cheapest residue the region has.

**Slang.** Coin a small set of words — three to six is enough to carry a region — and derive
each from the work, the institution or the danger. People name what they handle daily, what
they fear and what they despise: the unit they are paid in, the tool that kills them, the
person who cheats, the thing that cannot be salvaged. A coined word earns its place when it is
used in play, by more than one speaker, with its meaning recoverable from context the first
time it appears. Slang that is only in the bible is a declared input nothing reads. Slang
also has a gradient across a map with a centre of power: at the working edge it is blunt and
names the thing — paid out, broke, a lie; at the centre it turns to euphemism, because the
people who profit need words that hide what they do — a seizure called a retirement, a death
called a settlement, people called equity. Write the centre's vocabulary as the edge's
vocabulary laundered, and the player hears the class structure in the words.

**Code.** State the moral the economy would teach: what is shameful here, what is admired, what
is owed. Derive it from what the economy punishes. Where the necessity is scarce, waste is the
sin; where everyone works on credit, a broken word is; where the work kills, cowardice is
mocked and caution is quietly respected, and the gap between those two is a story. Write the
code as one sentence a local would say, in the region's own words, using its slang.

## Decision rules

- **When a field cannot be derived from the economy, change the field or change the
  economy**, because a field that stands alone generates nothing a writer can reason from.
- **When two regions have the same economy, merge them or differentiate the economy first**;
  differentiating only the slang and the code produces two costumes on one culture.
- **When a region has more than one institution in the bible, keep the one the player will
  meet twice** and fold the others into it or into background.
- **When a coined word appears in only one line, cut it or give it a second speaker.** A word
  used once is noise; a word used by several people is a culture.
- **When a coined word needs a gloss, gloss it with behaviour, not definition** — the first use
  should make the meaning recoverable from what happens next.
- **When the text surface is short — a three-line card, a bark — spend at most one coined word
  per unit**, because two unknown words in one short line are a riddle, not a culture.
- **When a regional character joins the player, keep their slang and their code**, turned
  toward the new side; a convert who starts talking like the protagonist's crew has stopped
  being from anywhere.
- **When the code sounds like a motto, rewrite it as something a local would say to a
  newcomer who just broke it**, because a code is learned at the moment of violation.
- **When writing the regions, write them side by side**, as one table read across, so
  overlaps between regions are visible at once rather than discovered in voice recording.

## The field nothing reads

Every field in the bible must have a reader in play: a line, a card, a price, a screen, a
place. The economy is read by the prices and the scenery; the institution by a location, a
character and its recurring object; the slang by lines; the code by a scene in which someone
is judged by it, and — where the region's power is fought before it is won — by that power's
taunts before the turn and its loyalty lines after, which is the code heard from both sides. A
beautifully derived field that no card, screen or line ever reaches is
[declaring an input is not consuming it](../../../../_laws.md#declaring-an-input-is-not-consuming-it)
in narrative form: it reads as authored and governs nothing. Audit the bible in both
directions — fields with no reader in play, and lines in play that reference a fact the bible
does not hold.

## When not to use this

A campaign whose regions are deliberately one culture — a single city's districts, a
pilgrimage through one faith's land — should not invent four cultures to fill a template;
there the fields describe the one culture and the regions vary only in which of its sides they
show. And a short game with one or two regions needs the economy and the code, but may let the
institution and the slang be implicit rather than spend lines on them.

## Evidence status

The derivation chain from material conditions to economy to custom to slang rests on primary
sources — a novel's ecology read closely, and game writers' own statements that politics
should be reached through material conditions (high). One institution and a memory object per
region rest on a showrunner's account of building a town through its civic institutions and
rituals (high). The one-word-per-card cap, the slang gradient from edge to centre, and the
reader for each field are the research's own design inference and practitioner judgement
(low to medium). The convert keeping his culture rests on secondary criticism of a film
(medium-high). None of it has been tested in a played game.
