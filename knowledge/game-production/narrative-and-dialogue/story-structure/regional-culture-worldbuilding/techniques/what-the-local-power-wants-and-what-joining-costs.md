# What the local power wants, and what joining costs

The named concern: every region has a local power with a want of its own, lives under a
specific hold of the central power, and pays a visible price for siding with the player. These
three facts are written together because each defines the others: the hold is what stands
between the local power and its want, and the price of joining is the hold tightening.

## The local power's want

The local power is whoever runs the region day to day — a boss, a council, a matriarch, a
foreman, a champion. Give them a want that is neither the protagonist's nor the central
power's: to keep the region's young from being taken, to get their own product to market
without the toll, to win back a title their family lost, to be left alone. The want must be
**concrete and local**, something the region's economy makes desirable, so that the boss
belongs to the region rather than being a generic gatekeeper with the region's colours.

A local power whose only want is to stop the player is a level boss with dialogue. A local
power whose want the player could plausibly serve is a negotiation, and a negotiation is
where a region's culture becomes play.

## The central hold

Write how, specifically, the central power holds this region: a tithe on the product, a
license to work, a monopoly on a necessity the region cannot make, a debt, a garrison, a
hostage — a son in the capital's school, a champion owned by the league. The hold should
differ from region to region, because a single hold repeated (the central power taxes
everyone) makes the regions politically identical however different their cultures. And the
hold should touch the local power's want: it is what makes the want unattainable now.

## The price of joining

When the player wins the region — beats its champion, earns its trust, recruits its people —
the central power responds, and the region pays. Write the price before writing the victory,
in the region's own currency and on the hold already established: the tithe doubles, the
license is revoked, the necessity is cut off, the debt is called, the hostage is moved. The
local power may also pay inside the region, losing face with its own people for siding with an
outsider. Show the price on screen, at a moment the player sees, and ideally in a later region
too, so the coalition the player is building has a running cost.

The sharpest price is not a sum but the thing the region's own code exists to protect. A pass
whose whole moral is neutrality pays by taking a side; a keeper of the record pays by producing
the honest record and being erased from it; a foreman whose code is that no one is left behind
pays in his crew's jobs. A price of that shape costs the region a piece of who it is, and it
ties the culture fields to the politics so tightly that neither can be rewritten alone.

The price also sets the terms of joining. A local power does not join because it was beaten;
it joins when the player has done something that protects its want against the price — the
receipts that keep the crew safe, the papers that make the creditor's move public. Defeat
opens the negotiation, and terms close it.

The price is what turns the late-campaign coalition from a roll call into a debt the player
owes. When the regions turn up for the finale, the player knows what each paid to be there.

## Decision rules

- **When the local power wants only to stop the player, give them a want of their own** that
  the region's economy explains and the player could serve.
- **When two regions share the same central hold, differentiate the hold** before
  differentiating anything else; the hold is the region's politics.
- **When winning a region costs the region nothing, write the price first** and design the
  victory scene to lead into it.
- **When the price is paid off screen or in text only, move it into a scene or a screen the
  player handles** — a price on a later receipt, an empty seat, a closed shop.
- **When a price is named, measure it against the region that did not join** — a later
  region, or the same region's own schedule without the player. A loss the region's ordinary
  progression would have produced anyway is not a price, however it is voiced.
- **When a balance rule forbids allies any mechanical power, put the price on the region's
  side**, not the player's: the region's own purse, car, stock or road can shrink without
  adding a point of power to the player; and a payout voiced as the region's loss — the money
  that would have bought what the region now goes without — turns the reward and the price
  into one transfer.
- **When every region pays the same kind of price, vary it**; a coalition built from identical
  sacrifices reads as a mechanic, not as people.
- **When the price is a sum, look for the version of it that costs the region's code** — the
  neutrality, the record, the crew — and prefer that one.
- **When the local power joins the moment it is beaten, give it terms** the player has to meet
  first; the refusal-and-turn arc itself is character work owned elsewhere, but the terms are
  the region's.
- **When the central power is itself a region's local power, it never joins**; its want is the
  hold, and the campaign ends it rather than wins it.
- **When the price would make the region refuse to join, let some region refuse**, or delay,
  or join on terms; a campaign where every power joins the player on the same beat is a
  campaign where joining was never a choice.

## When not to use this

A campaign in which the player does not win, recruit or ally with regions — a pure tour, a
lone escape — needs the local power's want but not the price of joining; invent a coalition
only if the story's end uses one. And when the central power is absent or the map has no
centre, the hold is replaced by the regions' holds on each other, which is the same technique
pointed sideways.

## Evidence status

That allies recruited for a campaign's finale must matter to it, and that a turned enemy pays
for the turn, rest on a game writer's statement about building a campaign around its final
mission (high) and on secondary criticism of a redemption arc (medium). The local power's own
want, the per-region hold and the price paid in the region's own code are practitioner
judgement and the research's own design inference (low to medium); the research's table of
regional bosses applies them but cites no source for the table itself. The counterfactual test and the
region-side price come from one shipped campaign's data read on 2026-10-10, where the only
price carried by data was the ordinary schedule every rival follows (one case, design
inference). None of it has been tested in a played game.
