# Foreshadow the loss on the ledger

The named concern: plant the coming loss where the player reads every time, early enough and
plainly enough that when it happens they recognise it, and quietly enough that they do not
treat it as a puzzle to avoid. A loss without a plant is a twist and is judged as one — was it
fair. A loss with a plant is a consequence and is judged differently — I should have seen it.
The second judgment keeps the player's anger on the antagonist. The target state is the one a
well-planted betrayal reaches on screen: surprising the first time, inevitable afterwards.

## Why the ledger and not a scene

A warning in a scene is seen once by the players who watched it. A line on the ledger is seen
after every event by every player, and its repetition is what turns a fact into a dread. The
ledger also has the right register for a plant: a clause, a figure, an entry. A plant that looks
like bookkeeping does not announce itself as foreshadowing, which is what lets the player
overlook it the first time and find it the second.

## The four placements

**The plant.** The collateral, the clause, the creditor's interest in the object — on the ledger
from the event where the deal is made, stated as a term, not a threat. "Collateral: the car."

**The double reading.** Every appearance of the taker carries one detail that reads one way now
and another after the take. He compliments the car, not the driver. He knows its chassis number.
His garage has an empty bay with the player's plate already painted on the floor. Seen the first
time, he is generous; in hindsight, he was foreclosing. This is the placement that makes the take
feel inevitable without making it predictable.

**The witnessed bite.** Before the leash takes from the player, it takes from someone the player
knows — a lien on a car another character cares about. The player has now seen the mechanism
work, and their own loss arrives as a promise kept.

**The recognition.** When the take happens, the scene or the ledger returns to the plant — the
same words, now meaning the opposite. A motif that recurs with the same meaning is a wink; one
whose meaning has flipped is an arc, and the flip is the payoff.

## Decision rules

- **Plant at least one act before the take**, so the line has been seen many times as an
  ordinary term before it is called. A plant one event before the payoff is a warning, and a
  warning invites the player to prevent what the story will not let them prevent.
- **Escalate once, with the player's success, never with a timer.** The collateral line acquires
  a valuation after the car's big win; the creditor notes it. An escalation that follows from
  winning makes the take the price of the player's competence; one that follows from the calendar
  makes it the designer's schedule.
- **When the plant implies the loss is avoidable, make it avoidable or reword it.** A band that
  visibly approaches "called" promises a rule — pay down and you are safe. Taking the object after
  the player has paid down breaks that rule, and reads as cheating unless the story shows the
  creditor breaking it. Plant the creditor's power and appetite, not a condition the player can
  meet.
- **Use the same words for the plant and the recognition.** A paraphrase loses the click.
- **Do not plant more than one major loss at a time.** Two dreads compete, and the player
  discounts both.
- **Test the plant by prediction.** In playtest, ask before the take what the player thinks will
  happen. Prediction plus dread is the goal; no prediction means the plant is too quiet;
  certainty plus attempts to avoid it means it is too loud.

## When not to use this

When the loss is meant as a genuine shock — an event the fiction itself could not foresee — a
plant would misrepresent it, and the craft is instead to make the shock originate plainly
outside the player's play. When the game has no recurring post-event surface, the plant moves to
whatever the player does read repeatedly; planting in a once-seen scene and calling it
foreshadowing overstates what the player was told.

## Evidence status

The surprising-then-inevitable plant rests on a primary statement by a series writer about
planting a betrayal in every earlier scene (high in the source dossier). The motif that flips
its meaning rests on on-screen evidence and a creator's warning against callbacks that change
nothing (high to medium). The witnessed bite is the dossier's own generalisation (medium), and
the double-reading details and the prediction test are dossier proposals for one unplayed
design. The ledger as the planting surface is design inference. None of this has been tested in
a played game for this subject yet.
