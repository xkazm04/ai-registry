# Locking Death Ride's final card from three research dossiers

Death Ride is a top-down vehicular combat racer for Fire TV with a five-region
debt-and-defection campaign (the player races for the league boss Marrow, has their car
seized, turns regional bosses into allies, and ends in a fight to the death against Marrow
in a rig built by the young Mechanic). Its narrative lead commissioned four research
dossiers on 2026-10-04, held in the `firetv-deathride` tree under
`docs/narrative/research/`; every anchor below is root-relative to that tree
(`C:\Users\kazda\kiro\firetv-deathride`). The game has not been played by anyone. What
follows is a plan derived from research, not a practice proven in play, and nothing here
claims the card works.

## The source practice, confirmed

The prestige-series dossier (R1) states the rule this technique carries:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:50 "write the final scene between the central pair before anything else and treat it as fixed"`.
Its evidence is two showrunners speaking directly — Amanda Overton describing the last scene
of Arcane's central pair, written six years before the final season and never changed,
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:54 "It has stayed our North star, our guiding light for the entire series"`,
and Shinichirō Watanabe saying that every Cowboy Bebop episode featuring Vicious is tied to
the ending — rated `docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:58 "High for Arcane and Bebop."`
Sources: Screen Rant red-carpet interview (https://screenrant.com/arcane-season-2-cast-red-carpet-interview/),
Popverse (https://www.thepopverse.com/tv-netflix-arcane-was-always-going-to-be-the-last-season-2-5-seasons-league-of-legends-rumors),
Bebop Attic transcript (https://bebopattic.weebly.com/watanabe-interviewhouston.html).

The Death Ride use is R1's first implication:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:258 "Before region 1 is scripted, write the last three-line card of the finale"`,
followed by `docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:258 "Freeze it."` — every
later card, boss and motif is checked against it. "Card" is literal here: Death Ride's
story is told in three-line story cards over picture, so the final card is a real screen.

Web hardening agreed with the dossier and added the reverse-planning habit that makes a lock
productive: setup and payoff is "almost always written in reverse", the payoff first and
then a search back through the story for where to plant it
(https://scriptmag.com/features/column-d-setup-and-payoff-writing-foreshadowing-script;
https://www.shorescripts.com/working-backwards/; https://nofilmschool.com/plant-and-payoff-in-screenwriting).

## Upward lessons the dossiers gave the technique

**The card carries a price and one survivor.** R1's ending pattern —
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:158 "That cost must be the direct price of the victory, not an unrelated tragedy."`
(Tartakovsky on Samurai Jack, Yee on Arcane, via Wikipedia and Collider:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai_Jack_season_5, https://collider.com/arcane-jinx-dead-explained-alex-yee-amanda-overton/) —
became the price-and-surviving-image property. Death Ride's proposal is
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:357 "The victory should cost the player what they started racing for"`,
with the surviving image being the Mechanic's rough rig still running, and
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:357 "No sequel hook that undoes the cost"`.

**The decisive act belongs to the player.** The game-narrative dossier (R2) states
`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:207 "the decisive act must be performed by the player"`,
with the evidence that Hades II's ending was changed after release —
`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:210 "Supergiant revised it so she"` makes
the call herself (Aftermath interview, https://aftermath.site/hades-2-supergiant-narrative-interview-ending-changes/).
That case taught the technique two things at once: the player-verb rule, and that a locked
ending can and sometimes must change, which is why the technique writes a change procedure
instead of a vow.

**The card returns.** The wasteland dossier (R4) synthesises:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:119 "end where the journey began, changed by the people gathered on the way, with a visible cost and one concrete image of the new order"`.
Its finale option for Death Ride makes the rhyme concrete — open on a card at the Crown,
where the debt is signed, and close there:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:147 "The finale returns to the same place, stands empty."`
A second option leaves the restored car with one scar, because
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:153 "an ending lands when something was paid"`.
R4 rates its own ending synthesis low to medium:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:230 "the ending synthesis (P28)"` is
listed among the claims that are the dossier's own.

## Deviations: where the dossiers fall short of the standard

**The lock is stated as absolute.** R1 says
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:50 "Everything else may be rewritten; that scene may not."`
The technique holds the stricter and more honest rule: the card may change, through a
re-plan that re-derives every plant. R2's own Hades II case contradicts the absolute form.

**The card is placed at the climax.** R1's Death Ride card shows the Mechanic, the rig and
Marrow at the moment the fight resolves. The technique places the card after the climax — the
quiet frame in which its meaning settles — so the card should show what the resolution
left, not the resolution itself. R4's empty-stands return and R1's surviving-rig image both
point at the post-climax frame; the plan should pick that one.

**Three candidates were not written.** Each dossier proposes one finale direction (R4 offers
several frame changes, but they elaborate one ending rather than competing with it). The
technique's first step — three candidate cards, chosen by how far back their prerequisites
reach — has not been run for Death Ride.

## What this does not prove

That the locked card moves anyone. The dossiers' D-items and R4's options are, in their own
words, `docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:367 "are design proposals inferred from the patterns, not facts"`,
and none has met a player. The lock is the necessary first rung; a cold read of the written
campaign and a playtest of the finale are the rungs above it, and neither has happened.
