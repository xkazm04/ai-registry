# The twenty-second story budget

The named concern: each event in a race campaign carries about twenty seconds of story before
the grid and about twenty seconds after the flag, stated in seconds with a basis, enforced on
every free beat and overrun only by fixed spine beats. The barks inside the race are a separate
allowance of short lines and are not charged to either side.

## Why twenty seconds

An event lasts two to four minutes, and the menus, shop and ledger around it add more. Forty
seconds of story per event therefore holds story to somewhere between a tenth and a fifth of
the session, depending on how much else the session contains. The figure is chosen, not
measured: nobody has established a ratio at which players stop reading, and a team that
quotes one should say whose session it was computed over. What is established well enough to
design against is the direction of the failure. Past some length the words compete with the
race for the player's attention and the race wins; the skip habit forms within a few events
and, once formed, it is applied to every later card, including the ones that mattered. The
budget is therefore not a measure of how much story the campaign can afford; it is a bet on
how much a player will actually receive per event, and anything beyond it is written for
nobody.

The budget shapes the writing as well as capping it
([a-budget-shapes-the-output](../../../../_laws.md#a-budget-shapes-the-output)). A writer, or a
generator, handed two minutes of allowance produces two minutes of text, and the extra is
almost always exposition the rule variant was already carrying. Handed twenty seconds, the same
writer produces a stake and a name, which is what the card is for.

## The basis

Twenty seconds means nothing until it says what is counted
([a-number-carries-its-unit-and-basis](../../../../_laws.md#a-number-carries-its-unit-and-basis)).
The budget counts from the card's first frame to the first accepted input on the grid, and from
the finish line to the player's first menu input, at a stated reading rate for text or the
recorded length for voice, on the display the game ships on and at the distance it is viewed
from. A television read from across a room shows fewer words per screen than a monitor, so
the same twenty seconds carries fewer words on it, and a budget stated in words alone would
silently overrun. Where text and voice both run, the longer of the two is counted, not the sum
and not the shorter.

Converted to words, twenty seconds at a comfortable on-screen reading rate is a few dozen words,
which is one image, a name, a stake and one rule; that conversion is a starting point to be
checked against the shipped display, not a constant.

## Skipping and the budget

Every story surface is skippable, and the skip skips only words. The rule variant still
applies and is still shown on the heads-up display; the post-event state is still written; the
next card still reads it. A campaign whose skip also skips the state has made its story
optional in the worst sense, because the player who skips is then playing a different campaign
from the one that was written.

## Procedure

1. State the budget per event class: free beats at about twenty seconds each side, fixed spine
   beats at a stated larger figure, free-play events at zero.
2. State the basis: what starts and stops the count, the reading rate, the display and viewing
   distance.
3. Hand the budget to whoever writes the card and the outcome, as the intended length rather
   than a ceiling.
4. Time every card and outcome on the shipped display, and record the figure beside the event.
5. Flag any free beat over budget, and cut words before cutting any of the four parts.

## Decision rules

- **When a card overruns, cut exposition the variant already carries first**, because the player
  will learn it from the race.
- **When a fixed beat needs a minute, give it the minute and spend the saving elsewhere**; the
  spine is few precisely so that it can afford this.
- **When players skip a card that is within budget, the card is wrong, not long**: check that it
  names a stake the player cares about.
- **When the budget is handed to a generator, derive each part's share from the event's**
  rather than repeating the event's allowance for the card and again for the outcome.

## When not to use this

A campaign whose players come for the story, a narrative game that happens to use racing as a
verb, sets its own ratio; the budget is calibrated for players who came to race. A first event
that teaches the game may run longer, provided the extra time is teaching rather than story.

## Evidence status

The twenty-second figure is a design proposal in the research this subject distils, with no
source behind the number itself, and the ratio the research attaches to it, story near a
tenth of session time, is stated without the session length that would make it arithmetic.
The supporting direction is better grounded: arcade games that keep story to brief framing
between levels are described that way by their developers through an encyclopedia summary,
medium confidence; players modding a racing game to silence its repetitive commentary comes
from community sources, low. The basis rule, counting on the shipped display and taking the
longer of text and voice, is inference. No card has been timed against a player, and none of
this has been tested in a played race campaign; the figure is a starting bet to be measured,
not a finding.
