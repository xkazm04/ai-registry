# Rule variants carry the story

The named concern: the content of a race event's story beat is a change to what the rules or
the controls do, and the words around it are a label. An event whose stakes live only in text
carries no story for anyone who skipped the text, which after the first few events is nearly
everyone.

## The deletion test

Remove the card and the barks and play the event. If it plays exactly like an unmodified race,
the beat was a scene attached to a race, not a beat carried by one. The test is cheap, it can
be run on a design sheet before anything is built, and it is the single most reliable way to
find the events in a campaign that only *say* something. A card that declares a crushing debt,
with nothing in the event costing more because of it, is a declared input no rule consumes
([declaring-an-input-is-not-consuming-it](../../../../_laws.md#declaring-an-input-is-not-consuming-it)).

## From idea to variant

The variant is the literal enactment of the idea, chosen so that the player's hands feel the
proposition before the words name it. Debt becomes repairs the player cannot afford, so damage
carries into the next event. Protection becomes a fragile passenger or cargo whose integrity is
a second health bar that fails the event when it empties. Betrayal becomes an ally's car whose
behaviour switches from blocking for the player to ramming them. Injury becomes steering that
pulls to one side. Exile becomes a start from the back of the grid with no weapons fitted. The
direction of derivation matters: start from the idea and ask what the player would have to do
if it were true. Starting from an existing modifier and writing a reason for it produces the
gimmick with a pretext that players see through.

## A starter library

A campaign benefits from a small library of variants before it has a script, because the
library is the vocabulary the writers compose with and the list the engineers build once. A
workable starter has about ten entries, each a single rule with the meanings it can carry:

**Pursuit** — one named opponent must finish behind the player or be wrecked; carries rivalry,
revenge, a debt collected. **Escort** — a fragile car or cargo must finish intact; carries
protection, duty, a promise. **Carried damage** — no repair between this event and the next;
carries poverty, consequence, a cost that follows the player. **Handicapped start** — back of
the grid, reduced car, or a time penalty; carries exile, punishment, an outsider's entry.
**Restricted arsenal** — no weapons, or contact only; carries a truce, a vow, a test of
clean driving. **Pressure clock** — fuel or time that runs out; carries urgency, a rescue, an
escape. **Elimination** — the last car each lap is removed; carries a cull, a selection.
**Failing system** — brakes, steering or a weapon degrades mid-race on a stated schedule;
carries sabotage, age, a machine near its end. **Shared fate** — an ally must also finish in a
stated position; carries loyalty, a partnership. **Reversed objective** — winning is the wrong
result, finishing in a stated place is the right one; carries a fixed race, a bribe, a lesson.

Three more earn a place early. **Hunted** — a force enters mid-race and targets only the
player, and surviving is the win; carries collection, persecution, the system turning on one
driver. **Draining stake** — the purse or a resource is visibly taken during the race, at
checkpoints, so the loss is felt while driving rather than read afterwards; carries debt, a
levy, a cut taken first. **Cannot-lose trial** — a run with no failure state, used to try a
new part or verb with a character talking over it, whose failure lines are allowed to be
funny; carries a reveal, a bond, and the teaching of a verb before the story needs it in
anger.

Each variant may carry many ideas over a campaign; each idea uses one variant once.

## What is hit is what the story says

In a combat racer the variant also chooses the target of the game's violence, and the target
decides how the act reads. A ram against an enforcer who came to collect is self-defence;
the same ram against an anonymous field is cruelty; against the spectacle's own machinery it
is satire. Decide once, for the campaign, who the violence's weight or joke lands on, and keep
every variant pointing there. When a censoring market has had a game swap what the player
hits, the act's meaning changed with the target and nothing else did, which is the clearest
evidence that the target is the framing. A card that asks for sympathy over a variant that
rewards wrecking whoever is nearest loses the argument to the player's hands.

## Fairness constraints

A variant changes a rule or what an input achieves, never which input does what. Remapping
controls punishes muscle memory, is indistinguishable from a bug at speed, and says nothing
about the story. A variant that degrades control, a failing brake or a pulling wheel, is named
on the card and shown on the display for the whole event, with the schedule of its degradation
stated where the player can see it; a handicap the player did not see coming is experienced as
a defect, not as a plot. The same disclosure binds help: an ally's contribution, a friendly
crowd, a story favour is either presentation only or a stated rule on the display, never an
invisible boost, because a hidden help devalues every result it touches and cannot be told
apart from the game tilting the table. And a variant must remain finishable by an average
player of the tier, because a beat the player cannot finish delivers only its loss outcome,
and that outcome had better have been written.

## A beat with no rule leaves the track

When an idea cannot be expressed as a rule variant, it does not belong on the track at all.
It belongs in the hub between events, the garage, the shop, the ledger, where a scene does
not compete with a race. The naive compromise, a card describing the idea in front of an
unmodified race, is the worst placement available: it spends the player's patience before the
race and delivers nothing during it.

## Decision rules

- **When an event passes the deletion test only because of its track or opponents, it still
  fails.** Scenery and a harder field are difficulty, not story.
- **When two variants are needed to carry one event, check that both express the same idea**;
  if they express two, split the event.
- **When a variant would change the meaning of an input, show the change on the display before
  the grid and throughout**, and never change the input's mapping.
- **When a new idea fits no variant in the library, add a variant rather than stretching one**,
  and add it to the library so the next writer can use it.

## When not to use this

A fixed spine beat sometimes needs a scene the rules cannot carry, a death, a reunion, a
reveal; it may take one, inside the latitude its fixed status buys, and it still keeps a rule
variant in the event that follows. Pure difficulty tuning, a faster field for a later tier,
belongs to difficulty design and is not a story variant at all, however it is presented.

## Evidence status

That the mechanic should carry the story, and that a story contradicted by its play reads as
dishonest, rests on primary statements by game directors and writers about games where the
controls themselves tell the story, high confidence, and on the named critical concept of
dissonance between story and play, whose original essay is offline and is known through
secondary sources. A racing game carrying story through rule-variant events is medium, from a
search summary. The framing of violence by its target rests on documented facts about
censored releases and on a film director's account of the satire in a car-combat film, high on
the facts, with the generalisation the research's own. The starter library, the
leave-the-track rule and the disclosure-of-help rule are design proposals; the last is
consistent with a stated design rule against invisible boosts but was not observed in play.
None has been tested in a played race campaign.
