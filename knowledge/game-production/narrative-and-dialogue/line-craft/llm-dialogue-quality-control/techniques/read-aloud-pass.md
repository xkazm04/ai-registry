# Read-aloud pass

The named concern: hear every shortlisted line spoken, in scene order and at performance
speed, before it is accepted, and record what the ear found, because the ear catches defects
that no reading of text reaches.

## What the ear finds

A line written for the eye and a line written for the mouth differ in ways a page review
misses and a listener notices at once. The **breath**: a line with no place to breathe, or a
clause that keeps the meaning back until the end of a long sentence. The **mouth**: sibilant
runs, consonant clusters, tongue-twisting adjacent words, a name that cannot be said the same
way twice. The **written construction**: a parenthetical aside, a semicolon, a nested clause,
an inverted order nobody speaks. The **misheard word**: a homophone that changes the meaning, a
name that sounds like a common word, a word spelled one way and said two (lead, wind, tear),
a numeral or abbreviation that a voice will read differently from the way the writer meant.
The **punctuation**: in a spoken line every mark is a direction — a full stop a beat, a comma a
breath, an ellipsis a weighted pause, a dash a cut-off — and a generated line strews them, so a
line with three hesitation marks plays as mostly silence. The **cadence**: two speakers whose lines, heard
back to back, have the same rhythm even though their words differ — the convergence a blind
attribution test catches on paper, heard much faster. And the **duration**: a line that does
not fit the moment it plays in.

## Procedure

Read the shortlist, not the whole batch; the pass is costly and belongs after the cheap
filters. It belongs **before** any synthesis or recording, because those are the expensive
stages and they faithfully reproduce whatever the text gets wrong; listening to the rendered
take afterwards is a second, separate pass, and a clean signal check on that take does not
prove the delivery worked. Every line meant to get a laugh is read aloud without exception,
because humour is the register the page misjudges most. Read in **scene order**, with the lines before and after the slot spoken too, because
a line's rhythm is heard against its neighbours. Read at **performance speed**, not a careful
reading pace, and once more under the scene's pressure — shouted over noise, whispered, rushed —
when the line will be performed that way.

**One breath per line** is the default shape for anything spoken in play — a dozen words or
so, split where longer — and a line that needs a second breath has to earn it. **Time each
line** and compare it with the slot: a line that plays over a moment of action, a
gap between events or a fixed on-screen duration has a window, and a line that overruns it is
cut or talked over in play. For text shown on a screen rather than spoken, read it aloud at
the pace of reading at the viewing distance and size it will be shown at; the ear still finds
the construction a reader stumbles on.

Record findings per line: the words where the reader stumbled, the line that ran long and by
how much, the pair of speakers that sounded alike. A stumble is marked even when the reader
recovers, because a performer will hit it too. A finding sends the line back to the person's
pick with the note, or into one revision round with the stumble quoted.

## Who reads

A person reads where possible — ideally not the writer, who hears the line they meant rather
than the one written. Synthesized speech is a cheaper floor and finds duration, breath and
misheard-word defects reliably, but it smooths over stumbles a person would hit and it cannot
tell a flat line from a restrained one. When it is used, the record says so. A synthesized
reading is an instrument for hearing the text; whether the synthesized clip is itself
acceptable audio is a separate question, judged elsewhere.

## Why it is a rung of its own

A line can pass the filter, top the judge's shortlist and win a person's pick on the page, and
still fail when spoken. Text evidence does not imply spoken evidence, which is
[structural proof is necessary and never sufficient](../../../../_laws.md#structural-proof-is-never-sufficient)
in its plainest form. So acceptance names the rung a line reached: picked on the page, or heard.
A line accepted without a read-aloud is labelled unheard rather than recorded as passed, which
is [unmeasured is not a pass](../../../../_laws.md#unmeasured-is-not-a-pass).

## Decision rules

- **When a reader stumbles on a line, mark it**, even after recovery.
- **When a spoken line carries more than one hesitation mark, cut marks first**, before
  cutting words.
- **When a line will be shown as a caption as well as spoken, the caption matches the spoken
  words exactly**, minus any performance direction.
- **When a line overruns its slot's window, shorten it or send it back**; never rely on the
  performer to speed up.
- **When two speakers sound alike aloud, return to the voice entries**, not the single line.
- **When only synthesized speech was used, label the line as machine-read** and schedule a
  person's read before it is recorded or locked.
- **When a line is meant to be awkward — a character who stumbles — mark it as intended** so
  the finding is not fixed away.

## When not to use this

Lines never spoken or read in time — reference text, logs a player reads at leisure — need a
careful read, not a performance read. The pass does not replace a performer's session; it
removes the defects that would waste one.

## Evidence status

Reading dialogue aloud before accepting it is long-standing writing-room and voice-direction
practice, recorded in practitioner teaching rather than measured. That punctuation directs a
synthesized voice, and that numerals, abbreviations and stacked pauses are weak spots, comes
from a speech vendor's primary documentation; one recorded case of a generated line rendering
with over half its length silent is a single observation, not a rate. The one-breath length is
an editorial heuristic. That synthesized speech finds duration and pronunciation defects but
misses delivery defects is practitioner experience. The
per-line record and the labelled unheard state are this document's synthesis. None of this has
been tested in a played game.
