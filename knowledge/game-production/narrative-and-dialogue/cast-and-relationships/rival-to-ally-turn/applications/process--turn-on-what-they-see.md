# Death Ride's boss turns: refused, witnessed, paid for

This application reconciles the turn half of the subject — refuse once, turn on what is seen,
pay for the crossing — against the research written for Death Ride, a top-down vehicular combat
racer for a living-room TV whose campaign turns each region's boss into an ally against the
league's owner, Marrow. The tree is the Death Ride worktree; every anchor below is relative to
its root and was re-opened and re-read on 2026-10-04. Death Ride has not been played by anyone:
what follows is a design reconciled against research, not a turn proven in play.

## What the dossiers confirm

The refusal is the dossier's own rule, stated almost as the technique states it:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:60 "A defector must refuse once before they turn"`,
with the reason given at
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:62 "A refused turn makes the eventual turn a decision rather than a plot convenience."`
and the naive failure named at
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:64 "Defeat becomes conversion, and the conversion means nothing because it cost nothing."`
Death Ride's use is that each boss's defeat card shows the boss choosing the league again —
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:282 "Their defeat card should show them choosing Marrow again"` —
for a want the player understands, with
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:282 "Two refusal lengths are enough"`.

The witness rule is confirmed by the sequence the dossier rates highest:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:74 "Witness lets the character convince themselves; debate undermines the turn."`
and the scene itself,
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:78 "Ashi watches him gently free a ladybug, and she drops her weapon"`,
rated
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:82 "High. The sequence can be checked on screen."`
For Death Ride the act witnessed is the player's own:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:286 "make it a real player choice from an earlier race, replayed on the boss's card"`.
The cost is confirmed at
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:57 "a turn without sacrifice reads as the writer moving a piece"`,
and the region table names a concrete price per boss — for the salt-flats boss,
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:165 "producing the unedited log gets her whited out"` —
which is the technique's best case: the cost is the thing the rival was fighting for.

## Upward lessons the dossiers taught the draft

The draft had the turn as one image. The dossier's sequence has two stages — the act cracks the
belief, and only after the character travels among the people the hero saved does allegiance
move: `docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:78 "only then does her allegiance change"`.
The technique now carries the confirmation stage. The draft also lacked the shape of the
strongest refusal, a want granted and found empty:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:66 "after he gets exactly what he wanted and finds it empty"`,
and the scaling of the road with the character:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:66 "the harder the character, the longer the road"`.
The draft's "pay in something they value" became "pay in their own currency" from
`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:285 "A hoarder pays cash, a builder pays a part."`

## Where the dossiers fall short of the standard

**The cost is paid out of skill.** The dossier proposes that turned bosses donate parts of their
own cars to the Mechanic's rig, and accepts the consequence
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:290 "each turn has a cost (the boss is weaker on the track)"`.
The parts-as-cost idea is strong; the weakening is the demotion this subject forbids. The
standard stays: take the equipment, never the skill. Let the donated part be visibly missing
from the ally's car while the ally's call-in move keeps its full menace.

**The witnessed player act has no fallback.** Replaying a real earlier choice on the turn card
is the strongest turn a game has, and the dossier does not say what the card shows for a player
who never made that choice. The standard requires the act be read from recorded play with a
written alternative witness for the player who did not perform it.

**Two dossiers, one example.** The refusal in R1 and the relapse in
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:57 "betrays once more, then repairs damage with actions"`
are the same scene from the same series. Their agreement is not independent corroboration, and
R4 itself rates it `docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:57 "medium (secondary, strong consensus)"`.
The refusal is relayed partly second-hand:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:70 "relayed second-hand"`.
