# Earn respect before the turn

The named concern: make the contest with the rival produce respect, not merely a result. Defeat
gives a rival a reason to fear or resent the hero. Respect gives them a reason to listen, and a
turn without it is the strongest-side turn — a calculation the audience expects to be reversed
the moment the calculation changes.

## Respect is measured against the rival's code, not the hero's

Every rival worth the name has a code: what counts as a fair win, what is beneath them, what
they admire even in an enemy. Respect is earned when the hero meets that code — when the hero
wins the way the rival believes a race should be won, or shows the rival's own virtue more
purely than the rival does. The hero's code is irrelevant here; a hero who wins honourably by
their own lights and dishonourably by the rival's has deepened the grudge.

This is why the style-and-grudge work comes first. The rival's code is legible only through
their style: a rival who never brakes first respects nerve; a rival who races clean and expects
it back respects restraint; a rival who would do anything to win respects only winning, and is
a poor candidate for a turn at all.

Respect can also run the other way. A hero who loses to the rival and behaves in defeat as the
rival's code demands — no excuse, no foul, back on the grid next time — earns the same thing,
and often earns it more cheaply than a win. The strongest arcs use both: an honourable loss
that plants the seed, and a later win on the rival's own terms that confirms it.

## Respect is mutual, and it comes from different excellences

A rival who comes to respect a hero they do not themselves impress is a fan, not a peer. The
respect that supports a turn runs both ways, and it is sturdiest when the two are excellent in
*opposite* ways — one calculating and one reckless, one patient and one fast — so that each
has something the other lacks and has seen it up close in a contest. Mirrored competence gives
the later alliance a reason beyond sentiment: each side is better with the other. It also
protects the rival from the strongest-side reading, because the hero's win did not prove the
hero better at everything; it proved the hero better at one thing the rival can name.

In play this means the rival must have beaten, or nearly beaten, the player at the rival's own
excellence before the contest that turns them. A rival the player only ever outclassed has
shown nothing to respect.

## The defeat line reframes the contest

The rival's first reaction to losing is the most valuable line they will speak, and the naive
version spends it announcing a new attitude. The line that earns its place reframes the
contest the player has just finished: it reveals *why* the rival drove that way — what they
owe, to whom, what losing now costs them — so that the player re-reads the race they just
played. The attitude can crack in that line; it should not resolve. The rival then remembers
that contest for the rest of the story, and later lines may return to it.

## In a game, the contest is the player's, so the respect must be too

A film can declare that the hero won cleanly. A game cannot, because the player decided how
the contest went. Two constructions keep the rival's reaction honest:

**Condition the reaction on recorded facts of the contest.** The game records the things the
rival's code cares about — contact made, a pass on the outside, a car the player refused to
wreck, a lead given back after an opponent's crash — and the respect beat reads them. A player
who won dirty gets a rival who says so, and whose turn is delayed or costs more.

**Or shape the contest so that every win shows the quality.** If the rival respects nerve, a
course where the only way past is a committed line through the one corner they defend means
any win carries the respected quality. This is cheaper than conditioning and loses nothing
when the quality is the contest's whole skill.

What is never acceptable is a fixed respect line played regardless of how the player won. It
is the one moment in the arc where the game must visibly have been watching.

## Respect is shown, not stated

The rival does not say "I respect you." They do something that only a person who respects the
hero would do: give room on the next start, nod on the grid, say the hero's name rather than an
insult, refuse to let a third party foul the hero, pay a debt they did not have to. A rival who
announces respect has had the respect written for them; a rival who acts on it has earned the
audience's belief that it is real.

## Decision rules

- **When the hero's win would be called dishonest by the rival's own code, do not start the
  turn here.** Use the defeat to deepen the grudge and earn respect in a later contest.
- **When the player can win in ways that contradict the respect beat, record those facts and
  branch on them**, or reshape the contest until they cannot.
- **When the rival respects only winning, choose a different turn mechanism or a different
  rival.** A pure winner joins the strongest side and leaves it the same way.
- **When respect has to be put in dialogue, put it in a changed address or a changed act**, not
  in the word.
- **When a recorded contest fact is declared for the respect beat, confirm a line reads it.** A
  fact the game tracks and nothing branches on is a respect beat that ships fixed.
- **When the rival has never beaten the player at their own excellence, add a contest where
  they do** before the turning one.
- **When the defeat line announces friendship, rewrite it to explain the race just run.**

## When not to use this

- **For a turn driven by a shared enemy alone.** A rival forced into alliance by a common threat
  does not need to respect the hero first; the turn then belongs to a team-up structure, and
  respect is earned during the alliance rather than before it. It still needs the other
  obligations.
- **When the arc is deliberately transactional.** A mercenary who joins for pay is a legitimate
  character; write them as one, and do not dress the transaction as respect.

and its critical reception (high as a description of the work, though the film exaggerates the
real enmity and depicts respect rather than an alliance). The rule that the fight must express

character-driven boss fights (high) and on a documented action-game memory system (medium).

facts, are the research dossier's design proposals and this document's own synthesis — no
