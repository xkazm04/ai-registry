# The ledger as the leash: Marrow's control staged through the player's own economy

This application realizes the rationed necessity, and the presence and first-bite techniques
that hang off it, on Death Ride, a top-down vehicular combat racer for a television whose
campaign runs on a debt to the league boss Marrow and ends with the seizure of the player's
car. The tree is the `firetv-deathride` worktree as read on 2026-10-04 and re-resolved at `10974fa3` on 2026-10-09; the material is the
research dossiers in `docs/narrative/research/` (R1, R3, R4). The campaign has not been
played, so this is a staging plan reconciled against research, never a result. It is a
`process` application: it specifies which screens and cards carry the antagonist, not code.

## The necessity is the player's resource

Death Ride's necessity is the league itself as a supply line — fuel, parts and medicine
moving through the wasteland (R1 D3, see the companion application) — and the player's
spendable resource is the credit balance on the ledger. That binding satisfies the
technique's fourth property: every purchase, repair and purse is a transaction under his
name. The dossier's pattern, rated high on screen and medium on the architectural reading:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:42 "Control is rationing, made visible by architecture."`,
with the rule
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:43 "show the villain rationing a necessity from a place people must look up to"`.
R1 adds the results screen as the public counter: a fraction of each purse reaches the
player and
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:343 "the rest climbing to Marrow's ledger"`.

## A leash, not a loan

The upward lesson this application contributed to the technique is the leash-versus-loan
distinction:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:31 "A leash is a debt the creditor can rewrite, paid in his currency."`,
whose rule reads
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:32 "a debt you can pay off is a loan; one whose terms change is a leash"`.
The same line carries its honesty note, which the technique keeps:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:32 "Economic historians dispute how universal the gouging was"`.
Sources: Wikipedia, Company scrip (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_scrip); e-WV, Scrip
(https://www.wvencyclopedia.org/entries/190); Fishback, Journal of Economic History
(https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/abs/did-coal-miners-owe-their-souls-to-the-company-store-theory-and-evidence-from-the-early-1900s/F576B08F8DB66BDFBBA7D4E346B97873).

Two Death Ride proposals realize it. R4 makes the unit his
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:129 "The unit of account is his (P7)."`),
quoting a conversion rate on receipts without adding a spendable second currency. R1 makes a
revision visible: a lenient first region, then
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:262 "Mark the gap with one beat the player notices on the ledger screen, such as a changed rate."`
Together they are the technique's decision rule in practice — give the creditor a lever over
the terms and use it once, visibly. The language on every receipt follows the obligation rule
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:29 "let him speak only of obligation and fairness, never of force"`),
sourced to David Graeber's Debt (https://libcom.org/article/debt-first-5000-years-david-graeber),
whose application to games R4 itself marks as inference.

## The first bite lands on Rook

R4 stages the first bite on a region boss the player meets in Act 1:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:131 "The leash bites someone else in Act 1 (P8)."`
— a lien on a car Rook minds,
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:131 "so the Crown seizure later is a promise kept, not a twist"`.
That satisfies the technique's same-currency rule (a lien, not violence) and, read with the
companion application, is the only candidate in the dossiers for Marrow's funding act. The
open-door move came from R4's rule as an upward lesson:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:35 "let the controller point out that the player is free to go"`.
R4 rates the generalisation as its own
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:230 "as a general rule, the ending synthesis (P28)"`),
which is why the technique states it as the subject's weakest-sourced claim.

## Where the plan falls short

**The open door has no staged scene.** R4 states the rule but none of its Death Ride options
gives Marrow the line offering the player the exit, nor shows a driver who left and came
back. Until one card does, the leash is enforced by the campaign's structure, which the
technique names as the naive version.

**The presence register is borrowed from a loud villain.** R4's precedent for the broadcast
channel is a gleeful, entertaining antagonist; Marrow's PA lines must keep the channel and the
fairness register, and R3's "never says" list for him
(`docs/narrative/research/R3-dialogue-craft.md:76 "never makes a direct threat"`) is the guard
the voice run should be checked against.

**Scarcity is asserted, not reconciled.** The dossiers claim the league keeps necessities
moving; nothing reconciles that claim with how generous the race economy is actually tuned.
Under the technique's single-authority rule, the economy's numbers own that question and the
cards must read from them.
