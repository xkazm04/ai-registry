# Marrow: a grievance, a method and one protected thing, specified before a line is written

This application realizes the technique on Death Ride, a top-down vehicular combat racer for
a television, whose campaign antagonist is Marrow, the league boss who holds the player's
debt and runs the final speedway (the Crown). The tree is the `firetv-deathride` worktree as
read on 2026-10-04 and re-resolved at `10974fa3` on 2026-10-09; the material is the narrative research dossiers in
`docs/narrative/research/` (R1 prestige-series craft, R3 dialogue craft, R4 wasteland, rivalry
and debt craft). Those dossiers are research, not field experience: Death Ride's campaign has
not been played by anyone, so nothing below claims a result in play. It is a `process`
application because what it specifies is an authoring order, not code.

## The order of authoring

The process is three written artifacts, in this order, before any card, bark or receipt line
is drafted.

**1. The grievance-and-means sheet.** One paragraph for the grievance, told as a stranger
would tell it; one for the means, naming who pays; one sentence for the hero side's
complicity; one for what runs the roads after him. The dossier's proposal for Marrow is that
he is right about something the player benefits from:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:266 "the league is the only thing keeping fuel, parts and medicine moving through the wasteland"`,
with debt bondage, seizure and staged track deaths as the method, and the victory obliged to
answer what replaces the league. That last obligation is the dossier's, and it was an upward
lesson for this subject's draft:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:266 "The player's victory must then answer"`.
The source pattern, rated high, is
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:33 "Give the villain a legitimate grievance and an illegitimate method"`,
and its rule carries the complicity clause this subject adopted:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:35 "The hero should be right about the method and complicit in the problem."`
Its naive failure on the soft side —
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:40 "Both-sidesing so even that nothing is at stake and no choice matters."`
— is the failure the sheet's complicity sentence is there to prevent. Primary sources behind
R1 P2: Christian Linke (ONE Esports, https://www.oneesports.gg/league-of-legends/arcane-creators-interview/)
and Aaron Ehasz (korranews repost, https://www.tumblr.com/thethiefandtheairbender/178720557349/korranews-looking-back-on-avatars-and-ahead-to);
the class-geography reading is secondary
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:46 "Medium for the class-geography reading"`).

**2. The protected-thing card.** One attachment, outside the ledger, shown wordlessly two or
three times, collected by the finale. The dossier states it as
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:268 "Give Marrow one protected thing, show it without words, and make the finale charge for it (P1)"`
and forbids the history in speech:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:270 "Do not reveal its history in dialogue."`
Its source pattern
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:21 "Humanise the villain through one protected relationship, then make the climax charge for it"`)
names the naive failure this subject adopted for its golden path:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:25 "Pity that arrives without a cost reads as manipulation."`
The possession refinement — the attachment held through dependency and rules — came from R4,
rated medium-high:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:46 "because it makes him pitiable and more frightening at once"`.
Sources behind R1 P1: Alex Yee (AwardsDaily, https://www.awardsdaily.com/2022/08/18/arcane-co-creator-alex-yee-on-his-emmy-nominated-labor-of-love/)
and Warren Ellis (Bleeding Cool, https://bleedingcool.com/tv/castlevania-warren-ellis-talks-adapting-video-game-working-with-netflix-dracula-more-interview/).

**3. The menace register.** What his speech must do, handed to whoever owns the voice
bible: obligation language, precision, no direct threat. R4 proposes
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:127 "Marrow speaks only fairness (P6, P9)."`,
and R3 proposes the precision lever
(`docs/narrative/research/R3-dialogue-craft.md:85 "He knows a small true detail: a lap time, a bolt, a name."`)
with a sample that shows the register:
`docs/narrative/research/R3-dialogue-craft.md:94 "Win if you like. Interest doesn't watch the races."`.
R3 rates the four-lever list as its own synthesis
(`docs/narrative/research/R3-dialogue-craft.md:96 "Medium** for the four-point list, which is a synthesis."`);
the reasonable-controller rule itself is rated high in R4
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:40 "a rational tyrant frightens where a raving one amuses"`),
sourced to John Gonzalez (PC Gamer, https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/fallout-new-vegas-lead-writer-worries-caesars-argument-for-authoritarianism-was-done-a-little-too-well-but-still-believes-you-cant-just-make-your-tyrants-cardboard-villains/).

## Where the dossier falls short of the standard

**The Crown fails the outside-the-ledger test.** R4's option makes the attachment the
speedway itself:
`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:133 "Marrow's one attachment is the Crown (P11, P9)."`
The Crown is the seat of the league — his treasury, his broadcast, his lien wall — so losing it
is a business loss, not a price he feels as a man; under this subject's rule it is an asset.
The image in the same line ("He maintains the timing gear himself while the stands rot") is
strong and worth keeping as texture, but the protected thing should be one of R1's options,
which pass the test because they produce nothing for the league:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:272 "a car he has never raced;"` or
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:273 "a driver he lost and still keeps a grid slot for;"`.
The finale collection the dossier proposes stays compatible:
`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:305 "The death is his refusal to be owed."`.

**The draft chose a fourth option, and the corrected test admits it.** The head writer's
story bible, written the same day and still awaiting the owner, picked none of the dossiers'
three: `docs/narrative/STORY-BIBLE-V2.md:204 "**One protected thing: the Voice (R1 P1, D4; R4 P11).**"`
— the league's announcer, who co-founded it with Marrow and reads Marrow's book on air
before every boss race. Under this subject's first test ("an attachment he also uses ... is an
asset") the Voice fails: he is the league's broadcast, inside the system and working for it.
Under the test as corrected on 2026-10-09 — pricing, not use — he passes, because the bible
shows Marrow exempting him from the one rule he applies to everyone: the Voice is "the only
person Marrow lets round a number" (`STORY-BIBLE-V2.md:204`). The four candidates read
against both tests: the Crown fails both (Marrow books it); a car he never raced and a lost
driver's grid slot pass both; the Voice flips. The draft's collection is the leash-reaches-it
shape — reading the real book on air costs Marrow his secret, and the bible says so
(`STORY-BIBLE-V2.md:208 "**The bond costs him the league:**"`). Whether the Voice is the right
choice is the owner's call; the correction only stops this application calling it wrong. The
finale lines that pay it off are scripted to a finale-phase event the runtime does not raise,
which is recorded under the `kotlin` application.

**No funding act is specified.** Neither dossier gives Marrow an early, witnessed
follow-through that funds his later calm. The nearest is R4's lien on Rook's car in Act 1,
which this application reads as doing double duty — the first bite on another and the funding
act — and records under the companion application for the rationed necessity.

**Every Marrow proposal is a proposal.** R1 says so of its own design section
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:367 "are design proposals inferred from the patterns, not facts"`),
and R4 says the same of its options. None is an owner decision, and the sheet above is the
place the owner decides them.
