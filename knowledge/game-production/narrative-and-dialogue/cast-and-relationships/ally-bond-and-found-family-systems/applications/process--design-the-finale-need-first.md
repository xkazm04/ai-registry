# Death Ride: the duel at the Crown, written as four ally tasks before any recruitment card

This application realizes the technique on Death Ride, a top-down vehicular combat racer for a
television, whose campaign ends in a fight to the death at the Crown between the Mechanic's
improvised rig and Marrow, the league boss who holds the player's debt. Four regional bosses
(Rook, Ox, Vex, Mica) are beaten and later turn. The tree is the `firetv-deathride` worktree as
read on 2026-10-04; the material is the narrative research dossiers in
`docs/narrative/research/` (R1 prestige-series craft, R2 game narrative craft, R4 wasteland,
rivalry and debt craft). Those dossiers are research, not field experience: Death Ride's
campaign has not been played by anyone, so nothing below claims a result in play. It is a
`process` application because what it specifies is an authoring order, not code.

## The order of authoring

**1. The finale-needs sheet, written first.** One row per turned boss, naming the finale task
only that ally can answer. R4 states the rule
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:67 "Design the finale's need first, then recruit for it."`)
with its naive failure
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:68 "allies collected as trophies, irrelevant to the last fight"`),
and R4's option 9 already fills the sheet: Ox's crew rebuilt the rig's seams, Vex supplied the
timing of the Crown's pickups, Mica's people cut the floodlights, Rook brought the lien papers
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:143 "Each recruited ally gets one visible, named contribution announced before it"`).
Four allies, four irreducible tasks, none shared — the sheet passes the one-task-per-ally rule
as drafted. R2 states the same structure from the campaign side
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:38 "design every earlier beat as preparation for that event, and let the preparation be measured"`).
Primary source, rated high in both dossiers: Brian Kindregan to TheGamer,
https://www.thegamer.com/mass-effect-2-suicide-mission-characters-choices-archetypes/
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:40 "recruit your team for the suicide mission, try to make them loyal"`).

**2. Two beats per ally, back-planned from the sheet.** Each boss's region carries an
acquisition beat that shows the capability the sheet names and a trust beat that proves it at
a cost — the dossier's own minimum
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:68 "then give each an acquisition beat and a trust beat proving they can supply it"`).
The trust beat's cost is supplied by R1's proposal that turned bosses pay in parts from their
own cars, which leaves them weaker on the track
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:290 "each turn has a cost (the boss is weaker on the track)"`),
and those parts accumulate into the finale vehicle
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:299 "The rig visibly uses parts paid out by turned bosses, so the finale vehicle is made of the player's alliances"`).
That accumulation was an upward lesson for this subject's own-currency technique: a payout
drawn from the ally's own stock, building one object the player carries to the end.

**3. Two scales of call-on.** Across the campaign, R2 proposes a once-per-race call
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:286 "Once per race, call the turned ally in to perform the signature move the player learned to fear."`);
at the Crown, each ally's task fires once. This subject's first draft allowed only finale-scale
scarcity and rejected repeated calls as abilities; the dossier's per-race call, with its
once-per-encounter precedent rated low-medium
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:106 "Low-medium for the Hades summon mechanics"`),
taught the draft that once per event is scarcity too. Upward lesson, adopted.

**4. The decisive act stays with the player.** R2 binds the finale: the fight is won with the
trained verbs and
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:303 "No ally lands the final blow"`),
from the pattern
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:207 "Don't hand the climactic choice to a supporting character, because players read that as stolen agency."`),
primary source Greg Kasavin to Aftermath,
https://aftermath.site/hades-2-supergiant-narrative-interview-ending-changes/. R4 names the same
risk for this game as diluting the Mechanic's moment
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:143 "allies prepare, the Mechanic drives"`).
Upward lesson, adopted into the golden path.

**5. Every rule-changing contribution is disclosed.** R4 bounds the contributions to
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:143 "presentation only, or a disclosed rule"`).
Upward lesson: the draft had no disclosure rule, and an ally's contribution arriving as an
invisible modifier is indistinguishable from the game cheating.

## Where the dossiers fall short of the standard, or disagree

**Whether preparation changes the outcome is undecided.** R2 makes ally presence depend on
earlier play
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:303 "Which allies appear, and how well the rig performs, depend on earlier choices (ME2's rule)."`),
while R4's cheapest version keeps the duel fixed and the contributions presentational
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:143 "Q0's duel stays one-on-one"`).
The standard asks for a finale whose outcome is traceable ally by ally to how each bond was
tended; a presentation-only finale honours the allies but grades nothing. This is an owner
decision, and the sheet should record which version is chosen and, for the graded version,
what a neglected ally's failed task looks like on screen.

**The failure branch is unwritten.** Neither dossier specifies what the finale shows when a
boss never turned or a wavering ally was not brought back. The standard requires that branch to
be a scene; the sheet needs a fifth column for it.

**Every proposal is a proposal.** R1 says so of its design section
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:367 "are design proposals inferred from the patterns, not facts"`),
and R4's options are explicitly not owner decisions. The finale-first rule itself is the
best-sourced claim in this subject
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:228 "finale-first ally design (P17)"`);
its application to a racer is untested.
