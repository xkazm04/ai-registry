# Death Ride: the league's offer to the Mechanic, and the suspicion it explains

This application realizes the technique on Death Ride, a top-down vehicular combat racer for a
television, in which the player races to clear a debt held by Marrow, the boss of the league
that runs the circuit, and is helped throughout by the Mechanic, a young builder with a debt of
their own to Marrow. The tree is the `firetv-deathride` worktree as read on 2026-10-04; the
material is the narrative research dossiers in `docs/narrative/research/` (R1, R2, R4). Those
dossiers are research, not field experience: the campaign has not been played by anyone, so
nothing below claims a result in play. It is a `process` application because it specifies an
authoring order for a handful of cards, not code.

## The order of authoring

**1. Name the institution and give it a face.** The betrayer is the league, and its face is
Marrow. R4 states the rule
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:78 "Let the institution betray; let the friend be tested."`)
with the failure it prevents
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:79 "a friend is secretly a traitor, poisoning every relationship retroactively"`).
Its examples are checkable on screen and rated high, but they are a sports film and a
car-combat game, not a statement of the rule: Go Into The Story's breakdown,
https://gointothestory.blcklst.com/script-analysis-ford-v-ferrari-scene-by-scene-breakdown-22f96e4cc666/,
and David Jaffe to DualShockers,
https://www.dualshockers.com/interview-david-jaffe-discusses-twisted-medal-calling-all-cars-game-journalism-and-next-gen/.

**2. Write the honest explanation before the clues.** R4's option 8 plants suspicion on the
Mechanic and resolves it as loyalty
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:141 "plant two clues that could read as betrayal (absences, vanishing parts)"`),
explained by the institution's offer
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:141 "Marrow offered to clear the debt for the sibling's location and the Mechanic refused"`),
and it states the division of labour this technique is built on
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:141 "The institution supplies the pressure; the friend passes the test."`).
Upward lesson: the draft had no place for fair suspicion of the friend. It now borrows the
tension of a friend's betrayal without paying its price, provided every clue's honest
explanation is written first.

**3. Schedule the resolution an act early.** The dossier names the risk and the deadline
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:141 "Risk: lingering distrust; resolve before Act 5."`).
Act 5 is the Crown; resolving in Act 4 leaves one region in which the Mechanic is trusted
again before the finale calls on them, which meets the standard.

**4. Make the offer legible.** R1 stages the same offer as a scene and shows the pressure in the
craft rather than in words
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:296 "Marrow offers to cancel the Mechanic's debt in exchange for the rig, or for tuning the seized car."`).
The player should see or hear the offer, so the refusal reads as a choice.

**5. Corrode in witnessed steps, never sabotage.** R4's loyalty pattern
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:75 "Corrode loyalty in witnessed steps."`)
and R2's waver proposal
(`docs/narrative/research/R2-game-narrative-craft.md:288 "one ally hangs back instead of helping, shown in driving before any line explains it"`)
both reached the draft as upward lessons: the waver shows in play first, and it withholds
rather than harms.

## Where the dossiers fall short of the standard, or disagree

**Two crises where one would do.** R2 places the waver on a turned boss under Marrow's pressure
(line 288 above); R1 places the hesitation on the Mechanic
(`docs/narrative/research/R1-prestige-series-craft.md:296 "Let them hesitate, perhaps fail once."`).
The standard says the tested friend and the waverer are usually one person, because the
institution's offer is the cleanest cause a waver can have. Pick one: either the Mechanic is
both, or the turned boss's waver is caused by an offer of the same kind and the Mechanic's
suspicion stays a misdirection only.

**"Fail once" contradicts the fixed loyalty.** R1's "perhaps fail once" sits against R4's
reading of existing canon, in which the Mechanic is loyal and refuses. If the Mechanic fails
once, the failure must be a hesitation the player sees and reverses, never a betrayal that
lands; otherwise this is the friend's betrayal by another name and the player's investment in
the Mechanic is taxed.

**The player-trust cost is asserted, not shown.** That a friend's betrayal makes players
guarded with later allies is the dossier's inference; no source in R4 measures it
(`docs/narrative/research/R4-wasteland-and-rivalry-craft.md:230 "every option and table entry in sections 11 and 12"`
are marked as the dossier's own). It is plausible craft, carried here as unmeasured.
