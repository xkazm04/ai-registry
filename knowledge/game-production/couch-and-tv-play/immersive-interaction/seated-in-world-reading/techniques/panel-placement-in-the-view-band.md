# Place reading panels in the measured view band

The named concern: a reading panel in a seated scene has to stand somewhere, and the
somewhere is constrained before it is designed. The constraints are:
- the person's eye;
- the surface they work on;
- the agent or character that shares the space;
- the frame edge.

A placement chosen by a nominal distance ("at 60 centimetres") or a nominal region ("above the
far half") ignores those constraints, and lands over the wrong part of the furniture, cropped,
or across the work.

## Measure the band first

From the seated eye at the pose the scene is judged from, write down four things:

1. The pitch range the view covers, top to bottom.
2. The pitch band the work surface already takes. A table's far edge sits a few degrees below
   the view's top, and its near edge sits near the bottom.
3. The depth of each part of that surface from the eye. On a waist-high table seen from a chair,
   the far half is commonly 75 centimetres or more away.
4. Which parts of the view must never be covered: the focus surface, the agent's head, and any
   key plan or status the person needs while reading.

Then test the brief against the numbers. "60 centimetres and above the far half" is
impossible when the far half starts at 75. A screen at 60 stands over the near half, and is
seen against the far one. Resolve that conflict with the person who gave the brief before
building, not after the first capture.

## Put the panel being read straight ahead

The panel a person is reading goes where "in front of me" means: straight ahead of the eye,
upright, turned to face it, its lower edge just above the work surface's far edge, and not
cropped by any frame edge. In one project the first deck of screens was placed where an
earlier card had stood, high and to the right, angled, and clipped at the frame's top. Its
text was large enough. The owner still rejected it as "not in front of me", and it moved to
the centre.

## Move it farther, scale it up

Moving a panel farther away is allowed, and is often what clears the work surface. The panel
scales with the distance so its text keeps the same angle: moving from 60 to 75 centimetres
means scaling by 1.25. Re-measure the smallest text after every move, at the panel's farthest
corner, against the floor.

## A queue fans out; it does not stack

When several panels form a queue, the current one stands in front, and the next ones appear
as thin spines to the side, with their kind and title. Panels stacked behind and above the
front one show their bright text over and through it, and a dimmed panel behind a dark one
still bleeds where the display's range is high. The queue's position is a counter on the front
panel ("3 of 9"), not a pile.

Swapping panels animates the panels, briefly (a fifth of a second to a third), and never the
view.

## A body-anchored panel is small by anatomy

A panel strapped to a forearm by the watch is bounded by the forearm's length and by the angle
a wrist can turn toward the eye. At every placement a real arm allows, it is small and seen
obliquely. One project moved its wrist panel through more than ten placements across ten build rounds. A blind
panel of three judges still read it as small, steeply tilted and hard to read. So the wrist
carries status and a few rows in large type, angled toward the eye as far as the arm allows.
Anything to read line by line goes to the panel in front.

## Procedure

1. From the judged pose, measure the view's pitch range, the surface's band and depths, and the
   no-cover regions.
2. Check the brief against those numbers. Raise any conflict before building.
3. Place the reading panel straight ahead, upright and facing the eye, inside the band and clear
   of every frame edge.
4. Size it so its smallest text clears the floor at its farthest corner, and log that number at
   build time.
5. Fan any queue to the side as spines. Put the position on a counter.
6. Capture from the judged pose and confirm that nothing is cropped or covered.

## Decision rules

- **When a brief names a distance and a region**, check that they agree in the scene's geometry
  before building.
- **When a panel is called "off to the side" or "not in front"**, move it to straight ahead first.
  Adjust size after.
- **When rear panels show through the front**, fan the queue to the side. Do not dim it further.
- **When a body-anchored panel keeps reading small**, move content off it. Do not keep moving it.

## When not to use this

- For a standing, room-scale experience, where panels are placed in the world and the person
  walks to them. This technique assumes a fixed seated eye.
- For head-locked panels, which move with the head and have their own comfort rules. This
  technique places world-locked panels only.
