# Question-form criteria for open judgment

Some of the qualities that decide whether a piece of work reads were never going to
become thresholds. Does this space tell the player where to go? Does this ability read
differently from the three it sits beside? Does this motion have a moment the eye lands
on? Each of those is answerable from the stored artifact, each separates shipped work
from placeholder work, and none survives being rewritten as a number with a tolerance.
The instrument for them is an **interrogative criterion**: a question the examiner must
answer with evidence located in the artifact, rather than a bar the artifact clears.

Keep the naming clean. The whole graded instrument is the document; a single question
inside it is a criterion, phrased interrogatively, and never a document of its own. The
tradition this borrows from — a discipline's stock of design questions held up against a
work in progress — used the same word for its questions that a rubric corpus uses for its
versioned instruments, and letting the two senses meet makes both unreadable within a
week.

## When a question beats a bar

Ask a question rather than state a bar when all three hold. **The quality is relational or
whole-artifact**, existing in how parts sit together and disappearing when the parts are
inspected separately — readability against neighbours, legibility of intent, distinctness
within a set. **No measurable proxy exists that is not a lie**: the count of light sources
is not whether the room guides the eye, and a rubric that substitutes the count will be
satisfied by a room full of lights that guides nobody. **A practitioner recognises the
failure instantly but cannot state its threshold**, which is the signature of a genuine
craft question rather than an unfinished measurement.

State a bar, not a question, whenever a measurement exists. A question asked where a number
was available is a downgrade: it converts something two examiners always agree on into
something they can differ about, and hands back exactly the interpretive room the whole
instrument exists to close.

## The four parts, or it is taste with extra steps

This is the technique's own failure mode, and it is common enough to be the default
outcome: an author who cannot write a bar writes a question instead and calls the vagueness
open judgment. A question-form criterion is well-formed only with all four parts present.

- **The question**, asked about the artifact and answerable yes or no. Not "how good is the
  composition" — that is a score request wearing a question mark. "Does a first-time
  viewer's eye reach the intended focal element before anything else?"
- **The evidence that answers it**, named as a place in the stored artifact: the region, the
  sampled frame, the entry in the declared specification, the sibling it is compared
  against. An examiner who is not told where to look answers from impression, and impression
  is what the question form is always accused of.
- **The failing answer, written out.** One clause describing what the artifact looks like
  when the answer is no — competing focal elements of equal weight, a silhouette that
  matches its two neighbours, a motion whose fastest frame is not its most important one. A
  question with no written failure is a question the examiner will answer yes.
- **The answer form**: met, not met, or not answerable, each carrying the located evidence
  that justifies it. Never a number. A question-form criterion that returns a score has
  quietly become an unanchored scale.

If you cannot write the third part, you do not have a criterion yet. You have a quality you
have noticed and not yet understood, and it belongs in the incident collection until
somebody can describe its absence.

## Where an automated examiner is strongest and weakest at once

An open question is the one place a machine examiner outperforms the checklist it is
usually reduced to. It reads the whole artifact rather than one field of it, it notices a
relation nobody enumerated, and it articulates the finding in a sentence a producer can act
on — at a volume and cost no review panel reaches. That is the entire reason to carry
interrogative criteria into automated grading rather than reserving them for the leads.

The same property is the weakness. An open question has no natural refusal: asked whether
the space guides the player, an examiner that saw nothing conclusive still returns a fluent,
specific, confident answer, and that answer is indistinguishable by every surface property
from an observed one. What such an examiner verified and what it merely asserted stay
invisibly merged unless the instrument forces them apart. Three mechanisms force them apart,
and all three are cheap.

1. **Require the citation with the answer.** The answer names the region, the frame, the
   sibling. An answer that cannot point has observed nothing and is recorded as not
   answerable — never as met.
2. **Make the negative sayable.** Because the failing answer is written into the criterion,
   "not met" is a recognition rather than an accusation the examiner has to construct, and
   examiners return it far more readily.
3. **Ask each question against the artifact alone, in its own turn.** A question answered in
   the same breath as ten others inherits the momentum of the answers before it, and the set
   drifts toward uniform agreement.

## Composing the answers

Interrogative criteria produce findings, and findings compose differently from scores. A
not-answerable answer is an unmeasured criterion and renders as one; it must never fold into
a total as a neutral value, because an examiner that could not see the evidence and an
examiner that saw the bar met are not the same result. Where a level has to be derived,
derive it from the pattern of met and not-met answers with the unmeasured ones named beside
it, so the grade states what it was computed over.

Interrogative criteria also do not become disqualifiers. A cap's authority rests on being
uncontestable, and an answer that required judgment is contestable by construction — the
moment a capped rejection turns on a question, the cap mechanism starts being argued rather
than obeyed.

## Decision rules

- **When a question has no written failing answer, it is not a criterion yet.** Send it back
  to the author. This one check is what separates open judgment from a vague rubric, and it
  is worth applying pedantically.
- **When an examiner answers without pointing at anything, record not answerable.** A
  confident answer with no located evidence is an assertion, and an assertion is an input to
  a verdict, never the verdict.
- **When two examiners answer the same question differently on the same artifact, the
  question is under-specified, not the examiners.** Rewrite the evidence part first; it is
  almost always the missing half.
- **When a question could be answered from the brief rather than the artifact, it is the
  wrong question.** Rephrase it about what is visible in the stored thing, or move it to the
  instrument that legitimately reads intent.
- **When a question starts firing on nearly every artifact of a class, read it as a
  production signal before a quality one.** A universally failed question usually names a
  step that nobody in the pipeline performs.

## When not to use it

- **Where a specification supplies a number.** Loudness, budget counts, resolution, frame
  timings: state the figure and its unit. A question here trades an agreed answer for a
  discussion.
- **For anything a cap must catch.** Disqualifiers are recognitions, not judgments, and the
  two mechanisms lose their authority when mixed.
- **Where the evidence is not captured.** A question about motion asked of a single still is
  not a stricter criterion, it is an invitation to imagine the artifact. Either the behaviour
  is stored with the piece or the question does not belong in this instrument.
- **As a substitute for incidents nobody has collected.** A new class with no accepted and
  rejected examples behind it produces questions that sound right and discriminate nothing.
  The interrogative form is what you reach for after the incidents, when a real dimension
  turns out to resist measurement.
