# A look chosen for its beauty, then stopped at the hands

mage-arena (public, github.com/xkazm04/mage-arena) chose a stylised hand-drawn concept for its seated duel,
then ran nineteen look rounds with two agent builders, local generators and model judges. Read at
`0dc4a18` on 2026-10-29.

## What the tree records

docs/concepts/GRAPHICS-PUSH-RETROSPECTIVE-2026-10-29.md:29 "### 1. The target art was chosen before anyone asked whether our toolkit could make it"
docs/concepts/GRAPHICS-PUSH-RETROSPECTIVE-2026-10-29.md:45 "### 2. We polished the cheap layers first and the hardest asset last"
docs/concepts/GRAPHICS-PUSH-RETROSPECTIVE-2026-10-29.md:55 "### 3. The registry's own stop rule was not applied"

## What happened

- The rounds were ordered by builder speed: backdrop, sand, sky, effects.
- The player's hands, the most-seen asset in a hands-first game, got a dedicated pass only in the second
  round of the second route. Over eight rounds they moved through wood grain, plastic, cardboard and
  smeared highlights, and never read as drawn.
- The opponent, a generated painted card, held its identity but never gained volume.
- The frame combined three media (a painted plate, generated sprites and 3D hands) and read as a
  composite throughout.

The project was decommissioned under the bar.

## Verdict: not run

No slice was built, so this is the retrospective case for the technique, not a measurement of it. The
retrospective's estimate is that a slice of the hands and one animated opponent clip, judged by the owner
in motion, would have shown the same ceiling before any polish round.

## Deviation

None in the tree: the retrospective is committed. The return condition for an experiment verdict is a
slice run before a future look is chosen.
