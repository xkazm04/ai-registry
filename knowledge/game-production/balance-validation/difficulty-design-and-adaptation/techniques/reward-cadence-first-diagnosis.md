# Reward-cadence-first diagnosis

The concern: a section players report as too hard, too grindy or "a slog"
has two candidate causes that produce the same complaint — the challenge
curve is genuinely mis-shaped, or the *rewards* are too sparse or too
clumped across it. Restructuring a curve is expensive and touches
everything downstream; re-spacing rewards is cheap and touches almost
nothing. The technique is an ordering rule: **audit reward frequency and
placement across the complained-about span before restructuring any
curve**, because sparse or clumped rewards mimic curve problems, and a
curve fix applied to a cadence problem buys a re-tune and keeps the
complaint.

## Procedure

1. **Plot reward events against the span** — every drop, unlock,
   checkpoint, currency grant and narrative beat, on the same axis as the
   challenge curve. The instrument is the overlay, not either line alone.
2. **Look for droughts and clumps.** A drought reads as difficulty (effort
   without acknowledgement); a clump reads as anticlimax (acknowledgement
   without effort). Both arrive as pacing complaints with no vocabulary of
   their own.
3. **Interleave fixed and variable schedules.** A span rewarded only on
   fixed milestones telegraphs its own emptiness between them; one
   rewarded only variably produces droughts by chance. Interleaving the
   two prevents the clump-and-drought texture that either alone drifts
   into.
4. **Only then touch the curve.** If the cadence audit comes back clean —
   even spacing, no droughts, and the complaint persists — the curve has
   earned the diagnosis, and the four-term decomposition says which of its
   terms to move.

## When not to use this

A hard *wall* — one encounter with a measured failure spike — is not a
cadence problem and the audit will correctly find nothing; go straight to
the encounter's own numbers. And do not let the cheap fix become the only
fix: a genuinely mis-shaped curve papered over with rewards produces a
game that pays players to endure it, which the retention data will
eventually report less politely.
