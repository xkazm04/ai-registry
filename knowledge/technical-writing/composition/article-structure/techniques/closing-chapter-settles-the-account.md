# Closing chapter settles the account

The concern: the last section is read by two kinds of reader, the one who finished and the
one who skipped to the end to decide whether the post was worth it. A conclusion that
restates the headings in general terms ("X is a complex topic with many trade-offs")
serves neither and is the section most often quoted back as evidence a post was hollow.
**The closing chapter restates what was established in concrete numbers, returns to the
opening scene, and names what the reader should now do differently.**

## The three moves

1. **The recap in numbers.** Three to five lines, each a finding the body established,
   each with its number, each traceable to the same source the body used
   ([every number has a source and a date](../../../_laws.md#every-number-has-a-source-and-a-date)).
   No number appears here for the first time.
2. **The return.** One paragraph that restates the result as a fact about the opening
   scene's object: what the message, the request or the invoice turned out to show. This is
   where the through-line pays off, and it settles the question the opening raised
   ([the opening promises, the close settles](../../../_laws.md#the-opening-promises-the-close-settles)).
3. **The changed action.** Two or three specific things a reader would do differently, each
   concrete enough to start on: measure X per locale before a model upgrade, key the cache
   on normalized text, price quotas in messages rather than tokens. Each should correspond
   to an outcome the content preview promised.

## Procedure

- Draft the close from the preview's outcomes and the through-line's per-section numbers,
  not from memory of the body. Tick each outcome off as a line in the close settles it.
- Keep a summarizing figure here only if it is the post's integrating figure (a request
  path that shows every mechanism at once); a figure repeated from the body adds length,
  not closure.
- Read the close alone, without the body. A reader who only reads this section should come
  away with the result, its size and the next step.

## Decision rules

- **When the close needs a new fact to land, move that fact into the body.** The close
  settles; it does not introduce.
- **When a changed action is generic ("consider the trade-offs"), replace it or cut it.**
  An action a reader cannot start is a tip, and generic tips are what the reader skipped to
  the end to avoid.
- **When the evidence was mixed, the close says so.** Settling the account includes the
  part the evidence did not settle; a close that resolves a contested question the body
  left open has overstated the result.
- **Do not end on a rhetorical flourish.** The last line is either the final changed action
  or the last fact about the opening object; an inspirational sentence after it is the
  generated-prose closer every editor cuts.

## When not to use it

Very short posts, where the opening and the result are a screen apart and a recap would
repeat lines the reader can still see; there one closing sentence that names the next step
is enough.
