# Depth by replacement

The concern: a review asks for more depth, and the easiest response is to add sections.
The post grows by half, the new material lands after the point where most readers have
already left, and the depth reaches nobody. **Hold the length; buy depth by replacing
flat material, and by moving structured material out of prose into figures and tables.**
([depth by replacement, not addition](../../../_laws.md#depth-by-replacement-not-addition))

## What to cut first

A working order (judgment, not a measured ranking):

1. **Sustained analogies.** An analogy carried through several paragraphs costs every
   reader a translation and fits worse as the mechanism gets specific. It is also spread
   across sections, so cutting it frees space everywhere at once.
2. **Paragraph-length definitions.** Replace each with a clause (see term-in-a-clause).
3. **Prose that restates a figure.** If the figure shows it and the caption states its
   message, the paragraph that describes the figure again is redundant; keep only the
   sentence that interprets it.
4. **Generic background.** History an expert knows and a newcomer does not need to act on.
   Keep one line if it earns a contrast ("an older tokenizer split this word into eight
   pieces").
5. **Multi-paragraph comparisons and sequences.** Three or more paragraphs comparing
   systems, listing where a cost is collected, or walking a sequence are a table or a flow
   figure that was never drawn
   ([figures abstract, prose explains](../../../_laws.md#figures-abstract-prose-explains)).
   Moving them frees prose for reasoning and usually shortens the read.

## What to spend it on

The four layers of four-layer-depth: the mechanism stated as a predictive rule, the
production consequence in a named kind of system, the limit with its counter-evidence, and
the position the post has earned. Plus the non-obvious measured details an expert reader
came for (see non-obvious-detail-selection).

## Procedure

- Record the word count of the prose before the revision. Set the target at the same
  number, or lower.
- Make the cuts first, as a separate pass, and only then draft the new material into the
  freed space. Drafting first and cutting after reliably ends longer.
- Recompute the read time after moving material into tables and figures; tables carry
  words too, and the preview has to state what the reader will actually spend.
- Track each replacement as "cut X, added Y" in the run's notes, so a reviewer can see that
  depth rose by substitution and check that nothing load-bearing went with the cuts.

## Decision rules

- **When a cut removes the only explanation a newcomer had of a term, put a clause back.**
  The goal is depth for the expert without abandoning the newcomer.
- **When the post cannot reach the required depth at its current length, split it**, rather
  than letting it grow. Two posts each read to the end beat one read halfway.
- **Do not move reasoning into a figure to save words.** A figure that carries a causal
  argument in sentences has failed as a figure; see the `figures-and-tables` subject.

## When not to use it

When a review asks for depth *and* explicitly lifts the length constraint, adding is
legitimate. The default for a post that was praised for its length is to keep it.
