# Non-obvious detail selection

The concern: the facts that appear in every post on a topic are the ones an expert already
has. A post assembled from them is accurate and redundant, and an expert decides that in
the first section. **For each section, find at least one detail that an expert would not
have known, could not have derived, and can check.**

## What qualifies

A non-obvious detail is:

- **Specific**: a position, a count, a version, an exception, not a tendency.
- **Measured or primary**: from the post's own measurement or from a primary source such
  as a standard, a technical report or the system's own files, dated
  ([every number has a source and a date](../../../_laws.md#every-number-has-a-source-and-a-date)).
- **Current**: true of the systems a reader uses now, with older behaviour only as labelled
  history ([current data or labelled history](../../../_laws.md#current-data-or-labelled-history)).
- **Consequential**: it changes what a practitioner would do. A curious fact with no
  consequence is trivia.

Typical shapes that qualify: where in an ordered resource something first appears; an
exception in a standard that inverts the common assumption; a regression in a newer
version; two common estimates that fail in opposite directions; an ancestry between two
systems that explains a shared weakness; a formal and an informal variant that differ in
cost.

## Procedure

1. List the five facts every existing post on the topic repeats. These are the floor: state
   them briefly or assume them, do not lead with them.
2. Go one level below each: the primary source behind the common summary, the system's own
   published files, the standard's exceptions list. Non-obvious details live at that level.
3. Where no source gives the detail and it is measurable, measure it, and log the command
   and versions (see the `evidence-and-sources` subject).
4. Place each detail in the section whose mechanism it illustrates, ideally as the number
   at the end of a sentence where it carries the stress.

## Decision rules

- **When a detail is surprising but cannot be sourced or measured, leave it out.** An
  unverifiable surprise is exactly the claim an expert will check first.
- **When a detail contradicts the post's thesis, it is the most valuable one found.** It goes
  in as counter-evidence, not out.
- **Prefer one well-explained detail to five listed ones.** A list of non-obvious facts
  without their mechanism reads as trivia; one fact with its mechanism reads as expertise.

## When not to use it

Introductory posts that state in their preview that they cover the fundamentals; there the
common facts are the content. Even then, one non-obvious detail per post is what makes an
expert share it with a newcomer.
