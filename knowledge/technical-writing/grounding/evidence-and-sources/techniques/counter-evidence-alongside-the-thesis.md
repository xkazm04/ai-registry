# Counter-evidence alongside the thesis

The concern: a post whose sources all agree is either about a settled fact, in which case it
is probably not worth writing, or it has not looked. Expert readers know the contrary
papers; when a post omits them, they discount the whole page, including the parts that were
right. **Research the evidence against the thesis as deliberately as the evidence for it,
and present both together, with the limits, where the claim is made.**
([a claim travels with its counter-evidence](../../../_laws.md#a-claim-travels-with-its-counter-evidence))

## Procedure

1. **Search against the thesis.** For each central claim, run at least one search phrased to
   find the opposite: "X does not...", "no correlation between...", "X is shrinking". Record
   what was searched even when nothing was found.
2. **Classify each source** as for, against, or limit. A limit is evidence that the claim
   holds only under conditions: one corpus, one language family, one model generation, one
   price tier.
3. **Present contested evidence as a designed table**: the claim in the first column, then
   each study with its tag (for, against, limit), its scale (what was measured, on how
   much) and its source number. A table makes the balance visible at a glance, which prose
   about conflicting studies rarely does.
4. **Say what the evidence does not settle.** If the studies disagree, the post says so in
   the body and in the closing chapter; it does not resolve a contest the evidence leaves
   open.
5. **Add counter-evidence against the article's own measurement.** If an independent study
   measured the same thing and got a different number, report both, with the likely reason
   for the difference (corpus, version, method).

## Decision rules

- **When the counter-evidence is stronger than the thesis, change the thesis.** A post
  that keeps a claim its own research contradicts is advocacy.
- **Weight by design, not by count.** One controlled study outweighs several observational
  ones; the table shows scale and design so the reader can weigh them too.
- **Counter-evidence is not a hedge.** "Results may vary" is not counter-evidence; a named
  study with its finding is.
- **Look for the direction the thesis did not expect.** The most useful counter-evidence is
  often not "the effect is absent" but "the effect also runs the other way" (a cost that
  can rise for the favoured group too, a newer version that regresses).

## When not to use it

Posts that report a settled specification or a reproducible bug; there the evidence is the
standard or the reproduction, and manufacturing a controversy would mislead.
