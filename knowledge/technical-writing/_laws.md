# Laws - technical writing

Cross-cutting invariants of the long-form technical article. Techniques cite these by
anchor; they are not subjects and get no folder.

## <a id="every-number-has-a-source-and-a-date"></a>Every number has a source and a date

A number in the text, a cell in a table and a bar in a figure each trace to one numbered
source, and that source carries the date the page was read or the measurement was run.
A number without both is an assertion in a number's clothing: a reader cannot check it,
and the author cannot tell, six months later, whether it is still true.

## <a id="current-data-or-labelled-history"></a>Current data, or labelled history

The comparison a post draws is between the things a reader can use today. Older systems,
older prices and older benchmarks appear only as history, labelled as history and given
no more room than the line they earn. A table built on last generation's figures is not
a weaker argument; it is an argument about a world the reader no longer lives in.

## <a id="a-claim-travels-with-its-counter-evidence"></a>A claim travels with its counter-evidence

The thesis is stated together with where it fails, what contradicts it and what the
evidence does not settle. A post that presents only the supporting half is persuasion,
and an expert reader discounts the whole page the moment they recall the half that was
left out.

## <a id="report-the-topic-not-the-author"></a>Report the topic, not the author

The subject of the sentences is the mechanism, the system or the measurement, never the
writer's afternoon. Results are stated about the thing measured; how they were obtained is
stated impersonally and logged in full where a reader can repeat it.

## <a id="figures-abstract-prose-explains"></a>Figures abstract, prose explains

A figure turns a mechanism, a comparison or a sequence into shapes, positions and labels.
A paragraph carries reasoning, qualification and story. Each fails at the other's job: a
figure full of sentences is a paragraph in a box, and three paragraphs carrying a
comparison are a table nobody drew.

## <a id="depth-by-replacement-not-addition"></a>Depth by replacement, not addition

When a post must go deeper, the length stays and the flat material goes: the analogy, the
definition an expert skips, the paragraph that repeats the figure. Depth bought by adding
pages is paid for by the reader who stops halfway.

## <a id="the-opening-promises-the-close-settles"></a>The opening promises, the close settles

The opening scene and the content preview make promises: a question, a cost, a set of
things the reader will be able to do. The closing chapter pays each one back in concrete
terms and returns to the scene. A promise the close does not settle is a debt the reader
notices; a close that settles nothing the opening raised is a summary.

## <a id="check-the-page-where-it-is-read"></a>Check the page where it is read

A post is verified on the surface its reader will use, in both colour schemes and at the
narrow and the wide viewport, after it has been translated to the target platform's
capabilities. A source that renders well in the authoring tool proves nothing about the
page a reader opens.

## <a id="a-check-names-a-property-and-a-span"></a>A check names a property and a span

A quality finding about a draft names a text property and the span that shows it: a
first-person sentence, an undated number, a figure with four lines of prose inside it. It
never rests on a detector score or on a judgment that the text "sounds generated". A
finding that cannot point at a span cannot be fixed, and a fix that cannot be checked
again was not one.
