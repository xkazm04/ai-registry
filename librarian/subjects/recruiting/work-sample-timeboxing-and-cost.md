**Corrected in the golden path and techniques:**
- **"A half-day take-home costs about half the invited senior pool" is unsourced.** It is a code comment in kp ("UAT M8", copied into the policy constant's own comment), and the old application called it "measured, not assumed". No public measurement of take-home non-completion against length was found. The evidence: an interviewing.io survey of almost 700 engineers (2024): 6% outright refuse, 66% complete all or most, unchanged among seniors; a negligible relationship between interview performance or CV and willingness to do one, and its authors conclude take-homes do not necessarily weed out the best. One Dropbox recollection: about 20% did not complete, the less competitive more likely to finish because they had no competing offers. Both self-report or anecdote, neither conditioned on length. The golden path now argues from time inequality, which does not need the number, and the seniority technique says "may filter, find out".
- **"Strong practitioners are the most likely to decline" is not established.** Reframed as leverage (competing offers), with the survey against it.
- **The equity claim is inference for time, measured for grading.** A vendor analysis of about 384,000 submissions (2021-22): with non-anonymous grading Black candidates 7.4-12.4% and Hispanic candidates 2-7.5% less likely to pass an interview, largely erased when anonymised. It did not test the time argument. Its authors' second point, that candidates with more free time may ignore the limit, is what the overrun measurement is for.
- **"Routinely half the real time"** kept as a fair central case, no longer a constant: 1.6x for self-forecast, worse for expert-on-novice, no study of an exercise author against a cold candidate.
- **Hour figures are convention.** More than 80% of that survey said four hours or less, a plurality two. No study relates work-sample length to validity (the lane that searched found none, the blind lane knew of none). The golden path's "it improves the measurement slightly" is now stated as an assumption.
- **Pay.** "Roughly half a day, at any level" is a policy line, not a legal one. No statute or agency guidance in the US, UK or EU sets an hours threshold. UK minimum-wage guidance (read): the test is recruitment purpose, trial length against need, resemblance to the job, whether observed; a trial longer than one day is "likely" to attract the wage, a few hours "may be reasonable" unpaid; its worked example of a likely-paid trial is an 8-hour unobserved shift before interview. US turns on who benefits. Seniority enters neither test. 58% of the survey think they should be paid, 4% ever were. New: put the exercise after a screen. The IP position is sound practice, not settled law (no dispute found; ownership is inference).
- **Accommodation.** No-documentation extra time is a policy choice above the legal floor (EEOC, read: "may ask ... reasonable documentation" when the disability and the need are not obvious). One review found extended time lifted everyone's scores and disabled students' somewhat more, on educational tests. "Widening the window recovers a large share" was unmeasured and now says so.
- **Observation.** "A candidate who suspects being watched performs rather than works" was the technique's reason for disclosure. The evidence is one small randomised study of a person watching live (48 students, performance "half as well", authors: sample too small for firm conclusions). A follow-up on recorded unattended sessions and reviews of passive monitoring point the other way (search-summary level). Disclosure is now argued from entitlement and law (GDPR Art. 13 read; NYC LL144, Illinois video AI, Colorado from 2027; EU emotion-inference ban read to reach recruitment), not from a measurement effect.
- **CGNAT.** The rate-limit rule stands with a sourced mechanism (Cloudflare, read: one address may represent "hundreds or even thousands of users"; such addresses rate limited about three times as often). No user-weighted share found; none claimed.
