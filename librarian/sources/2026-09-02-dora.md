## Landed

### 1. `liveness-and-heartbeats` - "The clock arms at first contact, not at spawn"

The technique owned thresholds, signals and the stalled-versus-slow
distinction and never said when the clock starts. The source documents the
arming rule for both of its clocks (node liveness arms at first subscribe,
seeded from spawn but gated by connection; input deadlines arm on the first
message) and the failure each rule prevents (a cold start longer than the
timeout becomes an unescapable restart loop; an idle-at-startup input trips
the breaker before anything was owed) and, in its June audit, the failure of
not resetting the record on respawn (a restarted node inherits the stale
timestamp and is killed before it registers). Corroborated by
training-data convergence (readiness-gated liveness probes and initial-delay
settings are the same rule in container orchestration) and by the fleet
tree that already arms staleness on first success (a case-loop state model
that reports "not assessed" until a successful run exists). Measured in-run
with a three-arm harness; the numbers are in the technique and the
application. Candidate #2 folded in as the input-side clause: a staleness
detector belongs only on a channel that promised continuity.

### 2. `persistence-and-migration` - two clauses under "The versioned shape"

From the changelog: a persisted store's schema version 2 silently meant two
shapes because a field was added without a bump; the encoding was positional
so `default` could not backfill; the reader swallowed the decode failure as a
warning and terminal records vanished from listings; the fix was to bump to 3
and refuse *every* v2 store, including the ones whose rows still decoded,
because the number could not tell them apart. Then the same lesson twice more
(3 -> 4 for a barrier field; the move to a new encoding with a handshake
version in both directions because "both encodings are positional, so a
third-party subscriber would misparse rather than error"). The technique had
the migration chain and "each step is total over its input version" and
nothing about an input version that names two populations. Corroborated by
training-data convergence (never reuse a wire-format version; positional
formats require version negotiation) and, negatively, by the gravity tree's
policy comment, which is honest precisely because its encoding is keyed.

### 3. `gate-liveness` - "Assert the oracle, not only the instrument"

The QA report's meta-finding #10: a binary component scored 5.8% under
package-scoped mutation testing because its real tests live in a
workspace-level end-to-end suite belonging to another package; `test_workspace
= true` turned 21 missed into 21 caught in fourteen minutes; the report's
generalisation is "your first hypothesis should be 'I'm measuring the wrong
thing'; confirm by the smallest possible controlled experiment". The
technique's instrument assertions covered the target population only;
`excess-indicts-the-instrument` covers the other direction. Corroborated by
the fleet: two trees repaired the *target* population of the same score the
day before this run and left the oracle population at the default, and one
of them spawns eight scripts from tests that no coverage instrument can see.

### 4. `declared-deviation-register` - "Key the entry by the finding's identity, never by its position"

The QA report's meta-finding #4: an equivalent-mutant waiver written as a
`file:line:col` regex silently stops matching when unrelated edits shift the
code; the committed config now says "line/column numbers intentionally
omitted" and keys on the mutation's name inside its function. The technique
had the four-field entry and the checker's third outcome and nothing about
the join key. Corroborated by the law it cites (`identity-survives-reuse`:
index-based keys break under insert) and by the fleet, whose two ratchets
and one bundle budget all key by name.

## Already covered - catches, verified by reading the subjects

- **Restart window with reset after stability** (#3) -> `backoff-design`
  ("ladders reset only after demonstrated stability, never on first
  contact") and the golden path's "N restarts per window" is the same
  ladder. The source's best-practice paragraph is a restatement.
- **Blanket boot-fail on coordinator restart** (#5) -> `terminal-state-recovery`
  names this exact one-liner as the anti-pattern ("stamps failed onto
  everything non-terminal at boot ... destroys paused jobs, discards
  resumable work, one generic reason over N fates"). The source's own
  later changes agree with the corpus: it added a persisted barrier flag
  and a "Recovering" status precisely because the blanket fail lost work.
  Recorded as corroboration; nothing to write.
- **Founding baseline inflated by scope** (#9) -> `excess-indicts-the-instrument`
  ("the founding population gets the question before it gets the
  baseline") and `ratchet-design` ("the baseline names its counter").
  Second sighting of the exact trap - a ratchet whose first number counted
  test code - worth its line here for the next convergence check.
- **Adversarial review by a different model** (#12) -> `oracle-before-gate`
  ("a second party with standing to disagree - a different model") and
  fleet-orchestration's independent-panel section.
- **Enumeration failure spelled as empty** (#16) -> `failure-not-empty-success`.
  The changelog's `clean` entry: "if the persisted-store enumeration
  itself fails, hard-fail the request ... so the CLI never reports a
  misleading 'nothing to clean'". Third independent sighting; the law
  needs nothing.
- **"What would fail if this job were removed"** and **flaky tests teach
  retry over diagnosis** (#17) -> `gate-liveness` (time since last red)
  and `flake-lifecycle`.
- **Tautological tests, mutation testing** (part of #8's source) ->
  `negative-control-tests` is the manual form; the automated form adds
  scale, not a rule.

## Leads (banked, return conditions attached)

- **Infrastructure as a forcing function** (#11). Three of three
  production bugs in the QA report were found by the human or agent
  *preparing* a tool - writing the tests a memory checker would run, hand-
  surveying call sites to choose fuzz targets - and none by the tool.
  "Write the test code to use each new tool, even if you don't run the
  tool." Doctrine-shaped, no bundle subject owns "how to adopt a
  verification instrument". **Return condition:** a second first-party
  account reporting the same split (bugs found in preparation vs by the
  tool) with counts, or a fleet project adopting a new analysis tool where
  the preparation could be measured.
- **Verification tiers by latency budget, not tool category** (PR gate
  under fifteen minutes, nightly under four hours, pre-release in days).
  `gate-laddering` orders rungs; whether it names the budget as the
  organising axis was not checked. **Return condition:** the next run that
  opens `gate-laddering` for any reason reads it for the axis.
- **Six failure modes of AI-authored code** (tautological tests,
  unreachable defensive code, invariant violations, uniform confidence,
  architectural drift across sessions, no cross-session memory) with a
  gate per mode. Four of the six map to existing law or technique
  (`silent-state-is-ungoverned`, `negative-control-tests`, drift ->
  architectural fitness tests); the list as a *taxonomy* has no home.
  **Return condition:** a second independent taxonomy of the same shape,
  or a managed project measuring one of the modes.

## Reusable engineering, noted and not ported

- `scripts/qa/unwrap-budget.sh` brace-balances every `#[cfg(test)]`
  block with an awk state machine that also tracks raw-string spans, so a
  test fixture containing a `}` at column 0 cannot end the block early -
  a counter whose predicate is enforced in code. The registry's own
  counters are file-scoped and need no equivalent.
- `.cargo/mutants.toml` documents each waiver with the reasoning that makes
  the mutant equivalent and the condition under which the waiver must be
  removed - the register entry's "motivation stated as a cost" in a config
  comment. Noted as the form; the fleet has no mutation baseline yet.
- The QA report's case-study table (finding, caught by, severity, commit)
  with the explicit line "zero of the three production bugs were caught by
  the tool they were nominally targeting" - the honest form of a gate
  adoption report. Lead #1 carries it.
