## Workflow recovered

The creator installs a viewport add-on and connects an agent bridge, builds
a landscape and placeholder product from a sketch-derived prompt, then
supplies a storyboard and product reference for the model and animation.
The final generation uses the Blender animation for motion and the product
photo for appearance. Scene generation and video generation are distinct
steps with distinct outputs; a saved editable scene does not prove that
the final generated video preserves geometry, labels or timing.

## Primary verification and live test

The [vendor's Blender page](https://higgsfield.ai/plugins/blender), fetched
2026-09-10, specifies Blender 5.1 or later, a signed-in online account,
cloud inference and a bridge endpoint distinct from its regular asset MCP
endpoint. It describes scene-builder output as editable scene data and
shows generation prices before submission. These are vendor-stated
requirements, not results of a live bridge call in this run.

The locally executed Blender binary reported **4.2.1 LTS**. No Blender
connector was exposed to this session. The standard local user extension
directory contained a different add-on; no Higgsfield installation was
found there. Consequently the exact vendor-supported route was blocked.
No upgrade, account sign-in, plugin installation or paid generation occurred.

The operator requested and subsequently confirmed a **generic bottle** test.
An isolated background process generated a new scene through Blender's
Python API. This is a partial replication of the editable-scene stage,
using direct execution instead of the vendor bridge:

- A generic bottle body, separate cap, replaceable label surface and authored
  editable text; a pedestal, hills, ocean and camera.
- Four animated objects: product assembly, cap, camera and camera target.
- A saved scene with 17 objects, including 10 mesh objects; no external
  product photograph or inferred dimensions.
- A 12-second preview, independently probed as H.264, 384 by 384,
  24 frames per second and 288 decoded frames; a 640 by 640 rendered still.
- A paired edit probe changed one camera keyframe by 0.25 m and verified
  an unchanged hash of mesh vertex coordinates, then restored the keyframe.
  This proves mesh-data preservation only, not unchanged rendered pixels,
  all scene properties, or a comparison against a generative reroll.
- Separate-cap travel exceeded 0.59 m; the product reached its specified
  pedestal height. A second process reopened the saved scene and checked
  its object count, animation action, cap travel and frame range.
- Visual inspection of the still and six sampled preview frames confirmed
  a readable proxy, floating-cap sequence, assembly, close-up and pullback.
  This is a coarse blockout: no final fade, realistic ocean surface or
  exact product-identity comparison was claimed.

The initial reopen encountered sandbox-denied writes from an existing
unrelated extension. Repeating the same check with filesystem permission
succeeded with normal preferences. This was a sandbox restriction, not a
content verdict or an established add-on defect.

The build script, scene, still, preview, storyboard and measurements remain
local as requested test deliverables. They are not published application
evidence or counted as a managed-project A/B adoption. The fleet resolved
three relevant consumers; no consumer seam was changed because no new
