## Leads

- **Watermarking.** The claim is that closed-weight providers watermark generated
  text, that open-weight models served by third parties do not, and that only
  *new* model versions carry it — so an older open checkpoint stays clean
  indefinitely. Anchor `[00:05:56]`, `[00:14:00]`. Stated with confidence and no
  evidence, by a source with a commercial interest in the conclusion, and it is a
  strong factual claim about detectability. **Return condition:** a primary — a
  provider's own documentation or a published detection paper — states the
  mechanism and its scope. Do not land this from a second source of the same class.
- **"Code is content."** When inference is cheap enough to give away behind a rate
  limit, an interactive artifact that answers a question outperforms an article
  that answers it, because the artifact is the thing a chat interface cannot
  reproduce from a prompt. Anchor `[00:22:32]`–`[00:25:54]`. Survives the strip
  test, and the registry has no bundle that owns content strategy, so it has no
  home today. **Return condition:** a bundle acquires a growth or content-strategy
  category, or a managed project ships an interactive artifact as a funnel and the
  question becomes an application.

## What the apply phase found, and it is the run's second story

Four landings, four rows, and **three came back `not-better` because the fleet was
already ahead of the corpus.** That is not a failure of the technique and it is not
the same thing as a refutation, which the ledger's vocabulary cannot currently say.

The strongest of the three inverted the direction of the whole run. The pairing
technique told a disconnected comparison graph to report components rather than a
leaderboard; a fleet project publishes exactly such a graph — a pooled leaderboard
whose contributors each measure on their own private cases, disconnected by
construction and permanently so, because the privacy design that makes the buckets
publishable is what stops cases from travelling. Suppressing the order there means
shipping nothing. The tree's answer is to keep the order and widen the interval
with a between-source variance term, and its own module notes the defect that
replaced: naive pooling made five contributors who *disagreed* produce a narrower
interval than five who agreed. **That answer was written back into the technique
as its second branch**, with the discriminator being whether the ranking is an
internal selection or the deliverable itself. The corpus is better because a
managed project disagreed with it.

The other two were prior conformance: a benchmark product that selects its judge
on *spread* — the gap between what it gives good and bad answers — which is the
tie amendment operationalized and measured; and an extraction eval that pins the
production prompt into the fixture and guards the pin with a test named for the
failure it prevents.

The one `better` is the fourth axis, and it is the sharpest because the tree
argues for the distinction and then declines to route on it: its own module header
separates structured assessment from free-form text because the non-scan surfaces
need their own schemas, and the same header says it is not a second provider path,
so one function resolves the model and one zero-argument function reads the
reasoning budget for all three call classes. Shipped as a row in that project's
own ledger, on its active branch, with a pathspec and no push.
