## The finding: a register can claim a witness

The ranking format is built on three moves: a capture register per clip
("a different phone, room, voice, no narrator" `[00:07:12]`), one spoken
reaction line from an unseen filmer per clip (`[00:08:02]`), and deliberately
varied signatures so the set reads as "five separate strangers who each just
happen to film a sleeping cat" `[00:07:12]`. The source presents all three as
craft and never mentions disclosure, although it is publishing photoreal
scenes that did not occur to a platform that names exactly that as a
disclosure category.

The corpus had the grammar and not this carrier. `evidence-bound-visuals`
reads marks for claims and names an invented-referent family with two
carriers: the element (`performer-claims-need-a-person`, which triggers on
an on-screen performer's first-person line about a product) and the position
(`slots-claim-their-subjects`). `screenshots-claim-a-record` covers a
rendered interface. None owns footage whose **register** claims a camera, a
filmer and an event. None owns the third move either, which is the new
mechanism: varied capture signatures **manufacture independence**, so one
generator is read as several witnesses. That is fabricated convergence, the
same shape the corpus polices for sources.

**Convergence, not a single source.** The 2026-09-08 note
(`seedance-prompt-showcase`) banked a lead, "degrading generated output to
conceal that it is generated", with the return condition "when a second
independent source treats artifact-concealment as a technique rather than an
aside". This source builds a whole format on it. Different author, different
run, neither saw the other. The condition fired, and that note is updated in
the same commit.

**Corroboration.** Training-data convergence (the rule was reachable without
the source) plus one primary fetched in-run: the platform's own creator
disclosure policy lists "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually
occur" as requiring disclosure, exempts clearly unrealistic content, and puts
photoreal labels on the player rather than only in the description. That is
the same line the technique draws, reached from integrity policy rather than
from the grammar of marks. The upper layer states it without names and
leaves policy applicability outside the subject, as its neighbour does.

**The seam hunt corrected the draft (a second source).** systedo-case
generates ad images in six photographic styles (portrait, fashion, bokeh,
cinematic...). Reading them showed that photoreal is the wrong trigger: a
**produced** register is read as staged and claims a photographer, not a
witness. The discriminator is produced versus captured. It went into the
technique's "when not to use" before landing.
