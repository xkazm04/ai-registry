## Candidates

### 1. Rank landed features by the repairs they needed after a freeze - ACCEPTED

`[40 msgs / 22 authors]` "I asked Claude to evaluate which v19 patches were the
scariest based on the number and type of bugs fixed post-freeze... are any of
them likely to still be in bad enough shape that we should consider reverting
them before v19 ships?"

Not a poll for fear, which is what the subject line suggested and what the
title-based read predicted. A model mined the project's own post-freeze fix
record, ranked **features** by defect count *and severity class*, and the
ranking became a revert-decision agenda.

`revert-linkage` opens with a completeness claim - "a revert is the only failure
signal available from change history alone" - and its own *when not to use this*
names the population it cannot read: "a team that reverts readily and a team
that fixes forward differ in policy, not in reliability." The repair stream is
the second signal, in the same material, for exactly that population. The golden
path repeated the claim and filed fix-forward under *revert undercounting*
rather than as a distinct signal.

The thread draws its own discriminator, which is the half that made this worth
writing rather than restating:

- **scope erosion** ("cut back so far we would not have accepted it") -> rejected
  in-thread as "an argument for blocking progress by making perfect the enemy of
  good"
- **defect tail** ("a long tail of bugs we haven't found yet, in pretty critical
  areas") -> "I'll prepare a revert", said by a **co-author of the feature being
  pulled**, which is the strongest form that assent takes

And its own failure mode, in-source: a third correspondent's "Uh, I am confused.
We are now considering reverting these?" - the list went from discussion agenda
to revert in 25 minutes.

Landed: `delivery-analytics/techniques/post-landing-repair-density.md`, a
correction to the golden path's completeness claim, and an amendment to
`revert-linkage` re-framing it as the *terminal* signal that shares recognition
and linkage machinery with the new one.

### 2. Reclassifying an impossible error is not repairing it - ACCEPTED

`[5 msgs]` A patch proposed assigning real error codes to conditions where the
regression suite reached the internal-error class. The objection, from the
project's most senior committer:

> "I'm not on board with these proposed changes... They're basically band-aids
> rather than fixes of the root problems. That is, if someone passes OID 0 to
> relation_open, that IS an internal bug; labeling it otherwise is a lie and
> does nothing to fix the real problem at the caller level. A lot of these other
> proposed changes have the same whiff of blaming the messenger rather than
> looking for the root cause."

A **contradicted pick, kept.** The source located something true - a class whose
declared rate is zero makes the suite you already run a free detector - while
proposing the wrong repair for it. The finding is the fork, not the detector:
reclassify only when the declaration was wrong, and defaulting to reclassify is
`deletion-is-not-repair` in the taxonomy's own vocabulary.

`taxonomy-design` owns the closed set and the catch-all; nothing owned the
member whose entire value is that it never fires, or what to do when it does.
Landed: `error-handling/techniques/reclassification-is-not-repair.md` plus a
golden-path section.
