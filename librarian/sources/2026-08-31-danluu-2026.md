## The class, and the calibration that governs the run

Structurally a reference index (a link page whose own text is 1,600 words of
titles), but every reference is the same person's first-party practitioner
account. So the lane's strongest triage signal — **within-index convergence,
deduped by author** — is structurally unavailable. A claim that recurs across
four of these posts is *one* observation, not four. Recurrence here is one
person repeating himself.

That capped what could land: first-party accounts authorize applications and
leads on their own, and techniques only via a primary or training-data
convergence. Every landing below took the second route; **zero of three
fetches were spent**, which is the fourth consecutive run where a first-party
source corroborated corpus-internally.

**Convergence with a sibling run, same day.** `brooker-2026-08-31` mined a
different single-author archive this afternoon and independently reached the
identical calibration — "every reference shares one author… within-index
convergence deduped by author is structurally unavailable here. Every
convergence in this run is n=1." Two runs, two sources, one rule about a
source class. That is the two-sighting bar, and it is a lesson, not a lead
(see LESSONS.md).

**What made this source unusual for its class:** he runs the experiments and
publishes protocol, numbers, *and* the places he was refuted — including a
headline of his own that inverted under checking. That is the strongest thing
a non-primary can carry, and it is why a 6-reference run landed 4 techniques.

## Accepted

**1. `candidate-write-access` — new technique, `eval-harness`.**
*(bug: benchpocalypse + pl-tokens appendix)* The system under test is an
agent holding the same shell the harness holds. Two write surfaces: the
instrument (reads the suite, edits the harness interface, picks its own
holdout) and the neighbours (one condition's agent writes state that scores
its successors). Anchor for the first: *"despite instructions to run rebar
benchmarks as they're run in [the suite], the LLM changed the interface"* —
1.4x faster became 1.5x slower. Anchor for the corrective, which is the
counterintuitive half: *"telling the LLM there's a holdout set worked better
than just telling the LLM to do generalized work or not overfit or cheat"*,
observed on two independent tasks, with the tell that the **visible score got
worse while the holdout score rose**. Anchor for the second surface: *"every
later execution (for every language) actually executed the first Go run's
executable"* — a published conclusion about language difficulty that was an
artifact of run order.

**2. `resolution-precondition` — new technique, `eval-harness`.**
*(ai-coding)* Reporting variance beside the mean is a rule about the number;
the artifact people act on is the order. Precondition: between-condition
spread must exceed within-condition SD before a ranking is published.
Anchor: within-condition SD 0.075, full best-to-worst spread 0.069 — *"less
than 1 standard deviation"* — at trial counts far above what any reader runs.
Carries the non-monotonic-effort corollary (*"GPT-5.6 Luna gets monotonically
worse with more effort… ultra is better than max… and actually cheaper"*).

**3. `repeatability-floor` — new technique, `judge-calibration-and-drift`
(llm-observability).** *(exercise-7)* The uncontended home, and the best
finding of the run because it is a **cross-bundle asymmetry**: the builder
side already carries a repeatability floor in `eval-harness/judge-stability`
("a 0.3 delta is noise if the judge disagrees with itself by 0.4"); the
operator side has kappa, a trust bar, per-cycle drop alerts and windowed
regression — and no floor at all, so both its detectors fire on unmeasured
re-score noise. Anchor: re-grading **one fixed artifact** 10× with the same
judge model flipped the published verdict **23%** of the time; official
differed from median 21%; a different judge model more than halved the
passes. The half that made it a technique rather than a number: repeatability
is **per dimension** — 32% / 5% / 3% across three dimensions of one rubric —
so a composite figure hides that the heaviest-weighted dimension is the
noisiest.

**4. `grounding-over-deliberation` — new technique, `agent-chaining`.**
*(ai-coding)* Home was contested and took the longest to settle: not
`remediation-handoff` (that subject is explicitly about the agent you cannot
watch), not `plan-review` (pre-authorship). `agent-chaining` wires steps and
says nothing about what a step is *worth* — the asymmetry. Anchors: causal
explanations wrong ~50% of the time with *"multiple independent rounds of
analysis"* not fixing it; forced execution removing most; *"just adding forced
checking to a simple prompt has a better success rate than having agents do
independent analyses and cross-check each other."* The corollary is the
opening story of the post — a fully convincing recorded repro produced inside
a constructed environment — which yields the rule that a review must look at
a **different view** than the one the artifact advertises.

**5. `comparison-modes` — amendment (self-referential opponent).** Kept as an
amendment rather than a new technique because the file already says *"it does
not compare across time unless the opponent is frozen too"*; what it lacked is
the named violation teams reach for deliberately when no external reference
exists. Anchor: *"an apparent Elo gain of 1000 or 2000 against prior versions
that isn't any better against humans or bots that play in a different style."*

## Leads (return conditions attached)

- **Dogfooding saturates.** An operator who has adapted to a defect cannot
  observe it; the mouse-ball story and the catalogue of habitual mitigations.
  `research-map` on "dogfooding" returns **no prior art anywhere in the
  corpus** — a real hole. Not landed: the home is genuinely unclear (a UX
  quality subject that may not exist yet) and one author's account is thin
  evidence for a subject. *Return when a second independent source describes
  measuring the workaround rather than the defect, or when a UX-quality
  subject exists to hold it.*
- **Audit yield is a rate, not a count.** *"I looked at one benchmark for
  maybe a minute or two and found an issue, then I looked at another
  benchmark for a minute and found another issue."* A non-decaying discovery
  rate is evidence of undiscovered defects; the reporting unit should be
  defects-per-inspection-minute. *Return when a second source reports an
  audit as a rate.*
- **Asymmetric verification cost as a review-process constraint.** *"Someone
  can spend seconds… generating something that takes people minutes to hours
  to understand… companies with poor norms for this kind of thing are really
  struggling with productivity."* Plausibly a `machine-paced-delivery`
  amendment. *Return when a connected project's review queue can be measured.*
- **Trivial-task effects have unknown sign at real size.** Three independent
  instances in this source (a token-efficiency claim, a prompt-mode claim, and
  the 2014 static-vs-dynamic literature) where an effect measured on
  sub-threshold tasks vanished or inverted at real size. Overlaps
  `scenario-design`'s representativeness rule; the sharper claim is that the
  small-task result is *not a weaker version of the same result*. *Return when
  a second source measures the same effect at two task sizes.*

## Untriaged — extracted, reached the table, never picked

Recorded with anchors so a later run does not re-derive them. **Nobody
verified these.**

1. **Memory-safety class defects are invisible to a functional suite.** A
   quick sanitizer pass found issues in all C and all-but-one C++ programs
   that the correctness score never saw; fixing them pushes cost past the
   memory-safe language and still leaves lower confidence.
2. **Reliability demand scales with the workflow, not the agent.** ~1 rule
   violation per 100 rule-agent-days is fine at 10 agents with review and
   catastrophic at 1000 shipping unreviewed. The *unit* (rule-agent-days) is
   the reusable part. Partial overlap with `agent-instruction-files`.
3. **Effort/tier axes are non-monotonic per task.** Landed as a corollary
   inside `resolution-precondition`; the routing consequence for
   `model-routing/effort-calibration` was not examined.
4. **Workload-specific optimization is now cheap.** Fit to your own traced
   workload with a holdout drawn from the same trace; 2% and 7% wins for
   minutes of human time.
5. **Randomized/property-based generation dominates hand-written tests per
   unit of effort**, and LLM-generated fuzzers have *"curiously bad"*
   coverage. Home would be `test-harness`, held by a sibling this run.
6. **Throwing code away and regenerating beats editing it in place**, and
   context-preserving loops beat context-clearing ones on the one task
   measured.
