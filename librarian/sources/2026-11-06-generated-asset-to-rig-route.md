## Banked wording

**Measure**, new technique `foot-contact-measured-not-requested` under `motion-quality-gating`, giving
the rubric's "structural tests" its referent. The wording needs its hound figures re-checked.

> # Foot contact is measured, not requested
>
> An instruction that feet must not slide or sink describes a result. It checks nothing, and the agent,
> solver or animator that received it reports success either way. Contact is a structural fact and costs
> one pass over the clip.
>
> ## The measure
>
> For each clip, evaluate the skinned mesh at every frame and take the vertices bound mainly to each
> foot. Read two numbers.
>
> - **Sink:** the lowest vertex against the floor the asset declares, per clip. A check on the rest pose
>   does not cover this. One asset passed "origin at the feet" at 0.1 mm and stood 3.2 cm under that
>   floor on frame one of every clip.
> - **Slide:** horizontal travel of the foot while it is within the contact height. Benchmarks for
>   human-scale motion treat 5 cm as contact and 0.2 m/s as skating. Scale both by the character's height
>   and state the values used. On a 1-unit character whose foot lifted 2.9 cm, an unscaled 5 cm read every
>   frame of a walk as contact.
>
> Read vertices, not bone ends. An interchange format may re-orient a bone's tail, and one such tail sat
> 17 cm under the floor at rest and produced a false reading.
>
> ## An in-place cycle has no slide of its own
>
> A walk keyed in place moves its stance foot backward on purpose. Slide exists only against the speed
> the character will travel at. So the test is whether the stance foot's speed is steady and matches that
> speed. In one sine-keyed cycle the stance foot averaged 0.36 body heights per second with a standard
> deviation of 0.16. No single travel speed plants that foot. Report the implied speed and the spread, and
> report slide as unmeasured until the travel speed is known.
>
> ## Decision rules
>
> - **When a planted action slides, say by how much.** One clip's record said paws "slide slightly".
>   Measured, they travelled 24 cm on an animal 1.36 m tall.
> - **When a contact number fails, fix the keys or add a foot lock, then re-measure.** A cleanup pass that
>   reports success has not shown anything.
> - **When a contact is deliberately broken, declare the frames.** A fall leaves the ground, and an
>   unlisted frame is a finding.
> - **Keep this out of the perceptual rubric.** A critic shown sampled frames gives a fuzzy answer to a
>   question with a crisp one.
>
> ## When not to use it
>
> On clips for a camera that never shows the feet, and on motion for a character with no ground contact.

**Repair**, new technique `pin-planted-contacts-after-the-reading` under
`generated-video-motion-extraction`, `use_when: [feet slide on a clip solved from video, adding a cleanup
pass after a markerless solve, a planted foot drifts on the target rig]`.

> # Pin planted contacts, after the reading
>
> A markerless solve places each joint per frame, so a foot the clip shows as planted wanders by whatever
> the estimate wandered by. Retargeting adds a second source, because the target's legs are not the
> performer's. The clip then reads as skating, and every structural check passes. This repair is
> deterministic and needs no judgment per clip, so it belongs in the pipeline and not in a later cleanup
> by hand or by an agent.
>
> ## The repair
>
> 1. **Take contact from the solver if it reports it.** Decide per foot part, heel and ball separately,
>    because a heel raise pivots on a ball that stays put. Use hysteresis, and drop contacts too short to be
>    real. One open implementation enters at 0.8 confidence, exits at 0.5, and drops contacts under 0.1 s. It
>    states no basis for those numbers, so treat them as a starting point.
> 2. **Pin each planted part at its mean position over the contact.** Do this on the target rig, after
>    retargeting, because that is where the visible slide is.
> 3. **Fade the correction over a short window each side of the contact,** or the swing leg pops. The same
>    implementation uses 0.15 s.
> 4. **When heel and ball are both down, let the ball win.** The two pins come from different contacts and
>    are not a rigid foot length apart. Forcing both twists the foot.
> 5. **Solve the leg with two-bone IK, and keep the knee in the plane of its original bend.**
> 6. **When a straight leg cannot reach, move the hips by the smallest shift that brings every planted leg
>    into reach,** and count those frames.
> 7. **Ground as a separate step.** A pin holds a foot where it already was, and that height belongs to the
>    recovered skeleton. Set the lowest skinned vertex of the planted part on the floor by shifting the hips.
>
> ## Take the reading first
>
> The depth drift of a planted contact is how a single view shows it has run out. The pin removes that
> drift on every axis, so after it the reading is zero whether the depth was right or not. Measure before
> the pin, and keep the pin's mean and maximum correction, its hip-shift frames and its out-of-reach frames
> with the clip. **A large correction is a finding about the solve, not proof that the repair worked.**
>
> ## Record that it ran
>
> A repair with no contact signal to read does nothing and raises no error. The clip's record states whether
> the repair ran and its numbers, and a missing record reads as unmeasured, not as clean.
>
> ## When not to use it
>
> - **On a clip keyed in place.** Its feet slide because the root does not travel.
> - **On a clip whose feet are fused.** The trackability gate rejects it first.
> - **To clear drift outside contact.** Horizontal root drift between contacts is untouched.
> - **As a quality verdict.** A pinned clip can still be lifeless.
