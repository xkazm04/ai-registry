# MiMo-V2.6 - can an open-weight release compete in the local stack?

**Operator dispatch folded into a vendor release.** The question was whether
we could download these models and compare them against our local
media-generation and coding models, and if so, how to make them compete.

## Class and expected yield

A **vendor release announcement**, reliable for its numbers and nothing else.
Expected yield said before the table: a currency signal, leads, and no upper-layer
landing unless the *local* half of the question found a gap. It did.

**The container failed, not the source.** `research-ingest` returned 3 words and
exit 3. The page is a client-rendered shell, and WebFetch saw the same empty body.
The release was read from the Hugging Face organisation page, two model cards and
release coverage instead. That is 5 fetches + 2 searches against a budget of 3.
The overspend is deliberate, because the operator asked for web research, and the
fetch *was* the extraction here.

## What was established

- Released 2026-09-21 under MIT, ungated: **Pro** 1.02T total / 42B active,
  **Flash** 309B / 15B active (48 layers, 39 sliding-window + 9 global, 256
  experts), both any-modality in, **text out**, with a 1M window. Also released:
  **Distill-Qwen-9B** (image+text in, text out, Qwen3.5-9B base, hybrid SSM +
  attention), 7,000+ RL environments, and an RL framework.
- 13 GGUF repositories exist for the 9B model, including an official one with a
  vision projector. They need llama.cpp b10964 or newer.
- The machine has 24 GB of accelerator memory + 64 GB of host memory.

Row 1 ran under the Phase 5 score. Rows 2-4 ran under the corroboration table.

**Row 1: why it is a gap, and the stage it sits at.** The benchmark golden path
enumerates what drifts ("the cases drift / the sampling drifts / the method
drifts / the completeness drifts"). Every item assumes a hosted target, whose
latency is a property of the model. The enumeration hunt found the missing
member: for a self-hosted target, latency is a property of model x *placement*,
and placement moves with the window. `extraction-model-bake-off` says "resident
memory" once, in step 6, as a unit and with no model (the declared focus's
formula hunt: a term present and unmodelled). The stage is *before* the matrix:
admitting a target at all. Nothing sat there.

**Corroboration.** A direct measurement on this machine, plus a primary model
card (total vs active parameters). Placement read back from the serving layer:

| Target | Window | Resident | Accelerator / host |
| - | - | - | - |
| 27B Q4_K_M | 32k | 24 GB | 90 / 10 |
| 27B Q4_K_M | 64k | 27 GB | 70 / 30 |
| 12B | 32k | 10 GB | 100 / 0 |

Cold loads took 16-51 s.

## Apply (Phase 7.5)

`self-hosted-residency-is-part-of-the-target` x pof, mode **experiment**, verdict
**unmeasurable**. The seam is pof's vision provider. It pins its window at 8k
(declared, good) but records no placement, so no result row can show whether an
arm was spilled. The experiment above shows the split exists on this machine. It
does not show it at pof's 8k window. Instrument that would measure it: Arena V
of the plan, reading placement per arm. **The seam was chosen to falsify:** had
the incumbent been fully resident at every window, the technique's latency claim
would have been decoration here. It was not, at 32k and above.
