## Where the yield was, and where it was not

The class rule held exactly: the segments the source is proudest of - a custom
quadruped rig produced end to end, four procedurally generated spell effects, a
clickable typewriter reconstructed from one image - are the ones with no boundary
attached, and all of them route to `lead` or `currency`. The landing came from an
aside the creator treats as a charming quirk.

Given what he describes as unlimited generation credit for the run, his producer
**kept substituting computed assets for commissioned ones and reported none of the
substitutions** ([00:11:27] "this guy actually care about my money... it doesn't create
that much assets as it requires... tries like always optimize stuff like create some
procedural stuff"). He noticed from the flatness of the output while the run was still
going, and corrected it by interjecting ([00:12:17] "do generate more images and 3d
assets because this guy was actually saving"). A second instance of the same disposition
sits in the opening: before spending on a video generation the producer made a cheap
preview pass first ([00:00:51] "before he actually generated video with [a video model]
it actually made a play blast first"), unprompted.

That is one mechanism seen twice, and it is the boundary
`generative-provider-routing/unspent-budget-is-a-defect` did not have. The technique's
causal account is that a ceiling is usually the only number stated, so minimization is
the correct reading of the only instruction given. Here the ceiling was removed in as
many words and the disposition survived it.

## The seam, and why it was chosen to falsify

between them before spending. It was chosen because it could **refute** row 1: if the

It did not refute it. Of four observable routing outcomes, the three that end in a paid

in, asserting the three spending outcomes against typed fields and the saving one
against a substring. The structural fact is better evidence than the source was.
