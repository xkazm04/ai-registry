# Lago - metering and usage-based billing

**The run's one-line result: the corpus was missing an entire seat.** It models the
*payer* of a metered service (`cost-metering`) and the *consumer* of a payment
provider (`plan-entitlements`), and had nothing at all for the party that charges
its own customers for usage. `research-map` returns nothing corpus-wide for
`monetization`, `proration` or `rounding`.

**Three techniques into the existing `plan-entitlements`** - the three places where
that subject's rules INVERT once the entitlement model is a product surface served
to third parties rather than a private in-app tier table:

- `provider-publishes-state-not-decision` - inverts `capability-gate-predicates`'
  *"The most valuable move in this technique is refusing the boolean"*. The provider
  refuses the **decision** instead, for the corpus's own reason: one level up, those
  four outcomes are the *consumer's* enumeration, and a provider computing them
  would be the second tier model the golden path warns against. It cannot know
  whether an operator's `max: 0` means nothing or everything.
- `inherited-tier-not-cloned-tier` - the override service copies five collections
  into a child plan for a bespoke price and **deliberately skips entitlements**; all
  five entitlement call sites climb to `plan.parent_id || plan.id`. Price overrides
  copy, capability overrides inherit, and a record carrying both cannot express the
  difference.
- `subtractive-entitlement` - a negative grant: a row naming a thing it does *not*
  confer. Inverts *"the grant records what conferred it"*. Needed the moment a tier
  is inherited rather than assigned, because "everything in the plan except X"
  cannot be written as positive grants without cloning the plan - the forbidden move
  above. Paired with the collapse rule: an override *equal* to the plan value is
  normalized out of existence, so the subscription keeps tracking the plan.

**A worker argued an override and was half right.** The `inherited-tier-not-cloned-tier`
brief overstated the novelty: `tier-model-single-source` already says "express it as
an override layered over the tier model ... do not fork the tier set into
per-customer tiers". The distinct claim survives (that bullet forbids forking the
*vocabulary*; the new technique governs what a legitimate per-tenant record may
carry), but the overlap was real, so the old bullet now points at the new technique
instead of half-answering. That edit was made under the `content` lock, held for
seconds.

## Applied - kp, `task`/`better`, and it shipped

**Seam.** kp keys its monthly allowance ledger on `currentPeriod(now)` - a UTC
**calendar** month - at `entitlements.ts:210` and `:292`, while the provider charges
on the subscription's **anniversary**. kp already stores the true period
(`billing_state.current_period_start/end`, `db/billing.ts:46-47`) and reads it only
for expiry and display. The two windows coincide only for a subscription anchored on
the 1st.

**Instrument, calibrated before it was believed.** Two known-answer cases first: an
anchor on the 1st MUST yield 1 window, a mid-month anchor MUST yield 2. Both
separated before any measurement was read.

**Measured, over 10 representative anchors:**

- A (kp today, calendar-month key): **9/10 over-grant** - two allowances inside one paid period
- B (anniversary key, day clamped to the month's length): **0/10**
- B0 (control - the first draft of B, comparing the raw anchor day): **1/10**

The control earns its place: B0 broke on the `2026-01-31` anchor, reproducing the
clamp drift that `anchor-re-derived-not-chained` exists to describe. The correction
was worth exactly the month-end case, which is the technique predicting its own
violation.

The error direction is an **over-grant** - the invisible half of the pair
`plan-entitlements` names: *"A gate that under-grants generates support tickets; a
gate that over-grants generates revenue that was never collected."* Nobody reports
this one.

**Shipped the instrument, not the fix** (kp `66ee5f0d`, hook green, not pushed).
Re-keying changes the persisted `billing_usage.period` format and needs a cut-over
decision - a mid-period re-key must not hand a customer a *third* allowance - so the
B arm is a plan at `.ai/tasks/2026-09-07-allowance-period-anchor.md` and the pinned
`9` goes red the day it lands.

**Second independent sighting, same fleet.** ascent has the identical defect at
`credits.ts:306` - `monthStart` is a UTC calendar-month start, independent of the
subscription anchor. Not separately A/B'd (one project per finding per run), but two
independent projects with the same hole is what makes this a technique rather than
an anecdote.

## The structural fact neither project designed

The operator asked whether kp and ascent's Polar work could contribute. It did, and
the sharpest thing it gave was an **absence**.

kp's enterprise tier says entitlement is *"granted per signed contract (a mapped
product or a manual grant)"* and resolves to `limits: {all null}`. ascent's is
`includedCredits: null, unlimited: true`, with its own comment conceding that
enterprise bullets *"describe the DIMENSIONS that get scoped in the conversation"*.
A grep of kp's billing module for any override mechanism returns **zero**:
`billing_state` is one row per org carrying a plan id, and limits come only from
`PLANS[plan].limits`.

So both projects, independently, collapse "negotiated per-customer deal" to a single
`unlimited` boolean. Neither can represent *Growth plus 500 extra minutes*. Nobody
designed that; it fell out of having a tier record and no override layer - which is
precisely what `subtractive-entitlement` and `inherited-tier-not-cloned-tier` are
about. The fleet's two Polar consumers are the negative evidence for the techniques
the provider's tree supplied.

## Leads (3)

- **`half-open-interval-policy` has a boundary case.** Lago's intervals are
  closed-closed (`to` at 23:59:59, events selected `>= from AND <= to`), which that
  technique says double-counts. It survives because the boundary is a *date*, and pays
  with a hand-written `+ 1.second` stitch. **Two corrections the forge worker made to
  this run's own dispatch, both right:** the policy is a **technique** of
  `analytics-time-windows`, not a law - the director's brief said law, and citing it in
  a `laws:` list would have failed the gate - so it was linked relatively and the
  boundary stated in prose instead. And the boundary is sharper than "a date": half-open
  is correct whenever the boundary's identity is an **instant**; when the identity is a
  date and conversion to instants happens once at the storage edge, consecutive periods
  cannot share an edge by construction. Adopting closed-closed while *scattering* the
  adjustment takes the cost without the benefit. `_laws.md` untouched. **Return when**
  a second independent tree treats a closed-closed interval as correct on a date
  boundary - two sightings and `analytics-time-windows` gains the case directly.
- **`legacy_inputs/` outlived its decision.** One file, `BaseLegacyInput`, **zero**
  references repo-wide (director-verified). A directory named for a compatibility
  decision nobody is making any more. **Return when** the corpus grows a subject on
  dead-surface detection, or a second tree shows the same shape.
- **Two read surfaces disagree about provenance.** REST publishes `value`,
  `plan_value`, `override_value` and an `overrides` map; the GraphQL type publishes
  only `value` (director-verified at `schema.graphql:13517-13523` and the serializer).
  One value, three representations across three surfaces. `plan-entitlements` says
  the read gate and the write gate must agree; this is read-surface vs read-surface,
  which the subject does not model. **Return when** a project in the fleet ships a
  second public read surface over one model.

## Untriaged (12), with anchors

Banked with anchors so a later run does not re-derive them. Each carries the one
question that would promote it.

1. **Dunning ladder vs entitlement state** - is there a deliberate state where the
   customer owes money and still has access? (payments A8) *Promoting question: does
   any fleet project have a dunning-grace window whose length is stated as a policy
   rather than a constant?*
2. **The wallet's two balances** - an ongoing estimate (flag-and-drain, flagged on
   every ingested event, clock as batched drainer) beside a settled truth; the clock's
   cache gate is a **cost** gate, not a correctness one. (payments A5, `estimate-and-authority-split`)
3. **Provider abstraction leak, enumerated** - a redirect-only provider appears in the
   customer factory but not the payment factory and has no `payments/` directory; that
   enumeration gap *is* the model difference. (payments A1)
4. **`CHECKOUT_AUTO_PAYMENT_DELAY = 10.minutes`** - the machine waits for a human who
   may be paying the same invoice on a hosted page; no shared key, no shared row, no
   lock either party can join. (payments A3)
5. **Last-statement freshness check** - re-read the invoice one statement before the
   network call, because the three provider round-trips *are* the staleness window. (payments A4)
6. **The v1/v2 overlay is not the migration boundary** - a per-organization rollout
   flag decides which pricing model is authoritative, and the enforcement is mounted on
   the **old** surface (403 on writes, reads left open). (api A1/A3)
7. **The overlay's exit condition is a parity spec, not a date** - a set-difference
   assertion over the router keeps v2 a superset of v1 forever. (api A1)
8. **Webhook signing with two selectable algorithms** per endpoint (symmetric per-org
   key vs asymmetric JWT). Most systems pick one. (api A5)
9. **`retry: 0` by default**, with scheduled retry *jobs* as the real recovery path -
   inverts the normal queue posture. (api A6)
10. **Sparse daily snapshots** - no row on a day with no events, so the day-over-day
    diff is taken against the previous *existing* snapshot; the invariant is
    `SUM(usage_diff) == final usage` exactly. (metering A8)
11. **Age-bounded retry budget** - in the Go processor the retry budget is measured in
    *event age*, not attempt count, and a parse failure commits immediately. Proposed as
    a technique in `delivery-guarantees`, not a subject. (metering A7)
12. **A licence check inside the percentage charge model** changes one fixture's price
    from 301.691 to 16.142 - a pricing behaviour gated on an edition flag. (pricing, Part B)
