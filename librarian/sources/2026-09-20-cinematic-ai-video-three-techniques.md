## The landing: a bar that rides on its own leader

The source's subject is camera and lighting language, so the seam hunt opened
the fleet's video-studio project at the context where a shot's prompt is
composed. That file cites `cinematic-language` techniques **by name** in its
own comments, with two human-gated A/B cycles recorded against them — and the
project's generated context map joins that context to **zero**
`cinematic-language` pairs. The subject appears nowhere in the map at all.

It is not a top-N cut. The context holds three pairs where others hold five or
six. Unfiltered, `cinematic-language` is that context's **#2 match at 732.3,
`use_when`-grounded** — and the admission bar is 921.5, because the bar is 55%
of *this context's best*, and its best is an outlier at 1675.4.

The mechanism generalises, and it is an inversion rather than a distortion:

> **25 of 26 other contexts in the same project admit a pair weaker than the
> one this context rejects** — down to 298.1, a 2.46x inversion. The project's
> own median leader is 666.7, so the rejected pair would be the *strongest*
> match in half the repo's contexts.

The instrument's comment defends the relative cutoff on cross-context
comparability, and that reason is sound — raw scores scale with how much text
a context carries. But twenty lines below, the weak-governance pass computes
`medianTop`, a population statistic, for exactly the same comparability
reason, and admission never reaches for it. That asymmetry — normalised in one
decision, not in the other — is the finding. Phase 6 hunt #4: what a thing
mentions in one place and measures in another.

Landed as a new section in `scoring-rubrics/normalization`. It is an
**amendment and not a technique** because the file's own rule survives:
cohort-relative framing is still right for ranking within a group. What it did
not reach is the many-cohort case, where per-group frames feed a cross-group
decision — and there its stated fix (percentile anchors) does not help either,
because the incomparability is *between* frames, not within one.

## Apply — 2 rows, both `code`, both `better`

**Row 10 → the registry's own join instrument.** Seam chosen to falsify: the
change can only ever *lower* a bar, so the failure it had to be tested for is
junk admission, not recall. A caught outcome would have taught that the
population anchor buys recall by paying in precision, which would have killed
it.

- target: pairs the map can see, fleet-wide — **4208 → 4689 (+481, +11.4%)**,
  **461 of 481 `use_when`-grounded**. The named prediction held:
  `cinematic-language` joins the context whose source file cites it.
- floor: **0 pairs lost**; contexts with no map unchanged at 6; max pairs per
  context unchanged at 9; and the lexical-only share of the corpus **improved**,
  6.18% → 5.99% — the recovered pairs are better grounded than the population
  they joined. Contexts above the nominal cap go 35 → 41, a pre-existing
  merge behaviour present in both arms, not a new one.

**The measurement refuted itself once, and the correction is worth more than
the number.** A first pass reported 11 and then 17 pairs *lost*, which is
structurally impossible for a bar that only lowers. The cause was not the
treatment: two sibling intake runs committed to the corpus at 11:56, between
the arm-A build and the arm-B build. Interleaving the arms per project — A then
B, seconds apart — returned 0 lost. **In a shared checkout a paired measurement
must interleave its arms, not run them as two passes**, or it measures the
neighbours.

**Row 11 → the fleet's video A/B harness.** Found by using it: it cached a
readback entry whose every tag held only an error, and its resume guard is
`key not in readbacks`, so the failure was permanent and the run still printed
`4/4 read back`. Paired on the same cycle — HEAD: 4 cached / 0 successful /
`4/4` / 0 retried. Fixed: 0 cached / `0/4` / 4 retried. Floor: a seeded
successful entry is still cached, still skipped on resume, still counted.
Shipped, not pushed.

## Render proof — run, and refused

Row 2 is render-bound (it changes what a generator is told, in a bundle whose
only observable is the output), and the local instrument exists, so it could
not be banked as a lead for want of one. Both arms were rendered on the local
text-to-video stack through the fleet's own paired runner, at two seeds, one
variable: the **position** of an identical camera clause — declared as a
property of the shot (the shape the fleet's own composer emits) versus placed
at its beat in chronological order. Same content words, same speed adverb in
both so that was not the variable.

The director's pre-read was a story: at seed 7 the shot-property arm ran the
move **backwards**, opening tight on the padlock and ending wide on the figure,
while the beat-anchored arm ran it forwards. The gate refused it.

What *is* seed-robust is a fact about the premise rather than the treatment:

unit on this model in either arm, which is the thing to measure before the
onset question is even well-posed.
