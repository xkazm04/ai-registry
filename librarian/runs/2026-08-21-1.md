# Run 2026-08-21-1 - merging parallel work, and one defect it exposed

The `registry-longevity` branch merged into `main`, which had moved underneath it: a
new 64-subject `recruiting` bundle, a `voice-io` subject extended, and the bundle
digest made checkout-stable. Structural work only; no research worker was dispatched.

## What merged

Four content conflicts, all resolved rather than taken from one side:

- **`recruiting` arrived flat** with a `categories.json`, which this branch had replaced
  with `taxonomy.json`. Converted: 9 categories, one of which (`decision-and-fairness`,
  12 subjects) was over the cap and gained two subcategories along the seam the subjects
  already had - how a decision gets *made* versus what makes it *defensible*.
  `assessment` sits at exactly 10 and was deliberately left alone: the rule subdivides
  *above* ten, and pre-empting it would defeat the hysteresis the rule exists to provide.
- **`voice-io` gained a technique and an application on main** while this branch moved
  the subject three levels down. Git's directory-rename detection placed both files
  correctly, but their relative links were written at the old depth and pointed nowhere.
  The bundle gate caught all four. **Any branch authored before the nesting carries
  flat-depth links, and merging it is a link repair, not just a text merge** - the gate
  is what makes that repair findable rather than shipped.
- **`verified_on` was stamped wrong on 190 documents.** The backfill looked dates up
  with `git log -- <path>` against HEAD's history, and the recruiting files exist only on
  main, so every lookup came back empty and stamped today. Corrected from the real
  authoring dates, with a rule that matters more than this incident: **a move must never
  reset an age.** Re-deriving dates for already-migrated subjects would have found the
  *migration* commit and pushed 451 documents forward to the day their folder moved,
  making the whole corpus look freshly verified when nothing was re-verified.
  `verified_on` only ever moves earlier here; only a real re-check moves it forward.

## The defect this run exposed

**`build-catalog.mjs` had been publishing false counts for two commits.** It walked
`<domain>/<subject>/techniques` at a fixed depth, so once bundles nested it counted
*category* folders as subjects: `catalog.json` claimed "4 subjects / 0 techniques" for a
bundle holding 15 subjects and 91 techniques, and shipped that twice.

`--check` stayed green the entire time, because it compares the committed file against a
fresh build of the same wrong logic. **Freshness is not correctness**, and nothing in the
repository was asking the second question.

Fixed two ways. The walker now resolves subjects like everything else. And
`build-catalog.mjs` now cross-checks its own counts against each bundle's `index.json` -
two independent counts of one corpus that disagree mean one is wrong, and it exits fatal
rather than publishing a number it knows is contradicted. The check was proved by
injecting a disagreement before it was trusted.

The lesson generalises past this script: **every consumer of the corpus that assumed a
fixed depth broke at the migration, and the ones with input assertions announced it while
the one without published falsehoods quietly.** `check-currency.mjs` refused to report
"0 expired" from a walk that found nothing and exited fatal; `build-catalog.mjs` had no
such assertion and reported zeros as facts.
