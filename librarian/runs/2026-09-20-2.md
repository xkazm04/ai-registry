Run while the operator was away, with the standing instruction to leave blockers. Two
things ran in parallel: a general `/conform` wave on the projects that had never been
judged, and the lead drain that [[2026-09-20-1]]'s banking note called for — **batched by

The blockers are in [`docs/plans/fleet-backfill-2026-09-20.md`](../../docs/plans/fleet-backfill-2026-09-20.md).

## The drain — 22 leads, 4 bundles

3 NEW TECHNIQUE · 13 AMEND · 5 APPLICATION · 1 COVERED · 0 DECLINE.

The bundle batching worked: eight subjects in one batch, three in another, and no worker
had to read a subject it did not land in. Two more published rules were wrong.

- **`paired-per-case-testing` contradicted itself.** Step 2 said refuse to pair on any
  case-set mismatch; its own "When not to use it" said do not pair the intersection
  *without disclosing it*. The witnessing tree follows the second and argues the first is
  harmful — "refusing outright would delete the best line from most real matrices and buy
  no correctness." Step 2 now refuses only the four genuinely unmatched cases.
- **`declare-what-each-engine-ignores` stated a false absolute** — "every cross-generator
  pair answers no; that the answer is always no is the point." A replay engine consuming
  the preview's exported cells makes the agreement return **true**. Corrected to "every
  *regenerating* pair", with the route to a yes written down.

Three leads consolidated into one game-production technique: *a producer fact is observed,
never declared*. A capability the code can be asked about, an attestation about remote
work, and a fact nothing can observe are three shapes of one rule.

## What the wave taught the method — conform 1.7.0 → 1.7.3

Every one of these came from a worker running the method against real code, and each is
now in the skill:

1. **Commit by pathspec, not only stage by one.** A bare `git commit` takes the whole
   index; one run swept a sibling's staged file and recovered. Another repo was holding
   sixty-five staged files at the time.
2. **Take `evaluatedAgainst` from the registry index, not the pair.** Five maps were rebuilt
   at 14:35 and a technique landed the same afternoon.
3. **Read the golden path's "when NOT to use this" before adding a zero-scored pairing.**
   It stopped unearned pairings on card grids, a log feed, a four-card deck and a whole
   repository.
4. **"It resolves" is the wrong anchor check — a blank line resolves.** Four of twenty-three
   anchors in one run pointed at whitespace, a `*/`, or the line above the construct.
5. **A context merge promotes the flattering verdict.** A `conformant` and a contradicting
   `deviation` collided; the `conformant` survived onto the merged context and the true
   `deviation` was orphaned out of sight for four weeks.
6. **The map's `paths` is a twelve-path sample and it changes verdicts.** In one repo the
   sole anchor for a surviving deviation was outside the sample in three separate contexts.
7. **Never copy an orphan's `digest`** — it names the build that orphaned the pair.

## Two failure modes of the matcher, both measured

Recorded in [`docs/matcher-reach.md`](../../docs/matcher-reach.md).

- **It rewards subject size.** The score is a sum over matching tokens, so a subject with
  twenty-one techniques outranks one with six regardless of precision. `table` ranks #8 on
  a context whose files are `UnifiedTable.tsx`/`DataGrid.tsx`, and `TOP = 5` cuts it.
- **It pairs a subject to the layer that talks about the thing rather than the layer that
  is it.** `job-coordination` scored 545 on a BFF proxy and **zero** on the context holding
  the job table and the owner heartbeat; `mcp-tools` matched seventeen of one repo's
  forty-six contexts and none of the MCP code. Found independently three times.

Measured against the 53 pairings readers established despite the matcher — the only
uncontaminated labels the fleet has — the join publishes **6%** of them.

## A correction to this run's own instrument

`check-context-coverage.mjs` first counted test files in the denominator and reported the
fleet at 41% unreachable. A `/conform` worker in pumper re-measured its own repo, found
89% of non-test source declared against the 43% the brief handed it, and said so. Tests are
47% of that repo's matched files. Corrected to **38% unreachable, 62% reachable**; the
shape of the finding is unchanged and the headline number was wrong.
