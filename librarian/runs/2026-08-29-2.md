# Run 2026-08-29-2 — wave 2, and the end of localization's single-source debt

Six workers on the six subjects wave 1 left behind. All six landed; none rejected;
no wrong citation found. **Every subject in the bundle now carries a second
source** — 42 applications across 14 subjects — and the domain's attention score
fell from **67 to 2**, with thirteen subjects at zero.

The residual 2 is `translation-pipeline-topology`, and it is the proxy failing
rather than work left undone: that subject reads three *distinct sources* (a
desktop app, a web app, a foreign repository) which are all class-A tree readings
and so all legitimately `process`-stack. The brief says plainly that the stack was
always the proxy for the source; two of the three applications also predate the
`source:` convention, and the brief forbids backfilling it.

## The convergence is the run, and the sightings disagree

Four subjects were deliberately pointed at the same counterpart with an explicit
instruction not to coordinate and to write nothing about each other. The point was
to buy independent sightings of the plural-range mechanism, banked at one sighting
from wave 1's `arabic`. It worked, and it produced something better than agreement:

| locale group | range rows | rows overriding the spec default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ar` | 23 | 5 |
| `cs pl sk` | 14 | **0** |
| `ca es` | 3 | 1 |
| `be lt ru uk` | 16 | **0** |
| `fr pt` | 3 | **0**, and every `many` pair absent |

All verified by the director. **From `arabic` alone the family would have been
written as "range tables override the default" — and three of the four new
sightings disprove that.** The real rule is the mechanism: range selection is a
`(start, end)` pair lookup whose default is the **end** category; a published row
may *confirm* or *override* that default, so counting rows tells you nothing. The
`russian` worker put the consequence best — a run that only counts rows will
misread agreement as override.

That is five sightings. Four opens a law conversation, which the cycle is
explicitly forbidden to write. **The conversation is opened here and nothing more.**

Second family, three sightings, ready to land as technique edits: **a plural
category can be a property of the rendering rather than the value** — `czech`
(`many` *is* `v != 0`), `russian` (every non-`other` rule guards `v = 0`, so a
fixed-decimal surface puts 100% of counts in `other`), `spanish` and `french`
(compact notation moves one quantity between two categories).

## The best single findings

- **`german`:** the technique's safety advice is the unsafe half. It recommends
  presentation-layer uppercasing one sentence after naming ẞ the preferred
  all-caps form — but that is exactly where the *default* mappings run, and 0 of
  67 sample strings produce U+1E9E under any of them. ß→SS is unconditional and
  language-independent; `de` appears nowhere in `SpecialCasing.txt`. Runner-up:
  **no single caseless key serves German** — full folding matches
  `Straße`/`STRASSE` but merges `Maße`/`Masse`; simple folding does the reverse.
- **`spanish`:** *"2,5 millones de descargas"* is right compact and **wrong** for
  `2.500.000 descargas`. `2000000` plain is `many`; `2500000` plain is `other`;
  the same 2.5M compact is `many`. RBNF shows why — the rule tracks whether the
  *spelled* numeral ends in *millón/millones*, which is exactly when the noun
  attaches with *de*.
- **`french`:** `one` is the half-open interval **[0, 2)**, not "0 and 1" — 1,999
  selects `one`. And a `selectordinal` block for French **cannot express 1er vs
  1re**: CLDR's own ordinal minimal pairs are feminine, and the gender lives only
  in RBNF.
- **`russian`:** the control is the finding. A hand-rolled integer-modulo selector
  disagrees with CLDR on **0 of 1001 integers and 10001 of 10001 one-decimal
  values** — correct on exactly the inputs a developer would test with, wrong on
  every input they would not.
- **`czech`:** the technique's prose is right and its own summary table
  contradicts it four lines later. A defect that exists only in the table is
  precisely what a reading-based review misses.
- **`hindi`:** the odd five-category ordinal set is a *suffix inventory*, not a
  numeric pattern — which is why 6 earns a category and 5 does not.

## Upstream-reportable

- **CLDR 48.2 `SHASUM512.txt`** lists a digest for `cldr-common-48.2.zip` that
  belongs to `core.zip`. First seen in wave 1 by `arabic`; **independently
  reproduced here by `french`**, which computed the same served-file digest. Two

- **CLDR Hindi `ordinalMinimalPairs` for `few` carries no ordinal suffix** — the
  one pair whose job is to distinguish `few` does not. Byte-identical across
  releases 47, 48.2 and 49-alpha1, so not a regression. Verified by the director.

## The director was wrong four times, across two waves

Recorded because the pattern matters more than any one error. Wave 1: a
`vietnamese` pre-check read against unreleased CLDR `main` produced a false "the
corpus contradicts itself" claim. Wave 2: the `russian` prompt called its range
table "the richest in the corpus" (it ties for fourth) and gave a worked example
that was backwards; the `spanish` prompt asserted CLDR says nothing about gender,
when `grammaticalFeatures.xml` and RBNF both carry it.

**Workers caught all four, and every correction improved the finding.** The
contract is doing its job. The lesson for future dispatches is narrower than "be
careful": a director's pre-check is *reconnaissance*, and the prompt must say so —
the prompts that worked best were the ones that named a suspicion and told the
worker that refuting it was a legitimate outcome.
