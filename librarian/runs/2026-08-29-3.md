# Run 2026-08-29-3 — wave 3, consuming the surface the brief called its best lead

Three workers against **spell-out rulesets** and **numbering-system data** — the two
counterpart surfaces wave 2 identified and left unconsumed. All three landed. Every
subject in this bundle already had a second source, so this wave targeted *uncovered

bundle's single-source debt is discharged.

## The surface paid off, and it is reusable

The brief flagged the spell-out rulesets as the best unconsumed lead because they carry
gendered, cased and system-split numerals that plural data cannot express. Two workers
independently wrote interpreters for the published rule syntax and both reached parity
with their oracle — one against the publisher's reference implementation at **6030/6030**,
the other validated by a **cross-encoding check** (the file encodes its rules twice; both
encodings parsed to the same 1380 rows). Both harnesses are locale-agnostic. **The surface
is proven executable and the tooling transfers to any language subject in the bundle.**

## Findings

- **Korean.** `KO-PLURAL-OTHER` is right at 1 and wrong at 2 and 3: the ordinal count
  ruleset delegates to the **attributive** forms from 2 up. And the alternation is
  conditioned on the *following morpheme* rather than on a stem table — the two ordinal
  series differ at 21 of 99 values, with a private ruleset existing solely to restore the
  independent form in the hundreds residue. `KO-NUM-UNIT`'s "always Sino-Korean" is
  refuted for the counter the technique itself names.
- **Arabic.** Both of `AR-NUMERALS`' named regional claims are backwards — the Gulf locale
  it cites resolves to Western digits, and a Maghreb locale declares Arabic-Indic ones.
  Counted: **21 of 29 declare `arab`, zero declare `latn`**, 7 carry the inheritance
  marker. Base `ar` resolves to Western digits, so a product shipping the tag products
  actually ship gets the opposite of what the sentence implies. Two further refutations:
  guillemets appear in **zero of 29** delimiter elements, and the tatweel denylist **fires
  on the technique's own prose**.
- **Russian.** Gender changes the numeral at exactly two positions — units-1 and units-2,
  teens excluded — so **82% of integers spell identically in all four genders**. The
  technique's gender half is entirely verbs and participles and omits the numeral
  altogether.

## Three upstream defects, all verified by the director

Two are Russian rule-data faults: a masculine oblique ruleset carrying **nominative** forms
at two of its rows while all three sibling genders are oblique (2000 of 10000 integers
affected, and long-standing rather than a regression), and two ordinal rulesets emitting a
**Latin letter** where every sibling uses its Cyrillic homoglyph. The third is Arabic and
is the sharper one: **the counterpart violates the technique's own rule** — unit patterns
hardcode Arabic-Indic digits beside a placeholder that renders in the resolved system, so
plain `ar` produces one string with two digit systems in it.

None filed. Together with wave 1's manifest-digest defect and wave 2's suffix-less minimal
pair, the corpus now holds **five** verified upstream candidates awaiting an operator
decision.

## A control that passed, and why it is recorded

Russian's third control dropped the teen guard and produced **zero** errors — the very
guard the sibling plural application measured as load-bearing at 400/10001, because the
teens have no gendered forms. *Same-looking guard, opposite verdict.* A guard's necessity
is a property of the rule it sits in, not of the language, and a run that generalises one
measurement across a subject's techniques will be wrong.

## Two workers, two opposite oracle decisions, both right

Wave 2's Russian worker **declined** to use a reference implementation, on the brief's
grounds that it is class-A evidence about that implementation. Wave 3's Korean worker
**used** one — because its counterpart ships no sample sets at all, making the reference
implementation the only available oracle. Both said plainly which they had. The brief's
new B1/B2/B3 split is exactly what makes those two decisions legible as consistent rather
than contradictory, and this run is its first test.
