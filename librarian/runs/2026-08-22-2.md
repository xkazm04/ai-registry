## What the wave proved about the lane

**The hints were wrong twice, and the workers caught both by reading.** Prisma
v8 has no client-side dataloader (the batching hint was training-data-stale
against a restructured monorepo); Litestream v0.5.x replaced shadow-WAL
generations with LTX/TXIDs. Both workers pivoted to the technique the tree
actually evidences. A lane that assigned techniques rigidly instead of
evidence-first would have produced two forced documents.

**The citation re-check pass earns its cost every single time.** Catches per
worker: 5 (migrations), 7+ (embedded-db, including two wrong test-file counts),
13 (sync-replication), 12 (data-access) — roughly one error per ten citations
drafted, uniform across four independent Opus workers. Line-cite drift under
long reads is a property of the method, not of one careless draft; the re-check
stays mandatory at any scale.

**Deviations in world-class trees are the lane's richest product.** The wave's
findings include: a migration runner able to record a step as applied that
never ran (buffering errors swallowed at nine sites, pipe closed without its
error); SQLite's silent WAL-to-rollback fallback having no diagnostic channel
while a lesser hazard logs; Litestream computing the lag pair in one function
that only tests call — so lag is never surfaced; and Prisma's ORM mutation path
carrying a second, weaker transaction wrapper that can pool a suspect
connection. None of these is a bug report; each is the standard holding its
ground against a famous tree.

## The scan half

prisma L5/87 · litestream L4/72 · golang-migrate L2/39 · sqlite L2/28. Full
ladder spread on four world-class repos — the scanner discriminates. And the
sqlite row is the outside-in caveat made concrete: rigor 26 for the most
heavily tested C codebase in existence, because TH3 and the Fossil-side harness
are invisible in the GitHub mirror. No score from this lane publishes without
the outside-in label.
