# Run 2026-08-29-1 — the localization wave, and a bundle the brief had never classified

Eight workers, one subject each, all landed. Zero Director reverts, zero wrong
citations found across roughly a thousand reviewed lines. The wave's own most
useful output is not any single application: it is that **this bundle is the
corpus's best class-B ground and nobody knew, because the brief predates it.**

## The classification the brief was missing

`docs/reconcile-brief.md` enumerates "The 48, classified" across six bundles.
`localization` is in none of them — the brief was written 2026-08-24, before this
bundle existed. So the first director job was to classify it, and the answer
changes the lane's own picture of class B:

> The brief's class B is built around **prose procedures** — a regulation's
> arithmetic worked by hand, a validator run over a fixture. Unicode and CLDR ship
> **machine-readable conformance data**: `LineBreakTest.txt`, `NormalizationTest.txt`,
> and CLDR's `@integer`/`@decimal` sample sets embedded in the rules themselves.
> Executed evidence here is cheap, reproducible and adversarial by construction —
> a degenerate implementation fails visibly.

Two workers independently wrote UAX #14 implementations and independently scored
**19338 / 19338**. That is a stronger evidence standard than anything the lane has
run to date, and it was available in a bundle nobody had classified.

There is also a large, genuine **class D** residue: `de-anglicization-constructions`
and `register-and-address` appear in all thirteen language subjects and have no
conformance artifact anywhere. They will score single-stack forever. The brief's
`accepted: [single-stack]` instrument is the right answer and is **owed**.

## The director was wrong once, and the contract caught it

The dispatch for `vietnamese` carried a pre-check asserting the corpus contradicted
itself: CLDR gives `vi` two plural categories while the technique claims one, and
`bengali` documents the same rule correctly. **That was wrong.** The pre-check read
CLDR `main`; released CLDR 48.2 still has `vi` single-category, so the technique is
correct for every shipped release and the two subjects do not collide until CLDR 49.

The worker refuted it cleanly — *"the disagreement was a pin, not a fact"* — and
found the better finding underneath: the **ordinal** half of the claim has been false
since CLDR 21 in 2012. A decade-old error rather than a currency lapse.

The instruction that caught this was the one in every dispatch: **pin a release, not
`main`.** It was written to keep citations stable and it also invalidated the
director's own reconnaissance. Worth keeping verbatim in future dispatches, along
with the sentence telling the worker that refuting the director is a legitimate
outcome — three workers used that licence and all three were right to.

## Deviations and declines

- **`language-registry-single-source` was declined by its worker, correctly.** MDN has
  seven hand-maintained locale enumerations that *currently agree on membership*.
  Representation drift is real; membership drift is zero. Binding it would have
  documented seven agreeing lists rather than a registry. Banked: the real
  single-source test is **cross-repo**.
- **`chinese` declined a director hypothesis.** The dispatch suggested a
  width-class-to-column-count conflation; the worker checked, found the technique never
  makes that claim, and said so instead of manufacturing it. `japanese` independently
  checked the same thing against a sibling technique and found it budgets in *ems* —
  the unit UAX #11 §1 itself uses.
- **`arabic` omitted `status:` and flagged it.** It was right: 561 of 561 applications
  corpus-wide carry `status: forged` and the dispatch's frontmatter block omitted it.
  Fixed by the director across all eight at close-out. Documents that came in at
  exactly 130 lines are now 131; the added line is frontmatter, not prose, and trimming
  a finding to fit metadata would be the wrong trade.

## Upstream-reportable candidates (neither reported)

- **CLDR 48.2 checksum manifest.** `hashes/SHASUM512.txt` lists a digest for
  `cldr-common-48.2.zip` that does not match the served file — it is the digest of the
  byte-identical `core.zip`. Verifying the documented download by its documented
  checksum fails. One inference short of airtight.
- **CLDR `bn` percent grouping.** `percentFormats numberSystem="beng"` is `#,##0%`
  (Western) while `numberSystem="latn"` in the same file is `#,##,##0%` (Indic), and
  decimal/currency are Indic for both. Bengali's **default** numbering system therefore
  groups percentages wrongly above 99999%. Verified by the director.
- Not upstream but worth recording: `mdn/translated-content`'s `get-sourceCommit.js`
  emits 1,628 JSON entries and 3,344 CSV rows for the same locale, 1,716 of them
  `undefined` — `JSON.stringify` drops the misses, so the machine-readable format
  silently reports 100% coverage of the exact gap the key exists to expose.

## Instrument notes

- **A summarizing fetch produced a confident false negative.** Spot-checking `arabic`'s
  sharpest citation, a `WebFetch` of the 177 KB UTS #35 Numbers page reported that the
  spec "does not explicitly define" integer range semantics and that no worked example

  `3.5 = 2..4, 15 → false` verbatim, found by curl-and-grep on the raw HTML. **Had the
  summary been trusted, a correct worker would have been wrongly impeached.** Go to the
  raw document before overturning a citation.
- **CLDR `main` is not a release**, and on live data they disagree. See above.
- **Windows path length aborted the first class-A clone** — the `--filter=blob:none`
  promisor file, under the long scratch path. Repaired with a short base
  (`C:/tmp/rec/`) plus sparse checkout, and every worker was told to use it. Second
  recorded instance of the platform fault the brief already names.
- **A sparse-checkout pattern beginning with `/` is mangled by this platform's shell**
  (`!/files` became `!C:/Program Files/Git/files`), so the exclusion silently did
  nothing. Harmless here — it made all 37,200 documents readable without lazy fetches —
  but it means a sparse pin cannot be trusted without verifying what materialized.
